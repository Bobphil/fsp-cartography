# 2. Governing Principle

> **One canonical owner, many projections.**

A Research Region may orient, group, explain, route, expose relationships, and support progressive disclosure.

It may not create a duplicate canonical concept, strengthen a claim, replace a source ledger, create new historical evidence, originate a claim ceiling, or become a second owner of a case.

# 3. Region Registry

The first Research Environment contains seven regions.

## R1 — Governance and Translation

**Purpose:** How observations, models, concepts, judgments, and actions travel across boundaries.

**Primary owners:** FSP-CONSTITUTIONAL-COMPRESSION-1; FSP-FOUR-MOVE-GOVERNANCE-1; FSP-RECOMPOSITION-GOVERNANCE-1.

**Public question:** What survives when a warranted result travels into another context, and what must remain visible when it does not?

## R2 — Situated Agency and Transformation

**Purpose:** Agency as embodied, historically assembled, capability-dependent, institutionally embedded, and transformable.

**Primary owners:** FSP-PRICE-ISMAEL-SITUATED-PRAGMATISM-1; FSP-TRANSFORMATION-ARCHITECTURE-1; FSP-SITUATED-LIFE-NAVIGATION-1; FSP-LIVES-IN-TRANSFORMATION-1.

**Public question:** What can this situated agent actually do from here, what constrains or enables that agency, and how can future reachability change?

## R3 — Mind, Perception, and Phenomenology

**Purpose:** Relations among public scientific description, self-location, active sensing, first-person experience, and qualitative remainder.

**Primary owners:** Objective Phenomenology research architecture; FSP-PRICE-ISMAEL-SITUATED-PRAGMATISM-1; Predictive / Situated Perception research orientation.

**Public question:** How should first-person experience and publicly inspectable scientific descriptions constrain one another without being prematurely collapsed?

## R4 — Intelligence Across Scales

**Purpose:** Compare problem-solving, memory, adaptation, goal-directedness, and intelligence across biological and artificial systems without treating analogy as identity.

**Primary owners:** Heterogeneous Intelligence research architecture; Recursive Scale research architecture; Levin / planaria research dossiers; AI comparator research.

**Public question:** Which structural relations genuinely travel across biological and artificial intelligence, and which do not?

## R5 — Science, Models, and Interoperability

**Purpose:** Diachronic scientific inquiry, model change, underdetermination, controlled revision, and cross-domain interoperability.

**Primary owners:** FSP Five-Case Atlas; Duhem–Quine research sequence; Kuhn / Meaning Change; Thought Experiments; Ontological Interoperability research architecture.

**Public question:** How do scientific maps change, recombine, and travel across theory, evidence, scale, and ontology?

## R6 — Institutions, Power, and Ethical Coordination

**Purpose:** Standing, power, authority, consent, institutional capability, ethical coordination, and cooperation without convergence.

**Primary owners:** Judgment / Authorization architecture; Connected-Doing / institutional research; Cooperation without Convergence research; Ethics / Power & Flourishing architecture.

**Public question:** How can plural agents coordinate action without confusing practical power, expertise, legitimacy, consent, and moral authorization?

## R7 — Completion Horizon

**Purpose:** Govern research toward increasingly comprehensive integration without declaring present completion.

**Primary owners:** FSP-ECOSYSTEM-RECONCILIATION-2; Research Completion Ladder; Completion-Horizon research material.

**Public question:** What would increasingly comprehensive understanding have to explain, translate, and preserve before it could responsibly claim greater completion?

# 4. Region Registry Rule

Each region record must contain a stable region ID, public title, one public question, short description, canonical owner references, related regions, orientation artifact status, public readiness status, claim ceiling, and prohibited semantic drift.

No region record may contain an independent canonical definition of an owned concept.

# 5. Orientation-Page Grammar

A Research Orientation page should read as an essay, not a reference sheet.

Every orientation page must contain seven functions:

1. **Opening Problem** — begin with the human, scientific, or philosophical problem that gives the region stakes.
2. **Existing Maps** — identify the major kinds of description currently in play.
3. **Translation Burdens** — show where one description is being carried into another.
4. **FSP Contribution** — explain which FSP distinctions or governance structures clarify the problem.
5. **Claim Ceiling** — state what the current architecture does not establish.
6. **Research Frontier** — identify unresolved empirical, formal, conceptual, or translational work.
7. **Deeper Routes** — route to canonical concepts, cases, research builds, source ledgers, and related regions.

# 6. Orientation-Page Style Contract

Orientation pages should lead with readable prose, use technical vocabulary only when it earns explanatory value, preserve the domain's own language, make FSP appear as a governance architecture rather than a replacement science, distinguish source-supported claims from research proposals, preserve Remainder, and invite deeper inspection.

Orientation pages should not read like schema documentation, flatten the domain into Four-Move terminology, imply that FSP owns the domain, hide speculative status, or overstate present integration.

# 7. Orientation Ownership

The orientation page owns public framing, connective explanation, route selection, and progressive disclosure.

It does not own canonical concepts, source claims, case histories, empirical results, or claim ceilings.

# 9. Agency / Mind / Intelligence Nexus

The Research Environment should expose one cross-region nexus without creating a new region.

The nexus is:

**SITUATED AGENCY ↔ PERCEPTION / SELF-LOCATION ↔ CAPABILITY / TRANSFORMATION ↔ BIOLOGICAL INTELLIGENCE ↔ AI / DISTRIBUTED AGENCY ↔ CONSCIOUSNESS / PHENOMENOLOGY ↔ ETHICAL INTERVENTION**

This is a route graph.

It is not a new ontology.

# 10. Nexus Entry Points

A reader may enter through any of these questions:

- **Agency:** What kind of agency exists here?
- **Perception:** What can this system access and how is that access organized?
- **Transformation:** What capabilities, constraints, and future states can change?
- **Intelligence:** What problem space is being navigated?
- **AI / Distributed Agency:** Where are generation, recommendation, authorization, and responsibility distributed?
- **Phenomenology:** What relation is warranted between public structure and lived experience?
- **Ethical Intervention:** What may responsibly be changed, by whom, with what Return?

# 11. Nexus Routing Rule

Every nexus edge must declare source region, target region, structural relation, portability burden, claim ceiling, and remainder.

Example:

**Biological intelligence → consciousness**

Relation: possible organizational relevance.

Portability burden: problem-solving does not automatically establish subjective experience.

Claim ceiling: behavioral organization can motivate consciousness research but does not by itself establish phenomenology.

Remainder: qualitative experience.

# 12. Nexus Anti-Collapse Rule

The following remain distinct:

**AGENCY ≠ INTELLIGENCE ≠ CONSCIOUSNESS ≠ CAPABILITY ≠ MORAL STANDING ≠ AUTHORIZATION**

They may be related.

No route may silently substitute one for another.

# 13. Lives in Transformation — Public Contract

The public Lives route is a projection of FSP-LIVES-IN-TRANSFORMATION-1.

It may expose bounded biographical state, transformation timeline, source provenance, intended / actual distinction, capability and constraint change, historical Return, exemplar portability.

It may not claim total motive access, true first-person possession, complete causal history, inevitability, or exemplary authority.

# 14. Lives Public Route

**PERSON → BIOGRAPHY MAP → TRANSFORMATION TIMELINE → CRITICAL TRANSITION → SOURCE LEDGER → INTENDED VS ACTUAL → CAPABILITY / CONSTRAINT DELTA → OPENED / CLOSED FUTURES → HISTORICAL RETURN → WHAT MIGHT TRAVEL? → WHAT MUST NOT TRAVEL? → SITUATED COMPARISON → OPTIONAL LIFE NAVIGATION**

# 15. Lives Hindsight Contract

The route must preserve temporal asymmetry.

The learner must be able to inspect an earlier state without later outcome contaminating the earlier possibility field.

Freeze:

> **Later significance ≠ earlier foresight.**

# 16. Lives Provenance Contract

Claims should resolve to one or more of:

- direct statement;
- contemporary testimony;
- recorded action;
- institutional record;
- later memory;
- biographer interpretation;
- FSP inference.

Public prose may compress these labels.

Deep inspection must recover them.

# 17. Lives Person Firewall

Freeze:

> **Biographical dossier ≠ person.**

> **Public role change ≠ proven private identity change.**

> **Stated reason ≠ complete cause.**

> **Inferred cause ≠ demonstrated motive.**

# 18. Lives Portability Gate

Before personal transfer, require:

1. structural relation;
2. contextual difference;
3. analogy warrant;
4. non-portable element;
5. claim ceiling;
6. reversible test.

Freeze:

> **Exemplar as mirror ≠ exemplar as template.**

# 19. Lives → Life Navigation Contract

Life Navigation is optional.

The public handoff asks only:

> Do you want to examine whether one structural relation applies to a situation of your own?

If no, Return to the historical route.

If yes, open the existing Life Navigation projection.

Do not prefill a goal, recommendation, diagnosis, or personal interpretation.

# 20. Canonical-Owner Registry

Every Research Environment route must resolve to an owner class.

## Canonical Concept Owner

Owns stable FSP concept semantics.

## Case / Dossier Owner

Owns case-specific states, transitions, source ledger, and claim ceiling.

## Research Architecture Owner

Owns provisional research structures.

## Domain-Native Owner

Owns external scientific, historical, clinical, legal, or other domain claims.

## Projection Owner

Owns presentation, visibility, interaction, and return artifact only.

# 21. Canonical-Owner Regression

A route fails if:

1. a Research Region defines a concept already owned elsewhere;
2. an orientation essay strengthens a canonical claim;
3. a projection invents historical evidence;
4. a region substitutes its own bibliography for claim-level provenance;
5. a public summary removes a claim ceiling;
6. a route hides decision-relevant Remainder;
7. a research architecture is presented as settled domain fact;
8. a domain-native claim is attributed to FSP;
9. Life Navigation is treated as the owner of the historical lesson;
10. VR is treated as the owner of the historical dossier.

# 22. Route-Graph Regression

Every route must answer:

- Where did this claim originate?
- What canonical or domain owner supports it?
- What is the purpose of this transition?
- What does not travel?
- What claim ceiling applies?
- Where can the reader Return?

If any answer is unrecoverable, the route fails.

# 23. Four-Move Recoverability

A Research Region need not display Four-Move terminology.

But the route must preserve:

- **Portability:** What is being carried?
- **Ceiling:** How far may it go?
- **Authorization:** Who may act, if action is involved?
- **Return:** What consequence or re-location matters?
- **Remainder:** What remains unresolved?

# 24. Public Depth Contract

## Surface

Readable essay.

## Inspect

Concept, case, transition, comparison, and source cards.

## Deep

Research build, source ledger, competing interpretations, claim ceiling, Remainder, and formal architecture.

Depth changes presentation density.

It does not change truth conditions.
