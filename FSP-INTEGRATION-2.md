# FSP-INTEGRATION-2

## Human Compression Grammar, Research-Primitive-to-Ordinary-Language Mapping, Progressive Disclosure, and Cognitive-Load Gate

**Status:** Human-language and progressive-disclosure integration layer  
**Boundary:** INTEGRATION-1 crosswalk remains intact; Cartography kernel remains frozen; TRANSLATION-12 human evidence remains PENDING.

## 1. Purpose

INTEGRATION-1 established that the full Revision Research Base can remain as a research/encyclopedia overlay while the seven-view Cartography shell exposes only human-relevant compressions.

INTEGRATION-2 governs that compression.

The central problem is not merely jargon.

A user can still be overwhelmed by fifty perfectly ordinary questions.

Therefore the human-facing layer must regulate:

- wording;
- timing;
- depth;
- prompt count;
- trigger conditions.

## 2. Compression grammar

Each Cartography view has a small base grammar.

### START

- What are you trying to work through?
- Do you have a clear task yet, or are you still orienting?

### PEOPLE

- Who matters in this situation?
- What did they actually say or do, and what are you inferring?

### INSTITUTION

- What institutions, rules, or constraints matter here?
- Which constraints are verified, assumed, or still unclear?

### H/S/F

- What is inherited from the past, what matters now, and what may matter going forward?

### CONNECTIONS

- How are the people and institutions connected?
- Where is there practical power, authority, dependence, or a possible shared task?

### INVESTIGATE

- What possibilities are still live?
- What would help you learn more before committing?

### RETURN

- What is your current bounded judgment?
- What are you not claiming yet, and what would make you reconsider?

These are the default human grammar.

## 3. Progressive disclosure

The system uses three depth levels:

- BASE;
- PRESSURE;
- DEEP.

### BASE

No Revision-derived prompt is added.

The user sees only the ordinary seven-view grammar.

### PRESSURE

Case pressure has appeared, but the problem remains locally tractable.

The system may add one compressed Revision prompt.

### DEEP

The case contains deeper pressure such as:

- interpretive gaps;
- vocabulary conflict;
- historical inheritance;
- institutional feedback;
- lock-in;
- distributional conflict;
- emergency;
- framework challenge.

The system may add up to two Revision-derived prompts.

At most one may be a RARE prompt.

## 4. Prompt budgets

Prompt budgets are hard gates:

- BASE: maximum 2;
- PRESSURE: maximum 3;
- DEEP: maximum 4.

This is per view, not per entire case.

The budget exists to prevent ordinary-language complexity from reproducing formal complexity.

## 5. Case-pressure signals

The compression layer recognizes signals including:

- MULTIPLE_LIVE_PATHS;
- EVIDENCE_CONFLICT;
- PERSISTENT_DISAGREEMENT;
- POWER_AUTHORITY_TENSION;
- EVIDENCE_ASYMMETRY;
- CREDIBILITY_DISTORTION;
- INTERPRETIVE_GAP;
- VOCABULARY_CONFLICT;
- HISTORICAL_INHERITANCE;
- INSTITUTIONAL_FEEDBACK;
- LOCK_IN;
- REPAIR_WINDOW;
- DISTRIBUTIONAL_CONFLICT;
- IRREVERSIBILITY;
- EMERGENCY;
- FRAMEWORK_CHALLENGE.

These do not themselves decide the case.

They only control whether deeper questions become available.

## 6. Research primitive to ordinary-language mapping

Representative mappings include:

| Formal research construct | Human question |
| --- | --- |
| revision target | What might need to change? |
| revision frontier | Which possibilities are still live, and why? |
| revision-reason grammar | What supports this possibility? What counts against it? |
| path dependence | What is here because of how this situation developed? |
| practical convergence | Can you act together on anything without agreeing on everything? |
| minority report | What important disagreement should remain visible? |
| evidence asymmetry | Who can see, challenge, or verify the relevant information? |
| authority/power distortion | Who has expertise, who has formal authority, and who can actually impose consequences? |
| standing repair | What would let this person participate meaningfully in the inquiry? |
| hermeneutical gap | Do the available words or categories fail to capture something important? |
| vocabulary translation | What carries across between these ways of talking—and what does not? |
| translation remainder | What did not carry across? |
| concept genealogy | How has this idea changed as it moved through time and institutions? |
| historical lock-in | What makes this arrangement hard to change? |
| repair window | Is there a real opening for change now, and what could close it? |
| repair sequencing | What has to happen before the next repair step is safe or meaningful? |
| capability tradeoff | Who gains, who loses, and what remains unknown under this change? |
| reversibility | Can this change be rolled back if it goes badly? |
| compensation boundary | Can this loss really be made good by something else? |
| protected capability floor | What minimum ability must remain protected here? |
| burden shifting | Who is carrying the cost of this repair? |
| plural authorization | Can affected parties authorize this for different reasons without pretending they agree on everything? |
| temporary floor exception | Is ordinary governance genuinely insufficient, and what protection must still remain? |
| restoration | What has to be restored before this temporary arrangement ends? |
| FSP-as-object REOPEN | Should one of the framework's own rules be reconsidered here? |

The formal object remains in the research encyclopedia.

The human sees the question.

## 7. Suppression is a feature

When several research capabilities are triggered in one view, not all are displayed.

The compression layer sorts eligible prompts by exposure priority and respects the view budget.

Unshown capabilities remain available in:

- hidden governance;
- research overlay;
- later progressive disclosure.

Therefore:

```
triggered
!=
displayed immediately
```

## 8. Rare prompts

RARE prompts are reserved for cases where deeper machinery is genuinely relevant.

Examples include:

- testimonial credibility distortion;
- hermeneutical gaps;
- concept genealogy;
- protected capability floors;
- emergency exceptions;
- FSP-as-object challenge.

A rare prompt may not appear before DEEP mode.

At most one rare prompt may appear in a view at one time.

## 9. Cognitive-load gate

A compression plan fails when:

- prompt count exceeds budget;
- more than one rare prompt appears;
- a rare prompt appears before DEEP mode;
- formal research vocabulary leaks;
- a Revision-derived prompt appears in BASE mode.

This is an architectural gate, not a psychological measurement.

## 10. Simple cases remain simple

Jordan-style sparse orientation should remain BASE in most views.

A person can therefore traverse the shell using only:

- orientation;
- people;
- current constraints;
- possible next inquiry actions;
- bounded return.

The system must not manufacture complexity simply because the research layer contains it.

## 11. Pressure cases deepen selectively

A Maya-style case may activate:

- MULTIPLE_LIVE_PATHS;
- EVIDENCE_CONFLICT.

INVESTIGATE can therefore expose one additional question such as:

> What supports this possibility? What counts against it?

It need not expose a reason matrix.

## 12. Power cases deepen locally

A Leila-style case may activate:

- POWER_AUTHORITY_TENSION.

CONNECTIONS may add:

> Who has expertise, who has formal authority, and who can actually impose consequences?

That does not require the user to encounter the phrase:

`authority/power distortion audit`.

## 13. Deep interpretive cases

If a case develops both:

- INTERPRETIVE_GAP;
- VOCABULARY_CONFLICT,

INVESTIGATE may add questions such as:

> Do the available words or categories fail to capture something important?

and:

> What carries across between these ways of talking—and what does not?

The full hermeneutical and translation formalism remains beneath the surface.

## 14. Cognitive prosthesis principle

The human-facing layer should make relations easier to hold in mind.

It should not require the user to memorize the architecture that makes the relations available.

Therefore:

> The encyclopedia carries complexity so the person does not have to.

## 15. Relation to VR

This compression grammar should later become the verbal/interaction grammar of FSP-VR.

VR should not expose more formal machinery merely because it has more visual space.

The same prompt-budget and progressive-disclosure principles should apply spatially and temporally.

## 16. Human-validation boundary

Passing this build establishes deterministic prompt-budget and disclosure behavior.

It does not establish actual cognitive ease for humans.

Human-fidelity testing remains required.

TRANSLATION-12 human validation remains:

`PENDING`.

## 17. Research ceiling

The strongest current claim is:

> FSP can map its research primitives into an ordinary-language compression grammar with explicit progressive-disclosure triggers and prompt budgets that keep simple cases simple while allowing deeper inquiry to appear only under case pressure.

It does not establish that the chosen wording or budgets are psychologically optimal.
