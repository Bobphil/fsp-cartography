# 1. How to use this guide

This guide is for instructors.

Students should not be given the full tables below as a framework to memorize.

For each chapter or case, the instructor should know:

1. **Domain-native center** — what the chapter is really about.
2. **Primary Tool/function** — the one FSP function most useful here.
3. **Firewall** — the transition most likely to be overclaimed.
4. **Claim ceiling** — the strongest conclusion the chapter earns.
5. **Remainder** — what is important but incompletely represented, if any.
6. **Authorization** — whether this chapter needs a recommendation/authorization distinction.
7. **Return** — what students should carry back into judgment, action, or inquiry.
8. **Class/workbook cue** — where the governance move belongs.
9. **Encyclopedia route** — optional deeper reference.
10. **Do not add** — what would overload or distort the chapter.

The default decision is:

> **If the FSP layer does not improve visibility, boundedness, standing, corrigibility, or Return, do not add it.**

# 2. Course-level deployment rule

## Ethics

The sequence should feel like:

```text
historical ethical problem
→ chapter-native Tool
→ real-world case
→ bounded judgment
→ progressively richer standing / authorization
→ consequences / capability
→ REOPEN
→ Return
→ PFLAB synthesis
```

## Inquiry

The sequence should feel like:

```text
historical inquiry problem
→ native inquiry topology
→ representation / evidence / inference
→ alternatives
→ model or conceptual change
→ claim ceiling
→ remainder where relevant
→ REOPEN
→ Carry Forward / Return
→ DESI synthesis
```

Shared rule:

> **Shared governance grammar ≠ shared inquiry topology.**

# 4. Ethics Chapter 1 — Attention

## Instructor aim

Students should learn that ethical error can begin before argument, in the first representation of the situation.

## Use

- ask what entered the map;
- separate observation from addition;
- make omissions visible.

## Firewall

> **Map ≠ World ≠ Person.**

## Claim ceiling

> **A repaired first map improves inquiry without yet settling the ethical judgment.**

## Remainder

Use only if the case clearly contains an excluded person or fact.

## Authorization

Do not foreground.

## Return

Student should leave with:

> "What do I need to inspect before I judge?"

## Do not add

- standing tables;
- institutional amplification;
- JTS terminology;
- formal translation objects.

# 5. Ethics Chapter 2 — Socrates / Plato

## Instructor aim

Train reason-giving and the direction of justification.

Canonical question:

> Is a law just because authority promulgates it, or should authority promulgate it because there are reasons that make it just?

## Tool

Examination / Reason-Giving Audit.

## Firewall

> **Authority's approval ≠ sufficient explanation of justice.**

Secondary:

> **Showing that one justification fails ≠ proving the opposite conclusion.**

## Claim ceiling

Do not let Ch2 answer the later legitimacy question.

## Authorization

Only one light question:

> What does a failed justification authorize you to do now?

Preferred answer space:
ask for reasons, suspend confidence, challenge justification.

## Return

Next responsible inquiry move.

## Do not add

- full legitimacy theory;
- political resistance architecture;
- standing matrix.

---

# 6. Ethics Chapter 3 — Aristotle

## Instructor aim

Show formation, habit, practical wisdom, and why ethical perception can be cultivated.

## Tool

Formation / Practical-Wisdom Audit.

## Firewall

> **Formation ≠ justification.**

## Claim ceiling

A practice may form good judgment without proving every norm embedded in the practice.

## Remainder

Optional:
what tacit skill/practical knowledge is not captured by explicit rules?

## Authorization

No.

## Return

What kind of judgment/practice should be cultivated?

## Do not add

Do not convert Aristotle into a formal decision procedure.

---

# 7. Ethics Chapter 4 — Hellenistic Ethics

## Instructor aim

Calibrate agency under constraint.

## Tool

Control / Desire / Moral-Range Audit.

## Firewall

> **Remaining agency ≠ unlimited responsibility.**

## Claim ceiling

Identifying some control does not establish total responsibility for the outcome.

## Authorization

Only if the case includes intervention.

## Return

What is realistically mine to choose or influence?

## Do not add

Do not turn constraint analysis into excuse-making or total determinism.

---

# 8. Ethics Chapter 5 — Aquinas / Augustine

## Instructor aim

Move from good/common good to morally justified means.

## Tool

Moral Authority / Common-Good Audit.

## Firewall

> **Good purpose ≠ sufficient authorization.**

## Claim ceiling

A genuine good supplies a reason but not blanket permission.

## Authorization cue

Ask:

1. What good is being pursued?
2. What means are proposed?
3. Who may impose them?
4. What limits that authority?

## Return

What rule/means is justified now?

## Do not add

No full institutional-governance schema yet.

---

# 9. Ethics Chapter 6 — Social Contract

## Instructor aim

Make legitimacy and standing explicit.

## Tool

Legitimacy / Standing Audit.

## Firewalls

> **Power ≠ legitimacy.**

> **Illegitimacy ≠ automatic authorization of any resistance.**

## Authorization cue

This is the first chapter where:
- possible response;
- recommended response;
- authorized response

should be distinguished explicitly.

## Mandela

Use Mandela to show:
- internal/external strategy;
- resistance;
- cooperation/refusal;
- bounded legitimacy judgments.

## Return

What response is justified, by whom, under what constraints?

## Do not add

Do not imply that successful resistance proves moral legitimacy.

---

# 10. Ethics Chapter 7 — Hume / Smith

## Instructor aim

Expand moral visibility while preserving distance.

## Tool

Perspective / Moral-Visibility Audit.

## Firewall

> **Sympathy ≠ direct access.**

## Remainder

Required selective prompt:

> What part of the person's experience remains inferential or poorly represented?

## Claim ceiling

Improved perspective does not equal complete representation.

## Authorization

No.

## Return

What should I do differently because my map is better but incomplete?

## Do not add

Do not turn empathy into epistemic certainty.

---

# 11. Ethics Chapter 8 — Kant

## Instructor aim

Preserve persons as agents.

## Tool

Respect / Duty / Autonomy Audit.

## Firewalls

> **Help ≠ authorized agency bypass.**

> **Autonomy ≠ automatic deference to every present preference.**

## Authorization cue

Classify:
- inform;
- advise;
- persuade;
- constrain;
- decide for.

Ask what justifies crossing each boundary.

## Return

What action best preserves agency while responding to the actual problem?

## Do not add

Do not collapse Kant into consent doctrine.

---

# 12. Ethics Chapter 9 — Bentham / Mill

## Instructor aim

Perform consequence analysis before authorization analysis.

## Tool

Consequence / Welfare / Harm Audit.

## Firewall

> **Aggregate benefit ≠ unrestricted authorization.**

## Remainder

Ask:
- whose burden;
- whose liberty;
- what uncertainty;
- which affected person is least represented?

## Authorization cue

Use Benefit-to-Authorization Bridge:
1. strongest welfare case;
2. largest burden;
3. liberty constraint;
4. uncertainty;
5. who authorizes;
6. review condition.

## Return

What should be done, by whom, while monitoring what?

## Do not add

Do not let authorization replace utilitarian reasoning.

---

# 13. Ethics Chapter 10 — Marx / Nietzsche

## Instructor aim

Expose background power, genealogy, sedimented categories, and pre-choice structure.

## Tool

Background Power / Genealogy Audit.

## Firewall

> **Origin ≠ validity.**

## Claim ceiling

A genealogy may destabilize confidence or expose power without proving the opposite value true.

## Remainder

Optional:
what background condition remains invisible in the official map?

## Authorization

Light only.

## Return

What changes once the choice architecture is seen differently?

## Do not add

Do not treat genealogy as automatic refutation.

---

# 14. Ethics Chapter 11 — Rawls

## Instructor aim

Inspect fairness structurally rather than only procedurally.

## Tool

Fair-Structure / Veil-of-Ignorance Audit.

## Firewall

> **Formally equal rule ≠ equal practical burden.**

## Remainder

Use only where the formal rule hides unequal starting positions.

## Authorization

Institutional:
who may impose the structure and under what fair terms?

## Return

Which rule remains defensible across positions?

## Do not add

Do not turn Rawls into a capability chapter prematurely.

---

# 15. Ethics Chapter 12 — Care / Capability / Maathai

## Instructor aim

Return ethics to dependency, relationship, effective capability, and cooperation.

## Tool

Dependency / Effective-Capability Audit.

## Firewalls

> **Resource ≠ capability.**

> **Participation opportunity ≠ effective participation capability.**

## Remainder

Required:
care labor, safety, transport, time, support, know-how, institutional barrier.

## Authorization

Light:
support should expand capability without replacing local agency.

## Return

What support or institutional change actually expands capability?

## Handoff

Explicitly launch PFLAB.

## Do not add

Do not make capability a synonym for resource quantity.

# 16. Ethics PFLAB — Full Joint Task Space

## Instructor aim

Reveal the architecture students have already learned.

Use:

> **STATE → STANDING → TRANSLATION → REPAIR → AMPLIFICATION → REOPEN → RETURN**

Required:
- standing distinction;
- translation/remainder;
- repair sequencing;
- action-state grammar;
- amplification;
- REOPEN trigger;
- Return Card.

## Full action-state grammar

> **GENERATED → POSSIBLE → RECOMMENDED → AUTHORIZED → IMPLEMENTED → REVIEWED**

## Assessment

Assess governance quality, not vocabulary density.

## Encyclopedia

Use Gulf Pines as canonical worked route.

# 17. Inquiry — whole-course map

Inquiry should not be forced into a twelve-chapter Ethics-like matrix.

The instructor should track **inquiry topologies** and **case apertures**.

| Inquiry unit / case | Native topology | Primary FSP function | Firewall | Claim ceiling | Remainder | Authorization | Return |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Galileo | observational / mathematical / argumentative | representation + inferential license | Instrument reading ≠ uninterpreted fact | New evidence ≠ total theory settlement | optional | no | what changed in what can be defended? |
| Foucault light-speed | measurement / apparatus | provenance + measurement | precision ≠ interpretation | measured result ≠ full theory by itself | low | no | what did the experiment newly license? |
| Neptune/Vulcan | anomaly / repair | auxiliary-assumption diagnosis | Anomaly ≠ uniquely located refutation | failed prediction ≠ central theory falsified | **required** | no | what should be tested next / carried forward? |
| Newton→Einstein | model succession / domain shift | carry-forward + revision | Model success ≠ ontology | retained usefulness ≠ final ontology | semantic | no | what remains valid, what changes? |
| Duhem–Quine | inferential network | license / underdetermination | Evidence ≠ one forced repair | anomaly ≠ uniquely located revision | low | no | what alternatives remain live? |
| Kuhn | semantic / framework transition | translation + meaning change | Shared word ≠ shared role | Meaning change ≠ total incomparability | **required** | no | what can be compared responsibly? |
| Thought Experiments | controlled probe | counterfactual / dependency testing | Probe result ≠ historical fact | Possibility ≠ actuality | low | no | what dependency has been exposed? |
| DESI | contemporary collaborative science | JTS-lite | Institutional validation ≠ interpretive closure | Interesting tension ≠ established new cosmology | **required** | epistemic only | what should science claim now / watch next? |
| Asteria optional | distributed high-stakes governance | full comparator | Expertise ≠ mission authorization | expected risk reduction ≠ unilateral legitimacy | required | full/distributed | distributed Return |

# 18. Inquiry Unit — Galileo

## Instructor aim

Show interaction among observation, mathematics, instrumentation, argument, and conceptual restructuring.

## Use

Ask:
- what was made visible?
- what representation mattered?
- what inference became licensed?

## Firewall

> **Instrument reading ≠ uninterpreted fact.**

## Claim ceiling

Do not turn Galileo into a simple "observation defeated theory" story.

## Return

What changed in what investigators could responsibly defend?

## Encyclopedia

Optional:
Galileo dossier / Scientific Atlas.

---

# 19. Inquiry Unit — Foucault

## Instructor aim

Make apparatus, precision, and theory discrimination visible.

## Firewall

> **Measurement result ≠ full theoretical interpretation.**

## Remainder

Low:
calibration, apparatus dependence, interpretive assumptions.

## Return

What did this experiment newly license that prior inquiry could not?

## Do not add

No JTS or institutional ethics layer.

---

# 20. Inquiry Unit — Neptune / Vulcan

## Instructor aim

Teach anomaly localization.

## Firewall

> **Anomaly ≠ uniquely located refutation.**

## Remainder

Required:
which dependency remains unresolved?

## Carry Forward

Students must retain one licensed feature of the inherited framework.

## Return

What should investigators test next, and what should remain provisionally fixed?

## Encyclopedia

State timeline + whole-inquiry narrative.

---

# 21. Inquiry Unit — Newton → Einstein

## Instructor aim

Teach model succession without erasure.

## Firewalls

> **Model success ≠ ontology.**

> **Retained usefulness ≠ final ontology.**

## Remainder

Semantic/conceptual:
what does not transfer unchanged?

## Return

What should be carried forward and what revised?

## Do not add

Do not teach "Newton was wrong" as the takeaway.

---

# 22. Inquiry Unit — Duhem–Quine

## Instructor aim

Show why evidence bears on networks of assumptions.

## Firewall

> **Evidence constrains without always uniquely locating revision.**

## Claim ceiling

An anomaly does not identify one proposition as the only thing to change.

## Return

Which alternatives remain live?

## Do not add

No extra remainder block unless the case needs it.

---

# 23. Inquiry Unit — Kuhn

## Instructor aim

Teach framework/meaning change without collapsing into total incomparability.

## Firewalls

> **Shared vocabulary ≠ shared conceptual structure.**

> **Meaning change ≠ total incomparability.**

## Remainder

Required:
what changes in inferential role and cannot be carried unchanged?

## Return

What can be responsibly compared across frameworks, and what requires qualification?

## Encyclopedia

Meaning Change / Translation.

---

# 24. Inquiry Unit — Thought Experiments

## Instructor aim

Treat thought experiments as controlled revision spaces.

## Firewalls

> **Probe result ≠ historical fact.**

> **Possibility ≠ actuality.**

## Remainder

Optional:
what depends on idealization?

## Return

What conceptual dependency has the probe exposed?

## Do not add

Do not make thought experiments "experiments in the head" without qualification.

---

# 25. Inquiry Unit — DESI

## Instructor aim

End with live inquiry before historical closure.

Use JTS-lite:

> **STATE → TRANSLATION → REMAINDER → INSTITUTIONAL VALIDATION → REOPEN → RETURN**

## Firewall set

> **Data product ≠ final cosmological interpretation.**

> **Model fit ≠ ontology.**

> **Institutional publication ≠ interpretive closure.**

## Remainder

Required three-layer map:
- measurement/data;
- statistical/model;
- cosmological interpretation.

## Authorization

Epistemic only:
who may responsibly certify/communicate the claim?

Do not import moral/legal authorization tables.

## Return

What should the scientific community claim now, and what should remain open?

## Encyclopedia

Scientific Atlas + optional Asteria.

# 26. Optional Asteria comparator

Use only for deeper students or instructor development.

Five questions:

1. What changes when State becomes probabilistic?
2. Who has standing when affected populations may move?
3. What is lost when model output becomes public risk communication?
4. How does irreversibility change sequencing?
5. What does Return mean when authorship is distributed?

Do not assign full formal comparator unless the course explicitly includes it.

# 27. Workbook deployment map

## Every ordinary workbook

Must include:
- chapter-native mechanic;
- one distinctive claim ceiling;
- REOPEN;
- Return.

May include:
- one remainder prompt;
- one authorization prompt.

Must not include:
- full JTS;
- multiple redundant Firewalls;
- raw canonical IDs;
- full formal schemas.

## Ethics later chapters

Add authorization progressively:
- Ch5 intro;
- Ch6 explicit;
- Ch8 explicit;
- Ch9 integrated.

## Inquiry

Authorization generally absent unless claim crosses into public/institutional action.

# 29. Class-session deployment map

For a 75-minute session, use at most one explicit FSP governance turn unless the session is a Lab.

Suggested pattern:

1. historical / lived problem;
2. domain-native reasoning;
3. case pressure;
4. one governance turn;
5. bounded judgment;
6. Return.

Examples of one governance turn:
- claim ceiling;
- remainder;
- standing;
- authorization;
- Carry Forward.

Do not run all of them every session.

# 34. "Do not over-FSP" checklist

Before adding any FSP element, ask:

1. Does it expose a real failure risk?
2. Is it already implicit in the domain-native material?
3. Does it help the student reach a better bounded judgment?
4. Does it improve Return?
5. Is it worth the time cost?

If not, omit it.

Warning signs:
- framework vocabulary exceeds historical vocabulary;
- students discuss Tools instead of cases;
- every chapter gets the same prompt;
- the Journal starts looking like a form;
- Inquiry starts sounding like institutional ethics;
- Ethics starts sounding like a systems-engineering course.

# 36. Course-level culmination check

By the end of Ethics, students should recognize that:

> a supported moral judgment still leaves questions of standing, authorization, implementation, burden, capability, institutional amplification, REOPEN, and Return.

By the end of Inquiry, students should recognize that:

> a supported scientific or historical judgment still leaves questions of representation, inferential license, alternatives, model dependence, remainder, institutional validation, REOPEN, and Carry Forward.

Across both:

> **Cooperation can sometimes proceed without convergence when the relevant transitions are sufficiently governed.**

# 38. Final instructor rule

The instructor should not ask:

> "How do I fit FSP into this chapter?"

Ask:

> **"What is this chapter's own inquiry problem, and where does a thin FSP governance distinction help students see it more clearly?"**

That keeps the Pressbooks historical and domain-native while preserving the deeper FSP architecture.
