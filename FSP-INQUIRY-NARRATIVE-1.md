# FSP-INQUIRY-NARRATIVE-1

## Whole-Inquiry Narrative Spine, Temporal Anchoring, Problem-Context Recovery, Whole↔Part Return, and Four-Chapter Aperture Integration

This build responds to a reader-level failure revealed by the first four-chapter prototype: local FSP inspection can be technically correct while still asking the reader to reconstruct too much of the whole historical inquiry from fragments.

The new governing principle is:

> **No local FSP inspection should require the reader to rebuild the whole inquiry from disconnected pieces.**

## Whole inquiry first

Neptune/Vulcan now has a canonical public narrative spine: six connected paragraphs carrying the reader from the Uranus discrepancy, through the 1846 Neptune success, the 1859 Mercury/Vulcan problem, the long unsuccessful search trajectory, and the 1915 general-relativistic explanation.

This narrative does not replace the Atlas. It supplies the historical and philosophical whole within which Atlas states, transitions, probes, and claim ceilings become intelligible.

## Temporal anchoring

The build adds explicit milestones tied to the formal states and transitions.

The key distinction is constitutional:

`FSP state order is anchored in historical time, but state identity is not calendar time.`

A dated milestone may illuminate one or more formal states. A formal state may represent an extended inquiry condition rather than one instant.

Later success must not be projected backward as if it were already available to earlier investigators.

## Four chapter apertures

The four chapters are now defined as different apertures onto one inquiry:

1. **Revision pressure** — where should change go?
2. **Semantic stability/framework change** — what changes conceptually and what remains stable?
3. **Controlled revision spaces** — how can alternatives be explored without rewriting history?
4. **Diachronic formalism** — how can changing inquiry be represented without flattening history?

Each aperture declares:
- the chapter question;
- current local focus;
- connection to the whole inquiry;
- what FSP adds;
- a return prompt to the whole.

## Problem-context recovery

Every local view can now recover four things:

- **Whole:** What is the entire inquiry problem?
- **Time:** Where are we historically, and what later evidence is not yet available?
- **Local:** Which part of the larger problem are we inspecting?
- **FSP:** What distinction, dependency, or boundary is FSP making easier to see?

This becomes a reusable context object rather than relying on prose memory.

## Whole↔part return

Two returns are now distinct and required.

**Local Return** restores the exact chapter sentence or inspection origin.

**Whole-Inquiry Return** restores the case narrative spine with the relevant milestone and chapter aperture.

Authorial Return therefore gains a second scale without losing its exact local meaning.

## Sources

The chronology is anchored to authoritative historical/scientific references for Neptune's 1846 discovery, Le Verrier's 1859 Mercury residual/Vulcan repair, and the 1915 general-relativistic account of Mercury's perihelion.

## Claim ceiling

This build certifies a narrative and orientation architecture for one case. It does not yet certify equivalent spines for Foucault, Galileo, Newton→Einstein, or DESI. Those should inherit this contract only after Neptune/Vulcan passes public-reader regression.
