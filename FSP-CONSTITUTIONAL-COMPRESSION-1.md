## Relation, Transition, Return; Partial Maps; Governed Bridges; Consequential Return; and Formal Decompression

**Status:** CONSTITUTIONAL COMPRESSION / COMMON-OWNER CANDIDATE — VERSION 1  
**Repository role:** Compression layer beneath FSP canonical, research, pedagogical, and application architectures  
**Parent commitments:** FSP-CT-CANONICAL-1.1; FSP-CT-META-GOVERNANCE-1  
**Reconciles:** FSP-TRANSFORMATION-ARCHITECTURE-1; FSP-RECURSIVE-SCALE-ARCHITECTURE-1.1; FSP-ONTOLOGICAL-INTEROPERABILITY-1; FSP-OBJECTIVE-PHENOMENOLOGY-1; relation/translation/Return architectures

# 1. Why Compression Is Now Necessary

FSP has reached a stage at which the danger is no longer mainly under-description.

The danger is **successful proliferation**.

Across critical thinking, history of inquiry, ethics, scientific reasoning, heterogeneous intelligence, recursive scale, ontology, phenomenology, transformation, institutional analysis, VR, pedagogy, and public-reader architecture, FSP has accumulated useful distinctions:

- maps and worlds;
- evidence and reasons;
- Tools and Firewalls;
- claims and claim ceilings;
- standing and authorization;
- assembly histories;
- PRFs;
- scale relations;
- translation anchors;
- bridge burdens;
- remainders;
- capability structures;
- transformation ledgers;
- REOPEN;
- Authorial Return.

Many of these distinctions have earned their place locally. But a mature architecture must be able to answer a harder question:

> **What is the smallest structure that explains why these distinctions are needed, where they belong, and when they may be removed?**

The proposed answer is:

```text
RELATION
→ governed TRANSITION
→ consequential RETURN
```

This is not a three-step psychological sequence.

It is a constitutional grammar for inquiry and Doing.

---

# 2. The Constitutional Core

## 2.1 Relation

A **Relation** is any bounded connection that matters to an inquiry, action, translation, coordination, or consequence.

Examples include:

- person ↔ environment;
- observation ↔ claim;
- evidence ↔ conclusion;
- person ↔ person;
- person ↔ institution;
- model ↔ target;
- scale ↔ scale;
- representation ↔ representation;
- capability ↔ constraint;
- action ↔ consequence;
- present agent ↔ successor agent;
- lived situation ↔ reflective articulation.

FSP does not require all relations to be of one ontological kind.

> **Portable relation grammar ≠ universal relation ontology.**

A relation becomes constitutionally important when carrying something across it changes what may responsibly be claimed, inferred, recommended, authorized, done, or returned.

---

## 2.2 Transition

A **Transition** is a crossing in which something is carried from one bounded state, representation, scale, perspective, role, institution, or situation into another.

Examples:

- observation → interpretation;
- interpretation → explanation;
- reasons → judgment;
- judgment → recommendation;
- recommendation → authorization;
- local evidence → aggregate metric;
- neural evidence → phenomenal hypothesis;
- detector event → cosmological claim;
- one ontology → another representation;
- present capability → transformed capability;
- individual action → institutional amplification;
- inquiry result → lived action.

FSP’s central governance problem is therefore:

> **What may be carried across this transition, by what license, under what burden, with what loss, and with what remainder?**

This is the compressed meaning of a Firewall.

> **Firewall = transition governance.**

A Firewall is not primarily a slogan and not normally a prohibition.

It is a checkpoint requiring the crossing to expose its license.

---

## 2.3 Return

A **Return** is the re-entry of inquiry into lived Doing after reflection, modeling, testing, judgment, coordination, or intervention.

Return is not merely the last page of an analysis.

Return asks:

- What changed?
- What can now be done?
- What should not yet be done?
- What uncertainty travels forward?
- What consequences have been created?
- Whose capabilities have expanded or narrowed?
- What repair may be owed?
- What must remain reopenable?
- What has been left for successors?

Thus:

> **Return = consequential re-entry into a changed situation.**

And:

> **Inquiry result ≠ completed inquiry until its consequences, limits, and reopen conditions are carried back into Doing.**

# 3. Partial Maps as the Epistemic Starting Condition

The compression layer adopts the existing FSP constitutional commitment:

> **Map ≠ World ≠ Person.**

But the deeper compression is:

> **Every inspectable map is a partial relation-bearing artifact.**

A map selects:

- entities;
- distinctions;
- scales;
- relations;
- boundaries;
- variables;
- histories;
- perspectives;
- evidential routes;
- action possibilities.

A map can be highly accurate and still partial.

A map can be useful and still omit what later becomes decisive.

A map can be locally sufficient and globally misleading.

Therefore FSP should not ask first whether a map is complete.

It should ask:

1. What relations does this map preserve?
2. What relations does it suppress or leave unresolved?
3. For what transition is it being used?
4. What burden does that transition impose?
5. What follows if the map guides action?

This gives a more general interpretation of many existing FSP distinctions.

---

# 4. Governed Bridges

A **Bridge** is a transition that crosses a difference significant enough to create inferential burden.

Differences may include:

- scale;
- representation;
- perspective;
- ontology;
- institution;
- role;
- time;
- evidential modality;
- organism;
- discipline;
- model;
- lived/reflexive mode.

A bridge is not illegitimate merely because it crosses a difference.

The question is whether the crossing is governed.

A minimal bridge record is:

```text
Bridge = {
  source,
  target,
  relationPreserved,
  transitionPurpose,
  license,
  burden,
  loss,
  remainder,
  claimCeiling,
  reopenConditions
}
```

This compresses several existing FSP objects without abolishing them.

- `TranslationAnchor` specifies a bridge where relation-preservation across heterogeneous representations matters.
- `ScaleBridgeRecord` specifies a bridge where scale difference matters.
- `EvidenceBridgeGraph` specifies linked bridges where evidence moves through multiple registrations.
- `OperationalSufficiencyRecord` specifies how much shared bridge structure is enough for a bounded task.
- `InteroperabilityRecord` specifies how multiple agents can act together without complete representational or ontological convergence.
- `PhenomenalTranslationRemainder` specifies what remains after a bridge toward phenomenal structure reaches its current evidential limit.

The compression rule is:

> **Specialized bridge object only when a generic Bridge cannot preserve the burden that matters.**

---

# 5. Bridge Burden

A transition may appear simple while carrying hidden assumptions.

FSP therefore treats burden as typed rather than automatically scalar.

Candidate burdens include:

- evidential burden;
- translation burden;
- identity burden;
- mechanism burden;
- causal burden;
- scale-alignment burden;
- temporal-alignment burden;
- authorization burden;
- phenomenal burden;
- institutional burden;
- normative burden.

The constitutional rule is:

> **The farther a claim moves from what was directly established, the more of the crossing must become inspectable.**

This does not mean distance can always be measured numerically.

It means inferential work cannot disappear merely because a bridge has become familiar.

---

# 6. Remainder Is a Constitutional Output

A **Remainder** is what the current relation-transition system has not successfully carried across.

Remainder can be:

- evidential;
- mechanistic;
- structural;
- translational;
- scale-related;
- phenomenal;
- normative;
- authorization-related;
- historical;
- practical.

The constitutional rule is:

> **Remainder ≠ failure. Hidden remainder is failure-prone.**

A mature inquiry may progress by:

- reducing a remainder;
- partitioning it;
- localizing it;
- identifying why it persists;
- discovering that it belongs to another scale;
- determining that the current task does not require its resolution.

Thus:

> **Progress ≠ remainder elimination.**

This is especially important in consciousness, medicine, neuroscience, cosmology, institutional inquiry, ethics, and heterogeneous intelligence, where multiple detailed local maps can coexist with unresolved bridge relations among them.

---

# 7. Consequential Return

Return must now be stated more strongly than “reflect and continue.”

A Return is constitutionally adequate only when it carries forward consequences.

A minimal Return record is:

```text
Return = {
  changedSituation,
  boundedJudgment,
  licensedActions,
  prohibitedOverreach,
  capabilityEffects,
  distributionalEffects,
  institutionalEffects,
  remainder,
  repairObligations,
  reopenConditions,
  successorConditions
}
```

This produces five important distinctions.

## 7.1 Judgment ≠ authorization

A conclusion may be warranted without authorizing intervention.

## 7.2 Authorization ≠ harmlessness

A legitimate action may still create costs, risks, or enclosure.

## 7.3 Success ≠ closure

An action that works may generate new conditions that require renewed inquiry.

## 7.4 Return ≠ reporting

A report that never changes Doing has not completed the FSP arc.

## 7.5 Present Return ≠ terminal Return

Some Doings are intergenerational.

A Return may alter the assembly history, capability, evidence, or option structure available to successors.

# 8. Relation–Transition–Return as the Common Owner

The larger FSP grammar can now be compressed:

```text
LIVED DOING
↓
relations become salient / articulated
↓
partial maps
↓
governed transitions
↓
bounded judgments
↓
authorized or withheld action
↓
consequences
↓
remainder / repair / REOPEN
↓
RETURN
↓
changed LIVED DOING
```

The existing extended-critical-thinking grammar remains valid.

The compression layer does not replace it.

It explains why its distinctions occur where they do.

# 9. How Existing FSP Families Decompress from the Core

## 9.1 Critical Thinking

**Relation:** claim ↔ reasons/evidence  
**Transition:** reasons → judgment  
**Return:** what the judgment licenses in further inquiry or action

Traditional logic concentrates on a central family of transitions.

FSP extends governance before and after them.

---

## 9.2 Attention / ABCD

**Relation:** inquirer ↔ situation  
**Transition:** lived encounter → articulated map  
**Return:** revised visibility and next inquiry

ABCD exists because the first map is selective.

---

## 9.3 Assembly History

**Relation:** present pattern ↔ formative history  
**Transition:** origin story → present interpretation or justification  
**Firewall:** formation ≠ justification  
**Return:** altered understanding without genetic fallacy

Assembly history earns its place because historical relation and present validity can be confused at a transition.

---

## 9.4 Tools

A Tool is a selective operator for inspecting a relation under pressure.

> **Tool ≠ Verdict.**

Tools are decompressions of recurrent relation-transition failures.

---

## 9.5 Firewalls

A Firewall is a named recurrent transition constraint.

> **Firewall = compressed bridge-governance rule.**

Examples:

- observed behavior ≠ known intention;
- origin ≠ validity;
- benefit ≠ authorization;
- model success ≠ ontology;
- current judgment ≠ final closure.

---

## 9.6 Claim Ceilings

A claim ceiling bounds what may be carried into the target state after a transition.

> **Firewall governs the crossing; claim ceiling bounds the arrival.**

---

## 9.7 Recursive Scale

**Relation:** scale-indexed description/evidence ↔ another scale  
**Transition:** cross-scale bridge  
**Return:** scale-sensitive judgment/action

The recursive-scale architecture exists because relation grammar is portable while ontology, mechanism, evidence, and burden can change with scale.

> **Portability of relation grammar ≠ identity of ontology.**

---

## 9.8 Ontological Interoperability

**Relation:** heterogeneous PRF ↔ heterogeneous PRF  
**Transition:** representation/ontology A → bounded shared task with B  
**Return:** coordinated action without forced metaphysical convergence

Translation anchors and operational sufficiency are specialized bridge decompressions.

---

## 9.9 Objective Phenomenology

**Relation:** third-person evidence ↔ hypothesized phenomenal structure  
**Transition:** detectability/discrimination/actionability → phenomenal inference  
**Remainder:** qualitative first-person character not carried across  
**Return:** stronger constraint without counterfeit possession of another point of view

This architecture is therefore an unusually clear example of disciplined partial mapping.

---

## 9.10 Transformation

**Relation:** present organization ↔ reachable future organization  
**Transition:** current state/capability → altered possibility structure  
**Return:** changed capabilities, constraints, and successor options

The Transformation Architecture earns its specialized vocabulary only where it exposes reachability, path dependence, capability redistribution, lock-in, or coordination burden that generic transition language would hide.

---

## 9.11 Institutions

**Relation:** person/group ↔ rule/role/record/incentive structure  
**Transition:** individual/local judgment → amplified institutional consequence  
**Return:** distributed effects, repair burden, changed option structures

Institutional amplification matters because the same local judgment can have radically different consequences after organizational crossing.

---

# 10. Formal Decompression

Compression must not become vagueness.

The constitutional layer therefore requires **formal decompression on demand**.

A compressed statement should be expandable into a more explicit record whenever stakes, disagreement, opacity, cross-domain translation, or automation make that necessary.

Minimal decompression:

```text
RelationRecord = {
  relata,
  relationType,
  targetBinding,
  context,
  evidence,
  uncertainty
}

TransitionRecord = {
  sourceState,
  targetState,
  carriedContent,
  license,
  bridgeBurden,
  translationLoss,
  remainder,
  claimCeiling
}

ReturnRecord = {
  changedSituation,
  judgment,
  authorizedAction,
  capabilityEffects,
  consequences,
  repair,
  reopenConditions
}
```

These are constitutional schemas, not mandatory user-interface forms.

> **Formal availability ≠ compulsory form filling.**

The seven-view app and ordinary-language student projections should remain sparse unless pressure requires decompression.

---

# 11. Decompression Trigger

Formal decompression is warranted when one or more of the following holds:

1. the transition is contested;
2. the bridge crosses scales, ontologies, roles, institutions, or evidence modalities;
3. the action has significant consequences for others;
4. the remainder is decision-relevant;
5. the bridge burden is hidden or disputed;
6. an automated or institutional system will carry the judgment forward;
7. later audit, repair, or provenance recovery is likely to matter;
8. multiple agents must coordinate without full representational agreement.

Otherwise, compression should remain human-readable.

# 12. No Orphan Distinction

## Constitutional anti-proliferation test 1

A proposed new FSP distinction must answer:

> **What relation, transition, bridge burden, remainder, or Return consequence becomes materially less governable if this distinction is absent?**

If it cannot answer, it is an **orphan distinction**.

An orphan distinction may be:

- interesting;
- philosophically suggestive;
- domain-native;
- pedagogically vivid;

but it does not belong in the constitutional or canonical Core merely for those reasons.

### Pass conditions

A new distinction passes only if it does at least one of the following:

- separates relations that produce different warranted transitions;
- exposes a recurrent hidden bridge burden;
- changes a claim ceiling;
- changes authorization;
- changes the handling of remainder;
- changes Return, repair, REOPEN, or successor conditions;
- prevents a demonstrated recurrent failure.

### Firewall

> **Conceptual interest ≠ constitutional necessity.**

---

# 13. No Decorative Type

## Constitutional anti-proliferation test 2

A formal type must alter governance.

A type is **decorative** if deleting its label leaves:

- the same transitions licensed;
- the same burdens visible;
- the same claim ceiling;
- the same action permissions;
- the same remainder;
- the same Return;
- the same failure detection.

A type earns its place only if its presence changes what the system can responsibly distinguish, test, block, preserve, or reopen.

### Example

`ScaleKind` is not decorative when physical, organizational, temporal, institutional, or phenomenal scale differences change the bridge burden.

An `EmergenceRecord` becomes decorative if emergence language is applied where no distinct emergence claim is being made and no governance changes.

### Firewall

> **Typing ≠ explanation.**

and:

> **A named type ≠ an earned type.**

---

# 14. The Canonical Promotion Gate

A proposed Core object must now pass five gates.

## Gate A — Compression failure

Can Relation–Transition–Return govern the case without the new object?

If yes, prefer compression.

## Gate B — Recurrent burden

Does the new object expose a burden recurring across more than one case or domain?

If no, keep it domain-local.

## Gate C — Governance difference

Does the distinction change inference, claim ceiling, authorization, remainder handling, repair, or Return?

If no, reject as decorative.

## Gate D — Projection discipline

Can the object remain hidden in ordinary-language use until needed?

If no, assess whether it creates avoidable user burden.

## Gate E — Regression

Does adding the object preserve:

- Map ≠ World ≠ Person;
- Tool ≠ Verdict;
- Formation ≠ justification;
- Generate ≠ Recommend ≠ Authorize;
- Model success ≠ ontology;
- Shared grammar ≠ shared ontology;
- Judgment ≠ final closure;
- REOPEN;
- Authorial Return?

If not, do not promote.

# 15. Constitutional Minimality

The Core should own only what is needed to govern the architecture.

Candidate constitutional commitments are therefore:

1. **Partiality:** inspectable maps are partial.
2. **Relationality:** inquiry concerns bounded relations, not isolated labels alone.
3. **Transition governance:** crossings require licenses proportionate to their burden.
4. **Remainder visibility:** what does not cross remains explicit.
5. **Bounded judgment:** conclusions have claim ceilings.
6. **Authorization separation:** warrant does not automatically authorize action.
7. **Consequence tracking:** action changes the next inquiry situation.
8. **Corrigibility:** REOPEN conditions must survive closure pressure.
9. **Return:** inquiry returns to lived Doing.
10. **Decompression:** formal detail is available when burden requires it.
11. **Non-proliferation:** new distinctions must earn constitutional status.

Everything else should be owned at the lowest layer capable of preserving the needed distinction.

---

# 16. One Canonical Owner, Many Decompressions

This build strengthens the existing rule:

> **One canonical owner, many projections.**

The new refinement is:

> **One compressed constitutional owner, many burden-sensitive decompressions.**

Examples:

- Ethics decompresses authorization, standing, burden, capability, and repair.
- Philosophy of science decompresses evidence bridges, model-target relations, scale, ontology, and historical reopening.
- Consciousness research decompresses phenomenal bridge burden and translation remainder.
- Medicine decompresses population-to-patient bridges, evidence heterogeneity, expertise, consent, and consequence.
- Institutional analysis decompresses amplification, standing, rigidity, provenance, and repair.
- Transformation research decompresses reachability, task sequence, lock-in, and successor capability.

The Core does not need to contain all of those domain vocabularies.

It needs to explain when they are warranted.

# 17. Partial Maps and the Research Frontier

A central implication follows.

Many difficult domains are not primarily suffering from total absence of maps.

They often contain **multiple partially successful maps whose relations are under-governed**.

This is visible in:

- consciousness;
- neuroscience;
- psychedelics;
- medicine;
- psychiatric classification;
- cosmology;
- biological regulation;
- institutional systems;
- social measurement.

The frontier question is often not merely:

> Which map is correct?

It is:

> **Which relations does each map preserve, where do their transitions fail, what bridges are warranted, what remains unmapped or untranslated, and what consequences follow when one partial map is granted authority beyond its earned range?**

This gives FSP a disciplined way to work with heterogeneous mature sciences without pretending that constitutional compression supplies the missing empirical theory.

---

# 18. Simon Comparator

Herbert Simon's search for common structures across computation, organizations, design, problem solving, and other artificial systems is an important comparator.

FSP shares the impulse to identify portable organization without requiring sameness of material realization.

But the constitutional compression adds explicit governance questions Simon's framework did not make its central object:

- What is lost when one representation is carried into another?
- What licenses a bridge?
- What claim ceiling survives successful problem solving?
- Who has standing to act?
- What consequences alter future capability?
- What remainder persists?
- What must return to lived Doing?

Thus the Simon comparison should be pursued as a **structural comparator**, not as an ancestry or equivalence claim.

> **Structural portability ≠ theoretical identity.**

# 19. Formal Compression and Decompression Cycle

The full constitutional cycle is:

```text
LIVED DOING
↓
PARTIAL MAP
↓
RELATION
↓
TRANSITION PRESSURE
↓
GOVERNED BRIDGE
↓
BOUNDED ARRIVAL / CLAIM CEILING
↓
ACTION OR WITHHOLDING
↓
CONSEQUENCE
↓
REMAINDER / REPAIR / REOPEN
↓
RETURN
↓
CHANGED LIVED DOING
```

When pressure is low, this can remain ordinary language.

When pressure rises:

```text
compress
→ inspect
→ formally decompress
→ test bridge
→ recompress for use
→ Return
```

This is the intended relationship between public usability and research formalism.

# 20. Regression Against Existing Architectures

## 20.1 Critical-thinking canonical

**PASS** if traditional logic, evidence, and argument analysis remain intact while transitions before and after argument are preserved.

## 20.2 Meta-governance

**PASS** if ABCD, Tools, Firewalls, claim ceilings, REOPEN, and Return retain distinct functions.

## 20.3 Recursive scale

**PASS** if relation portability does not imply scale ontology identity and bridge burden remains typed.

## 20.4 Ontological interoperability

**PASS** if preserved relation does not imply shared representation or shared ontology.

## 20.5 Objective phenomenology

**PASS** if structural constraint can increase without converting into first-person equivalence.

## 20.6 Transformation

**PASS** if possibility, feasibility, salience, selection, authorization, implementation, consequence, and successor capability remain distinguishable when needed.

## 20.7 Seven-view human-facing shell

**PASS** only if constitutional compression does not force research vocabulary into ordinary user interaction.

# 21. Failure Conditions

This architecture fails if it:

- becomes a metaphysical theory of all relations;
- turns Relation–Transition–Return into a mandatory temporal sequence;
- treats every crossing as equally burdensome;
- hides domain-specific evidence requirements;
- collapses judgment into authorization;
- treats successful translation as ontology identity;
- treats remainder as mere ignorance to be eliminated;
- turns Return into a summary rather than consequential re-entry;
- requires users to fill formal records when ordinary language is sufficient;
- creates new types that do no governance work;
- promotes specialized research objects into Core without recurrent need.

---

# 22. Strongest Supported Claim

The strongest supported architectural claim is:

> **Across the current FSP ecology, a large share of the recurring machinery can be understood as burden-sensitive decompressions of a smaller constitutional grammar: partial maps preserve selected relations; inquiry and action require transitions across maps, perspectives, scales, roles, institutions, and states; those transitions must be governed by explicit licenses, burdens, ceilings, and remainders; and inquiry returns to lived Doing through consequences, repair, REOPEN, and renewed capability.**

This is a compression claim about the architecture.

It is not evidence that all domains share one ontology or mechanism.

---

# 23. Claim Ceiling

This build does not establish:

- that all cognition is representational;
- that all reality is fundamentally relational;
- that all domains instantiate one formal dynamics;
- that Relation–Transition–Return is a psychological developmental sequence;
- that every scientific bridge can be formalized in the same mathematics;
- that every remainder can be resolved;
- that governance alone supplies empirical truth;
- that FSP replaces domain-native methods.

---

# 24. REOPEN Conditions

Reopen this compression if:

1. an important FSP object cannot be reconstructed as a relation, transition, burden, remainder, or Return without losing essential governance;
2. two distinct transition types repeatedly produce different consequences but remain collapsed;
3. a proposed specialized object repeatedly passes No Orphan Distinction and No Decorative Type across heterogeneous cases;
4. formal decompression creates user burden without improving auditability or judgment;
5. consequential Return cannot represent a domain's relevant closure or continuation structure;
6. empirical or philosophical work shows that the compression systematically distorts a major inquiry topology.

---

# 25. Constitutional Decision

**FREEZE AS COMMON-OWNER CANDIDATE FOR REGRESSION.**

Do not yet replace the frozen canonical documents.

Instead use this build as a compression test over them.

The next phase should test whether major existing FSP object families can be:

```text
(a) retained as necessary decompressions,
(b) demoted to optional/domain-local support, or
(c) eliminated as redundant.
```

No vocabulary expansion should occur during that test unless a case demonstrates a genuine compression failure.

# 27. Final Statement

FSP can now be stated in a much smaller form:

> **We live and act through partial maps. We depend on relations those maps preserve. We cross from one map, state, scale, perspective, judgment, or institution to another. Those crossings carry burdens and can outrun their licenses. Good inquiry makes the crossings inspectable, preserves what does not cross, limits what follows, and returns the result to lived Doing where consequences create the next situation.**

Or in its shortest constitutional form:

```text
RELATION
→ GOVERNED TRANSITION
→ CONSEQUENTIAL RETURN
↺
```

**Compression is successful only if decompression remains available when reality, stakes, or disagreement demand it.**
