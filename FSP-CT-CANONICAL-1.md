---

## 1. Purpose

This document provides the canonical FSP account of expanded critical thinking for the Encyclopedia.

It is not a replacement for traditional critical thinking. It preserves logic, argument analysis, evidence assessment, deductive, inductive, abductive, and analogical reasoning, source evaluation, counterexamples, conceptual clarification, and fallacy analysis. FSP extends that familiar territory by making explicit several transitions that ordinary inquiry must also negotiate: from lived situation to representation, from representation to reasons, from reasons to judgment, from judgment to standing and authorization, from individual judgment to institutional action, from action to consequences, and from a current settlement to repair and REOPEN.

The central claim is therefore modest but consequential:

> **Critical thinking is not only the evaluation of arguments. It is the disciplined governance of how situated, fallible human inquirers construct and revise maps, move from evidence to bounded judgment, determine what that judgment licenses, act through social and institutional settings, and preserve conditions for correction.**

This architecture is meant to be recognizable before it is technical. Human beings do not first encounter inquiry as a formal method. We encounter it as part of living: noticing, interpreting, anticipating, acting, being surprised, learning, revising, coordinating with others, and sometimes discovering that what seemed obvious was incomplete or wrong.

FSP formalizes and governs these recurring transitions without claiming that every domain contains the same kinds of entities, uses the same evidence, or grants the same forms of authority.

# PART I — THE HUMAN STARTING POINT

## 2. Human beings as situated inquirers

Human beings begin in the middle of things.

We encounter environments through bodies, attention, perception, memory, affect, learned categories, language, relationships, technologies, institutions, and histories. We distinguish some features and miss others. We form expectations. We respond to other people whose experience is never given to us in the same way as our own. We interpret ourselves through memories, narratives, habits, roles, and the reactions of others. We act before uncertainty disappears.

None of these activities is infallible.

Perception can mislead. Attention is selective. Memory reconstructs. Categories omit. Prediction fails. Other people are partly opaque. Self-interpretation is revisable. Institutions distribute information unevenly. Language can reveal distinctions and conceal them. Power can stabilize one map while excluding alternatives.

Yet human beings remain capable of correction because the world, other people, and the consequences of action can resist our expectations.

A minimal lived-inquiry arc can therefore be represented as:

```text
encounter
→ notice
→ distinguish
→ interpret
→ anticipate
→ act
→ encounter consequences or resistance
→ revise
```

This is not offered as a literal universal psychological sequence. It is a philosophical reconstruction of recurring features of situated human inquiry, informed by but not reducible to empirical work in perception, cognition, development, learning, social cognition, memory, embodiment, language, decision research, institutional studies, and related fields.

### Claim ceiling

**A philosophical reconstruction of recurring inquiry structure ≠ a complete developmental or cognitive theory of human beings.**

---

## 3. From encounter to map

Human beings cannot operate on an unmediated totality of the world. We work through selective representations.

A map may be perceptual, linguistic, conceptual, emotional, narrative, statistical, diagrammatic, institutional, computational, or social. It may be explicit or largely tacit. It may concern a physical environment, another person, a historical event, a moral situation, a scientific system, or ourselves.

This gives FSP its first constitutional firewall:

> **Map ≠ World ≠ Person.**

A useful map need not be a false map. The point is that usefulness, accuracy, and completeness are different questions.

The first critical-thinking task is therefore often not “Is the argument valid?” but:

- What has entered the representation?
- What has been omitted?
- What was observed?
- What was inferred?
- Which categories organized attention?
- Whose perspective supplied the map?
- How much confidence does the map deserve?

This is the home of **CT-T01 — Ethical Attention / First Map Audit** and **CT-T07 — Perspective / Moral-Visibility Audit**.

The corresponding failure modes are immediately human and recognizable:

- **CT-F01 Map Substitution:** the representation is treated as the thing represented.
- **CT-F02 Omission Failure:** relevant actors, facts, burdens, perspectives, or uncertainty never enter the map.

### Claim ceilings

**CE-01:** Map ≠ World.  
**CE-02:** Map of Person ≠ Person.

# PART II — ASSEMBLY HISTORIES

## 4. How maps become ours

A human inquirer does not create every concept, habit, expectation, value, or distinction from scratch.

We are formed through repeated interaction with caregivers, languages, practices, bodies, places, educational systems, communities, technologies, institutions, media, traditions, rewards, sanctions, failures, successes, and accidents. Over time, some distinctions become easy to see and others difficult to notice. Some responses become habitual. Some expectations become “common sense.” Some social arrangements become nearly invisible because they are experienced as normal.

FSP calls the path through which a present pattern of perception, judgment, identity, or practice has been assembled an **assembly history**.

Assembly history does not imply that a person is merely the passive product of causes. Human beings also reinterpret, resist, adopt, revise, combine, and reject what they inherit. The point is to make formation inspectable.

The governing question is:

> **How did this map, disposition, expectation, or practice become available and compelling to this inquirer?**

This connects directly to **CT-T03 — Formation / Practical-Wisdom Audit** and **CT-T10 — Background Power / Genealogy Audit**.

Two failures become especially important:

- **CT-F05 Formation Blindness:** present choice is analyzed while the practices that formed perception and judgment disappear.
- **CT-F11 Background-Condition Blindness:** a choice is treated as self-contained although structures shaped its available options.

But assembly history must itself be governed. Explaining where a belief came from does not by itself show whether the belief is true, justified, good, or bad.

That yields the firewall:

> **Formation ≠ justification.**

and the related genealogical ceiling:

> **Origin ≠ validity.**

This prevents two opposite errors: pretending that present judgment has no history, and pretending that discovering its history settles its validity.

---

## 5. Assembly histories of self, others, and institutions

FSP treats assembly history as relevant at several scales.

A person develops maps of the environment, maps of other people, and maps of self. Groups develop shared vocabularies and expectations. Institutions stabilize categories, procedures, roles, permissions, metrics, records, incentives, and default pathways. Technologies alter what can be noticed, remembered, compared, predicted, and acted upon.

These layers interact.

A person may therefore inherit an institutional category, use it to interpret another person, receive feedback shaped by that category, and then incorporate that feedback into self-understanding. None of those transitions is automatically illegitimate, but each is a possible site of compression, overreach, or error.

FSP therefore treats assembly history not as a hidden causal story behind “real reasoning,” but as one of the conditions that reflective reasoning may need to inspect.

# PART III — FROM LIVED INQUIRY TO REFLECTIVE CRITICAL THINKING

## 6. Reflection makes inquiry inspectable

Critical thinking becomes explicit when some part of our own inquiry can itself become an object of inquiry.

We can ask:

- What did I actually observe?
- What did I add?
- Why do I believe this?
- What reasons support the claim?
- What would count against it?
- What else could explain the evidence?
- What is the strongest conclusion the evidence licenses?
- What am I uncertain about?
- What might another person see differently?
- How did I come to frame the problem this way?

This reflective turn does not remove fallibility. Reflection can itself be selective, biased, overconfident, or institutionally constrained. But it creates the possibility of governed correction.

---

## 7. Map, argument, and judgment

FSP distinguishes three structures that are often collapsed.

A **map** represents a situation.

An **argument** connects reasons to a claim.

A **judgment** adopts a bounded conclusion for some purpose.

Hence:

> **Map ≠ Argument ≠ Judgment.**

A map can be poor even when reasoning from it is valid. An argument can be strong without settling what action should follow. A judgment can be reasonable while remaining provisional. A person may have sufficient evidence to believe a proposition without having standing to impose an intervention on another person.

The familiar tools of critical thinking operate especially strongly in the transition from reasons to conclusions. FSP keeps them there and adds governance around them.

---

## 8. Traditional critical thinking retained

FSP explicitly retains the central achievements of traditional critical thinking.

These include:

- argument identification and reconstruction;
- premise/conclusion analysis;
- deductive validity and soundness;
- inductive strength;
- abductive inference and inference to the best explanation;
- analogical reasoning;
- conceptual clarification;
- counterexample construction;
- fallacy identification;
- source and evidence evaluation;
- causal reasoning;
- probability and uncertainty;
- disagreement and objection;
- revision in light of reasons.

FSP does not treat these practices as obsolete.

Its extension is motivated by a different observation: **good argumentation can occur inside a poor map, from an incomplete perspective, under distorted institutional conditions, or in support of a judgment that does not itself authorize the proposed action.**

Thus the canonical relationship is:

> **FSP extends rather than replaces traditional critical thinking.**

# PART IV — GOVERNED INQUIRY

## 9. Why reflection needs governance

Reflection is not automatically self-correcting.

An inquirer may:

- reason accurately from an impoverished representation;
- infer more than the evidence supports;
- treat an authority's assertion as its own justification;
- ignore how practices formed perception;
- assign responsibility without considering constraint;
- confuse power with legitimacy;
- bypass another person's agency for their supposed benefit;
- hide distributional burdens inside aggregate benefit;
- inflate causal confidence;
- ignore background conditions shaping choice;
- infer invalidity from origin;
- mistake procedural sameness for fairness;
- confuse nominal resources with effective capabilities;
- treat evidence sufficient for belief as sufficient for intervention;
- convert a present judgment into permanent closure.

These are not all ordinary formal fallacies. They are failures at different transitions in situated inquiry.

FSP therefore understands **governed inquiry** as the deliberate regulation of those transitions.

A useful canonical arc is:

```text
lived situation
→ representation
→ examination
→ evidence and reasons
→ alternatives
→ bounded judgment
→ standing
→ authorization
→ action or repair
→ consequences
→ REOPEN
```

The arc is not a mandatory linear algorithm. Inquiry may loop, branch, reverse, or re-enter earlier stages. The value of the map is diagnostic: it helps identify what kind of work is currently needed.

# PART V — THE TWELVE-TOOL ECOLOGY

## 10. Tools are inquiry operators, not verdict machines

The twelve canonical Tools are best understood as a functional ecology.

The governing rule is:

> **Problem topology → Tool selection → Tool sequencing → bounded judgment.**

No Tool automatically settles a case.

> **Tool ≠ Verdict.**

A Tool changes visibility, tests a transition, exposes a vulnerability, constrains inference, or generates a more responsible next state.

### CT-T01 — Ethical Attention / First Map Audit
**Function:** Inspect the representation before reasoning confidently from it.  
**Question:** What happened? What am I adding? What am I failing to notice?  
**Ceiling:** Improved representation ≠ final judgment.

### CT-T02 — Examination / Reason-Giving Audit
**Function:** Test claims, reasons, explanatory direction, and inferential licenses.  
**Question:** What is the claim? What reasons support it? What follows, and what does not?  
**Ceiling:** Failed justification ≠ opposite conclusion proved.

### CT-T03 — Formation / Practical-Wisdom Audit
**Function:** Examine how habits and practices form practical perception and judgment.  
**Question:** What is this practice forming people to notice, enjoy, avoid, choose, and become?  
**Ceiling:** Formation ≠ justification.

### CT-T04 — Control / Desire / Moral-Range Audit
**Function:** Calibrate agency and responsibility under constraint.  
**Question:** What is controllable, influenceable, or outside control?  
**Ceiling:** Remaining agency ≠ unlimited responsibility.

### CT-T05 — Moral Authority / Common-Good Audit
**Function:** Test whether a rule has moral standing beyond enforceability.  
**Question:** Why should this rule bind?  
**Ceiling:** Good purpose ≠ uniquely justified rule.

### CT-T06 — Legitimacy / Standing Audit
**Function:** Test who may exercise consequential authority and under what limits.  
**Question:** Why may this actor exercise consequential authority here?  
**Ceiling:** Illegitimacy ≠ unlimited resistance.

### CT-T07 — Perspective / Moral-Visibility Audit
**Function:** Test how other persons are represented and where perspective remains incomplete.  
**Question:** Whose situation is being represented, by whom, and with what limits?  
**Ceiling:** Sympathy ≠ direct access.

### CT-T08 — Respect / Duty / Autonomy Audit
**Function:** Test whether an action preserves another person as an agent.  
**Question:** Does this action preserve the person as an agent rather than merely manage them?  
**Ceiling:** Autonomy ≠ preference sovereignty.

### CT-T09 — Consequence / Welfare / Harm Audit
**Function:** Evaluate effects, uncertainty, distribution, and coercion.  
**Question:** Who is affected, what happens, how certain are we, and who bears the burden?  
**Ceiling:** Benefit ≠ complete justification.

### CT-T10 — Background Power / Genealogy Audit
**Function:** Inspect conditions shaping choices, categories, and values before explicit decisions occur.  
**Question:** What is shaping the choice before the choice appears?  
**Ceiling:** Origin ≠ validity.

### CT-T11 — Fair-Structure / Veil-of-Ignorance Audit
**Function:** Evaluate institutional rules without privileging one's present position.  
**Question:** Would I choose these rules without knowing which position would be mine?  
**Ceiling:** Inequality ≠ automatically unjust.

### CT-T12 — Dependency / Effective-Capability Audit
**Function:** Test whether nominal resources become real possibilities for action.  
**Question:** What can this person actually do and be under these conditions?  
**Ceiling:** Resource ≠ capability; capability ≠ functioning.

The Tool Rack is therefore not a twelve-step checklist and not twelve competing ethical theories. It is a repertoire of governed interventions into inquiry.

# PART VI — FAILURE ROUTING

## 11. Start from what seems to be going wrong

For many learners, the most usable entrance into the Tool ecology is not memorizing Tool names. It is recognizing a failure symptom.

The canonical routing grammar is:

```text
symptom
→ diagnostic question
→ likely failure mode
→ primary Tool
→ optional secondary Tool
→ claim ceiling
→ revised inquiry
```

The sixteen canonical failures are:

1. **Map Substitution** — representation becomes reality.
2. **Omission Failure** — relevant actors, facts, burdens, perspectives, or uncertainty never enter.
3. **Inferential Overreach** — the conclusion says more than the reasons establish.
4. **Direction-of-Justification Failure** — what requires justification is used as its own justification.
5. **Formation Blindness** — choice is detached from the practices that formed perception and judgment.
6. **Agency Miscalibration** — responsibility ignores control and constraint.
7. **Authority Substitution** — power, office, expertise, or enforcement is mistaken for moral standing.
8. **Agency Bypass** — intervention manages a person while bypassing them as an agent.
9. **Aggregation Blindness** — total benefit hides distributional burdens.
10. **Causal Confidence Inflation** — prediction, correlation, or association is treated as stronger causal knowledge than warranted.
11. **Background-Condition Blindness** — choice is treated as self-contained despite a shaped choice field.
12. **Genealogical Fallacy** — origin is treated as proof of invalidity.
13. **Procedural Neutrality Illusion** — formally identical rules are assumed fair despite unequal positions.
14. **Resource/Capability Collapse** — formal resources are treated as effective possibilities.
15. **Judgment/Authorization Collapse** — evidence sufficient for belief is treated as sufficient for intervention.
16. **Closure Inflation** — a present judgment is treated as permanently settled.

Failure diagnosis is itself bounded:

> **Failure diagnosis routes inquiry; it does not decide the verdict.**

# PART VII — JUDGMENT, STANDING, AND AUTHORIZATION

## 12. A conclusion does not act by itself

One of FSP's most important extensions of familiar critical thinking is the distinction between a supported judgment and the standing to act on it.

A conclusion may be well supported while leaving open:

- who may recommend;
- who may classify;
- who may constrain;
- who may impose costs;
- who may intervene;
- who must consent;
- who bears responsibility;
- whose expertise is relevant;
- what institutional authority is legitimate.

Thus:

> **Supported judgment ≠ unrestricted authorization.**

and:

> **Generate ≠ Recommend ≠ Authorize.**

The transition from judgment to action therefore requires its own inquiry.

This is especially important in medicine, education, management, law, public policy, research, science communication, and institutional ethics, but the grammar is broader than any one domain.

### Canonical failure

**CT-F15 — Judgment/Authorization Collapse**

### Canonical question

> What action is actually authorized by this evidence and standing?

### Claim ceilings

**CE-06:** Benefit ≠ authorization.  
**CE-07:** Power ≠ legitimacy.

# PART VIII — INSTITUTIONAL AMPLIFICATION AND CONNECTED DOING

## 13. Inquiry does not remain inside individual minds

Human Doing is connected.

Judgments become reports, rules, rankings, procedures, diagnoses, curricula, models, policies, interfaces, databases, defaults, incentives, and institutional routines. Once embedded, they can affect people who never participated in the original reasoning.

Institutions may preserve memory and expertise, coordinate action, distribute responsibility, and enable inquiry that individuals could never perform alone. They may also amplify omissions, harden categories, separate decision makers from consequences, stabilize power, and make inherited assumptions difficult to see.

The move from individual inquiry to institutional practice therefore introduces additional questions:

- Which representation became operational?
- Who can revise it?
- Which data or voices count?
- What incentives stabilize it?
- Who receives benefits and burdens?
- Where is responsibility distributed?
- What happens to dissent?
- What evidence can trigger repair?
- Can people affected by the institution contest its map?
- Has a provisional judgment become infrastructure?

This is where CT-T06, CT-T09, CT-T10, CT-T11, and CT-T12 often become especially important.

Institutional analysis does not imply that institutions are merely distortions of otherwise pure individual reasoning. Institutions are part of the environment in which human inquiry occurs. The task is to make their epistemic, ethical, and practical effects inspectable.

# PART IX — CLAIM CEILINGS

## 14. Ask what does not yet follow

FSP treats a claim ceiling as a standing discipline.

A claim ceiling marks the strongest conclusion the current evidence, Tool, model, reconstruction, or inquiry transition licenses.

The question is:

> **What can we responsibly say now—and what does not yet follow?**

The twelve reusable ceilings are:

- **CE-01 Map / World:** Map ≠ World.
- **CE-02 Map / Person:** Map of Person ≠ Person.
- **CE-03 Evidence / Conclusion:** Evidence does not license a stronger conclusion than it supports.
- **CE-04 Model / Ontology:** Model success ≠ ontology.
- **CE-05 Origin / Validity:** Origin ≠ validity.
- **CE-06 Benefit / Authorization:** Benefit ≠ authorization.
- **CE-07 Power / Legitimacy:** Power ≠ legitimacy.
- **CE-08 Formal Choice / Practical Freedom:** Formal choice ≠ equal practical freedom.
- **CE-09 Resource / Capability:** Resource ≠ capability.
- **CE-10 Current / Final:** Current judgment ≠ final closure.
- **CE-11 Probe / Fact:** Probe result ≠ historical fact.
- **CE-12 Shared Grammar / Ontology:** Shared inquiry grammar ≠ shared domain ontology.

Claim ceilings are not skeptical vetoes. They do not demand impossible certainty. They calibrate confidence and prevent a successful local inference from silently acquiring broader authority.

# PART X — REPAIR AND REOPEN

## 15. Inquiry is diachronic

A judgment changes its significance when it enters the world.

Policies create consequences. Models meet new evidence. Institutions sediment practices. Ethical decisions alter relationships and capabilities. Historical interpretations encounter new archives. Scientific frameworks meet anomalies, improved measurements, and rival explanations.

Responsible inquiry must therefore remain capable of learning after action.

A **repair** targets an identified failure while preserving what remains warranted.

**REOPEN** specifies the conditions under which a current settlement should return to inquiry.

Possible REOPEN triggers include:

- relevant new evidence;
- a failed prediction;
- a counterexample;
- newly visible affected parties;
- changed circumstances;
- unanticipated consequences;
- exposed provenance problems;
- a successful challenge to standing or authority;
- an alternative explanation with better support;
- evidence that an institutional repair has produced a new failure.

The governing distinction is:

> **Current judgment ≠ final closure.**

REOPEN is not indefinite indecision. It allows bounded action now while preserving a disciplined route for correction later.

# PART XI — RETURN TO LIVED DOING

## 16. The endpoint is not the framework

FSP formalizes inquiry in order to improve lived human Doing.

The endpoint is therefore not mastery of a vocabulary, a diagram, an app, or a Tool Rack.

The endpoint is a person or group better able to:

- notice what a first map omitted;
- separate observation from interpretation;
- give and examine reasons;
- recognize how formation shaped perception;
- calibrate agency under constraint;
- distinguish power from legitimacy;
- represent others without claiming direct access;
- preserve agency when intervening;
- inspect consequences and distribution;
- see background structures shaping choice;
- test institutional fairness;
- distinguish resources from effective capabilities;
- separate judgment from authorization;
- state a bounded conclusion;
- know what would make that conclusion reopen.

This is **Authorial Return**.

Inquiry aids may reconstruct, compare, simulate, retrieve, visualize, and expose. They do not become the final author of the human judgment.

> **Cognitive support ≠ outsourced authorship.**

The full FSP arc therefore returns to where it began:

```text
Lived Doing
→ situated inquiry
→ map
→ reflective inquiry
→ governed inquiry
→ bounded judgment
→ authorized action
→ consequences
→ repair / REOPEN
→ Authorial Return
→ Lived Doing
```

# PART XII — DOMAIN PROJECTION CONTRACT

## 17. One canonical owner, many projections

The FSP Encyclopedia owns the canonical architecture.

Domain books, courses, labs, apps, and cases may project this architecture into their own teaching context.

A projection may:

- use ordinary-language labels;
- foreground only the Tools needed for that domain;
- reorder presentation for pedagogical purposes;
- use domain-native cases;
- add domain-specific evidence standards;
- add domain-specific forms of standing and authority;
- compress the formal architecture;
- link back to the canonical Encyclopedia for depth.

A projection may not silently:

- redefine Map ≠ World ≠ Person;
- collapse possibility into recommendation or authorization;
- turn a Tool into a verdict;
- erase claim ceilings;
- treat formation as justification;
- treat origin as invalidity;
- treat shared grammar as shared ontology;
- convert a current judgment into final closure;
- replace domain expertise with FSP vocabulary;
- claim that FSP supplies a universal ontology or universal decision procedure.

Thus:

> **Projection ≠ competing canonical definition.**


# PART XIII — ACCEPTANCE TESTS

## 20. Canonical acceptance criteria

FSP-CT-CANONICAL-1 passes only if all of the following remain true:

1. **Human starting point:** Critical thinking begins from situated, fallible human inquiry rather than from abstract argument alone.
2. **No psychological overclaim:** The lived-inquiry arc is explicitly a philosophical reconstruction, not a universal empirical developmental theory.
3. **Assembly-history distinction:** Formation is inspectable without being confused with justification.
4. **Traditional CT preservation:** Logic, argument, evidence, inference, fallacies, counterexamples, and source evaluation remain explicit.
5. **Extension rather than replacement:** FSP's distinctive contribution lies in governing transitions surrounding argument.
6. **Tool ecology preservation:** CT-T01–CT-T12 retain their canonical functions and claim ceilings.
7. **Failure routing preservation:** CT-F01–CT-F16 remain diagnostic routes rather than verdicts.
8. **Judgment/authorization firewall:** Supported judgment does not automatically authorize intervention.
9. **Institutional layer:** Individual inquiry is connected to institutional amplification, sedimentation, and repair.
10. **Claim-ceiling discipline:** Every successful inquiry move remains bounded by what it licenses.
11. **REOPEN:** Current judgment preserves conditions for correction.
12. **Authorial Return:** Formalization returns to lived human Doing rather than replacing it.
13. **Shared grammar boundary:** Cross-domain reuse does not imply shared ontology.
14. **Projection governance:** Ethics and History of Inquiry inherit one canonical architecture while retaining domain-native evidence, ontology, authority, and cases.
15. **No vocabulary inflation:** This build adds no thirteenth Tool, no seventeenth failure mode, and no new constitutional primitive merely to express the situated-inquirer foundation.

---

# 21. Canonical compression

For short public use, the architecture may be compressed to:

> **Human beings are situated, fallible inquirers. We build partial maps of situations, other people, and ourselves; those maps have assembly histories; and we use them to reason, judge, and act before uncertainty disappears. Traditional critical thinking helps us test reasons, evidence, inference, and argument. FSP extends that work by governing the surrounding transitions: how maps are formed, what is omitted, what evidence licenses, whose perspective and standing matter, what a judgment authorizes, how institutions amplify decisions, and what consequences require repair or reopening. Its Tools do not supply automatic verdicts. They help human beings make more inspectable, bounded, revisable judgments and return those judgments to lived Doing.**

That compression may be used as the conceptual seed for domain-specific introductions, but the present document remains the canonical owner.

---

## 22. Final claim ceiling

FSP-CT-CANONICAL-1 proposes a unified philosophical architecture for understanding critical thinking as governed situated inquiry.

It does **not** establish that every person, culture, discipline, or institution follows one universal cognitive sequence. It does **not** replace empirical psychology, cognitive science, developmental research, history, ethics, logic, philosophy of science, or domain expertise. It does **not** claim that all domains share an ontology.

Its stronger but bounded claim is this:

> **Across many forms of human inquiry, recurring vulnerabilities arise when situated inquirers move from experience to representation, from representation to reasons, from reasons to judgment, from judgment to action, and from action to revision. Making those transitions inspectable—and governing them with Tools, failure diagnostics, claim ceilings, standing constraints, repair, and REOPEN—provides a reusable extension of critical thinking while preserving the differences that make each domain its own.**
