# FSP-OBJECTIVE-PHENOMENOLOGY-1

## Nagel's Bat, Umwelt Constraint Maps, Heterogeneous Sensory Reconstruction, Phenomenal Translation Remainder, and Cross-Species Evidence

**Status:** RESEARCH FRONTIER / EXPERIMENTAL FORMALISM — VERSION 1  
**Role:** Extend FSP-HETEROGENEOUS-INTELLIGENCE-FORMALISM-1 into a disciplined program for cross-species inquiry into phenomenal structure without claiming possession of another organism's first-person point of view.  
**Primary purpose:** Formalize how heterogeneous behavioral, physiological, neural, ecological, computational, instrumental, and comparative evidence can progressively constrain an organism-relative phenomenal-world map while explicitly preserving what remains inaccessible or untranslated.  
**Primary claim ceiling:** This architecture does not solve the hard problem of consciousness, does not derive qualitative character from third-person data, does not prove that any particular nonhuman system is conscious, and does not license identifying structural reconstruction with first-person possession.

---

# 1. Opening Problem

Thomas Nagel's question remains:

> **What is it like to be a bat?**

The familiar mistake is to treat the question as forcing a binary:

```text
either
humans can know what bat experience is like

or
bat phenomenology is scientifically inaccessible
```

A stronger research program distinguishes:

```text
phenomenal identity
≠
phenomenal structure
≠
behavioral competence
≠
sensory access
≠
environmental structure
```

The central FSP question is therefore:

> **How much can heterogeneous evidence constrain the structure of another organism's phenomenal world without confusing an increasingly adequate map with possession of that organism's point of view?**

---

# 2. Nagel's Current Clarification

Nagel's 2024 commentary, **Objective bat phenomenology**, explicitly rejects the interpretation that his original argument meant humans can know nothing about bat experience.

He states that empirical discoveries about bats and other animals are examples of the sort of partial **objective phenomenology** he had envisioned.

The enduring limit is not zero knowledge.

The limit is that biological and experiential difference may prevent complete first-person-equivalent access.

Thus the architecture begins from:

> **Partial objective phenomenology is possible.**

But:

> **Objective phenomenology ≠ first-person equivalence.**

---

# 3. Four-Level Distinction

The first formal distinction is:

```text
W
≠
D_i(W)
≠
A_i(D_i(W))
≠
P_i
```

where:

- (W) = environmental/world structure;
- (D_i(W)) = structure detectable by organism (i);
- (A_i(D_i(W))) = structure discriminable and/or actionable by organism (i);
- (P_i) = phenomenal structure or phenomenal field of organism (i), if present.

This yields the primary candidate Firewall:

> **Detectable ≠ discriminable ≠ actionable ≠ phenomenal.**

Evidence may constrain transitions among these levels.

It does not erase them.

# 5. Constraint Rather Than Duplication

The goal is not to duplicate the organism's experience.

The goal is to progressively exclude bad reconstructions.

A constraint may establish that:

- a distinction is detectable;
- a distinction guides behavior;
- a relation is spatially usable;
- a modality contributes to navigation;
- two signals are integrated;
- a stimulus is behaviorally salient;
- a memory trace is required.

Thus objective phenomenology can advance by narrowing possibility.

> **More constraint ≠ phenomenal duplication.**

---

# 6. Phenomenal Identity

Define **phenomenal identity** as the intrinsic qualitative character of an experience as lived by the subject.

The architecture treats phenomenal identity as a separate epistemic target.

It does not assume that third-person reconstruction can deliver it.

> **Third-person reconstruction ≠ possession of first-person experience.**

> **Structural similarity ≠ qualitative identity.**

---

# 7. Phenomenal Structure

Define **phenomenal structure** provisionally as relational organization within experience that may be constrained by evidence.

Candidate dimensions include:

- spatial organization;
- temporal organization;
- salience;
- figure/background relations;
- discriminable differences;
- modality interaction;
- continuity;
- uncertainty;
- orientation;
- action affordance;
- social significance.

A structural fact may be partially objective even when intrinsic qualitative character remains unresolved.

This connects to recent literature defending the possibility of objective phenomenal facts while retaining an explanatory gap.

> **Scientific progress ≠ elimination of all phenomenal remainder.**

# 13. Ecological Evidence

An organism's sensory world is structured by what matters in its ecological tasks.

For bats this may include:

- prey;
- obstacles;
- roosts;

> **Sensory capacity ≠ ecological significance.**

But:

> **Comparative similarity ≠ phenomenal identity.**

> **Behavioral analogy ≠ identical experience.**

> **Causal contribution ≠ phenomenal content.**

# 16. Heterogeneous Sensory Reconstruction

A key experimental strategy is to construct artificial systems that recover some of the same environmental information available to the organism.

Examples:

- ultrasonic microphones;
- robotic echolocation;
- spatial acoustic models;
- simulated sensory agents;
- VR or haptic remappings.

These systems may reveal:

- information availability;
- discriminability;
- temporal requirements;
- task sufficiency.

But:

> **Artificial sensory reconstruction ≠ reconstructed animal experience.**

# 17. Artificial System as Epistemic Prosthesis

An artificial agent should not be treated as a synthetic bat consciousness.

Its role is narrower.

```text

 shared environment

```

Comparing performance may help identify:

- which environmental features are available;
- which computations are sufficient;
- which task structures are possible.

Thus:

> **Artificial reconstruction can test accessibility without reproducing phenomenality.**

# 18. Bat Case — Echolocation

The bat case provides a canonical stress test.

The empirical question is not merely whether bats emit ultrasonic calls.

It is:

> What structures of the environment become available for discrimination and action through the echolocation loop?

Candidate variables include:

- echo delay;
- frequency structure;
- intensity;
- motion;
- spatial direction;
- texture;
- landmark regularity;
- call timing.

The architecture asks what each variable licenses.

# 19. Bat Case — Long-Distance Acoustic Navigation

Goldshtein et al. (Science, 2024) reported that Kuhl's pipistrelle bats could navigate several kilometers after translocation using echolocation alone under experimental sensory manipulations, with navigation improving when echolocation and vision were both available.

A large-scale echolocation model supported the possibility that environmental acoustic information could support map-based navigation.

This evidence strengthens claims about:

- actionable spatial structure;
- long-range acoustic landmark use;
- multimodal integration.

It does not establish:

- what acoustic space qualitatively feels like;
- that echolocation is experienced as vision;
- that a computational acoustic map is phenomenally identical to the bat's experience.

# 28. Exclusion as Knowledge

Objective phenomenology can progress by ruling out reconstructions.

For example:

If a bat reliably discriminates spatial relations unavailable under a proposed model, that model is inadequate.

Thus:

> **Knowing what experience cannot be like can be genuine progress.**

This is particularly important when positive qualitative description is unavailable.

# 29. Phenomenal Equivalence Classes

A possible later formalism is to define equivalence classes of phenomenal structures compatible with current evidence.

Let:

```text

```

be the set of candidate phenomenal structures consistent with evidence at time (t).

New evidence may yield:

```text
Φ_t+1 ⊆ Φ_t
```

without selecting one uniquely.

This is a stronger formal representation of partial objective phenomenology than claiming a single reconstruction.

# 30. Constraint Operators

Let:

```text
C_k
```

be an evidential constraint operator.

Then:

```text
C_k(Φ_t)
```

removes phenomenal structures inconsistent with evidence (k).

A sequence:

```text
Φ_0
→ C_1(Φ_0)
→ C_2(...)
→ ...
→ Φ_n
```

represents progressive constraint.

This formalism is neutral about whether (Φ_n) ever becomes a singleton.

# 34. Active Sensing

Bat echolocation is not passive reception.

The organism emits signals and changes them during action.

Thus the loop is:

```text
action
→ emitted signal
→ world interaction
→ return signal
→ updated action
```

This matters phenomenologically because sensory access is partly produced through Doing.

> **Sensory input ≠ passive world copy.**

This connects objective phenomenology to the broader FSP Doing architecture.
