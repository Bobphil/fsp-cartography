# FSP-VR-FIELD-EXPERIENCE-REVIEW-1

## Temporal Possibility Field versus Layered Diagram — Visual Experience Diagnostic

**Status:** VISUAL REVIEW / EXPERIENCE REGRESSION — VERSION 1  
**Parents:** FSP-VR-VISUAL-GRAMMAR-1; FSP-VR-MAATHAI-FIELD-1; FSP-VR-TEMPORAL-LENS-1  
**First object reviewed:** Maathai 1976 non-deployed SVG field  
**Primary question:** When the learner freezes, slows, normalizes, and accelerates the field, does the experience become one of moving through a lived possibility space, or does it remain a diagram whose layers are toggled?

---

# 1. Why This Review Exists

A technically correct visual encoding can still fail as a cognitive prosthesis.

A diagram can represent:
- time;
- relation;
- possibility;
- gates;
- evidence;
- uncertainty.

But representation alone is not enough.

FSP-VR aims to let the learner temporarily inhabit a decompressed field in which several relations can be held together, inspected, recomposed, and returned from.

Therefore:

> **Correct diagram ≠ lived possibility field.**

---

# 2. Two Competing Experience Profiles

## A. Layered Diagram

Typical signs:

- buttons switch visibility states;
- paths appear or disappear abruptly;
- the learner remains outside the field;
- temporal modes behave like filters;
- gates open explanatory panels but do not restructure perceived reachability;
- distant futures appear as additional objects;
- the center remains a symbol rather than a standpoint;
- no continuity is felt between near and far temporal scales.

## B. Lived Possibility Field

Typical signs:

- the historical present remains experientially anchored;
- temporal reach changes continuously;
- previously inspected relations persist as memory / trace;
- near-field details become decomposable when time freezes;
- distant possibilities emerge with visible prerequisite chains;
- gates are encountered as structured thresholds;
- moving outward increases contingency without becoming prediction;
- moving back inward restores the present without erasing what was learned;
- the learner can compare possibilities while remaining situated.

---

# 3. Eight Experience Criteria

Each criterion is scored:

- **0 — absent / diagram-only**
- **1 — partial / representational**
- **2 — field-like / experiential**

Maximum score: 16.

## E1 — Anchored Historical Present

Question:

> Does Maathai 1976 remain the stable situated reference point while temporal reach changes?

Pass requires more than a static center icon.

The user should understand that the present has not changed merely because the horizon has widened.

## E2 — Continuous Temporal Expansion

Question:

> Does Freeze → Slow → Normal → Accelerate feel like changing temporal resolution rather than turning layers on and off?

Field-like behavior requires visible continuity, interpolation, or unfolding.

Abrupt opacity changes score no more than 1.

## E3 — Near-Field Decomposition

Question:

> When time freezes, does the nearby field become richer and more inspectable?

Freeze should increase logical / relational resolution, not merely hide distant paths.

Possible decompositions:
- provenance;
- relation type;
- constraint;
- requirement;
- source ceiling;
- alternative interpretation.

## E4 — Contingency Growth

Question:

> As temporal reach expands, do distant possibilities visibly carry larger dependency structures?

The contingency stack should be spatially connected to the path rather than existing only in a side panel.

## E5 — Gate as Threshold

Question:

> Is a gate experienced as a threshold in a pathway whose requirements can be decomposed?

A label reading GATE plus a side panel is representational.

A field-like gate should make missing conditions visibly interrupt or redirect reachability.

## E6 — Persistence / Trace

Question:

> When the learner changes temporal scale, do previously inspected relations leave a recoverable trace?

Without persistence, each mode behaves like a separate diagram.

Potential traces:
- inspected-path memory;
- breadcrumb;
- ghosted relation;
- comparison pin;
- learner annotation.

## E7 — Reversible Temporal Movement

Question:

> Can the learner accelerate outward and then return inward without losing the relational discoveries made at the wider scale?

The movement should support recomposition, not reset.

## E8 — Situated Comparison

Question:

> Can the learner hold two or more paths in comparative awareness while remaining anchored in the same present field?

Field experience requires simultaneous relational comparison.

Single-selection replacement panels score no more than 1.

# 4. Current Maathai Field Review

The current implementation is reviewed against its actual behavior, not its intended semantics.

## E1 — Anchored Historical Present

**Score: 1 / 2**

Strength:
- Maathai 1976 remains visually centered.

Limitation:
- the center is still primarily a node in a diagram rather than an experienced standpoint.

## E2 — Continuous Temporal Expansion

**Score: 0 / 2**

Current behavior:
- temporal modes change maximum visible depth;
- distant paths change opacity / pointer availability.

This is functionally a layer filter.

There is no continuous unfolding, interpolation, travel, or temporal zoom.

## E3 — Near-Field Decomposition

**Score: 1 / 2**

Strength:
- selecting paths and gates exposes requirements;
- provenance and remainder can be inspected.

Limitation:
- Freeze currently reduces visible depth but does not increase the spatial resolution of near relations.

## E4 — Contingency Growth

**Score: 1 / 2**

Strength:
- a distant path has a larger computed contingency burden.

Limitation:
- the contingency stack appears mainly in the side panel rather than along the visual path.

## E5 — Gate as Threshold

**Score: 1 / 2**

Strength:
- gated paths have decomposable PRESENT / PARTIAL / ABSENT / UNKNOWN requirements.

Limitation:
- the gate is still a labeled icon; missing requirements do not yet visibly block, branch, or reshape the path.

## E6 — Persistence / Trace

**Score: 0 / 2**

Changing selection replaces the side-panel content.

The field does not retain inspected-path traces or learner comparison marks.

## E7 — Reversible Temporal Movement

**Score: 1 / 2**

The learner can move Freeze ↔ Accelerate without changing the historical state.

However, the system does not preserve discoveries made while moving between scales.

## E8 — Situated Comparison

**Score: 0 / 2**

Only one selected path is foregrounded at a time.

The learner cannot yet pin or compare multiple paths while remaining situated.

# 5. Current Score

**5 / 16**

Classification:

> **SEMANTICALLY STRONG DIAGRAM / EARLY FIELD PROTOTYPE**

The current implementation successfully encodes the visual grammar.

It does **not yet** produce a sufficiently field-like experience.

That is a useful result.

The next iteration should not add more historical content first.

It should make existing relations behave more like an inhabitable temporal field.

# 6. What the Current Prototype Already Gets Right

Retain:

- anchored 1976 reference point;
- radial temporal depth;
- multiple simultaneous possible paths;
- anti-prediction firewall;
- capability / authorization separation;
- decomposable gate requirements;
- provenance / remainder availability;
- temporal modes;
- contingency burden;
- Return / scaffold fading.

These are semantic assets.

The problem is experiential binding.

# 7. Highest-Value Experiential Changes

## X1 — Temporal Breathing

Replace abrupt mode switching with animated radial expansion / contraction.

Freeze:
- outer field recedes;
- near field expands spatially;
- local relations separate and become inspectable.

Accelerate:
- horizon moves outward;
- distant paths emerge gradually;
- prerequisite chains appear before the terminal possibility.

Goal:

> temporal zoom rather than layer toggle.

## X2 — Path-Borne Contingencies

Move prerequisites out of the side panel alone.

Represent them as intermediate beads / thresholds on the path.

Example:

Maathai  
→ public voice [present]  
→ organizational support [partial]  
→ coalition [absent]  
→ political opportunity [unknown]  
→ broader advocacy

This turns logical depth into visible path structure.

## X3 — Gate Expansion

Clicking a gate should temporarily expand the gate into its component requirements inside the field.

The path should visibly stop at missing requirements.

The learner should see why the gate cannot yet be crossed.

## X4 — Persistent Inspection Traces

Allow the learner to pin a path.

Pinned paths remain visible across temporal-mode changes.

This creates memory across scales.

## X5 — Comparative Pinning

Permit two or three paths to remain foregrounded simultaneously.

The learner can compare:
- distance;
- requirements;
- missing authority;
- uncertainty;
- reversibility.

## X6 — Present-State Zoom

Freeze should not merely hide distance.

It should enlarge the central field and reveal:
- institutions;
- relationships;
- assembly-history traces;
- local constraints;
- evidence status.

This is the visual analogue of “make time stop so logical depth becomes inspectable.”

# 8. Next Prototype Contract

The next renderer should preserve the same canonical scene graph and add an experience layer:

**TEMPORAL MODE  
→ VIEW TRANSFORM  
→ PATH DECOMPOSITION  
→ TRACE MEMORY  
→ COMPARATIVE FIELD  
→ RETURN**

Renderer state may include:

- temporal zoom factor;
- focal depth;
- pinned path IDs;
- expanded gate ID;
- trace history;
- inspection annotations.

None of these becomes canonical historical content.
