# 1. Frozen thesis

Full-Stack Philosophy addresses an interoperability and governance problem created by the success, specialization, institutionalization, and technological mediation of modern inquiry.

Its distinctive contribution is **architectural rather than elemental**.

FSP does not claim novelty for pluralism, participation, translation, uncertainty, systems thinking, argument analysis, institutional learning, or adaptive governance taken separately.

It proposes:

> **integration by governed relation rather than integration by reduction.**

Its canonical governance chain is:

```text
lived Doing
↔ selective attention
↔ articulation / map
↔ evidence and reasons
↔ Tool-guided inspection
↔ Firewall-governed transitions
↔ bounded judgment
↔ standing / recommendation / authorization
↔ coordinated action
↔ institutional amplification
↔ consequences / capability effects
↔ repair
↔ REOPEN
↔ Authorial Return
↔ lived Doing
```

Its Joint Task Space grammar is:

> **STATE → STANDING → TRANSLATION → REPAIR → AMPLIFICATION → REOPEN → RETURN.**

Its governing aspiration is:

> **cooperation without convergence.**

---

# 2. Frozen originality claim

The canonical paper claims originality at the level of **composition, transition governance, and recursive architecture**, not at the level of every component concept.

The strongest current formulation is:

> **FSP is a distinctive attempt to compose established and newly sharpened governance insights into a recursive, cross-domain, publicly navigable, and partially executable architecture that treats transitions among representation, inference, translation, standing, authorization, institutionalization, revision, and Return as first-class philosophical objects.**

# 3. Frozen constitutional commitments

The paper freezes the following distinctions as part of the current FSP constitutional core:

- Map ≠ World ≠ Person
- Tool ≠ Verdict
- Formation ≠ justification
- Origin ≠ validity
- Generate ≠ Recommend ≠ Authorize
- Benefit ≠ authorization
- Power ≠ legitimacy
- Translation ≠ identity
- Shared grammar ≠ shared ontology
- Current judgment ≠ final closure
- Joint task ≠ legitimate task
- Cooperation ≠ obligation to cooperate
- REOPEN ≠ rejection
- REOPEN ≠ perpetual reconsideration
- Return is recursive, not restorative
- Executable visibility ≠ practical importance
- Remainder ≠ unspecified mystery
- FSP map ≠ inquiry itself

# 4. Frozen neighboring-traditions position

The canonical paper explicitly recognizes strong neighboring traditions, including:

- boundary objects;
- trading zones;
- post-normal science;
- responsible innovation;
- adaptive governance;
- polycentric governance;
- co-production;
- discourse ethics;
- epistemic injustice;
- argumentation theory;
- systems thinking;
- inter/transdisciplinarity.

Their existence is not treated as a weakness in the FSP program.

Instead:

> **Many of the intellectual resources needed for responsible inquiry already exist, but they are themselves often distributed across disciplinary, philosophical, institutional, and technical silos.**

FSP therefore treats neighboring traditions as intellectual resources that may themselves require provenance, translation, remainder, bounded contribution, and governed interoperability.

- a new comparator cannot fit without serious distortion;
- empirical use reveals systematic harm or failure;

- formalization systematically dominates lived or domain-native evidence;
