# FSP-ENCYCLOPEDIA-USABILITY-1

## Public usability and content-density pass

Scope: Home → Chapter 1 → CH1.2 → Neptune/Vulcan.

This pass establishes first-reader conventions before they are propagated across the Encyclopedia.

### Home page convention

The public home page now answers the reader's question before exposing the architecture:

1. What problem is FSP trying to help me see?
2. What can I do here?
3. What is the recommended first journey?
4. What other entrances are available if I prefer to explore differently?

The home page therefore leads with a short explanation of inquiry under pressure and recommends the complete Chapter 1 → Neptune/Vulcan journey before listing the broader indexes.

### Chapter 1 convention

Chapter 1 now has a clear **Start here** block pointing to CH1.2 as the current complete public path. The rest of the chapter remains visible as a map, but the interface no longer gives a new reader five equally weighted section choices.

### Prose-density convention

Reader prose preserves paragraph boundaries from canonical pilot text instead of collapsing all paragraphs into one block. The chapter prose remains the primary reading surface.

### Atlas choice-density convention

The initial case card no longer exposes every routed transition and probe as simultaneous buttons.

The first-reader path is:

`Case card → Follow the inquiry through time → choose routed transition → controlled probe → source → claim ceiling → return`

The case card also permits direct access to the case claim ceiling for readers who want the bounded conclusion without traversing the full inspection path.

Full-case exploration remains explicit and secondary.

### Public return convention

The live Neptune/Vulcan reader now visibly links back to the Chapter 1 public map in addition to its exact internal Return-to-reading semantics.

This distinguishes two returns:

- **Authorial Return**: restore the exact prose origin inside the reader;
- **Public Return**: leave the vertical slice and return to its place in the Encyclopedia.

### Claim ceiling

This pass freezes first-reader information-density and progression conventions. It does not yet certify visual polish, user research with outside readers, complete Chapter 1 prose, or accessibility testing with assistive technology.
