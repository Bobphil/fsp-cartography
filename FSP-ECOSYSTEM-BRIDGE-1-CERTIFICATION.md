## Certified cross-environment contract

The bridge defines five projection environments:

1. Encyclopedia
2. Classroom
3. VR
4. Application
5. Research

All five are governed by one projection principle:

`ONE CANONICAL OBJECT → MANY PROJECTIONS`

Projection may alter presentation, interaction, scaffolding, spatialization, domain-native metadata, and instrumentation. It may not silently alter canonical identity, evidence, provenance, transition structure, probe status, or claim ceiling.

## Environment firewalls

### Encyclopedia

May explain and progressively disclose. May not invent chapter↔Atlas routes or inflate claims.

### Classroom

May scaffold, simplify notation, and attach learning metadata. May not replace learner authorship or convert pedagogical simplification into historical fact.

### VR

May spatialize, replay, embody perspective, and stage controlled manipulation. May not convert spatial or immersive salience into semantic, causal, historical, or evidential authority.

### Application

May bind domain-native vocabulary, governance, permissions, and contestability. May not inherit domain authority, convert possibility into recommendation/authorization, or erase affected-party standing.

### Research

May instrument, compare, measure transfer, and define failure thresholds. May not treat usability or performance gains as truth or normative validation.

## Return contract

Each projection carries an `EcosystemReturnToken` preserving:

- environment;
- origin ID;
- origin label;
- depth/mode;
- contextual path.

Return semantics are environment-native but constitutionally equivalent:

- Encyclopedia → exact prose/public context.
- Classroom → learner-authored judgment.
- VR → explicit debrief and non-immersive judgment.
- Application → real people/institutions/practices and decision authority.
- Research → evidence for architecture revision, limitation, or retention.

## Constitutional rules

1. One canonical inquiry object may have many projections; projection multiplicity does not imply semantic multiplicity.
2. Projection may change presentation, interaction, scaffolding, and domain-native metadata, but may not silently change canonical identity, provenance, transition history, or claim ceiling.
3. Concept and tool overlays explain or operationalize canonical material; they do not become new historical evidence.
4. Counterfactual and simulated states remain possibilities unless independently represented as canonical history.
5. Application projections may bind domain-native authority and governance, but FSP does not inherit that authority merely by representing it.
6. Every environment preserves a return path to human-authored judgment in the originating Doing.
7. Research evaluates projections and transfer; it does not certify the FSP ontology as unique or exhaustive.

## Claim ceiling

This certification establishes cross-environment identity and projection governance.

It does not prove that the current canonical FSP object model is unique, exhaustive, universally adequate, or societally authoritative. It does not establish that VR improves inquiry merely because canonical objects can be spatialized. It does not authorize real-world intervention merely because an application projection can represent a domain problem.

Those remain REOPENable philosophical and empirical questions.
