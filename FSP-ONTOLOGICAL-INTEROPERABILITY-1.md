# FSP-ONTOLOGICAL-INTEROPERABILITY-1

## Ontology-Relevant PRFs, Non-Privilege, Translation Anchors, Operational Sufficiency, Shared-Task Cooperation, and Substrate-Plurality Claim Ceiling

**Status:** RESEARCH ARCHITECTURE / CORE-CANDIDATE CLARIFICATION — VERSION 1
**Role:** Clarify how FSP can treat ontology as locally relevant to situated access, representation, and action without requiring one privileged ontology for heterogeneous inquiry or cooperation.
**Primary purpose:** Formalize ontology-relative interoperability across heterogeneous PRFs, distinguish substrate realization from universal ontological privilege, and provide a bounded bridge from Quinean ontological relativity and Putnamian conceptual relativity to FSP's translation and cooperation architecture.
**Primary claim ceiling:** This architecture does not establish metaphysical relativism, deny mind-independent reality, prove that all ontologies are equally adequate, or show that every sentient system realizes cognition through different substrates. It specifies conditions under which heterogeneous inquiry can proceed without prior ontological convergence.

# 1. Opening Problem

Human communication commonly presupposes substantial background overlap:

- object categories;
- causal expectations;
- temporal conventions;
- logical vocabulary;
- evidential standards;
- linguistic practices;
- social norms.

When this background is shared, communication often appears to proceed:

    shared ontology
    → shared propositions
    → shared inference
    → agreement / cooperation

When background ontology diverges, humans often infer:

    ontological disagreement
    → incompatible propositions
    → failed inference
    → failed cooperation

FSP rejects that inference as necessary.

It instead permits:

    different ontology-relevant PRFs
    → partial translation
    → preserved relations
    → calibrated evidence
    → bounded shared task
    → cooperation

> **Shared task ≠ shared ontology.**

# 2. Quine Comparator — Ontological Relativity

Quine's ontological relativity undermines the idea that reference and ontology can always be fixed absolutely and independently of a background theory or translation scheme.

Important FSP inheritance:

- reference is not always uniquely recoverable from behavioral or translation evidence;
- alternative ontological assignments may preserve the same observable inferential structure;
- translation is constrained but may remain underdetermined;
- ontology is linked to a theory or representational scheme rather than read directly from reality without mediation.

FSP does **not** simply adopt Quine's whole naturalistic program.

FSP adds explicit objects for:

- access route;
- translation topology;
- target binding;
- bridge burden;
- remainder;
- joint inquiry;
- task-specific interoperability;
- authorization.

> **Quinean underdetermination of reference ≠ FSP claim that ontology is irrelevant.**

# 3. Putnam Comparator — Conceptual Relativity

Putnam's work on conceptual relativity and internal realism challenges the idea of a single God's-eye description whose categories uniquely carve reality at its joints independently of all conceptual schemes.

Important FSP inheritance:

- more than one description may be legitimate without being mere verbal error;
- object individuation can depend on conceptual organization;
- truth and rational constraint need not require one uniquely privileged vocabulary;
- realism need not mean access to reality from nowhere.

FSP should not be identified with one phase of Putnam's evolving realism.

The useful comparator is narrower:

> **Conceptual plurality can coexist with non-arbitrary constraint.**

FSP adds operational machinery for asking what is preserved across differing conceptualizations and whether enough is preserved for inquiry or action.

# 4. FSP Difference from Quine and Putnam

Quine and Putnam primarily pressure the idea of uniquely privileged description.

FSP's additional problem is:

> **How can heterogeneous agents become mutually inspectable and selectively interoperable when their ontology-relevant partitions, access routes, and representational resources differ?**

FSP therefore shifts from:

    Which ontology is uniquely correct?

toward:

    Which relations must be preserved for this inquiry, translation, or task?

without declaring the metaphysical question meaningless or settled.

# 5. Ontology-Relevant PRF

A PRF is not ontologically neutral in the sense of lacking world-partitioning structure.

PRFs organize:

- salience;
- discrimination;
- affordances;
- expectation;
- representation;
- capability;
- memory;
- normative orientation.

These structures presuppose or enact distinctions concerning what counts as:

- object;
- event;
- agent;
- cause;
- boundary;
- persistence;
- evidence;
- relevant difference.

# 6. Three Ontology Conditions

FSP distinguishes:

## 6.1 Ontology-Relevant

An ontological distinction affects access, discrimination, representation, inference, or action.

## 6.2 Ontology-Required

A bounded inquiry or task requires some stable distinctions to proceed.

## 6.3 Ontological Convergence

Participants agree that the same categories provide the correct or fundamental account of what exists.

Firewall:

> **Ontology relevance ≠ ontology requirement ≠ ontological convergence.**

# 7. Ontological Non-Privilege Principle

## Decision

**CORE-CANDIDATE PRINCIPLE**

> **No representation, scale, ontology, or agent-native partition receives universal privilege merely because it is native to a particular agent, discipline, instrument, or mode of access. Privilege must be local to a task, claim, evidence relation, explanatory purpose, or demonstrated calibration.**

This is not equality among ontologies.

It is denial of unearned universal privilege.

# 8. Ontological Sufficiency Principle

## Decision

**CORE-CANDIDATE PRINCIPLE**

> **Heterogeneous agents need share only enough structure to support the relevant translation, evidence comparison, prediction, intervention, or coordinated task; they need not converge on a complete shared ontology.**

Thus:

    ontology-relevant
    ≠ ontology-privileged
    ≠ ontology-shared

# 9. Substrate Realization

PRF cannot float free of realizing conditions.

For a human, relevant realizing conditions may include:

- biological embodiment;
- perceptual organization;
- memory;
- language;
- social history;
- material environment.

For other systems, relevant realization may involve different organizations.

FSP should therefore distinguish:

    realizing substrate / organization

from:

    privileged fundamental ontology

> **Realization requirement ≠ universal substrate privilege.**

      assemblyHistory,
      accessEffects,
      capabilityEffects,

This object is deliberately inquiry-relative.

> **SubstrateProfile ≠ final metaphysical substrate.**

# 11. Substrate Plurality

Different systems may realize relevant capabilities through different structures.

Examples may include:

- biological neural and sensorimotor organization;
- non-neural biological organization;
- artificial hardware/software systems;
- institutional human-record-rule networks.

FSP does not infer equal agency or sentience from formal comparability.

> **Common formal role ≠ common ontological realization.**

# 12. Substrate-Plurality Claim Ceiling

FSP may claim:

> **A shared formal role can be multiply realized across differently organized systems.**

FSP may not yet claim:

- that all sentient systems use radically different substrates;
- that sentience is substrate-independent;
- that every substrate supporting discrimination supports phenomenality;
- that one physical description is false merely because another scale is useful.

# 14. OntologyCommitmentRequired

Suggested values:

    NONE
    LOCAL
    PARTIAL
    STRONG

Examples:

- two navigation systems may preserve distance/order relations with LOCAL ontology commitment;
- a metaphysical identity claim may require STRONG ontology commitment;
- a shared warning threshold may require almost none beyond operational calibration.

# 15. Translation Anchor Firewall

> **Preserved relation ≠ shared representation.**

> **Shared representation ≠ shared ontology.**

> **Shared ontology ≠ phenomenal identity.**

# 16. Operational Sufficiency

## Decision

**ADD — `OperationalSufficiencyRecord`**

    OperationalSufficiencyRecord = {
      participants,

      translationAnchors,
      minimumSharedStructure,

      evidence,
      failureConditions,

Definition:

> **Operational sufficiency is the minimum shared or translatable structure required for a bounded inquiry or coordinated task to succeed under stated criteria.**

# 17. Operational Sufficiency ≠ Truth Equivalence

Two agents may coordinate successfully while retaining incompatible wider ontologies.

> **Task success ≠ total representational equivalence.**

> **Operational sufficiency ≠ metaphysical agreement.**

# 19. Cooperation Without Ontological Convergence

FSP already preserves cooperation without convergence.

This build makes the ontological condition explicit:

    PRF_A
    + PRF_B
    ↓
    identify ontology-relevant differences
    ↓
    TranslationAnchor
    ↓
    OperationalSufficiency
    ↓
    bounded Interoperability
    ↓
    Joint Inquiry / Joint Task Space

> **Cooperation does not require ontological convergence when operational sufficiency is established.**

# 20. Joint Task Space Reconciliation

JTS should not be defined as an already shared world-model.

Revise the conceptual entry condition:

    heterogeneous PRFs
    → partial translation
    → translation anchors
    → shared constraints / task relations
    → operational sufficiency
    → JTS

> **JTS requires sufficient interoperability, not complete ontology sharing.**

# 21. Joint Inquiry Space Reconciliation

JIS can be even thinner than JTS.

Participants may jointly investigate a target while:

- using different categories;
- preserving different explanatory commitments;
- translating only selected variables;
- disagreeing about ontology.

> **Joint inquiry ≠ shared ontology.**

# 22. Logic and Ontology

Ordinary logic is often applied within a shared representation scheme.

FSP should distinguish:

## Object-Level Logic

Inference within a sufficiently shared representational system.

## Translation Logic

Rules governing preservation, loss, and transformation across representations.

## Task Logic

Relations that must hold for successful bounded coordination.

## Governance Logic

Rules governing what may be inferred, recommended, authorized, or left unresolved across heterogeneous PRFs.

These are functional distinctions, not claims that FSP has invented new formal logics.

# 23. Logic Firewall

> **Logical validity within one representation ≠ guaranteed translatability into another.**

> **Translatability ≠ ontological identity.**

> **Shared conclusion ≠ shared reasons or ontology.**

# 24. Quine–FSP Crosswalk

    reference may be underdetermined by translation evidence

FSP response:

    preserve translation alternatives + target binding + remainder

Quine pressure:

    ontology is relative to background theory / language

FSP response:

    index ontology relevance to PRF, task, scale, and access route

Quine pressure:

    no simple absolute reference fixing

FSP response:

    do not require global reference identity for bounded interoperability

# 25. Putnam–FSP Crosswalk

Putnam pressure:

    no God's-eye vocabulary guaranteed to uniquely describe reality

FSP response:

    Ontological Non-Privilege Principle

Putnam pressure:

    conceptual relativity can coexist with rational constraint

FSP response:

    calibration + bridge burden + task success + claim ceilings

Putnam pressure:

    object individuation can depend on conceptual organization

FSP response:

    `TargetBinding` + `OntologyRelevanceProfile`

# 26. FSP Is Not Quinean Indeterminacy Alone

FSP does not stop at:

    translation is underdetermined

It asks:

    what relations are preserved?
    what task requires them?
    what evidence calibrates them?
    what burden does the bridge carry?
    what remains untranslated?
    what can participants still do together?

# 27. FSP Is Not Putnamian Conceptual Relativity Alone

FSP does not stop at:

    more than one conceptual scheme can be legitimate

It adds:

    explicit interoperability objects
    + cross-perspectival calibration
    + remainder tracking
    + shared-task governance
    + authorization boundaries

# 28. Anti-Relativism Firewall

> **Ontological non-privilege ≠ all ontologies are equally adequate.**

Ontologies and representations remain answerable to:

- coherence;
- empirical fit;
- predictive success;
- intervention success;
- explanatory adequacy;
- translation performance;
- task performance;
- error correction;
- claim ceilings.

# 29. Anti-Absolutism Firewall

> **Empirical success of one ontology ≠ universal ontological privilege.**

A representation may be superior for one task and inferior for another.

# 30. Anti-Anthropocentrism Firewall

> **Human-native ontology ≠ universal ontology by default.**

Human categories may be privileged for human practices without being privileged for all sentient or sensing systems.

# 31. Sentience and Ontology

The bat case pressures a further research hypothesis:

> **Shared physical environment ≠ privileged shared phenomenal ontology.**

Different sentient organisms may organize actionable and phenomenal structure differently.

This remains a research claim, not a Core axiom.

# 32. Sentience Claim Ceiling

FSP does not infer:

- consciousness from detection;
- phenomenal ontology from functional ontology;
- substrate independence from cross-system formal similarity;
- identical worlds from shared task success.

# 33. PRF Realization Loop

A PRF may be represented as:

    substrate / realizing organization
    → access structure
    → discrimination
    → salience
    → representation
    → action
    → changed environment / history
    → revised realizing conditions / PRF

This is compatible with the Recursive-Scale Architecture.

# 34. Ontology-Relevance and Scale

`OntologyRelevanceProfile` should link to:

- `ScaleKind`;
- `RelationDomain`;
- `TargetBinding`;
- `AccessRelation`;
- `TranslationTopology`;
- `BridgeBurden`.

Reason:

what counts as an object or relation may differ with explanatory aperture.

# 35. Translation Anchor and Bridge Burden

Every TranslationAnchor should expose:

- what relation is preserved;
- what ontology commitment is required;
- what assumptions make preservation credible;
- what is lost;
- what remains unresolved.

> **Translation success ≠ ontology convergence.**

# 36. Failure Modes

The architecture fails if it:

- treats ontology as irrelevant;
- assumes every participant has a complete ontology;
- treats human conceptual partitions as universally privileged;
- treats successful cooperation as proof of shared ontology;
- treats translation failure as irrationality by default;
- treats all ontology differences as harmless;
- uses operational sufficiency to evade normative disagreement;
- infers sentience or moral standing from formal interoperability.

# 37. Regression Cases

## Bat / Human / Robot

Shared anchors may include:

- distance ordering;
- obstacle relation;
- route viability;
- temporal delay.

Ontologies and phenomenology need not converge.

## DESI / Astronomical Measurement

Detector counts, spectra, redshift models, and cosmological representations need not be ontologically identical to preserve calibrated inferential relations.

## Institution

Local events, classifications, aggregate metrics, and policy categories may preserve enough relations for coordination while losing local ontology.

## Human Communication

Two people may coordinate despite different causal, moral, or metaphysical interpretations if translation anchors and shared task relations are sufficient.

# 39. Regression Invariants

**OI1** Ontology relevance remains distinct from ontological convergence.

**OI2** No agent-native ontology receives automatic universal privilege.

**OI3** Translation anchors preserve relations without implying representation identity.

**OI4** Operational sufficiency is task-bounded.

**OI5** JTS requires interoperability, not complete ontology sharing.

**OI6** Quine comparator does not collapse FSP into reference indeterminacy.

**OI7** Putnam comparator does not collapse FSP into unconstrained relativism.

**OI8** SubstrateProfile remains inquiry-relative rather than metaphysically fundamental.

**OI9** Formal interoperability does not imply consciousness, standing, or phenomenal identity.

**OI10** Bridge burden and remainder remain visible.

# 40. Strongest Supported Claim

> **FSP can model heterogeneous inquiry in which PRFs are ontology-relevant and materially realized while no participant's ontology is granted universal privilege in advance. Selective interoperability can be established through translation anchors, calibration, operational sufficiency, and bounded shared tasks without requiring complete ontological convergence.**

# 41. Claim Ceiling

This architecture does not establish:

- metaphysical relativism;
- that reality is wholly scheme-dependent;
- that every ontology is equally good;
- that reference is always indeterminate;
- that sentience is substrate-independent;
- that ontology can be ignored in translation;
- that operational success proves truth;
- that cooperation settles metaphysical disagreement.

# 42. REOPEN Conditions

Reopen if:

- TranslationAnchor cannot distinguish genuine preservation from superficial task success;
- operational sufficiency hides unresolved normative conflict;
- SubstrateProfile becomes a disguised fundamental ontology;
- new cases require stronger shared ontology than the current framework permits;
- target binding fails across radically heterogeneous PRFs;
- Quinean indeterminacy or Putnamian conceptual relativity prove materially stronger/weaker comparators than represented here;
- ontology non-privilege becomes confused with ontology equality.
