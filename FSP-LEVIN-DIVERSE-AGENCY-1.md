# FSP-LEVIN-DIVERSE-AGENCY-1

## Multiscale Agency, Biological Plasticity, Morphogenetic Goal States, Enhanced Humans, Synthetic and Hybrid Organisms, Agential-Boundary Firewalls, Moral-Standing Profiles, Brandom/Turner Comparators, and Full-Stack Extension Test

**Status:** COMPARATOR / EXTENSION ARCHITECTURE — VERSION 1  
**Purpose:** Test whether FSP can represent multiscale biological agency, synthetic and hybrid organisms, enhanced humans, and unfamiliar forms of cognition without collapsing agency, phenomenal experience, classification, moral standing, or authorization into one another.  
**Primary comparator:** Michael Levin's diverse-intelligence, basal-cognition, bioelectricity, morphogenesis, and multiscale-agency research program.  
**Secondary comparators:** Robert Brandom on discursive normativity and reciprocal recognition; Steven Turner on anti-reification and social explanation; FSP Phenomenal-Node Ecology and Normative Profile architecture.  
**Primary claim ceiling:** Structural resonance between FSP and Levin-style multiscale agency ≠ endorsement of every theoretical interpretation of basal cognition, consciousness, or moral standing.

---

# 1. Why Levin matters for FSP

FSP has recently developed several ideas that create a natural point of contact with Michael Levin's research:

- phenomenal nodes;
- lossy PRFs;
- agency;
- capability;
- multiscale coordination;
- sedimentation;
- Joint Task Spaces;
- classification;
- moral-standing profiles;
- institutional amplification;
- REOPEN.

Levin's work pressures the assumption that agency belongs only to whole adult human organisms.

His research treats development, regeneration, physiological regulation, and behavior as cases in which living matter can display competencies across multiple scales.

This creates an immediate FSP question:

> **What happens if the relevant unit of agency is not fixed in advance?**

FSP should be able to represent the possibility that:

- cells exhibit local competencies;
- tissues coordinate toward larger anatomical outcomes;
- organs participate in regulatory control;
- organisms pursue behavioral goals;
- collectives may display higher-scale coordination;
- synthetic biological systems may exhibit unfamiliar forms of agency;
- hybrid biological / technological systems may produce new combinations.

But FSP must not infer too much from this.

---

# 2. The first new firewall

The central new Firewall is:

> **Detected agency ≠ moral standing.**

Agency is evidence about what a system can do.

Moral standing is a normative status under an adopted profile.

They must remain separate.

Likewise:

> **Goal-directed behavior ≠ phenomenal experience.**

> **Phenomenal experience ≠ full moral personhood.**

> **Biological organization ≠ moral authorization.**

These distinctions allow FSP to investigate unfamiliar life without either automatic anthropomorphism or automatic exclusion.

---

# 3. Multiscale agency

Levin's program suggests that biological competence can be distributed across levels.

A simple representation is:

```text
molecular processes
↓
cellular competence
↓
cell collective
↓
tissue-level coordination
↓
organism-level behavior
```

But the arrows should not be read as a single linear ladder.

Different levels may exhibit different task spaces, memory, feedback, and control.

The key FSP insight is:

> **Agential locus may be scale-relative.**

This does not imply that every scale is equally agent-like.

It means the scale of agency is an empirical question rather than an assumption.

---

# 4. Biological boundary ≠ agential boundary

The anatomical boundary of an organism may not always coincide neatly with the boundary of the process doing the relevant problem-solving.

Examples can include:

- distributed immune coordination;
- cellular collectives;
- developmental regulation;
- microbiome interactions;
- symbiotic systems;
- synthetic multicellular constructs;
- organism–technology hybrids.

Therefore:

> **Biological boundary ≠ agential boundary.**

A body may contain multiple nested loci of competence.

A larger agent may emerge from smaller agents.

A smaller agent may retain local competencies inside the larger whole.

---

# 5. Agential boundary ≠ moral boundary

Even if a cell collective counts as an agential system for a particular task, that does not determine its moral status.

So:

> **Agential boundary ≠ moral boundary.**

FSP must type:

1. agency;
2. cognition;
3. phenomenal-status evidence;
4. normative classification;
5. moral standing.

No step silently determines the next.

# 7. Goal spaces

Levin's work is particularly useful because it broadens the spaces in which goal-directed activity may occur.

A system can navigate:

- anatomical space;
- physiological space;
- transcriptional space;
- behavioral space;
- perhaps other abstract state spaces.

FSP can therefore generalize:

> **Doing need not mean locomotion or verbal decision.**

A system may act by:

- regulating;
- repairing;
- reorganizing;
- maintaining;
- differentiating;
- signaling;
- changing morphology.

# 8. Morphogenesis as a task space

Morphogenesis can be interpreted as a system moving through possible anatomical states.

A simple FSP representation is:

```text
current morphology
→ sensed deviation
→ distributed signaling
→ local cell actions
→ coordinated repair / development
→ revised morphology
```

This resembles a governed state-transition system.

But:

> **Morphogenetic regulation ≠ conscious deliberation.**

The similarity is structural, not an identity claim.

---

# 9. Morphogenetic goal state ≠ human intention

A tissue can behave as though a target morphology constrains its regulation.

That does not imply a human-like intention.

So:

> **Morphogenetic goal state ≠ human intention.**

This protects FSP from importing psychological vocabulary too quickly.

The system may exhibit goal-directedness without possessing the phenomenology associated with human wanting.

> **Agency evidence ≠ phenomenal-status evidence.**

# 14. Diverse intelligence as an FSP classification problem

"Intelligence" can become an overly broad category.

It should expose:

- what task is being solved;
- at what scale;
- with what memory;
- under what perturbation;
- through what control architecture;
- with what adaptability.

Thus:

> **Intelligence label ≠ explained competence.**

# 80. Final synthesis

Some may be evolved.

Some engineered.

Some hybrid.

Some nested within familiar organisms.

Some may emerge from combinations we have not yet encountered.

FSP should neither declare them persons nor dismiss them as mere mechanism in advance.

> What can this system do?

> Which normative profile is active?
