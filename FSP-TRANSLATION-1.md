# FSP-TRANSLATION-1

## Ontological-Agnostic PRF Translation, Claim/Warrant Decomposition, Translation Remainder, Cooperation-Without-Convergence Conditions, Agency/Anti-Domination Grammar, and Repair Test

**Status:** Research formalism / first executable stress-test layer  
**Repository boundary:** Outside the Cartography kernel. No new metaphysical commitment is added to the frozen state machine.

## 1. Research question

Can agents with non-identical PRFs and divergent ontological commitments achieve bounded mutual intelligibility, criticism, and cooperation without requiring global ontological convergence?

FSP-TRANSLATION-1 treats this as an empirical/formal research question rather than as an axiom.

The formalism is deliberately ontologically agnostic. It does not choose physicalism, idealism, theism, process ontology, relational ontology, information ontology, or any other substantive metaphysics as the privileged language into which other frameworks must be translated.

Instead it asks whether task-relative structure can be preserved across differences.

## 2. Wittgensteinian ladder constraint

The formalism is a scaffold.

Its success is not measured by whether users permanently think in FSP notation. Its purpose is to make distinctions visible strongly enough that a person can later act without the explicit scaffold and return to it only when difficult inquiry requires decompression.

Therefore:

- formal representation is not ultimate ontology;
- successful translation does not prove metaphysical identity;
- the formalism may be set aside after it has done its work;
- it may later be reused when disagreement, domination, institutional complexity, or uncertainty requires renewed mapping.

## 3. Minimal claim bundle

For a situated agent, a claim is represented for translation purposes as:

```
C_i = <p, E_i, W_i, B_i, T_i, Γ_i, O_i, S_i>
```

where:

- `p` = proposition or claim content;
- `E_i` = evidence recognized by agent i;
- `W_i` = warrants licensing relevant transitions;
- `B_i` = background commitments;
- `T_i` = bounded task in which the claim matters;
- `Γ_i` = claim ceiling;
- `O_i` = ontological commitments implicated by the claim;
- `S_i` = relevant standing.

The purpose is not to reduce a PRF to a tuple. The tuple exposes enough structure to test whether two claims diverge because of proposition, evidence, warrant, background commitment, task, scope, ontology, or standing.

## 4. Task-relative translation

For agents i and j:

```
τ^(T)_(i→j)
```

is a task-relative translation attempt.

It is not a total translation of `PRF_i` into `PRF_j`.

The system records explicit correspondences supplied by the investigator or participant. It does not infer equivalence automatically.

Three correspondence types are permitted in this first build:

- **DIRECT** — local content can be carried with minimal change;
- **STRUCTURAL** — vocabulary/content differs, but a relation, role, constraint, or inferential structure is preserved;
- **TASK_EQUIVALENT** — conceptual translation remains weak, but the elements play sufficiently similar roles for the bounded shared task.

## 5. Translation remainder

Every attempted translation may carry a remainder:

```
R^(T)_(i→j)
```

The remainder records what cannot presently be carried into the target representation without unacceptable loss or distortion.

Remainder is not failure by definition. It is a first-class result.

The formalism recognizes five outcomes:

1. **FULL_LOCAL** — all relevant local structure is preserved for this task;
2. **PARTIAL** — some relevant structure is preserved and some remains untranslated;
3. **STRUCTURAL** — inferential or relational structure is preserved despite divergent vocabulary or ontology;
4. **TASK_ONLY** — meaningful conceptual translation remains poor, but enough practical structure exists for bounded coordination;
5. **FAILURE** — no locally adequate translation has been established.

A serious FSP translation system must be allowed to return FAILURE.

## 6. No universal metalanguage claim

FSP does not claim a God's-eye language into which every PRF can be losslessly translated.

Translation may be asymmetric:

```
τ^(T)_(i→j) != τ^(T)_(j→i)
```

The remainder may also be asymmetric.

This protects the formalism against quietly reinstalling one ontology as the universal standard.

## 7. Cooperation without convergence

A shared task is assessed separately from ontological agreement.

For participants `P = {i...n}`, cooperation requires in this first build:

- a bounded task is explicitly stated;
- every participant accepts that task as a legitimate coordination target;
- at least one mutually compatible action exists.

It does **not** require:

```
PRF_i = PRF_j
```

or shared metaphysics.

The executable model distinguishes:

- **COOPERATION_POSSIBLE** — shared task accepted by all and compatible action exists;
- **COORDINATION_ONLY** — some practical alignment exists without full shared-task acceptance;
- **NO_SHARED_TASK** — the task itself is not mutually constituted.

This preserves the possibility that refusing a proposed shared task is the correct result.

## 8. Coordination is not automatically legitimate cooperation

FSP must not treat mere behavioral coordination as normatively sufficient.

A coerced worker, captive population, or dominated participant may coordinate behavior while lacking meaningful standing to define, contest, or reopen the task.

Therefore cooperation results must be read beside the Agency Audit rather than as proof of legitimacy.

## 9. Agency and anti-domination grammar

This build operationalizes agency protection through six deliberately coarse dimensions:

- **informationAccess** — can the agent access material information relevant to the task?
- **interpretiveContestability** — can the agent challenge how their situation or claims are represented?
- **optionVisibility** — are meaningful alternatives visible rather than strategically suppressed?
- **standingToParticipate** — can the agent participate in decisions that substantially affect their Doing?
- **reopenability** — can consequential decisions be revisited when evidence, context, consequence, or standing changes?
- **materialCapability** — does the agent possess enough effective capability to act on formally available options?

Each is coded:

- OPEN;
- CONSTRAINED;
- BLOCKED.

These categories are diagnostics, not measurements of human worth.

## 10. Domination-risk heuristic

FSP-TRANSLATION-1 uses a deliberately transparent first-pass heuristic:

- two or more BLOCKED agency dimensions → **SEVERE** domination risk;
- one BLOCKED dimension, or three or more CONSTRAINED dimensions → **ELEVATED** risk;
- otherwise → **LOW** within this narrow diagnostic.

This is not a theorem that domination has been morally established.

It is a trigger for deeper investigation.

The heuristic is intentionally visible so it can be criticized and replaced.

## 11. Repair grammar

A repair proposes changes to one or more agency dimensions.

A repair passes the minimal first test only when:

- at least one agency dimension improves; and
- no agency dimension becomes worse.

This is intentionally weak.

Later builds must add:

- effects on other participants;
- distributive consequences;
- institutional stability;
- externalities;
- feasibility;
- reversibility;
- power shifts;
- capability effects;
- new domination risks.

FSP-TRANSLATION-1 therefore tests whether repair can be represented without pretending to solve institutional ethics automatically.

## 12. Four-framework stress case: contested forest

The first stress case uses four intentionally divergent PRFs addressing a threatened forest.

### PRF-A — Ecological scientific naturalism

Salient entities may include ecosystems, biodiversity, hydrology, carbon, species viability, and measurable ecological processes.

Typical evidence:
- field observations;
- ecological models;
- species surveys;
- hydrological measurements.

### PRF-B — Property / market framework

Salient entities may include title, ownership, contract, investment, opportunity cost, legal rights, and economic productivity.

Typical evidence:
- property records;
- prices;
- legal instruments;
- projected returns.

### PRF-C — Relational Indigenous ecological framework

Salient relations may include kinship with land, obligations across generations, place-specific stewardship, community continuity, and non-reducible relationships among human and more-than-human life.

The test must not translate these commitments automatically into "ecosystem services" or market value.

### PRF-D — Religious stewardship framework

Salient relations may include creation, stewardship, obligation, sacred value, community responsibility, and limits on use.

The test must not assume that stewardship is simply ecological naturalism expressed religiously.

## 13. Stress-test task

Initial proposed task:

> Maintain ecological viability while determining permissible land use.

The formalism must allow any participant to reject this framing.

Task negotiation is therefore itself part of the inquiry.

If a bounded task is eventually accepted, the test asks whether cooperation can arise from task-relative overlap while ontological disagreement remains explicit.

## 14. Required stress-test findings

The formalism must be able to represent all of the following:

- DIRECT local correspondence;
- STRUCTURAL correspondence across different vocabularies;
- TASK_ONLY coordination;
- non-empty translation remainder;
- asymmetric translation;
- translation FAILURE;
- shared-task rejection;
- cooperation without ontological convergence;
- coordination under domination that does **not** count as legitimate cooperation by itself;
- a repair that improves agency;
- a proposed repair that worsens another agency dimension and therefore fails the minimal repair test.

## 15. Duhem–Quine / underdetermination research bridge

This build does not claim to prove the Duhem–Quine thesis.

It establishes a framework in which underdetermination-like claims can later be tested.

Candidate theorem schema:

If two ontologically non-identical PRFs preserve the same task-relevant empirical and practical structure, and both satisfy the relevant licensing constraints, success on task T does not by itself license ontological uniqueness.

Schematically:

```
PRF_A != PRF_B
and
E_T(PRF_A) = E_T(PRF_B)
does not entail
Ontology_A = Ontology_B
```

The important FSP principle is:

> task success does not automatically authorize ontological promotion.

A later build should define `E_T` precisely enough to test this rather than merely state it.

## 16. Falsification / failure conditions

FSP-TRANSLATION-1 should count against the research program if:

- meaningful local translation repeatedly requires one framework to adopt another's ontology;
- "remainder" becomes a rhetorical place to hide systematic translation failure;
- every disagreement is classified as translatable;
- shared-task logic manufactures cooperation where participants reject the task;
- agency diagnostics simply encode the researcher's political preferences;
- the formalism cannot distinguish voluntary cooperation from imposed coordination;
- repair tests reward one agent while invisibly worsening another's agency;
- the formalism's own categories cannot be contested or reopened.

## 17. Relationship to FSP-VR

If this research program survives adversarial testing, VR can embody its results.

A learner could:

- inhabit evidence-bounded reconstructions of divergent PRFs;
- inspect claim/evidence/warrant differences;
- attempt explicit translations;
- see untranslated remainder remain visible;
- enter a bounded shared-task space without worldview convergence;
- observe when coordination is coercive or agency-blocking;
- rehearse repairs;
- return to their own Doing with the formal scaffold progressively faded.

The formalism should therefore precede, not dictate, deeper VR implementation.

## 18. Ladder exit

The strongest success condition is not that a learner permanently uses terms such as `τ`, `R`, or `ClaimBundle`.

It is that after using the scaffold they become better able to notice in ordinary life:

- what another person actually claims;
- what counts as evidence for them;
- what licenses their inference;
- which assumptions differ;
- what cannot safely be translated;
- where a bounded shared task exists;
- where apparent cooperation is domination;
- what repair might restore agency;
- what would require reopening.

At that point the formal ladder can disappear from view while the capability remains.

That is the intended relationship between FSP formalism and lived Doing.
