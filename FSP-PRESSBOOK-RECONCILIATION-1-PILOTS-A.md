# FSP-PRESSBOOK-RECONCILIATION-1-PILOTS-A

## Front-Matter Alignment + Four Output-Card Return Pilots

**Status:** IMPLEMENTATION PILOT PACKAGE  
**Source contract:** FSP-PRESSBOOK-RECONCILIATION-1  
**Targets:** Ethics Ch2, Ethics Ch9, Inquiry Neptune/Vulcan, Inquiry Newton→Einstein  
**Rule:** Preserve existing workbook mechanics and 60-minute target. Replace or append only the Output Card terminal section.

---

# 1. Pilot objective

Test whether the reconciled Output Card ending can add:

- one distinctive claim ceiling;
- remainder where genuinely relevant;
- authorization boundary for Ethics;
- carry-forward for Inquiry;
- REOPEN;
- Return;

without:

- increasing workbook burden substantially;
- converting domain-native work into FSP vocabulary exercises;
- duplicating earlier workbook moves;
- weakening the Journal firewall.

---

# 2. Shared terminal pattern

## Ethics

```text
Bounded judgment
→ Claim ceiling
→ Authorization boundary
→ Remaining remainder
→ REOPEN
→ Return
```

## Inquiry

```text
Current inquiry state
→ Strongest licensed claim
→ Live alternative / remainder
→ Carry forward
→ REOPEN
→ Return
```

The labels may be lightly adapted to the chapter voice.

Do not expose internal FSP IDs.

# 3. Ethics Pilot 1 — Chapter 2 Socrates / Plato

## Domain-native center

Euthyphro-style reason-giving and Direction-of-Justification Test.

## Existing chapter firewall

> **Authority’s approval ≠ sufficient explanation of justice.**

Secondary:

> **Showing that one justification fails ≠ proving the opposite conclusion.**

## Output Card replacement

### Your bounded judgment

In 2–4 sentences, state what the authority-based justification establishes and what it fails to establish.

### Claim ceiling — What does not yet follow?

Complete:

> Because citing authority does not yet explain why the law is just, it does **not** follow that ______________________________.

Preferred student target:
the law is unjust / the opposite conclusion is true / authority is always irrelevant.

### Authorization boundary

Suppose you have shown that the stated justification is inadequate.

What, if anything, does that authorize you to do now?

Choose one and explain briefly:

- ask for further reasons;
- suspend confidence;
- challenge the justification;
- refuse the conclusion;
- something else.

Do **not** assume that failed justification automatically authorizes every form of resistance.

### Remaining remainder

What important reason, fact, or perspective might still be missing from the current argument?

One sentence only.

### REOPEN

What new reason or evidence would make you reconsider your current judgment about the law’s justification?

### Return

What should you now do as an inquirer?

Complete:

> My next responsible move is to ______________________________ because ______________________________.

## Time ceiling

5–7 minutes.

# 4. Ethics Pilot 2 — Chapter 9 Bentham / Mill

## Domain-native center

Consequences, welfare, liberty, burden distribution, uncertainty.

## Distinctive claim ceiling

> **Aggregate benefit ≠ unrestricted authorization.**

## Output Card replacement

### Your bounded judgment

State which option currently appears to produce the best-supported balance of welfare, harm reduction, and liberty.

### Claim ceiling — What does not yet follow?

Complete:

> Even if this option produces the largest expected benefit, it does **not** yet follow that ______________________________.

Potential targets:
- every burden is justified;
- the same policy should apply to everyone;
- uncertainty is irrelevant;
- liberty can be ignored;
- implementation is automatically authorized.

### Authorization boundary

Who, if anyone, has standing to authorize the proposed intervention?

Name:
1. the relevant decision-maker;
2. the person/group bearing the largest burden;
3. one condition that must be met before implementation is justified.

### Remaining remainder

What burden, liberty loss, uncertainty, or affected person is least well represented in the current welfare comparison?

### REOPEN

What consequence, distributional effect, failed prediction, or new evidence would require you to revisit the recommendation?

### Return

What should the decision-maker now do?

Complete:

> The next responsible action is ______________________________, while monitoring ______________________________.

## Time ceiling

6–8 minutes.

# 5. Inquiry Pilot 1 — Neptune / Vulcan

## Domain-native center

Anomaly localization, auxiliary assumptions, asymmetric repair success.

## Distinctive claim ceiling

> **Anomaly ≠ uniquely located refutation.**

## Output Card replacement

### Current inquiry state

In 2–3 sentences, state what problem the anomaly creates for the current theoretical system.

### Strongest licensed claim

Complete:

> The evidence currently supports the conclusion that ______________________________.

Do not claim more than the historical state licenses.

### Live alternative / remainder

Identify one live possibility that the current representation has not eliminated.

Examples:
- unseen body;
- measurement/calculation issue;
- auxiliary assumption;
- deeper theoretical problem.

Then answer:

> What does the current map still fail to carry forward?

### Carry forward

What should investigators retain from the current framework even though the anomaly remains unresolved?

This is not the same as saying the framework is finally correct.

### REOPEN

What observation, failed search, successful prediction, or theoretical development would change where you locate the problem?

### Return

Complete:

> The next responsible inquiry move is to ______________________________ because ______________________________.

## Time ceiling

6–8 minutes.

# 6. Inquiry Pilot 2 — Newton → Einstein

## Domain-native center

Domain, approximation, model succession, conceptual revision.

## Distinctive claim ceiling

> **Retained usefulness ≠ final ontology.**

## Output Card replacement

### Current inquiry state

What changed when Einsteinian relativity widened or revised the domain in which Newtonian mechanics had been understood?

### Strongest licensed claim

Complete:

> Newtonian mechanics remains justified for ______________________________, but this does not establish ______________________________.

### Live alternative / remainder

What feature of Newtonian language or conceptual structure does not transfer unchanged into relativity?

One or two sentences.

### Carry forward

List two things legitimately retained:

1. ______________________________
2. ______________________________

Then name one thing that must be revised:

3. ______________________________

### REOPEN

What kind of evidence or theoretical development would force another revision of the current relativistic framework?

### Return

Complete:

> What I should now carry forward is ______________________________, while remaining open about ______________________________.

## Time ceiling

6–8 minutes.

# 7. Journal firewall test

None of these Output Card pilots changes the Personal Ethics Journal.

After completing an Output Card, students may use the Journal to reflect on:

- discomfort;
- lived experience;
- identity;
- uncertainty;
- relationships;
- changing self-understanding.

The Journal does not require:

- repeating the Output Card;
- Tool names;
- standing tables;
- claim-ceiling labels;
- authorization-state labels.

Firewall:

> **Public bounded judgment ≠ private reflective authorship.**

# 8. Pilot acceptance tests

## Time

- each Output Card remains within 5–8 minutes;
- no workbook exceeds its 60-minute target because of this patch.

## Domain fidelity

### Ethics Ch2
- remains a reason-giving / justification-direction exercise;
- does not become a legitimacy chapter.

### Ethics Ch9
- remains consequences / welfare / liberty;
- authorization extends the reasoning without replacing utilitarian analysis.

### Neptune / Vulcan
- remains anomaly / auxiliary-assumption inquiry;
- does not become a generic FSP case.

### Newton → Einstein
- remains model succession / domain / conceptual revision;
- Return means Carry Forward, not policy authorization.

## Remainder

- remainder is concrete;
- no prompt asks students to invent mysterious missing content.

## Claim ceiling

- exactly one distinctive claim ceiling anchors each pilot.

## Return

- terminal prompt asks for the next responsible move or carry-forward;
- Return is not merely a summary.

## Journal

- unchanged.

# 9. Instructor observation checklist

During pilot use, record:

1. Did students understand the claim ceiling without extra FSP explanation?
2. Did remainder produce concrete answers?
3. Did authorization confuse Ch2 or clarify the boundary?
4. Did Ch9 students distinguish benefit from authorization?
5. Did Inquiry students treat Carry Forward differently from “the old theory was wrong”?
6. Did Return produce a genuine next move?
7. Did the new terminal section increase completion time?
8. Did students begin using framework jargon instead of domain reasoning?

If #8 rises substantially, compress labels further.

---

# 10. Pilot verdict

These four Output Cards are intentionally selected to test four different pressures:

- **Ch2:** failed justification without premature legitimacy theory;
- **Ch9:** welfare judgment crossing into authorization;
- **Neptune/Vulcan:** unresolved anomaly + remainder;
- **Newton→Einstein:** revision without erasure + Carry Forward.

If all four pass, the terminal architecture can be projected chapter-by-chapter with local adaptation.

> **Pilot pattern ≠ permission for mechanical copy-paste across the books.**
