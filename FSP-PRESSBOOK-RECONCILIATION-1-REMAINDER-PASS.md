# 1. Selective remainder hypothesis

Remainder should not appear mechanically in every chapter.

It is warranted when:

- a map is clearly lossy;
- a perspective is mediated;
- a representation compresses lived or empirical complexity;
- translation changes conceptual role;
- an anomaly resists current localization;
- a model ensemble hides unresolved interpretation;
- an institution amplifies a partial representation.

The five selected targets represent five distinct remainder types:

```text
Ethics Ch7
PERSPECTIVE REMAINDER

Ethics Ch12
CAPABILITY / DEPENDENCY REMAINDER

Neptune / Vulcan
ANOMALY / AUXILIARY-ASSUMPTION REMAINDER

Kuhn
SEMANTIC / FRAMEWORK-TRANSLATION REMAINDER

DESI
MODEL / UNCERTAINTY / INSTITUTIONAL REMAINDER
```

The progression should help students see that "what remains" is not always the same kind of thing.

---

# 2. Shared remainder discipline

Across all five targets preserve:

> **Untranslated remainder ≠ irrelevant remainder.**

> **Deferred remainder ≠ resolved remainder.**

> **Remainder ≠ unspecified mystery.**

A valid remainder prompt should identify:

1. what source object or feature is not represented well;
2. why the current representation loses or compresses it;
3. whether it could affect the judgment;
4. what further inquiry might reduce or reclassify the remainder.

Do not ask:

> "What might we be missing?"

without specifying the domain of the remainder.

# 3. Ethics Chapter 7 — Hume / Smith

## Historical / philosophical center

Sympathy, moral sentiment, perspective-taking, moral visibility.

## Remainder type

**Perspective remainder**

The chapter already protects:

> **Sympathy ≠ direct access.**

The remainder patch should make the consequence of that firewall concrete.

A sympathetic reconstruction may improve moral visibility while still leaving:

- first-person meaning;
- local context;
- hidden burden;
- fear;
- dependency;
- relationship history;
- culturally specific significance

only partially represented.

## Student-facing chapter addition

Add near the transfer from historical discussion to contemporary case:

> Sympathy can enlarge moral attention, but it does not erase the difference between imagining another person's situation and living it. A better moral map may therefore still contain **perspective remainder**: parts of another person's experience that our reconstruction does not carry well enough to settle the judgment.

Then add:

> **Sympathy ≠ direct access.**

> **Improved perspective ≠ complete representation.**

## Workbook patch

Add one short move after perspective-taking:

### Perspective remainder

After reconstructing the other person's situation, answer:

1. What part of their situation have you represented more clearly?
2. What part remains dependent on inference rather than direct access?
3. Could that remainder change your moral judgment?

One sentence each.

Do not add a second full perspective exercise.

## Output Card terminal addition

Add before REOPEN:

### Remaining perspective remainder

> What part of the affected person's experience is least well represented in your current map?

Then:

> What would help you understand it better without pretending you could eliminate all distance?

## Claim ceiling

> **Sympathy can improve moral visibility without giving complete access to another person's experience.**

## REOPEN trigger

New testimony, changed context, evidence that the current perspective map misrepresents burden, or discovery of a previously excluded standpoint.

## Return

> What should you now do differently because your perspective is better—but still incomplete?

## Time ceiling

4–6 minutes.

# 4. Ethics Chapter 12 — Care / Capability / Maathai

## Historical / philosophical center

Dependency, care, effective capability, relational support, cooperation.

## Remainder type

**Capability / dependency remainder**

A resource or formal opportunity can appear adequate while leaving unrepresented:

- care labor;
- transportation;
- safety;
- social recognition;
- time;
- health;
- relational support;
- institutional barriers;
- practical know-how.

Primary firewall:

> **Resource ≠ capability.**

Secondary:

> **Formal option ≠ effective freedom.**

## Student-facing chapter addition

Add near the capability analysis:

> Capability questions often reveal remainder that resource lists hide. Two people may receive the same formal opportunity while differing sharply in what they can actually do with it. The remainder is not mysterious: it may consist of care responsibilities, safety, transportation, social support, health, time, institutional barriers, or local knowledge that the resource map does not capture.

## Workbook patch

Add a **Capability remainder test**:

1. What formal resource, opportunity, or right is available?
2. What practical conversion conditions are required to use it?
3. Which conversion condition is missing or uncertain?
4. What capability is therefore not actually secured?

Use concrete case facts only.

## Maathai transfer

Use Maathai to show that cooperation can work without total agreement, but local capability depends on more than formal participation.

Add:

> **Participation opportunity ≠ effective participation capability.**

And:

> **Cooperation without convergence still requires enough practical capability to participate.**

## Output Card terminal addition

### Remaining capability remainder

> Which practical condition is least visible in the current institutional or resource map?

### Claim ceiling

> **Providing the resource does not yet establish that the person or community has the effective capability to use it.**

### REOPEN

What evidence of non-use, exclusion, dependency, unequal burden, or failed conversion would require repair?

### Return

> What concrete support or institutional change would most expand effective capability without replacing local agency?

## Time ceiling

5–7 minutes.

## Ch12 handoff

End with:

> The next Lab asks what happens when several such capability, standing, institutional, and repair questions have to be governed together.

This becomes the direct handoff to PFLAB.

# 5. Inquiry — Neptune / Vulcan

## Domain-native center

Anomaly localization, auxiliary assumptions, asymmetric repair success.

## Remainder type

**Anomaly / auxiliary-assumption remainder**

The anomaly is not itself the remainder.

The remainder is the part of the inferential situation not yet localized:

- incorrect observation?
- calculation error?
- auxiliary assumption?
- unseen object?
- limit of the theory?
- unmodeled effect?

Primary firewall:

> **Anomaly ≠ uniquely located refutation.**

## Student-facing case addition

Add near the comparison between Neptune success and Vulcan failure:

> An anomaly creates pressure without telling investigators exactly where the problem lies. Until that location is better constrained, part of the inquiry remains as **anomaly remainder**: unresolved dependence on auxiliary assumptions, measurement, calculation, background theory, or unmodeled causes.

## Workbook patch

Use the existing Pilot-A structure, but make the remainder object explicit:

### Anomaly remainder

Identify one unresolved dependency:

- observation;
- calculation;
- auxiliary assumption;
- unseen entity;
- theoretical structure;
- other.

Then answer:

> What evidence would reduce this remainder?

## Claim ceiling

> **Anomaly ≠ uniquely located refutation.**

## Carry forward

Students must state one feature of the inherited framework still licensed for use.

This preserves:

> **Revision ≠ erasure.**

## REOPEN

Successful search, failed search, improved measurement, revised calculation, or new theory.

## Return

> What should investigators test next, and what should they keep provisionally fixed while doing so?

## Time ceiling

5–7 minutes.

# 6. Inquiry — Kuhn / Meaning Change

## Domain-native center

Framework change, conceptual role, exemplars, problem structure, semantic instability.

## Remainder type

**Semantic / framework-translation remainder**

Primary firewall:

> **Shared vocabulary ≠ shared conceptual structure.**

The remainder arises when a term travels across frameworks but does not retain the same:

- inferential role;
- measurement relation;
- exemplar;
- problem function;
- theoretical dependency.

## Student-facing chapter addition

Add near the discussion of meaning change:

> When a term survives a framework transition, not everything about its role necessarily survives with it. Translation may preserve a word while changing its inferential connections, exemplars, measurements, or place inside the theory. The difference that cannot be carried cleanly is a form of **semantic remainder**.

Add:

> **Same word ≠ same inferential role.**

## Workbook patch

Add a **semantic carry-forward test**:

Choose one key term or concept.

1. What remains stable enough for comparison?
2. What changes in its role?
3. What cannot be translated without qualification?
4. What would be distorted if we treated the two uses as identical?

Do not ask students to solve all of incommensurability.

## Claim ceiling

> **Meaning change ≠ total incomparability.**

This is important.

The chapter should preserve both:
- semantic change;
- possibility of historically responsible comparison.

## REOPEN

New source evidence, revised historical reconstruction, better comparison of exemplars, or a stronger account of continuity/discontinuity.

## Return

> What can we responsibly compare across frameworks, and what must remain qualified?

## Time ceiling

5–7 minutes.

# 7. Inquiry — DESI / Contemporary Cosmology

## Domain-native center

Live scientific frontier, large collaboration, statistical inference, model comparison, cosmological interpretation, institutional validation.

## Remainder type

**Model / uncertainty / institutional remainder**

This is the richest Inquiry-side remainder case.

Potential remainder includes:

- model-form uncertainty;
- parameter dependence;
- alternative cosmological interpretations;
- calibration/systematic uncertainty;
- assumptions embedded in data processing;
- ensemble disagreement;
- public-communication compression;
- institutional consensus that exceeds interpretive consensus.

Primary firewalls:

> **Statistical result ≠ final cosmological interpretation.**

> **Model fit ≠ ontology.**

> **Institutional validation ≠ interpretive closure.**

## Student-facing case addition

Add near the live-frontier framing:

> Contemporary collaborative science produces highly structured public results, but those results can compress several layers at once: measurement, statistical model, parameter estimate, model comparison, interpretation, and public claim. A result may be robust at one layer while leaving substantial remainder at another.

Then ask:

> **What is settled at the measurement/statistical level, and what remains open at the interpretive level?**

## Workbook patch

Add a **frontier remainder map** with three rows only:

| Layer | What is currently supported? | What remains? |
|---|---|---|
| Measurement / data product | | |
| Model / statistical inference | | |
| Cosmological interpretation | | |

Do not expand to a full formal schema.

Then ask:

> Which remainder is most decision-relevant for what scientists should claim publicly?

## Claim ceiling

> **Interesting tension ≠ established new cosmology.**

or, if the exact case state warrants:

> **Improved fit ≠ unique interpretation.**

Instructor selects the one that matches the current source state.

## Institutional remainder

Ask:

> Does collaboration-wide publication agreement mean all deeper interpretations are settled?

Expected structure:
no; publication/validation can coexist with interpretive plurality.

## REOPEN

New data release, calibration revision, model comparison, replication, alternative analysis, or changed statistical significance.

## Return

> What should the scientific community claim now, and what should remain explicitly open?

## Time ceiling

7–9 minutes.

# 10. Instructor guidance

Remainder should be marked as **decision-relevant incompleteness**, not mere ignorance.

Good remainder:
- specific;
- source-linked;
- potentially consequential;
- reducible/reclassifiable by further inquiry.

Bad remainder:
- vague;
- metaphysical placeholder;
- "anything we don't know";
- excuse to avoid judgment.

Instructor firewall:

> **Remainder awareness ≠ refusal to conclude.**

Students still make bounded judgments.

# 15. Regression tests

The selective remainder pass fails if:

## Genericity

FAIL if the same remainder prompt is copied into all five targets.

## Mystery inflation

FAIL if students can answer remainder with vague "unknown unknowns."

## Closure avoidance

FAIL if remainder becomes a reason not to make a bounded judgment.

## Historical flattening

FAIL if:
- Hume/Smith become "remainder theory";
- Kuhn becomes only translation theory;
- Neptune/Vulcan become generic underdetermination;
- DESI becomes a governance lesson rather than live cosmology inquiry.

## Capability distortion

FAIL if Ch12 treats capability remainder as merely resource shortage.

## Student burden

FAIL if ordinary workbook time exceeds target.

## JTS overreach

FAIL if full JTS grammar is required before culmination.

# 18. Final implementation verdict

The selective remainder pass should teach students that different inquiries leave different things behind.

The progression is:

```text
Ch7
another person's experience exceeds my sympathetic map

Ch12
effective capability exceeds the resource map

Neptune / Vulcan
the location of anomaly pressure exceeds the current diagnosis

Kuhn
conceptual role exceeds literal word-to-word translation

DESI
scientific interpretation exceeds any one statistical or institutional summary
```

This gives remainder a precise curricular function without making it a universal slogan.
