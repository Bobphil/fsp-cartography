# FSP-REVISION-8

## Institutional Sedimentation Networks, Multi-Concept Coevolution, Policy/Practice Feedback, Historical Lock-In, Repair Windows, and Cross-Generational Capability Audit

**Status:** Institutional-ecology and cross-generational capability revision layer  
**Boundary:** REVISION-1 through REVISION-7 remain intact; TRANSLATION-12 human evidence remains PENDING.

## 1. Purpose

REVISION-7 tracked one concept across historical versions and sedimentation events.

REVISION-8 scales that historical model outward.

The central question is:

> What happens when multiple concepts, policies, practices, institutions, resources, and capabilities co-evolve and reinforce one another across generations?

The build adds:

- institutional sedimentation networks;
- multi-concept coevolution;
- policy/practice feedback;
- historical lock-in auditing;
- repair windows;
- cross-generational capability effects.

## 2. Institutional sedimentation networks

A network may contain nodes representing:

- CONCEPT;
- POLICY;
- PRACTICE;
- INSTITUTION;
- CAPABILITY;
- RESOURCE.

Edges may represent:

- ENCODES;
- REINFORCES;
- DEPENDS_ON;
- CONSTRAINS;
- ENABLES;
- TRANSLATES;
- FEEDS_BACK_INTO.

This allows concept history to become an ecology rather than a linear genealogy.

## 3. Multi-concept coevolution

Concepts may develop together through shared institutions.

A coevolution record tracks:

- participating concepts;
- shared institutions;
- mutual reinforcement;
- tensions;
- joint drift.

Shared institutional uptake does not imply conceptual identity or harmony.

## 4. Policy/practice feedback

Policies shape practices.

Practices also feed back into policies through:

- implementation experience;
- administrative routines;
- accumulated cases;
- training;
- appeals;
- failures.

The model therefore permits loops rather than one-way institutional causation.

## 5. Historical lock-in

Lock-in is represented through:

- switching costs;
- reinforcing loops;
- suppressed alternatives.

Lock-in may be LOW, MEDIUM, or HIGH.

The key firewall is:

```
historical lock-in
!=
current justification
```

Lock-in can explain why a system persists.

It does not justify continuation.

## 6. Lock-in is not automatically bad

Path dependence can preserve:

- expertise;
- coordination capacity;
- useful standards;
- stable expectations;
- institutional memory.

Therefore lock-in itself is not treated as pathology.

The audit asks what is being preserved and at what cost.

## 7. Repair windows

A repair window contains:

- a trigger;
- opening conditions;
- closing conditions;
- available repairs;
- affected network nodes.

The window opens only when the declared opening conditions are present and no closing condition blocks action.

Repair remains:

`repairMandatory = false`

The system exposes opportunity; it does not command institutional change.

## 8. Repair-window examples

A window may open when:

- scheduled review is active;
- new evidence is available;
- affected participants have standing.

It may close or become unusable when:

- policy is frozen;
- retaliation risk becomes severe;
- contestability is removed.

## 9. Cross-generational capability

An inherited institutional ecology may affect different generations differently.

Capability effects are:

- EXPANDS;
- CONSTRAINS;
- MIXED;
- UNKNOWN.

A policy may expand effective voice for its first beneficiaries and later constrain new participants if its categories harden or drift.

## 10. Historical benefit does not justify current burden

The cross-generational firewall is:

```
past capability expansion
!=
justification of present capability constraint
```

The audit therefore fixes:

`historicalBenefitJustifiesCurrentBurden = false`.

## 11. Test ecology

The fixture contains two co-evolving concepts:

- recurring interpretive exclusion;
- interpretive access failure.

They sediment into:

- review policy;
- intake practice;
- review institution;
- training infrastructure.

Policy shapes practice.

Practice feeds back into policy.

Training stabilizes practice.

The resulting ecology can both expand and later narrow effective voice.

## 12. Reinforcing loops

The ecology contains reinforcing loops such as:

```
policy
-> practice
-> training
-> practice
-> policy
```

Such loops can stabilize useful coordination.

They can also make alternatives more costly to introduce.

## 13. Capability trajectory

The fixture represents:

Generation 1:
- concept formation expands effective voice.

Generation 2:
- formal policy expands effective voice.

Generation 3:
- standardized practice yields mixed effects.

Generation 4:
- locked-in categories constrain effective voice.

This is not a deterministic law.

It is a structured cross-generational test case.

## 14. Repair question

The capability audit ends not with:

> abolish the inherited institution.

It asks:

> What present repair would preserve warranted inherited benefits while reducing avoidable constraints on current and future capability?

This is the historical version of capability-preserving repair.

## 15. Relationship to formation != justification

REVISION-8 generalizes the seam again.

Formation explains:

- why concepts coevolve;
- why institutions stabilize;
- why routines reproduce themselves;
- why alternatives become costly.

Justification remains separately required.

## 16. Human-validation boundary

Nothing in REVISION-8 changes TRANSLATION-12.

Human-validation status remains:

`PENDING`.

## 17. Research ceiling

The strongest current claim is:

> FSP can represent institutional sedimentation as a network of co-evolving concepts, policies, practices, resources, and capabilities; detect reinforcing feedback and historical lock-in; identify bounded repair windows; and compare cross-generational capability effects without treating persistence or past benefit as sufficient justification for present constraints.

It does not establish a complete theory of institutional evolution, causal path dependence, or intergenerational justice.
