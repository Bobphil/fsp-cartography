# 1. Purpose

This gate asks a stricter question than the prior review.

The issue is no longer whether FSP-CT-CANONICAL-1.1 is broadly compatible with nearby empirical and philosophical work. The issue is whether the canonical narrative can now be **frozen as a reusable foundation** without forcing any of four major comparator positions into an alien theory, and without causing semantic drift when projected into the two intended Pressbooks.

The six required tests are:

1. representationalist cognitive science;
2. phenomenology;
3. pragmatism;
4. enactivism / 4E cognition;
5. History of Ethics Pressbook projection;
6. History of Inquiry Pressbook projection.

The freeze rule is strict:

> **A comparator passes only if it can use the FSP governance architecture without being redescribed as FSP, and a projection passes only if it can inherit the canonical distinctions without redefining them or importing domain-inappropriate commitments.**

# 2. Comparator Gate A — Representationalist Cognitive Science

## Test

Can a cognitive scientist who treats cognition as involving internal representations and computational or information-processing operations use FSP-CT-CANONICAL-1.1 without contradiction or distortion?

## Result: PASS

Yes.

The 1.1 revision explicitly leaves open whether cognition relies on internal representations. It does not prohibit representational explanation. A representationalist may treat some FSP maps as corresponding to mental representations, structured contents, models, concepts, images, or other representational states.

The important restriction is only this:

> **FSP map ≠ automatic identity with a particular mental representation theory.**

This preserves the representationalist's explanatory freedom while preventing FSP from using "map" as if one cognitive architecture had already been established.

The architecture remains fully usable:

```text
internal representation or model
→ inspectable map for governance
→ inferential audit
→ bounded claim
→ standing / authorization where relevant
→ revision
```

FSP therefore constrains **what may be inferred or authorized from a representation** without deciding the empirical mechanism by which the representation was produced.

## Comparator ceiling

**Representational compatibility ≠ endorsement of computationalism, language-of-thought theory, predictive processing, or any specific representational format.**

## Freeze judgment

**PASS — no repair required.**

# 3. Comparator Gate B — Phenomenology

## Test

Can a phenomenologist recognize embodied, prereflective, perspectival engagement without having lived experience redescribed as an explicit representation or map?

## Result: PASS

Yes.

The decisive 1.1 sentence is:

> Much practical engagement is not first experienced as an explicit representation.

The narrative now gives prereflective lived inquiry temporal and conceptual priority over reflective thematization. Reflection is not said to create inquiry; it makes some ongoing inquiry available for inspection.

This preserves a phenomenological distinction between:

```text
lived / prereflective engagement
≠
reflective articulation of that engagement
```

FSP introduces "map" only when some selective articulation becomes available for recall, comparison, reasoning, communication, modeling, coordination, or deliberate action.

A phenomenologist may therefore reject representationalist accounts of ordinary skilled coping while still using FSP to govern what happens when experience is articulated, compared, reasoned from, institutionalized, or acted upon.

FSP also retains the opacity of self and others:

- Map of Person ≠ Person.
- Reflective articulation ≠ exhaustive self-transparency.
- Assembly history of another person ≠ ownership of that person's self-understanding.

## Comparator ceiling

**Phenomenological convergence ≠ phenomenology reduced to FSP governance.**

## Freeze judgment

**PASS — no repair required.**

Can a pragmatist recognize habit, situated problem-solving, inquiry, consequence, and revision without FSP claiming these as uniquely FSP discoveries or forcing them into a rigid stage sequence?

## Result: PASS

The 1.1 revision explicitly makes the inquiry arc recursive and analytic rather than a natural sequence. It also treats acting, perceiving, consequence, and revision as mutually conditioning.

This accommodates the pragmatist emphasis on inquiry as activity:

```text

↔ habit / action
↔ consequence
↔ reflection
↔ reconstruction
```

Assembly history also now captures reciprocal formation rather than passive socialization, which is compatible with pragmatist accounts of habits as organism-environment transactions.

FSP's delta remains appropriately modest and explicit. It does not claim novelty for:

- inquiry as process;
- fallibilism;
- habit;
- action;
- consequence;
- revision.

Its distinctive contribution remains the explicit governance layer around:

- map/person separation;
- provenance;
- claim ceilings;
- Tool/failure routing;
- standing and authorization;
- institutional amplification;
- REOPEN;
- Authorial Return.

**Pragmatist convergence ≠ FSP ownership of inquiry-as-action or fallibilism.**

# 5. Comparator Gate D — Enactivism / 4E Cognition

## Test

Can an enactivist use the architecture without accepting the thesis that cognition fundamentally consists of internal representations?

## Result: PASS

Yes.

This is the sharpest comparator test, and 1.1 passes it.

The narrative now states:

> **Map language ≠ theory of cognitive architecture.**

and restricts map language to selective articulations that become inspectable enough for governance.

An enactivist may therefore describe cognition in terms of sensorimotor coupling, embodied skill, affordances, organism-environment interaction, or sense-making. FSP does not require those processes themselves to be reclassified as internal maps.

When an enactive process generates an explicit description, institutional category, model, remembered articulation, communicable judgment, or decision object, FSP can govern that transition without deciding what cognition fundamentally is.

This yields the compatible relation:

```text
embodied / enactive engagement
→ [when articulation becomes inspectable]
→ FSP map governance
→ claim ceiling / standing / revision
```

FSP is therefore neutral between strong representationalism and strong anti-representationalism at the level needed for its governance project.

## Comparator ceiling

**Enactive compatibility ≠ FSP endorsement of radical enactivism, extended cognition, autopoiesis, or any single 4E thesis.**

## Freeze judgment

**PASS — no repair required.**

# 6. Cross-Comparator Contradiction Test

The strongest freeze test asks whether the same canonical wording can remain usable across positions that disagree with one another.

## Representationalist vs. Enactivist

**PASS.**

A representationalist may identify some maps with internal representational structures.

An enactivist may deny that ordinary cognition requires such structures.

FSP requires neither position. It governs selective articulations when they become inspectable enough to support inference, communication, coordination, judgment, or institutionalization.

## Phenomenology vs. Representationalist Cognitive Science

**PASS.**

Phenomenology may insist that prereflective lived experience is not first an explicit representation.

Representationalist cognitive science may nevertheless posit subpersonal representational mechanisms.

FSP does not settle that disagreement because its map category is functional-governance language, not a theory of phenomenal or subpersonal cognitive constitution.

## Pragmatism vs. Stage Models of Cognition

**PASS.**

The canonical arc is explicitly a diagnostic decomposition, not a fixed natural sequence.

## Cross-comparator conclusion

The architecture survives disagreement among the comparators because its canonical claim is now at the correct level:

> **FSP governs vulnerable transitions once they become sufficiently inspectable; it does not legislate the underlying ontology or mechanism of cognition.**

No further conceptual repair is required.

# 11. Freeze Decision

## FSP-CT-CANONICAL-1.1 — FROZEN

The final comparator and projection regression yields no conceptual defect requiring a 1.2 corrective pass.

FSP-CT-CANONICAL-1.1 is therefore frozen as the canonical Encyclopedia foundation for governed critical thinking.

The freeze applies to the following architecture:

```text
Lived Doing
↔ situated inquiry
↔ selective articulation / map
↔ assembly history
↔ reflective inquiry
↔ traditional critical-thinking operations
↔ governed inquiry
↔ Tool / failure routing
↔ bounded judgment
↔ standing / authorization
↔ institutional amplification
↔ consequences
↔ repair / REOPEN
↔ Authorial Return
↔ Lived Doing
```

The order above is a conceptual navigation map, not a mandatory natural sequence.

### Frozen canonical interpretation of "map"

> **A map is an FSP governance term for a selective articulation of a situation that becomes available for recall, comparison, reasoning, communication, modeling, coordination, memory, or deliberate action. "Map" does not assert that all cognition consists in internal mental representations.**

### Frozen canonical interpretation of "assembly history"

> **Assembly history names the reciprocal, temporally extended formation through which bodies, activity, relationships, environments, practices, institutions, inherited categories, resistance, and self-directed revision shape the capacities and maps now available to an inquirer. Formation does not itself establish justification.**

### Frozen canonical interpretation of "reflective inquiry"

> **Inquiry precedes reflection. Reflective inquiry occurs when some aspect of ongoing lived inquiry becomes thematically available for inspection, comparison, reason-giving, or deliberate revision.**

### Frozen FSP delta

FSP does not claim novelty for embodiment, situatedness, active learning, habit, fallibilism, inquiry-as-process, or organism-environment coupling.

Its canonical contribution in this section is:

> **to make vulnerable transitions among lived inquiry, maps, reasons, judgments, standing, authorization, institutions, consequences, and revision inspectable and governable through Tool functions, failure diagnostics, provenance, claim ceilings, repair, REOPEN, and Authorial Return.**

# 12. Post-Freeze Change Gate

After this freeze, changes to the canonical foundation require one of the following:

1. a demonstrated contradiction among frozen constitutional commitments;
2. a comparator showing that the supposedly neutral architecture actually entails a disputed cognitive, phenomenological, ethical, or scientific ontology;
3. a Pressbook projection that cannot be generated without redefining a canonical Tool or firewall;
4. a real case exposing a systematic failure not expressible through the current Tool/failure/claim-ceiling grammar;
5. empirical evidence invalidating an empirical claim that the canonical narrative actually relies upon rather than merely cites as compatible background.

Mere preference for alternate terminology, additional theoretical detail, or domain-specific nuance does not reopen the canonical foundation. Such material belongs in projections, comparator notes, or deeper reference layers unless it crosses the change gate.

# 13. Freeze Ceiling

This freeze certifies **architectural adequacy for canonical use and projection**.

It does not certify that FSP is the uniquely correct theory of critical thinking, cognition, ethics, inquiry, or human development.

It does not establish that representationalism, phenomenology, pragmatism, or enactivism entail FSP.

It does not establish that the History of Ethics or History of Inquiry Pressbooks must expose the entire canonical architecture to students.

The frozen claim is narrower:

> **FSP-CT-CANONICAL-1.1 now provides a representation-neutral, situated, reciprocal, fallibilist, and domain-portable foundation from which governed critical-thinking projections can be generated without requiring semantic redefinition of the canonical core.**
