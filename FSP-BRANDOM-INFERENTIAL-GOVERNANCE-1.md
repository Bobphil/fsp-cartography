# FSP-BRANDOM-INFERENTIAL-GOVERNANCE-1

## Commitment, Entitlement, Material Inference, Partial Maps, Boundary Portability, Claim Ceilings, Authorization, Remainder, and Return

**Status:** REPLACEMENT-GRADE COMPARATOR REVISION — VERSION 1  
**Supersedes in part:** FSP-BRANDOM-RECIPROCITY-1  
**Parents:** FSP-CONSTITUTIONAL-COMPRESSION-1; FSP-CONSTITUTIONAL-REGRESSION-1; FSP-RECOMPOSITION-GOVERNANCE-1; FSP-RECOMPOSITION-ROUTING-REGRESSION-1  
**Comparator:** Robert Brandom — inferentialism, normative pragmatics, commitment, entitlement, material inference, incompatibility, scorekeeping, practical reasoning, recognition  
**Primary purpose:** Recast Brandom as FSP’s strongest comparator for discursive inferential transitions while distinguishing inferential entitlement from broader FSP governance of representation, translation, authorization, consequence, and Return.  
**Primary claim ceiling:** This build does not claim that FSP refutes Brandom, that Brandom’s inferentialism is incomplete on its own terms, or that FSP supplies a superior semantics. It specifies how Brandomian inferential governance can be embedded within FSP’s wider architecture of partial maps and governed transitions.

---

# 1. Why This Revision Is Needed

Earlier FSP work treated Brandom through a broader contrast:

```text
phenomenal node
vs.
discursive scorekeeper
```

That comparison was directionally useful but now appears too layer-heavy.

The constitutional compression has clarified FSP’s smaller core:

```text
PARTIAL MAP
→ RELATION
→ TRANSITION
→ LICENSE / WARRANT
→ CLAIM CEILING
→ AUTHORIZATION
→ CONSEQUENCE
→ REMAINDER / REOPEN
→ RETURN
```

This lets Brandom be placed more precisely.

Brandom should not be treated as an alternative “full stack.”

He is best treated as the strongest comparator for one especially important family of transitions:

> **discursively articulated inferential transitions among commitments and entitlements.**

---

# 2. Brandom’s Core Contribution

Brandom’s inferentialism shifts semantic emphasis from representation to inference.

Conceptual content is articulated through:

- what commitments follow from a claim;
- what entitles one to a claim;
- what is incompatible with a claim;
- how those normative statuses are attributed and acknowledged in social practice.

The relevant Brandomian structure can be compressed:

```text
ASSERTION
→ COMMITMENT
→ ENTITLEMENT
→ MATERIAL INFERENTIAL CONSEQUENCES
→ INCOMPATIBILITIES
→ SCOREKEEPING
```

This is a rich account of discursive normativity.

FSP should preserve it.

---

# 3. Inferential Transition as a Species of FSP Transition

The key architectural decision is:

> **Inferential transition is a species of FSP Transition.**

FSP Transition is broader.

Examples include:

```text
perception → articulation
measurement → evidence claim
evidence → inference
population → patient
representation A → representation B
local → institutional
judgment → authorization
action → consequence
```

Only some of these are best modeled as material inferential relations among commitments.

Therefore:

```text
FSP TRANSITION
├── discursive inferential transition
├── evidential transition
├── measurement transition
├── translation transition
├── cross-scale transition
├── authorization transition
├── institutional-amplification transition
└── action/consequence transition
```

Brandom is central in the first branch.

He need not own the others.

---

# 4. Commitment

A Brandomian commitment is not merely a proposition stored in a mind.

It is a normative status undertaken or attributed within discursive practice.

For FSP, commitment can be represented as:

```text
CommitmentRecord = {
  content,
  acknowledgedBy,
  attributedBy,
  provenance,
  context,
  inferentialConsequences,
  incompatibilities,
  entitlementStatus
}
```

This record is a decompression.

It is not a new constitutional primitive.

---

# 5. Entitlement

Entitlement is especially important for FSP.

It answers:

> **Is this participant entitled to this commitment?**

This is narrower than FSP’s generic word “warrant.”

The recent constitutional work suggests a useful refinement.

```text
FSP LICENSE / WARRANT
├── inferential entitlement
├── evidential support
├── measurement calibration
├── causal warrant
├── translation warrant
├── interoperability warrant
├── institutional authority
├── consent / authorization
└── domain-native licensing conditions
```

Thus:

> **Entitlement = one important subtype of FSP warrant.**

FSP should stop using “warrant” as though every transition were licensed in the same way.

---

# 6. Material Inference

Brandom’s account of material inference is particularly useful because it shows that inferential relations are not exhausted by formal logic.

Examples:

```text
pure copper
→ conducts electricity

barometer falling
→ storm likely

square
⊥
circular
```

The inference depends on contentful practices.

FSP strongly benefits from this.

It means that transition governance cannot be reduced to formal validity.

Domain-native material inferential practices matter.

---

# 7. Three Inferential Relations

Brandom distinguishes importantly among different normative inferential relations.

FSP can preserve this distinction.

## 7.1 Committive consequence

```text
committed to p
→ committed to q
```

## 7.2 Permissive / entitlement-preserving consequence

```text
entitled to p
→ prima facie entitled to q
```

## 7.3 Material incompatibility

```text
committed to p
→ not entitled to incompatible q
```

These should not be collapsed into one generic “supports.”

This is a concrete improvement FSP can inherit from Brandom.

---

# 8. Scorekeeping

Brandomian discursive practice includes deontic scorekeeping.

Participants track:

- who is committed to what;
- who is entitled to what;
- what follows;
- what conflicts;
- whose testimony or authority may be relied upon.

FSP should interpret scorekeeping as:

> **a provenance-sensitive partial map of another participant’s normative statuses.**

This creates a direct crosswalk:

```text
Brandom scorekeeping
↔
FSP MY_MAP_OF_OTHER
```

But with an important Firewall:

> **Attributed commitment ≠ acknowledged commitment.**

---

# 9. Said vs Inferred

FSP’s existing People architecture adds a crucial provenance distinction.

```text
B SAID p
≠
A ATTRIBUTES p TO B
≠
A INFERS q FROM p
≠
B ACKNOWLEDGES q
```

This should become a formal Brandom–FSP interface.

A scorekeeper may be correct to attribute q.

But the attribution still needs provenance.

Thus:

> **Normative attribution ≠ source-independent fact about the person.**

---

# 10. Partial Maps

Brandom’s scorekeeping is perspectival.

Different participants may maintain different deontic scores.

FSP can therefore interpret each score as a partial map.

```text
A's score of B
≠
B's score of B
≠
C's score of B
```

None is automatically final.

This fits the constitutional rule:

> **Map ≠ World ≠ Person.**

---

# 11. The Space of Reasons as a Local Ecology

The earlier comparator treated Brandom as occupying “Level 2” of a three-layer system.

That framing should now be replaced.

The cleaner architecture is:

```text
LIVED DOING
↓
partial articulation
↓
discursive claim
↓
SPACE OF REASONS
commitment ↔ entitlement ↔ incompatibility
↓
boundary / recomposition governance
↓
bounded judgment
↓
authorization
↓
action
↓
consequence
↓
RETURN
```

Brandom’s space of reasons is therefore a central local ecology within the wider FSP architecture.

---

# 12. Inferential Entitlement vs FSP Claim Ceiling

These concepts are related but not identical.

Brandom asks:

> Am I entitled to p?

FSP additionally asks:

> **How far may the entitlement to p travel?**

A claim ceiling limits what may be inferred, recommended, authorized, or generalized from an entitled commitment.

Thus:

```text
ENTITLEMENT
→ local discursive status

CLAIM CEILING
→ boundary on further licensed transition
```

This is a central distinction.

---

# 13. Claim Ceiling as Portability Control

A claim ceiling can be interpreted as controlling the portability of an entitlement.

Example:

```text
entitled:
X is associated with Y

claim ceiling:
≠ X causes Y
≠ changing X will change Y
≠ intervention on X is authorized
```

Therefore:

> **Entitlement to p ≠ unrestricted entitlement to every recomposed use of p.**

This is where recent FSP work adds something distinctive.

---

# 14. Boundary Portability

The central new Brandom–FSP concept is **boundary portability**.

Question:

> **Does an entitlement survive when the commitment crosses into a changed target, scale, representation, role, institution, or action context?**

The structure is:

```text
p is entitled in context A
↓
boundary crossing
↓
p is used in context B
```

FSP asks:

- what changed?
- what warrant survives?
- what new burden appears?
- what no longer follows automatically?

---

# 15. Recomposition and Entitlement

Recomposition governance now gives Brandomian entitlement a wider test.

A commitment may be entitled locally and lose entitlement portability when:

- the target changes;
- scale changes;
- evidential conditions change;
- authority changes;
- affected parties change;
- action consequences change.

Therefore:

> **Local entitlement ≠ inherited entitlement after recomposition.**

This is the key FSP addition.

---

# 16. Inferential Portability Test

For any commitment p:

## Step 1

Was p locally entitled?

## Step 2

What inferential consequence q is being claimed?

## Step 3

Did the target or context change?

## Step 4

Which warrant conditions survive?

## Step 5

What new burden appears?

## Step 6

What claim ceiling now applies?

This embeds Brandomian entitlement inside FSP recomposition governance.

---

# 17. Discursive Entitlement ≠ Practical Authorization

This distinction must now be explicit.

A participant may be entitled to assert:

```text
p
```

without being authorized to:

- impose a policy;
- intervene medically;
- classify another person;
- constrain another agent;
- allocate burdens;
- alter institutional rules.

Thus:

> **Discursive entitlement ≠ practical authorization.**

This is not a criticism of Brandom’s practical philosophy.

It is an FSP governance distinction between different normative statuses.

---

# 18. Judgment and Authorization

FSP constitutionalizes:

```text
SUPPORTED JUDGMENT
≠
AUTHORIZED ACTION
```

This makes Brandomian entitlement one upstream status.

A typical path is:

```text
commitment
→ entitlement
→ bounded judgment
→ authorization check
→ action
```

Each transition may require a different license.

---

# 19. Standing Decompression

The older Brandom comparator used “standing” too broadly.

The recent FSP architecture supports a typed decomposition.

```text
discursive standing
epistemic standing
institutional standing
effective standing
moral standing
authorization standing
```

These may diverge.

Example:

A person may have:

- moral standing;
- little institutional standing;
- strong epistemic standing;
- no authority to impose a decision.

This is especially important in ethics and institutions.

---

# 20. Recognition

Brandomian recognition concerns socially attributed normative authority and responsibility.

FSP should preserve this.

But:

> **Socially attributed normative status ≠ exhaustive moral standing.**

This prevents morally exclusionary recognition structures from determining the full normative field.

---

# 21. Recognition and Normative Profiles

FSP’s Normative Profile architecture now clarifies the relation.

```text
SOCIAL PRACTICE
→ recognizes standing/status

NORMATIVE PROFILE
→ evaluates whether that recognition is morally adequate
```

These layers must remain distinct.

A community may fail to recognize someone who retains moral standing under the active normative profile.

---

# 22. Recognition Without Endorsement

FSP must be able to represent:

- racist recognition structures;
- caste systems;
- exclusionary religious systems;
- colonial legal orders;
- discriminatory institutions;

without endorsing them.

Therefore:

> **Representability ≠ endorsement.**

Brandom helps model the internal normative practice.

FSP adds explicit normative-profile evaluation.

---

# 23. Authority

Brandom’s work makes authority and responsibility central to discursive practice.

FSP benefits greatly from that.

But it must type authority.

```text
epistemic authority
discursive authority
institutional authority
legal authority
practical power
moral legitimacy
```

These should not be collapsed.

---

# 24. Practical Reasoning

Brandom’s work on action and practical reasoning is important because it prevents a crude interpretation of inferentialism as merely theoretical.

Practical commitments can lead toward action.

FSP should therefore not say:

> Brandom stops at assertion.

That would be false.

The better distinction is:

> Brandom gives a rich normative account of practical reasoning; FSP adds a more explicit architecture for downstream authorization, institutional amplification, consequence, and Return.

---

# 25. Exit Transitions and FSP

Brandom explicitly analyzes action as a kind of discursive exit transition.

FSP can adopt this terminology selectively.

But FSP asks additional questions at the exit boundary:

- who may act?
- who bears the consequence?
- who must consent?
- what capability changes?
- what institutional effects persist?
- what reopens the action?

Thus:

> **Discursive exit transition ≠ complete action governance.**

---

# 26. Entry Transitions and Perception

Brandom also treats perception as connected to discursive entry transitions.

FSP should preserve the insight that perception can enter the space of reasons through norm-governed reporting.

But FSP begins earlier.

It allows:

```text
lived encounter
→ selective articulation
→ reportable claim
```

This prevents the architecture from implying that all meaningful encounter is already conceptual or discursive.

---

# 27. Lived Doing

This replaces the older “phenomenal node” framing.

FSP should now begin with:

> **lived Doing by situated agents whose access is partial.**

This is deliberately thinner than a universal phenomenology.

It can include:

- embodied action;
- attention;
- affect;
- tacit skill;
- perception;
- language;
- social practice.

Discursive articulation is one important later inspectable form.

---

# 28. Why the Phenomenal-Node Framing Should Be Retired

The phrase “phenomenal node” risks implying:

- one settled phenomenological ontology;
- one theory of consciousness;
- a sharp architecture FSP does not need.

The constitutional compression has shown that FSP only needs:

- situated Doing;
- partial access;
- partial maps;
- governed transitions.

### Decision

> **RETIRE “Phenomenal Node vs Discursive Scorekeeper” as the primary Brandom framing.**

Preserve it only as historical provenance.

---

# 29. Remainder

Remainder is one of FSP’s clearest additions.

Not all relevant content is successfully carried into discursive articulation.

Remainder may include:

- tacit skill;
- unarticulated pain;
- affect;
- sensory difference;
- cultural significance;
- uncertainty;
- inaccessible first-person structure;
- unrepresented affected parties.

Therefore:

> **Inferential articulation ≠ total human significance.**

This is not a claim of ineffability.

It is a governance rule against treating current articulation as exhaustive.

---

# 30. Remainder in Scorekeeping

A scorekeeper’s map of another person is always potentially incomplete.

Thus:

```text
AttributedCommitments(B)
≠
TotalSignificance(B)
```

This is especially important in:

- psychiatry;
- education;
- law;
- institutional classification;
- interpersonal conflict.

---

# 31. Capability

A participant may be normatively entitled but practically unable.

Examples:

- entitled to speak but unable to access the forum;
- legally free but materially constrained;
- recognized as competent but deprived of effective opportunity.

Thus:

> **Normative entitlement ≠ effective capability.**

FSP’s capability layer remains distinct.

---

# 32. Institutional Sedimentation

Discursive practices can sediment into:

- rules;
- offices;
- classifications;
- curricula;
- software;
- metrics;
- procedures;
- infrastructures.

This creates a major downstream transition:

```text
normative practice
→ institutional rule
→ durable environment
```

Brandom helps explain the normative statuses inside the practice.

FSP emphasizes what happens when the practice becomes infrastructure.

---

# 33. Sedimentation and Future Agents

A sedimented norm can outlive the agents who instituted it.

Future agents encounter it as part of their assembly environment.

Therefore:

> **Past recognition structures can become present constraints.**

This is where Assembly History and Institutional Amplification enter.

---

# 34. Institutional Amplification

A discursive commitment may become:

```text
claim
→ repeated norm
→ workflow
→ metric
→ policy
→ infrastructure
```

Once amplified, the consequences may far exceed the original discursive context.

FSP therefore asks:

> **What happens when an entitled claim becomes institutionally executable?**

This is one of the strongest FSP extensions.

---

# 35. Recollection and Historical Reconstruction

Brandom’s Hegelian recollection is a major comparator for diachronic rationality.

FSP should preserve the insight that later agents reconstruct earlier commitments as part of an intelligible trajectory.

But FSP adds provenance constraints.

```text
past Doing
→ surviving trace
→ present reconstruction
→ present rational interpretation
```

Firewall:

> **Recollective intelligibility ≠ recovered historical actuality.**

---

# 36. Historical Trace

FSP requires:

- sources;
- provenance;
- competing reconstruction;
- remainder;
- claim ceiling.

This prevents rational reconstruction from laundering uncertainty.

---

# 37. Commitment Portability Across Time

The recomposition problem also applies diachronically.

A commitment may be:

- valid in one institutional setting;
- reinterpreted later;
- sedimented into law;
- detached from its original justification.

Thus:

> **Historical persistence ≠ preserved entitlement.**

This is a useful Brandom–FSP bridge.

---

# 38. Material Inference and Domain Portability

A material inference may be valid within one practice.

Example:

```text
pure copper
→ conducts electricity
```

But transferring an inferential pattern into another domain requires renewed warrant.

Therefore:

> **Material inference portability ≠ automatic cross-domain portability.**

FSP recomposition governance becomes especially important when inferential practices travel between domains.

---

# 39. Translation

When two groups use different vocabularies, Brandomian inferential roles may help identify content relations.

FSP adds TranslationAnchor and OperationalSufficiency.

The question becomes:

> Which inferential relations need to be preserved for the shared task?

Full semantic convergence may not be required.

---

# 40. Inferential Role and Translation Anchor

A TranslationAnchor may preserve:

- consequence relation;
- incompatibility relation;
- practical role;
- evidential relation.

This creates a productive connection:

```text
Brandom inferential role
↔
FSP relation-preserving translation
```

But:

> **Shared inferential role ≠ shared ontology.**

---

# 41. Brandom and Firewalls

Brandom helps FSP refine the meaning of an inferential Firewall.

A Firewall can be decomposed into three questions.

## Commitment question

Does p commit the participant to q?

## Entitlement question

Does entitlement to p support entitlement to q?

## FSP portability question

Does q retain warrant when carried into this new target, scale, role, or action context?

This three-part structure is a major gain.

---

# 42. Brandom and Claim Ceilings

Claim ceilings are not Brandomian entitlements under another name.

They regulate downstream portability.

```text
entitled to p
≠
entitled to every stronger q
≠
authorized to act on q
```

This distinction should remain explicit.

# 46. Brandom and Public-Facing FSP

Brandom’s technical vocabulary should not dominate the public interface.

Public compression:

> **What are you claiming?**

> **What gives you the right to claim it?**

> **What else follows?**

> **What does not follow?**

> **Does that license still hold when you carry the claim into a new decision or context?**

This preserves the philosophical gain without requiring “deontic scorekeeping” terminology.

# 50. Strongest Affinity

The strongest affinity is:

> **Both Brandom and FSP treat normative movement as something that must be licensed rather than merely associated.**

Brandom develops this most deeply inside discursive inferential practice.

FSP generalizes the governance problem beyond that practice.

---

# 51. Strongest Difference

The strongest difference is:

> **Brandom asks what commitments and entitlements are instituted and articulated within the space of reasons; FSP additionally governs how the products of that space cross representational, evidential, scalar, institutional, normative, and practical boundaries.**

This is the central comparator result.

---

# 52. The Portability Thesis

The most important new thesis from this build is:

> **Inferential entitlement is local to a norm-governed practice unless and until its portability across a boundary is re-earned.**

This does not imply relativism.

It means the warrant conditions must survive or be supplemented.

7. the retirement of “phenomenal node” removes a distinction still needed elsewhere.
