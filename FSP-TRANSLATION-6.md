# FSP-TRANSLATION-6

## Third-Domain Replication, External Comparator Formalism, Primitive-Minimality Challenge, and Publication Architecture

**Status:** Comparative replication and publication-architecture layer  
**Boundary:** No Cartography-kernel change.

## 1. Purpose

TRANSLATION-6 moves beyond internal validation.

It adds:

- a third structurally different domain;
- an external comparator formalism;
- a primitive-minimality challenge;
- a publication architecture with an explicit claim ladder.

The central question is no longer merely whether FSP works internally.

It is:

> What does FSP represent that a simpler framework does not, and how strong a scholarly claim is justified by the current evidence?

## 2. Third domain: historical interpretation

The third comparator domain is a bounded public interpretation of Galileo's 1633 trial.

Its native topology includes:

- incomplete archives;
- source provenance;
- retrospective interpretation;
- scientific controversy;
- institutional authority;
- causal explanation;
- later public memory.

This differs substantially from both forest governance and persistent-pain care.

## 3. Four historical PRFs

The case uses:

- A — archival-historical framework;
- S — history-of-science framework;
- I — institutional-political framework;
- P — public-memory framework.

The case preserves several domain-native boundaries:

- surviving evidence does not recover complete inner experience;
- scientific controversy is not reducible to a simple science-versus-religion narrative;
- institutional power is not by itself a total intellectual explanation;
- later public symbolism is historically real but not identical with seventeenth-century context.

## 4. Third-domain replication criterion

Replication across three domains requires:

- complete directed translation matrices in all three;
- persistence of the same core translation/governance primitives;
- preservation of domain-native topology;
- no requirement that one domain's ontology become the privileged metalanguage.

Passing this test strengthens the claim from two-domain replication to a three-domain replication candidate.

It does not establish universal domain generality.

## 5. External comparator: Claim–Evidence–Action

The external comparator is intentionally simpler:

```
CEA = <Claim, Evidence, Action>
```

It retains:

- claim content;
- evidence list;
- licensed action list.

It omits:

- explicit task identity;
- warrant;
- background commitments;
- claim ceiling;
- ontology commitments;
- standing;
- translation remainder;
- auxiliary assumptions;
- agency diagnostics.

CEA is not offered as a straw person or as a named rival theory.

It is a minimal comparator baseline.

The research question is:

> How much of FSP's representational work disappears when we retain only claim, evidence, and action?

## 6. Comparator delta

For each PRF, the CEA projection is audited for loss.

A loss is material when the omitted structure would prevent recovery of a distinction already shown to matter in prior tests.

Examples include:

- insurer power without clinical truth;
- patient standing without mechanism overclaim;
- public memory without retrospective projection;
- shared action without shared ontology;
- translation remainder;
- auxiliary sensitivity;
- anti-domination structure.

If these disappear under CEA projection, the FSP delta is not merely additional terminology.

## 7. Primitive-minimality challenge

TRANSLATION-5 established that each primitive has at least one current ablation role.

TRANSLATION-6 asks a stronger question:

> Can we remove combinations of primitives while preserving the capabilities the research program actually needs?

The challenge tests retained subsets against required capabilities including:

- task-boundedness;
- evidence provenance;
- licensing;
- context sensitivity;
- claim restraint;
- ontological pluralism;
- standing;
- loss tracking;
- auxiliary sensitivity;
- anti-domination.

A reduced set is:

- LOSSLESS_FOR_TESTED_CAPABILITIES;
- LOSSY_BUT_USABLE;
- TOO_LOSSY.

This is not a proof of logical minimality.

It is a challenge to the current ten-primitive set.

## 8. Publication architecture

The evidence now separates naturally into four scholarly outputs.

### Empirical / formal validation paper

Focus:

- three-domain replication;
- directed translation matrices;
- failure and remainder cases;
- primitive ablation;
- comparator loss.

### Formal philosophy paper

Focus:

- task-relative translation;
- underdetermination candidate theorem;
- perturbation robustness;
- false-equivalence detection;
- comparator delta.

### Philosophical architecture paper

Focus:

- ontological agnosticism;
- cooperation without convergence;
- standing;
- anti-domination;

- Authorial Return.

### FSP-VR program paper

Focus:

- experiential visualization of translation remainder;

## 9. Publication claim ceiling

If three-domain replication passes and the CEA comparator exhibits material loss, the strongest current claim becomes:

> **The FSP translation grammar has replicated across three structurally different constructed domains, while a simpler Claim–Evidence–Action baseline systematically loses distinctions concerning warrant, standing, ontological remainder, auxiliary support, and agency that the tested cases require.**

This remains below:

- universal PRF translatability;
- universal domain generality;
- proof of Duhem–Quine;
- a complete theory of domination;
- empirical validation with real human participants.

## 10. What would raise the ceiling further

The next ceiling requires evidence not generated entirely inside this repository:

- externally sourced cases;
- historical or empirical corpora;
- alternative formal comparators;
- independent reconstruction by another researcher;
- human fidelity tests;
- adversarial review;
- replication across additional domains.

## 11. Relevance to FSP-VR

TRANSLATION-6 matters for VR because it clarifies which structures the experiential layer must preserve.

A simpler visualization of claim, evidence, and action is not enough if it erases:

- who has standing;
- what licenses an inference;
- what remains untranslated;
- what auxiliary assumptions are carrying the conclusion;
- whether action convergence hides conceptual divergence;
- whether coordination occurs under domination.

The VR architecture should therefore visualize only those primitives that earn non-redundant roles, while allowing the formal notation itself to fade when users no longer need it.

## 12. Research ceiling

TRANSLATION-6 is still a constructed three-domain program.

Its strongest justified conclusion is comparative:

> FSP currently preserves more task-relevant philosophical structure than a deliberately simpler CEA baseline across three distinct constructed domains.

That is a publication-worthy candidate claim, but it still requires external validation before being treated as a general theory of plural human inquiry.
