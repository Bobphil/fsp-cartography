# FSP-REVISION-6

## Concept-Formation Dynamics, Interpretive-Resource Competition, Translation Across Emergent Vocabularies, Concept Capture/Co-option Audit, and Shared-Language Nonconvergence

**Status:** Concept-formation and vocabulary-governance revision layer  
**Boundary:** REVISION-1 through REVISION-5 remain intact; TRANSLATION-12 human evidence remains PENDING.

## 1. Purpose

REVISION-5 established that previously excluded claims and interpretive resources may regain standing without becoming automatically true.

REVISION-6 asks what happens next:

> What if new concepts enter inquiry, compete with established vocabularies, translate only partially, or are adopted by institutions in ways that alter their meaning?

The build therefore adds concept provenance, cross-vocabulary translation, interpretive-resource competition, concept-capture auditing, and shared-language nonconvergence.

## 2. Concept records

A concept record contains:

- concept ID;
- label;
- origin;
- contributors;
- evidence;
- definition;
- contrast set;
- claimed scope;
- provenance visibility;
- contestability.

This lets FSP distinguish a label from the concept-history and inferential role carried by that label.

## 3. Concept formation

A concept can originate as:

- DOMINANT;
- COUNTER_PUBLIC;
- SHARED;
- EMERGENT;
- INSTITUTIONAL.

Origin does not authorize truth.

A counter-public concept can be insightful, mistaken, partial, or transformed through later inquiry.

A dominant concept can likewise remain useful, become inadequate, or be revised.

## 4. Translation across vocabularies

Concept translation can be:

- FULL_LOCAL;
- PARTIAL;
- STRUCTURAL;
- TASK_ONLY;
- NO_ADEQUATE_TRANSLATION.

Every translation records:

- preserved features;
- lost features;
- remainder;
- asymmetry.

This preserves the FSP rule:

```
translation != identity
```

and:

```
translation success in one task != global semantic equivalence
```

## 5. Interpretive-resource competition

Multiple vocabularies may coexist.

The audit records:

- overlapping concepts;
- incompatible concepts;
- translation bridges;
- unresolved remainders.

It deliberately returns:

`winner = null`

The architecture does not assume that one vocabulary must defeat the others.

## 6. Emergent shared concepts

A shared concept can emerge when contributors from previously separate interpretive resources jointly construct a bridge.

Such a bridge may preserve enough structure for a shared task while leaving historical or cultural remainder.

This is not treated as a final universal vocabulary.

## 7. Concept capture

Institutional adoption is not automatically co-option.

The audit asks whether adoption changes:

- definition;
- provenance;
- contributor standing;
- contestability;
- scope.

Possible capture requires a semantic shift plus at least one governance loss such as:

- provenance erasure;
- contributor displacement;
- reduced contestability.

## 8. Co-option

Possible co-option is stronger than simple semantic change.

It requires:

- retained label;
- institutional authority expansion;
- concept capture conditions.

This allows FSP to distinguish:

```
healthy institutional adoption
!=
semantic adaptation
!=
concept capture
!=
co-option
```

## 9. Healthy adoption case

A concept may be adopted by an institution while preserving:

- original definition;
- provenance;
- contributors;
- contestability;
- scope.

That adoption is not flagged as capture.

## 10. Captured adoption case

A concept can retain its original label while:

- changing definition;
- erasing origin provenance;
- excluding original contributors;
- removing contributor contestability;
- expanding scope;
- gaining institutional authority.

That pattern is flagged as possible capture and possible co-option.

The claim ceiling remains explicit: this is a represented governance diagnosis, not proof of wrongful intent.

## 11. Shared-language outcomes

The build distinguishes:

- SHARED_LANGUAGE_CONVERGENCE;
- PARTIAL_TRANSLATION_BRIDGE;
- PRACTICAL_COORDINATION_WITH_LEXICAL_PLURALISM;
- IRREDUCIBLE_LEXICAL_NONCONVERGENCE;
- DISTORTED_BY_CONCEPT_CAPTURE.

This extends the earlier convergence grammar from theory choice to language itself.

## 12. Coordination without lexical convergence

Two groups may retain different vocabularies yet still coordinate on:

- evidence collection;
- procedural repair;
- institutional safeguards;
- bounded shared tasks.

Therefore:

```
shared action
!=
shared vocabulary
```

and:

```
shared vocabulary
!=
shared ontology
```

## 13. Lexical nonconvergence

If no adequate translation exists for a task and no shared bridge emerges, FSP preserves:

`IRREDUCIBLE_LEXICAL_NONCONVERGENCE`

rather than forcing a synthetic common language.

This is especially important for future cross-cultural and historical applications.

## 14. Capture-distorted convergence

A community may appear linguistically convergent because a dominant institution has adopted and redefined a previously contested concept.

If capture conditions are present, the result is:

`DISTORTED_BY_CONCEPT_CAPTURE`

rather than genuine shared-language convergence.

## 15. Human-validation boundary

Nothing in REVISION-6 changes TRANSLATION-12.

Human-validation status remains:

`PENDING`.

## 16. Research ceiling

The strongest current claim is:

> FSP can represent concept formation, partial and failed translation across vocabularies, competition among interpretive resources, and institutional concept capture while preserving the possibility of shared action without lexical convergence and refusing to treat any single vocabulary as a universal metalanguage.

It does not establish a complete theory of conceptual engineering, semantic change, or cultural translation.
