# 1. Culmination purpose

The two courses should culminate differently.

## Ethics

Students have already encountered:

- partial maps;
- reasons;
- formation;
- agency under constraint;
- authority;
- legitimacy;
- perspective;
- autonomy;
- consequences;
- power;
- fairness;
- capability;
- authorization;
- remainder;
- REOPEN;
- Return.

PFLAB can therefore reveal the **full Joint Task Space** as a synthesis of already-practiced functions.

## Inquiry

Students have already encountered:

- observation;
- representation;
- instrument dependence;
- inference;
- alternatives;
- auxiliary assumptions;
- model succession;
- meaning change;
- thought experiments;
- institutional science;
- source ledgers;
- remainder;
- claim ceilings;
- REOPEN;
- Return.

DESI should therefore reveal a **JTS-lite scientific coordination grammar** without importing the full Ethics-side authorization architecture.

## Asteria

Asteria remains optional deeper exploration.

Its role is not to become required course content.

Its role is to answer:

> **Does the same governance architecture still help when time horizon, authority, uncertainty, reversibility, and affected populations change radically?**

# 2. Ethics culmination — PFLAB

## Canonical role

PFLAB becomes the first place students see the full compact grammar:

```text
STATE
→ STANDING
→ TRANSLATION
→ REPAIR
→ AMPLIFICATION
→ REOPEN
→ RETURN
```

This is not introduced as a new theory.

Instructor framing:

> You have already practiced each of these questions separately. The Lab puts them together because this case cannot be governed adequately from one perspective, Tool, or institution alone.

---

# 3. PFLAB shared task

Use the existing Power & Flourishing Lab case architecture.

The shared task should be stated as a bounded, plural task rather than a demand for consensus.

Recommended student-facing form:

> **How can the participants reduce avoidable harm, preserve dignity and effective capability, use authority legitimately, coordinate across different kinds of knowledge, and keep the decision open to repair when conditions change?**

Do not say:

> "Find the one ethically correct solution."

Primary firewall:

> **Joint task ≠ requirement of theoretical agreement.**

Secondary:

> **Cooperation ≠ convergence.**

---

# 4. PFLAB Stage 1 — STATE

## Student question

> **What changed, and what is now under pressure?**

Students identify:

- current facts;
- uncertainty;
- affected persons;
- institutional constraints;
- decision pressure.

They should separate:

- observation;
- inference;
- interpretation.

## Output

A short State Card:

1. What changed?
2. What is directly observed?
3. What is inferred?
4. What remains uncertain?
5. What requires attention now?

## Claim ceiling

> **Current state description ≠ complete ethical diagnosis.**

---

# 5. PFLAB Stage 2 — STANDING

## Student question

> **Who may know, recommend, authorize, implement, and bear the burden?**

Students distinguish:

- evidential authority;
- moral standing;
- legal/institutional authority;
- recommendation standing;
- authorization standing;
- practical implementation power.

## Student-facing simplification

Use one compact table:

| Participant | What do they know? | What standing/authority do they have? | What burden do they bear? |
|---|---|---|---|

Do not expose formal field names.

## Firewalls

> **Expertise ≠ authorization.**

> **Power ≠ legitimacy.**

> **Concern ≠ unrestricted proxy authority.**

---

# 6. PFLAB Stage 3 — TRANSLATION / REMAINDER

## Student question

> **What travels across participants, and what does not?**

Students identify one example of:

- a clinical / empirical representation;
- a lived-experience representation;
- an institutional / policy representation.

Then ask:

1. What is preserved?
2. What is compressed?
3. What remainder matters?

## Required remainder

At least one remainder must be concrete and decision-relevant.

Examples:

- resident capability burden;
- frontline feasibility;
- family knowledge;
- moral distress;
- institutional exception pattern.

## Firewalls

> **Translation ≠ identity.**

> **Untranslated remainder ≠ irrelevant remainder.**

---

# 7. PFLAB Stage 4 — REPAIR

## Student question

> **What can be tried, in what order, under what bounds?**

Students generate strategy families rather than one instant solution.

Required categories:

- immediate harm reduction;
- capability / relationship repair;
- implementation feasibility;
- institutional / governance repair.

Students then sequence:

```text
stabilize
→ restore visibility
→ restore minimum capability
→ address implementation constraint
→ authorize bounded repair
→ monitor
```

## Action-state grammar

This is the first required exposure to:

```text
GENERATED
→ POSSIBLE
→ RECOMMENDED
→ AUTHORIZED
→ IMPLEMENTED
→ REVIEWED
```

Students must classify one proposed action through these states.

## Firewalls

> **Generated ≠ recommended.**

> **Recommended ≠ authorized.**

> **Implemented ≠ successful.**

---

# 8. PFLAB Stage 5 — AMPLIFICATION

## Student question

> **What happens if the local judgment becomes a policy, metric, default, or rule?**

Students inspect one proposed repair at two scales:

### Local

What does it do in this case?

### Amplified

What happens if it becomes:
- facility policy;
- standard procedure;
- reporting rule;
- incentive;
- default.

## Required test

Students identify one condition under which a locally useful rule would become unsafe when generalized.

## Firewall

> **Local usefulness ≠ safe institutional amplification.**

---

# 9. PFLAB Stage 6 — REOPEN

## Student question

> **What would make us reconsider?**

Students must specify:

1. trigger;
2. changed condition;
3. which earlier stage reopens;
4. what evidence is preserved.

Examples:

- new harm → Repair;
- changed standing → Standing;
- translation loss → Translation;
- recurrent metric failure → Amplification;
- changed empirical state → State.

## Firewall

> **REOPEN ≠ restart from zero.**

and:

> **REOPEN ≠ perpetual reconsideration.**

---

# 10. PFLAB Stage 7 — RETURN

## Student question

> **Who must now author the next judgment, action, or monitoring step?**

Students state:

- who acts next;
- what they are authorized to do;
- what uncertainty remains;
- what must be monitored;
- what cannot yet be claimed.

## Return card

Complete:

> **Our bounded judgment is:** ______________________________  
> **It authorizes:** ______________________________  
> **It does not authorize:** ______________________________  
> **The most important remainder is:** ______________________________  
> **We will REOPEN if:** ______________________________  
> **The next responsible action belongs to:** ______________________________

## Firewall

> **Return ≠ final closure.**

---

# 11. PFLAB time architecture

For a two-session Lab:

## Session 1

- 10 min — shared task / case state
- 15 min — State
- 15 min — Standing
- 15 min — Translation / remainder
- 10 min — initial repair families

## Session 2

- 15 min — Repair sequencing + action states
- 15 min — Amplification
- 10 min — REOPEN
- 10 min — Return Card
- 10 min — whole-class comparison / debrief

Keep the workbook portion near the existing ~60-minute target per session.

---

# 12. PFLAB instructor debrief

The debrief should not ask:

> "Which group got the right answer?"

Ask:

1. Where did groups differ in their map?
2. Which remainder changed the repair?
3. Who had standing but little practical power?
4. Which recommendation lacked authorization?
5. Which local repair became risky when amplified?
6. Which REOPEN trigger mattered most?
7. Where did cooperation occur without full agreement?

This makes the Lab a culmination of course architecture rather than a case contest.

---

# 13. PFLAB acceptance tests

PASS only if:

- students recognize earlier course functions inside the JTS;
- no new theoretical mastery is required;
- standing remains distinct from expertise;
- translation preserves remainder;
- repair does not bypass authorization;
- amplification is separate from local success;
- REOPEN has a trigger and target;
- Return names an author/actor and bounded next action;
- groups may disagree while still coordinating.

FAIL if:
- the Lab becomes one long checklist;
- students must memorize layer names before use;
- a single Tool is treated as decisive;
- framework language replaces case reasoning.

# 14. Inquiry culmination — DESI JTS-lite

## Canonical role

DESI becomes the Inquiry-side contemporary culmination.

It should expose enough shared architecture to show that contemporary science is:

- collaborative;
- model-mediated;
- institutionally validated;
- publicly communicated;
- still open.

But it should not become an Ethics-style institutional authorization exercise.

Use:

```text
STATE
→ TRANSLATION
→ REMAINDER
→ INSTITUTIONAL VALIDATION
→ REOPEN
→ RETURN
```

---

# 15. DESI Stage 1 — STATE

## Student question

> **What is the current scientific state of the case?**

Students separate:

- data release / measurement;
- statistical result;
- model comparison;
- interpretation;
- public claim.

## Required output

A five-line state summary:

1. What was measured?
2. What statistical/model result was reported?
3. What interpretation is being considered?
4. What remains unsettled?
5. What claim ceiling applies?

## Firewall

> **Interesting tension ≠ established new cosmology.**

Use a more exact ceiling if the chosen DESI source state warrants it.

---

# 16. DESI Stage 2 — TRANSLATION

## Student question

> **How does the result move from data product to cosmological claim?**

Students trace:

```text
measurement
→ processed data product
→ statistical model
→ parameter estimate
→ model comparison
→ interpretation
→ public statement
```

At each transition ask:

> What assumption is added here?

## Firewall

> **Data product ≠ final cosmological interpretation.**

---

# 17. DESI Stage 3 — REMAINDER

Use the three-row remainder map:

| Layer | What is supported? | What remains? |
|---|---|---|
| Measurement / data product | | |
| Model / statistical inference | | |
| Cosmological interpretation | | |

Students choose one remainder that most constrains public claims.

## Firewalls

> **Model fit ≠ ontology.**

> **Institutional publication ≠ interpretive closure.**

---

# 18. DESI Stage 4 — INSTITUTIONAL VALIDATION

## Student question

> **What does collaboration-wide validation establish—and what does it not?**

Students distinguish:

- quality-control procedures;
- collaboration publication;
- expert review;
- interpretive consensus;
- public certainty.

## Claim ceiling

> **Institutional validation can strengthen confidence in the result without settling every interpretation.**

This keeps Inquiry domain-native.

Do not turn this into authority/legitimacy theory.

---

# 19. DESI Stage 5 — REOPEN

Students identify what would reopen the scientific judgment:

- new data release;
- calibration change;
- changed significance;
- alternative model;
- replication;
- revised systematics;
- improved analysis.

## Firewall

> **REOPEN ≠ failure of science.**

It is part of live scientific inquiry.

---

# 20. DESI Stage 6 — RETURN

Return asks:

> **What should the scientific community, science writer, or student now carry forward?**

Student output:

> **Strongest current claim:** ______________________________  
> **Most important unresolved remainder:** ______________________________  
> **What should not be claimed yet:** ______________________________  
> **What evidence should be watched next:** ______________________________

## Firewall

> **Return to inquiry ≠ policy authorization.**

---

# 21. DESI time architecture

For one 75-minute class:

- 10 min — current case orientation
- 12 min — State
- 12 min — Translation chain
- 12 min — Remainder map
- 10 min — Institutional validation
- 8 min — REOPEN
- 8 min — Return
- 3 min — optional Asteria launch

If DESI spans two sessions, expand source comparison rather than adding new FSP layers.

---

# 22. DESI acceptance tests

PASS only if:

- students distinguish data/statistical result/interpretation;
- remainder remains concrete;
- institutional validation is not equated with truth;
- current scientific claim remains bounded;
- REOPEN is visible;
- Return identifies what to carry forward;
- no full Ethics standing/authorization table is required.

FAIL if:
- DESI becomes a generic JTS exercise;
- cosmology content becomes secondary;
- students are asked to memorize full JTS layers.

# 23. Asteria optional deeper comparator

## Role

Asteria is **not required** for either Pressbook culmination.

It is an Encyclopedia depth route for students or instructors who want to ask:

> **What happens to the architecture when uncertainty is long-horizon, authority is distributed, affected populations are probabilistic, and intervention may be irreversible?**

## Link label

Use:

**Compare with Asteria-2047: Planetary Defense Under Deep Uncertainty**

Do not show internal IDs.

## Entry point

The optional route should present a short orientation before the full comparator:

> Gulf Pines showed the full Joint Task Space in a local institutional ethics problem. DESI showed a lighter version inside live scientific inquiry. Asteria asks whether the same governance functions remain useful when the task becomes global, probabilistic, distributed, and potentially irreversible.

---

# 24. Asteria comparator questions for students

Optional exploration should use five questions only:

1. What changes when State becomes a probability distribution over futures?
2. Who has standing when affected populations may shift?
3. What gets lost when model output becomes public risk communication?
4. How does irreversibility change repair sequencing?
5. What does Return mean when there is no single final author?

Do not require the full formal comparator.

# 26. Cooperation without convergence as culmination theme

Both courses should now be able to show the same deeper principle differently.

## Ethics

People and institutions may disagree about values, priorities, burden, and legitimate authority while still coordinating bounded repair.

## Inquiry

Scientists may disagree about interpretation while sharing data standards, methods, source ledgers, statistical results, and conditions for further testing.

## Asteria

Scientific, technical, national, international, and affected populations may need to coordinate without one shared ontology, risk tolerance, or sovereign authority.

Shared culmination claim:

> **Cooperation does not require convergence when translation, provenance, standing, claim ceilings, and REOPEN are sufficiently governed.**

# 29. Journal / reflection firewall

PFLAB may be followed by a Journal prompt, but the Journal remains non-formal.

Possible prompt:

> What part of the group inquiry changed how you saw the case, and what still does not fit comfortably into the shared map?

DESI reflection may ask:

> What is it like to reach the end of a course with a scientific case that is still genuinely open?

Do not require students to reproduce JTS tables in reflection.

---

# 30. Instructor debrief — cross-course

After either culmination, instructors should ask:

1. What did the shared architecture help make visible?
2. What remained domain-native?
3. Where did translation help?
4. Where did remainder matter?
5. What did the claim ceiling prevent?
6. What changed enough to REOPEN?
7. What did Return actually return us to?
8. Did cooperation require agreement?

This makes the culmination explicitly about **governed relation rather than integration by reduction**.

# 31. Regression tests

The culmination build fails if:

## Framework dump

FAIL if students encounter the full JTS as unexplained new vocabulary.

## Domain flattening

FAIL if:
- PFLAB becomes generic systems analysis;
- DESI becomes institutional ethics;
- Asteria becomes required cosmology content.

## Authorization leakage

FAIL if DESI requires full moral/legal authorization analysis.

## Remainder loss

FAIL if institutionalization erases earlier remainder.

## Return failure

FAIL if students end inside a Tool, table, or formal schema rather than a changed human/inquiry situation.

## Overload

FAIL if culmination adds more governance burden than the task warrants.

## Convergence pressure

FAIL if successful groups are defined by agreement rather than governed cooperation.
