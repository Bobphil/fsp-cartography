# FSP-VR-VISUAL-GRAMMAR-1

## Situated Field; Agent Loci; Relations; Constraints; Institutional Fields; Capability Paths; Authorization Gates; Provenance Layers; Remainder Boundaries; Possibility Horizons; Transformation/Re-location; Return; and Scaffold Fading

**Status:** VISUAL-LANGUAGE ARCHITECTURE / EXECUTABLE GRAMMAR — VERSION 1  
**Parents:** FSP-VR-2.0; FSP-LIVES-PUBLIC-ROUTE-1; FSP-VR-MAATHAI-2.0-PROJECTION-1; FSP-LIVES-IN-TRANSFORMATION-REGRESSION-1  
**First testbed:** Wangari Maathai, circa 1976  
**Primary purpose:** Define a reusable visual-spatial language through which FSP relations can be shown, traversed, revealed, constrained, and transformed rather than merely narrated in prose.  
**Primary claim ceiling:** This build defines visual semantics and an executable scene graph. It does not establish that any one rendering style, color system, 3D engine, or immersive hardware produces better learning. It does not claim that spatial coherence equals historical completeness.

---

# 1. Governing Design Claim

FSP-VR should not primarily convert text into illustrated screens.

Its distinctive task is to make otherwise compressed relations temporarily experienceable.

The design target is:

**FIELD → RELATION → TENSION → POSSIBILITY → MOVEMENT → CONSEQUENCE → RE-LOCATION → RETURN**

The learner should be able to perceive and interact with a situated possibility field before being asked to summarize it.

---

# 2. Core Visual Principle

> **Show relations before naming abstractions.**

The public-facing experience should permit the learner to notice:
- who or what is situated where;
- what is connected;
- what is blocked;
- what is reachable;
- what requires permission;
- what is uncertain;
- what remains outside the reconstruction;
- what becomes possible after action.

Only later, and only when useful, should deeper labels such as capability, authorization, portability, provenance, or remainder appear.

---

# 3. Minimal Visual Vocabulary

The grammar contains twelve first-wave primitives.

## VG-01 — Situated Field

A bounded scene representing the current historically or practically relevant situation.

A field contains:
- loci;
- relations;
- constraints;
- institutions;
- possible paths;
- evidence layers;
- remainder boundaries;
- a possibility horizon.

The field is not the world.

> **Situated field ≠ complete reality.**

---

## VG-02 — Agent Locus

A bounded center of action or perspective.

Types:
- PERSON;
- GROUP;
- ORGANIZATION;
- INSTITUTION;
- SYSTEM.

An agent locus can expose:
- access;
- capability;
- standing;
- relationships;
- source-bounded self-description where evidenced.

It must not imply transparent possession of another person's mind.

> **Agent locus ≠ person.**

---

## VG-03 — Relation Edge

A typed connection between two loci or between a locus and an object.

Relation types:
- KNOWS;
- MEMBER_OF;
- WORKS_WITH;
- DEPENDS_ON;
- AFFECTED_BY;
- REPORTS_TO;
- INFLUENCES;
- AUTHORIZED_BY;
- CONSTRAINED_BY;
- SUPPORTS;
- CONTESTS;
- EVIDENCES;
- INFERRED_RELATION.

Relation presentation must distinguish evidence status from visual attractiveness.

---

## VG-04 — Constraint Boundary

A barrier that changes what can be reached, altered, or coordinated.

Constraint types:
- MATERIAL;
- LEGAL;
- INSTITUTIONAL;
- SOCIAL;
- ECONOMIC;
- INFORMATIONAL;
- TEMPORAL;
- PHYSICAL;
- NORMATIVE;
- UNKNOWN.

Constraint strength:
- VERIFIED;
- PROBABLE;
- POSSIBLE;
- UNKNOWN.

> **Constraint ≠ inevitability.**

---

## VG-05 — Institutional Field

A zone in which roles, permissions, procedures, norms, resources, and power relations change.

Entering an institutional field may alter:
- what the agent can access;
- what the agent can do;
- what requires authorization;
- what evidence counts;
- which consequences attach.

Institutions are not background scenery.

They are active relation structures.

---

## VG-06 — Capability Path

A path representing an action or transition that is currently reachable, partially reachable, blocked, or unknown.

Path states:
- OPEN;
- CONDITIONAL;
- BLOCKED;
- UNCERTAIN;
- NOT_YET_VISIBLE.

Path properties:
- required capability;
- required resource;
- affected parties;
- expected but uncertain consequence;
- reversibility;
- source basis.

> **Possible ≠ feasible ≠ selectable ≠ authorized ≠ enacted.**

---

## VG-07 — Authorization Gate

A gate placed on a path when action requires standing, consent, jurisdiction, institutional permission, or another authorization condition.

Gate states:
- AUTHORIZED;
- REQUIRES_CONSENT;
- REQUIRES_ROLE;
- CONTESTED;
- NO_AUTHORITY;
- UNKNOWN.

A learner may be able to see a path without being permitted to traverse it.

> **Can observe ≠ can alter ≠ may alter.**

---

## VG-08 — Provenance Layer

A visual overlay that reveals how each scene element is supported.

Source classes:
- DIRECT_STATEMENT;
- CONTEMPORARY_TESTIMONY;
- RECORDED_ACTION;
- INSTITUTIONAL_RECORD;
- LATER_MEMORY;
- HISTORICAL_SYNTHESIS;
- FSP_INFERENCE;
- NAVIGATIONAL_RECONSTRUCTION.

The layer must make reconstructed elements visually distinguishable from directly evidenced elements.

> **Spatial coherence ≠ evidential completeness.**

---

## VG-09 — Remainder Boundary

A visible boundary marking what the current reconstruction does not contain or cannot responsibly claim.

Remainder types:
- UNKNOWN_MOTIVE;
- MISSING_SOURCE;
- DISPUTED_INTERPRETATION;
- UNAVAILABLE_PERSPECTIVE;
- UNOBSERVED_CONSEQUENCE;
- UNRESOLVED_CAUSATION;
- OTHER.

The boundary should be inspectable but not theatrically mysterious.

> **Remainder is epistemic structure, not decorative fog.**

---

## VG-10 — Possibility Horizon

The boundary between currently visible possibilities and futures not yet warranted for display.

The horizon enforces temporal perspective.

It can move as:
- evidence appears;
- capability grows;
- constraints change;
- time advances;
- new institutions or relationships become relevant.

> **Future-hidden ≠ historically situated.**

The field must contain enough assembly history and current structure for visible possibilities to be meaningful.

---

## VG-11 — Transformation / Re-location

After action or consequence, the field may reconfigure.

Re-location may alter:
- node position;
- relation strength;
- accessible institutions;
- capability paths;
- constraint boundaries;
- authorization gates;
- possibility horizon;
- remainder.

Transformation should be represented by changed relational geometry, not merely a new text card.

> **Transition changes what can happen next.**

---

## VG-12 — Return / OFF

Return requires the learner to leave the decompressed field with a bounded reconstruction.

Return should preserve:
- one relation noticed;
- one important difference;
- one uncertainty;
- one question or possible reversible test.

OFF removes the scaffold.

> **The visual environment is successful only if the learner can leave it.**

---

# 4. Visual Channels

The grammar does not freeze one aesthetic style.

It freezes semantic channels.

A renderer may use:

- position for relation / access;
- distance for practical reachability;
- width for relation strength;
- opacity for evidential confidence;
- texture or line style for provenance class;
- boundary shape for constraint type;
- gate geometry for authorization;
- path brightness for visibility;
- motion for transition;
- layering for source / institution / perspective;
- open edge for remainder.

Color alone must never carry indispensable meaning.

Accessibility requires a second channel such as line style, label, pattern, shape, or motion.

---

# 5. Relation Semantics Must Survive Renderer Change

The same scene may later render as:

- 2D SVG;
- 2.5D spatial canvas;
- WebGL;
- desktop 3D;
- headset VR;
- AR.

The semantic model must not depend on one renderer.

Therefore:

**VISUAL GRAMMAR ≠ RENDERING ENGINE**

---

# 6. Situated Perspective

Perspective is represented through access and action structure, not a camera trick.

A perspective profile may alter:
- visible loci;
- visible evidence;
- path reachability;
- salience;
- institutional access;
- authorization.

Freeze:

**SAME PUBLIC WORLD ≠ SAME ACCESS ≠ SAME SALIENCE ≠ SAME ACTION POSSIBILITIES ≠ SAME SITUATED SIGNIFICANCE**

---

# 7. Assembly History Layer

Before asking a learner to reason from a state, the field should expose enough assembly history to make the state intelligible.

Assembly-history elements may include:
- formative places;
- education;
- prior roles;
- relationships;
- institutional inheritance;
- prior constraints;
- earlier actions;
- historical conditions.

Assembly history should be selectively traversable.

It should not become a complete biography.

Admission rule:

> Include an assembly-history element only if removing it materially changes what a learner could reasonably see as salient, constrained, or possible in the current field.

---

# 8. Possibility Generation Assistance

The environment may assist imagination without choosing an answer.

First-wave possibility lenses:

- KNOW / LEARN;
- ORGANIZE;
- RELATE;
- COMMUNICATE;
- EXPERIMENT;
- REQUEST;
- ADVOCATE;
- COORDINATE;
- WITHDRAW / WAIT;
- BUILD_CAPABILITY;
- CHANGE_PROBLEM_MAP.

These are generative lenses.

They are not exhaustive action menus.

> **Possibility prompt ≠ recommendation.**

---

# 9. Scaffold Fading

The visual prosthesis should reduce assistance over time.

## Level 3 — Strong scaffold

Display:
- labeled capability families;
- example path types;
- visible constraint classes;
- source cues;
- explicit difference between possible and authorized.

## Level 2 — Moderate scaffold

Display:
- lenses;
- relation types;
- provenance access;
- fewer examples.

## Level 1 — Thin scaffold

Display:
- field;
- relations;
- paths;
- constraints;
- optional provenance.

## Level 0 — Return

No scaffold.

The learner explains the field in ordinary language.

The empirical target is not vocabulary recall.

It is whether relational discrimination survives scaffold removal.

# 10. Maathai 1976 — First Visual Testbed

The first scene should not begin with one biographical sentence.

It should reconstruct a bounded situated field containing at least:

## Central locus

Wangari Maathai — bounded public reconstruction, circa mid-1970s.

## Assembly-history loci

- rural Nyeri childhood / land experience;
- Kenyan education;
- U.S. university education;
- European / Nairobi doctoral formation;
- scientific / biological expertise;
- university career;
- marriage / family role;
- women's organizational participation.

These remain source-governed and may require further dossier enrichment before historical promotion.

## Institutional fields

- University of Nairobi;
- National Council of Women of Kenya;
- Kenyan state / postcolonial political institutions;
- family / gender-role expectations.

## Connected affected-party / information loci

- rural women reporting livelihood pressures;
- academic colleagues;
- women's organizations;
- public / government actors;
- communities affected by environmental degradation.

## Environmental signal objects

- firewood pressure;
- water stress;
- soil / food concerns;
- land / ecological change.

## Initial capability paths

- research / investigate;
- teach / communicate;
- organize through women's networks;
- build relationships;
- seek institutional support;
- create a bounded practical experiment;
- advocate;
- wait / gather more evidence.

Not every path is equally feasible or authorized.

---

# 11. Maathai 1976 — Visual Tensions

The first scene should make several tensions simultaneously visible:

### Expertise vs authority

Scientific knowledge does not imply formal political authority.

### Network access vs institutional power

Women's organizations create reach without guaranteeing state control.

### Public role vs gendered expectations

Professional and organizational standing exist inside social constraints.

### Transnational education vs local situatedness

International training expands repertoire but does not remove Kenyan institutional conditions.

### Environmental signal vs problem definition

Resource pressures can be observed before their political meaning is settled.

### Possible action vs known consequence

Several paths remain open.

No path should visually glow as the destined answer.

---

# 12. Maathai 1976 — Possibility Horizon Test

Before historical reveal, the renderer must not display:

- future Green Belt Movement scale;
- Uhuru Park activism;
- political-prisoner protest;
- parliamentary office;
- Nobel Prize;
- later global standing.

But hiding these is insufficient.

The field must still contain enough structure for several historically plausible action families to be visible.

Thus:

> **Hindsight control requires both future suppression and present-world richness.**

---

# 13. First Interaction Grammar

The learner may:

1. LOOK — inspect the field.
2. APPROACH — move toward a locus or institution.
3. REVEAL RELATION — inspect why two elements are connected.
4. TOGGLE PROVENANCE — see what supports the element.
5. TEST PATH — inspect what would be required to act.
6. ENCOUNTER GATE — discover authorization limits.
7. MARK UNKNOWN — preserve a remainder.
8. COMPARE PATHS — hold several live possibilities at once.
9. ACT / REVEAL HISTORY — only after exploration.
10. RETURN — inspect how the field changed.

These are interaction verbs, not mandatory buttons.

---

# 14. Anti-Singularity Rule

A scene fails if visual salience collapses the field prematurely into one obvious future.

Before the historical reveal:

- at least three materially different paths must remain visible;
- no historical path may be designated “correct”;
- path visibility must distinguish possibility from probability;
- at least one non-action / information-gathering path should remain possible;
- at least one constraint must alter path feasibility.

---

# 15. Provenance Requirement

Every visually meaningful object must resolve to:

- source IDs;
- source class;
- reconstruction status;
- claim ceiling where relevant.

Navigational additions must be marked:

**NAVIGATIONAL_RECONSTRUCTION**

This includes:
- spatial placement;
- scale;
- symbolic shape;
- compressed geography;
- composite institutional representation.

---

# 16. Historical Reconstruction Firewall

The renderer may spatialize evidence.

It may not silently invent:
- dialogue;
- motives;
- exact private thoughts;
- exact room layouts where unknown;
- exact encounters;
- precise causal relations unsupported by sources.

When spatial coherence requires invented navigational structure, label that structure as navigational reconstruction.

---

# 17. Transformation Grammar

A transition should support a before / after relational comparison.

For each transformed element ask:

- what relation changed?
- what capability changed?
- what constraint changed?
- what institution became relevant?
- what path opened?
- what path closed?
- what uncertainty remains?

The learner should be able to return to State A and compare it with State B.

---

# 18. Capability Visualization

Capability is not a score.

Represent it as a reachable action topology.

Possible display:
- number and type of reachable paths;
- path depth;
- required dependencies;
- access to institutions / collaborators;
- reversibility;
- cost / constraint burden.

Freeze:

> **More paths ≠ better life.**

A new capability may create:
- obligations;
- exposure;
- risks;
- lost alternatives.

---

# 19. Authorization Visualization

Authorization must remain visually distinct from capability.

An agent may:
- be able to perform action;
- lack legal authority;
- lack consent;
- possess influence but no jurisdiction;
- possess formal authority but lack knowledge.

A path can therefore be open in capability-space but gated in authorization-space.

---

# 20. Remainder Visualization

Remainder must remain present throughout.

A renderer should support:
- unresolved edge;
- unlabeled interior;
- missing-source marker;
- disputed-interpretation marker;
- inaccessible-perspective marker.

Do not use visual ambiguity merely for drama.

Every remainder marker must explain what kind of incompleteness it represents.

---

# 21. Return and Recompression

At Return, the learner should no longer see the whole decompressed field by default.

Ask for ordinary-language reconstruction:

- What changed?
- What became newly possible?
- What became harder?
- Which relation surprised you?
- What remained unresolved?
- Which possibilities had looked open before the historical reveal?

Then OFF.

This tests whether decompression produced better recompression.

---

# 22. Visual-Grammar Invariants

VG-I01 — Every scene has a bounded field.

VG-I02 — Every agent locus has an owner / source status.

VG-I03 — Every relation is typed.

VG-I04 — Every constraint has a type and evidence status.

VG-I05 — Capability and authorization remain distinct.

VG-I06 — Provenance is recoverable.

VG-I07 — Remainder is representable.

VG-I08 — Future outcomes can be hidden.

VG-I09 — At least three paths can remain live in a possibility-field test.

VG-I10 — No path is marked as the historical “correct answer” before reveal.

VG-I11 — Transformation can alter relational geometry.

VG-I12 — Return / OFF are represented.

VG-I13 — Color is never the only semantic channel.

VG-I14 — Navigational reconstruction is marked.

VG-I15 — Scaffold level is explicit and reducible.

---

# 23. First Renderer Recommendation

Do not begin with headset VR.

First renderer:

**interactive 2D / 2.5D field using SVG or Canvas**

Reason:
- inspect visual grammar cheaply;
- rapidly revise semantics;
- test spatial understanding;
- test multiple simultaneous paths;
- test provenance overlays;
- test accessibility;
- avoid confusing rendering spectacle with conceptual success.

Only after visual-language regression should the same semantic scene graph be projected into WebGL / 3D / headset VR.

---

# 24. Empirical Questions for the Visual Grammar

The first visual test should ask:

1. Can a learner identify at least three different live possibilities without predicting one inevitable future?
2. Can the learner distinguish capability from authorization?
3. Can the learner identify a constraint without treating it as inevitability?
4. Can the learner recover why a relation is present?
5. Can the learner identify something the scene does not know?
6. Can the learner explain what changed after re-location?
7. After scaffold fading, can the learner reconstruct the relation field without the labels?

This is a developmental test, not a right-answer quiz.

---

# 25. Strongest Supported Claim

> **FSP-VR can be developed as a renderer-independent visual language in which situated agents, institutions, evidence, constraints, capabilities, authorization, possibilities, uncertainty, transformation, and Return are represented as relations in a field rather than as sequential explanatory prose.**

---

# 26. Claim Ceiling

This build does not establish:
- that twelve primitives are final;
- that spatial representation automatically improves understanding;
- that the Maathai scene is historically thick enough yet;
- that SVG / Canvas is superior to other rendering approaches;
- that users will interpret the proposed channels as intended;
- that eventual 3D or headset immersion will improve transfer.

---

# 27. REOPEN Conditions

Reopen if:
1. primitives cannot be learned without a legend;
2. users confuse path visibility with recommendation;
3. constraint boundaries appear deterministic;
4. institutional fields become decorative backgrounds;
5. provenance overlays interrupt rather than deepen inquiry;
6. remainder becomes spectacle;
7. Maathai requires historically unsupported spatial invention;
8. Aristotle or Mandela cannot use the grammar without forcing Maathai's topology;
9. renderer demands leak into canonical semantics;
10. scaffold fading destroys comprehension.

---

# 28. Constitutional Decision

**PASS — RETAIN AS FIRST FSP-VR VISUAL LANGUAGE.**

No new FSP Core primitive is added.

The visual primitives are projection semantics.
