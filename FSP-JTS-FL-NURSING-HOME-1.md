# FSP-JTS-FL-NURSING-HOME-1

## Florida Nursing-Home Joint Task Space Pilot

**Status:** CROSS-DOMAIN PILOT — IMPLEMENTATION CANDIDATE  
**Role:** First concrete test of FSP cooperation without convergence  
**Human case:** Gulf Pines — fictional/composite teaching facility  
**Empirical hinge:** Florida nursing-home historical backtest (BT-1A / BT-1A.3)  
**Governance rule:** Fictional case ≠ empirical facility. Historical association ≠ causal effect ≠ moral quality.

---

# 1. Why this is the right first Joint Task Space

A nursing home is not merely a medical facility.

It is simultaneously:

- a home;
- a workplace;
- a regulated institution;
- a clinical environment;
- a family relationship network;
- a labor system;
- an economic organization;
- a site of legal authority and constraint;
- a public-health node;
- a place where residents may have unequal mobility, cognition, communication, and exit options.

A disruption such as an infectious outbreak therefore cannot be governed adequately by one discipline alone.

Clinical epidemiology may identify transmission risk.

Staffing data may reveal capacity constraints.

Ethics may expose burdens, dignity, agency, and standing.

Law and regulation may define authority and minimum duties.

Management may confront operational feasibility.

Families may carry knowledge unavailable to the facility.

Residents may experience harms that aggregate metrics cannot represent.

Workers may face moral distress, fatigue, exposure, and conflicting obligations.

Public agencies may possess regulatory authority without possessing complete local knowledge.

The Joint Task Space therefore begins with a problem no participant can honestly claim to own completely.

---

# 2. The human surface — Gulf Pines

**Gulf Pines** is a fictional/composite Florida nursing-home facility used only as a teaching and coordination surface.

A respiratory outbreak begins to appear.

Several residents develop symptoms. Staff absences increase. Families begin calling. Visitation policy becomes contested. Administrators worry about staffing coverage, infection spread, resident isolation, regulatory exposure, and family trust. Frontline workers report that some formal policies are difficult to carry out under actual shift conditions. Residents differ sharply in health status, cognitive capacity, communication ability, family support, mobility, and tolerance for isolation.

The task is not:

> “What single rule should Gulf Pines adopt?”

The task is:

> **How can the participants build a sufficiently governed shared map to coordinate action while preserving uncertainty, role differences, resident standing, domain-native evidence, and conditions for revision?**

---

# 3. The Joint Task Space

The pilot uses this shared task:

> **Reduce avoidable outbreak harm while preserving resident dignity, effective capability, worker safety, family relationships, lawful authority, and the ability to revise policy as evidence and conditions change.**

No participant is asked to converge on a single ethical theory, causal model, professional worldview, or institutional priority.

They are asked to make enough of their inquiry mutually inspectable to cooperate responsibly.

# 4. Participants and native contributions

## Residents

Native contribution:
- lived effects of restrictions;
- preferences;
- fear, loneliness, safety concerns;
- functional limitations;
- dependence;
- communication barriers;
- practical consequences invisible in aggregate data.

Standing:
- primary moral standing over burdens imposed on their lives;
- variable legal decision authority depending on capacity and existing arrangements.

Firewall:

> **Map of Resident ≠ Resident.**

## Families / trusted supporters

Native contribution:
- longitudinal knowledge of residents;
- communication and advocacy;
- practical support;
- observations outside staff shifts.

Standing:
- relational and sometimes legal standing;
- not automatic authority over every resident decision.

Firewall:

> **Concern ≠ unrestricted proxy authority.**

## CNAs, nurses, and other frontline staff

Native contribution:
- shift-level operational reality;
- resident changes;
- staffing feasibility;
- workflow bottlenecks;
- exposure;
- moral distress;
- informal repair practices.

Standing:
- strong practical and clinical input;
- role-bounded decision authority.

Firewall:

> **Frontline visibility ≠ complete institutional picture.**

## Clinical leadership / infection prevention

Native contribution:
- symptom patterns;
- transmission hypotheses;
- testing and infection-control practices;
- clinical risk;
- uncertainty.

Standing:
- clinical expertise;
- not sole authority over all ethical or relational burdens.

Firewall:

> **Clinical expertise ≠ total authorization.**

## Administration

Native contribution:
- staffing allocation;
- logistics;
- contracting;
- policy implementation;
- reporting;
- organizational continuity.

Standing:
- substantial operational authority;
- constrained by law, regulation, professional duties, resident rights, and evidence.

Firewall:

> **Operational authority ≠ evidential truth or moral completeness.**

## Regulators / public-health authorities

Native contribution:
- legal requirements;
- inspection history;
- reporting standards;
- population-level guidance;
- enforcement authority.

Standing:
- public and regulatory authority within defined jurisdiction.

Firewall:

> **Regulatory authority ≠ complete local knowledge.**

## Empirical / analytic layer

Native contribution:
- staffing patterns;
- inspection data;
- facility characteristics;
- outbreak outcomes;
- matched comparisons;
- uncertainty and discordance.

Standing:
- evidential contribution only.

Firewall:

> **Historical association ≠ causal effect ≠ prospective prediction.**

# 5. ABCD first-map expansion

Before a Tool is chosen, the Joint Task Space applies ABCD.

## Agency

Who can actually do what?

- Which residents can understand and choose among options?
- Which workers can alter workflow?
- Which managers can authorize staffing or visitation changes?
- Which actions require regulator approval?
- Which families can participate?
- Which apparent choices are unavailable because of staffing, transport, cognition, cost, or law?

## Beauty, awe, and mattering

What is at stake beyond measurable outcomes?

- safety;
- touch;
- companionship;
- trust;
- dignity;
- familiar routines;
- the meaning of home;
- workers' sense of professional integrity;
- families' ability to remain connected.

## Context and contingency

What shaped the present situation?

- staffing history;
- inspection history;
- ownership and finance;
- building design;
- prior infection-control practices;
- local disease prevalence;
- workforce mobility;
- resident acuity;
- regulatory requirements;
- family access patterns.

## Dynamism

What is changing?

- infection prevalence;
- staffing availability;
- resident condition;
- testing information;
- family pressure;
- policy feasibility;
- worker fatigue;
- public guidance.

Firewall:

> **Expanded attention ≠ complete representation.**

# 6. Tool sequence

The pilot deliberately uses multiple Tools because no single audit owns the problem.

## T01 — Ethical Attention / First Map Audit

Question:
What has entered the shared map, and what has been omitted?

Likely omissions:
- nonverbal resident distress;
- worker burden;
- family knowledge;
- actual shift feasibility;
- unequal impact of isolation.

## T07 — Perspective / Moral-Visibility Audit

Question:
Whose situation is being represented, by whom, and with what limits?

Key check:
A dashboard can show infection counts without showing what a restriction does to a resident with dementia, sensory loss, or dependence on family communication.

## T09 — Consequence / Welfare / Harm Audit

Question:
Who is affected, what happens, how certain are we, and who bears the burden?

This includes:
- infection;
- hospitalization;
- death;
- isolation;
- deconditioning;
- distress;
- worker exposure;
- staffing loss;
- family separation;
- unequal burden.

## T12 — Dependency / Effective-Capability Audit

Question:
What can each participant actually do and be under these conditions?

A formal option is not necessarily an effective capability.

## T06 — Legitimacy / Standing Audit

Question:
Who may decide which part of the intervention?

This separates:
- clinical recommendation;
- administrative decision;
- resident consent;
- legal proxy decision;
- regulator mandate;
- family advocacy.

## T10 — Background Power / Genealogy Audit

Question:
What is shaping the choice before the choice appears?

Potential background conditions:
- chronic staffing patterns;
- reimbursement;
- ownership;
- labor-market conditions;
- regulatory history;
- inherited facility design.

## T02 — Examination / Reason-Giving Audit

Question:
Which claims are evidence, inference, extrapolation, or assumption?

This is especially important when historical facility data are used to justify present intervention.

# 7. Empirical hinge — Florida historical replay

The pilot imports the Florida nursing-home backtest only as an empirical contribution to the Joint Task Space.

It does not treat the backtest as the Gulf Pines case.

The replay froze pre-outcome structural information before later COVID outcomes were inspected.

Its strongest result was not a general master score.

The most informative preregistered contrast was the combined profile of low staffing and low health-inspection ratings versus matched high/high facilities.

The broader descriptive signal count did not validate as a reliable general COVID risk ordering.

Discordant facilities remained visible.

The surviving lesson is therefore structural and methodological:

> **Differentiated warnings can be useful even when a universal risk score fails.**

And:

> **An incomplete administrative map may still identify a constraint worth investigating without becoming a prophecy or moral verdict.**

Empirical firewalls:

> **SignalCount ≠ ValidatedRiskScore.**

> **HistoricalAssociation ≠ CausalEffect ≠ ProspectiveValidation.**

> **LaterOutcome ≠ MoralQualityOfFacility.**

# 8. Translation table

| Source domain | Native object | Shared translation | Remainder that must stay visible |
|---|---|---|---|
| Clinical/public health | infection/transmission evidence | outbreak-risk claim with uncertainty | individual prognosis, future variant behavior |
| Staffing/operations | hours, absences, coverage | effective care-capacity constraint | informal labor, skill mix, local workarounds |
| Resident experience | lived burden | capability / dignity effect | irreducible first-person meaning |
| Family knowledge | longitudinal observation | perspective/provenance contribution | proxy limits, family conflict |
| Regulation | rule / inspection / authority | authorization constraint | local feasibility, moral sufficiency |
| Ethics | reasoned judgment | bounded normative claim | disagreement among theories/values |
| Administration | operational plan | implementation pathway | hidden burdens, incentive effects |
| Historical data | association / matched contrast | structural warning | causality, facility-specific prediction |
| Finance/ownership | resource and incentive structure | background constraint hypothesis | intentionality, direct causal path |

This table is the core of metaphysically agnostic translation.

Nothing in it says that these native objects are the same kind of thing.

# 9. Remainder ledger

The Joint Task Space must explicitly retain what the shared representation cannot settle.

Initial remainder:

- exact causal contribution of baseline staffing;
- future outbreak trajectory;
- resident-specific tolerance for isolation;
- worker-specific burden;
- family conflict;
- unobserved informal care;
- ownership intent;
- local community transmission;
- tradeoffs between immediate infection control and longer-term flourishing;
- whether one policy can serve residents with radically different capability profiles.

Remainder statuses may include:

- DOMAIN_NATIVE_ONLY
- PARTIALLY_TRANSLATED
- CONTESTED
- UNKNOWN
- DEFERRED

Firewall:

> **Untranslated remainder ≠ irrelevant remainder.**

---

# 10. Standing and authorization partition

The Joint Task Space must never collapse evidence into authority.

A useful matrix is:

| Question | Primary standing | Required consultation |
|---|---|---|
| Is a resident clinically deteriorating? | clinical team | resident/proxy, frontline staff |
| What does isolation do to this resident? | resident where possible | family/supporters, staff, clinicians |
| Can the shift actually implement the protocol? | frontline + operations | clinical leadership |
| What infection-control practice is medically indicated? | clinical/public-health expertise | operations, resident/proxy |
| May a resident be restricted or isolated? | lawful institutional authority within rights constraints | resident/proxy, clinical, ethics as needed |
| Should a policy apply facility-wide? | authorized governance body | all materially affected domains |
| When should policy change? | distributed | evidence owner + affected participants + authorized decision-maker |

Firewall:

> **Expertise ≠ standing ≠ authorization.**

---

# 11. Cooperation without convergence

The participants need not agree on one theory of flourishing.

They need not agree on one account of institutional justice.

They need not agree on whether aggregate welfare, autonomy, care, rights, or capability is morally primary.

They can still cooperate if they can agree to preserve:

- evidence provenance;
- resident visibility;
- uncertainty;
- role boundaries;
- standing;
- reversible options where possible;
- burden monitoring;
- explicit claim ceilings;
- REOPEN conditions.

Thus the Joint Task Space tests the FSP hypothesis:

> **Cooperation does not require convergence when translation, provenance, standing, claim ceilings, and REOPEN are sufficiently governed.**

---

# 12. Bounded joint judgment

A defensible joint judgment will not look like:

> “The data prove that Gulf Pines must impose policy X.”

It should look more like:

> “Current clinical evidence and operational conditions justify a heightened infection-control response, but the form and duration of restrictions must remain sensitive to resident-specific capability and burden, actual staffing capacity, lawful standing, and new evidence. Historical Florida data support treating staffing and inspection weakness as structural warnings worth investigating; they do not establish Gulf Pines' causal risk or moral quality.”

That is a bounded joint judgment.

---

# 13. Repair families

The pilot should generate strategy families, not a single optimized answer.

Possible families:

### Clinical / infection-control repair
Testing, cohorting, PPE, ventilation, monitoring, clinical escalation.

### Staffing / capacity repair
Contingency staffing, schedule redesign, skill-mix protection, workload triage, external support.

### Resident-capability repair
Communication support, safe contact alternatives, individualized visitation exceptions, mobility and activity preservation.

### Family / relational repair
Structured communication, designated contacts, remote access support, transparent policy explanation.

### Institutional repair
Escalation rules, standing map, rapid review group, documentation of exceptions and burdens.

### Structural repair
Post-crisis review of staffing, inspection, ownership/resource constraints, physical plant, and emergency capacity.

Firewall:

> **Generated repair ≠ recommended repair ≠ authorized repair.**

---

# 14. REOPEN conditions

The current settlement must reopen when:

- infection trajectory changes materially;
- staffing capacity crosses a predefined threshold;
- resident harm from restrictions becomes materially visible;
- new clinical guidance changes the evidence;
- implementation proves infeasible;
- unequal capability effects emerge;
- family or resident evidence reveals an omission;
- regulatory conditions change;
- an intervention produces significant unintended consequences;
- the shared representation systematically fails to capture a participant's relevant experience.

Meta-REOPEN:

The Joint Task Space grammar itself reopens if repeated use shows that its categories obscure clinically or morally important distinctions.

---

# 15. Authorial Return

The Joint Task Space does not issue the final decision.

It should return to participants:

- the shared task;
- each domain's contribution;
- provenance;
- disagreements;
- remainder;
- bounded judgments;
- standing map;
- strategy families;
- authorization state;
- claim ceiling;
- REOPEN conditions.

Participants then author and own the decision appropriate to their legitimate roles.

> **Cognitive support ≠ outsourced authorship.**

---

# 16. What this pilot tests

The pilot passes only if it demonstrates that FSP can:

1. preserve human experience and empirical evidence without collapsing them;
2. combine multiple Tools without turning them into a checklist;
3. distinguish evidence, interpretation, recommendation, and authorization;
4. preserve domain-native differences;
5. represent remainder rather than hiding it;
6. coordinate participants with different values and authority;
7. generate bounded strategy families;
8. keep historical evidence within its claim ceiling;
9. preserve Return and REOPEN;
10. reveal when the FSP grammar itself needs repair.

---

# 17. Claim ceiling

This pilot does not show that FSP solves nursing-home governance.

It tests whether FSP can create a more inspectable and corrigible coordination environment for a problem that no single participant, discipline, dataset, or institution can adequately represent alone.

The pilot's strongest warranted claim is:

> **The Florida nursing-home case is a credible stress test of cooperation without convergence because clinical evidence, staffing, lived experience, institutional authority, family knowledge, regulation, empirical history, and ethical judgment must interact while remaining non-identical.**
