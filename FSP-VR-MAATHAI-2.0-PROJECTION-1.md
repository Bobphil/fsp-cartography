# FSP-VR-MAATHAI-2.0-PROJECTION-1

## Wangari Maathai — VR 2.0 Projection Specification Compiled from the Three-Topology Lives Regression

**Status:** VR PROJECTION SPECIFICATION — VERSION 1  
**Parents:** FSP-VR-2.0; FSP-PROJECTION-PROFILES-1; FSP-LIVES-PUBLIC-ROUTE-1; FSP-LIVES-IN-TRANSFORMATION-PILOT-1; FSP-LIVES-IN-TRANSFORMATION-REGRESSION-1  
**Canonical dossier owner:** FSP-LIVES-IN-TRANSFORMATION-PILOT-1  
**Projection role:** Experiential decompression of a bounded Maathai transition; not a second historical owner.  
**Deployment status:** SPECIFICATION ONLY — NOT YET EXECUTABLE VR.  
**Primary claim ceiling:** This specification defines how one historically sourced Maathai transformation may be projected into VR while preserving provenance, hindsight control, person/dossier separation, optional topology modules, Authorial Return, and OFF. It does not claim that VR improves learning, empathy, judgment, or transfer until tested.

---

# 1. Why This Projection Must Be Built After the Three-Case Regression

The Maathai pilot was initially selected because it provides a strong experiential sequence:

**bounded practical task → repeated coordination → capability expansion → encounter with institutional constraint → widened problem map**

The Aristotle / Mandela regression now prevents this sequence from becoming the generic Lives or VR template.

Therefore this projection is explicitly tagged:

**TOPOLOGY PROFILE: MAATHAI**

It may use only modules warranted by the Maathai dossier.

It may not imply that all Lives projections share the same scene grammar.

---

- state chronology;

- historical Return.

VR projection may transform:

- spatial organization;
- temporal reveal;

- interaction order;

- comparative view;
- Return artifact.

- new historical facts;

- new quotations;

- historical inevitability.

# 4. Selected Historical Slice

The first executable slice should cover:

**mid-1970s academic / women's-organization position  
→ 1977 tree-planting initiative  
→ early Green Belt capability expansion  
→ later encounter with institutional / governance constraint**

The slice must stop before making later fame the organizing frame.

Do not begin with:

- the Nobel Prize;
- elected office;
- international celebrity;
- a retrospective "hero" introduction.

---

# 5. Why This Slice

It tests whether VR can make visible:

- bounded practical need;
- available capability;
- low-cost action;
- repeated coordination;
- distributed participation;
- capability growth;
- new constraints becoming visible;
- change in problem definition.

This is stronger for FSP than merely presenting a dramatic historical event.

The experiential target is:

> **How can action change the field of what the agent and organization are able to notice, coordinate, and attempt next?**

---

# 6. Scene 0 — Pre-Entry Orientation

Before immersion, state:

- this is a historically bounded reconstruction;
- the environment is based on public evidence;
- some relations are modeled;
- private experience is not being simulated;
- later outcomes are intentionally hidden;
- the learner may exit at any time.

Freeze:

> **VR reconstruction ≠ Wangari Maathai.**

---

# 7. Scene 1 — Situated Entry

## Historical state

Approximate target: 1976.

Expose:

- Maathai's public academic / organizational role;
- biological / scientific background;
- National Council of Women context;
- reports of rural women's practical environmental concerns;
- institutional setting available at that time.

Hide:

- later Green Belt scale;
- democracy activism;
- Uhuru Park;
- political-prisoner protest;
- electoral candidacy;
- elected office;
- Nobel Prize.

## Learner task

Identify:

- present problem;
- available resources;
- constraints;
- who is affected;
- what actions are presently imaginable.

No recommendation is scored as correct.

---

# 8. Scene 2 — Evidence and Need Field

Present source-grounded concerns from the dossier:

- firewood pressure;
- water scarcity;
- food / soil concerns;
- household burden;
- need for practical income / livelihood support.

Each item should be source-tagged.

The learner can inspect:

**WHAT IS DOCUMENTED?**  
**WHO REPORTED IT?**  
**WHAT IS INFERRED?**

Do not transform the environment into an omniscient map of Kenya.

---

# 9. Scene 3 — Bounded Action Possibility

Expose candidate actions available within the historical field.

The environment may include:

- community tree planting;
- training;
- seedling / nursery coordination;
- environmental education;
- seeking institutional support;
- doing nothing / postponing;
- other bounded options supported by historical plausibility.

The VR system must not announce which option "Maathai chose" until the learner completes inspection.

This is a controlled probe, not a game of guessing the right answer.

---

# 10. Scene 4 — Historical Reveal: Tree Planting

Reveal:

- the 1977 Green Belt initiative;
- relation to National Council of Women of Kenya;
- Maathai's later account of responding to practical environmental / livelihood needs.

Expose provenance:

- retrospective first-person statement;
- institutional history;
- later biographical synthesis.

Claim ceiling:

> The sources support a bounded account of the practical problem and intervention. They do not establish a complete motive map.

---

# 11. Scene 5 — Capability Growth

The learner should experience changed organizational possibilities rather than merely receive a text summary.

Possible representations:

- more local nurseries;
- more participating women / communities;
- expanding communication pathways;
- training routines;
- repeated action;
- new public recognition;
- wider organizational network.

Important:

These are relational representations.

Do not invent exact quantitative growth unless sourced.

The key experiential contrast is:

**ACTION AT t1  
→ NEW CAPABILITIES AT t2**

---

# 12. Scene 6 — Re-Location

Return the learner to a transformed field.

Ask:

- What can the organization now do that it could not do before?
- Which problems are now newly visible?
- Which institutions now matter?
- Which constraints have become more consequential?

This is Return as re-location.

---

# 13. Scene 7 — Governance Constraint Appears

Introduce later institutional friction gradually.

Possible historically grounded route:

- environmental protection conflicts with state / development decisions;
- public land questions;
- civic participation constraints;
- restrictions on organizing;
- state opposition.

Do not jump directly from tree planting to democracy as if the latter were present as the full original plan.

Freeze:

> **Later political significance ≠ original total intention.**

---

# 14. Scene 8 — Problem-Map Expansion

The learner should be able to compare:

## Earlier map

**environmental / livelihood problem**

with:

## Later map

**environmental problem + institutional constraint + public participation + rights / governance problem**

The scene should make visible that the task changed.

This is not merely "Maathai became political."

It is:

> **Repeated action exposed constraints that changed the problem definition.**

---

# 15. Topology Modules Enabled

For the Maathai profile enable:

## Intended / Actual

Use for:
- 1977 practical intervention;
- later expansion of significance.

## Capability / Constraint Delta

Required.

## Institutional Role

Required.

## Authorization / Standing

Use later in the slice only when public influence and institutional authority become relevant.

## Hindsight Control

Required.

## Failed Plan

Not required in this first 1976–early-governance slice.

It belongs to a later 1997 module.

---

# 16. Topology Modules Disabled

Do not include:

## Text / Concept Development

This is Aristotle-specific in the present regression.

## Negotiation / Collective Authorization

This is not the dominant Maathai module and should not be imported from Mandela.

## Presidency / State-Authority Transition

Not part of this slice.

This explicit disabling proves that the projection is compiled from the topology profile rather than from a universal Lives scene stack.

---

# 17. Hindsight-Control Rules

At every scene, maintain a visible or inspectable **KNOWN THEN** boundary.

Later information must remain unavailable until its historical reveal.

Examples:

- no Nobel labels in early scenes;
- no "future MP" labels;
- no democracy-leader framing before the relevant transition;
- no inevitability language.

The learner may optionally switch to retrospective mode only after completing the bounded sequence.

---

# 18. Provenance Interaction

Every historical assertion should support one of:

- source badge;
- provenance inspector;
- pause-and-check ledger;
- claim ceiling card.

The learner should be able to distinguish:

**PRIMARY / FIRST-PERSON**  
**CONTEMPORARY DOCUMENT**  
**LATER HISTORICAL / INSTITUTIONAL**  
**FSP INFERENCE**

Immersion must never make these differences less visible.

---

# 19. Perspective Typing

The learner does not "become Maathai."

The projection may offer:

## Observer Access

What public information is visible.

## Situated Access

What the bounded historical agent could plausibly access.

## Organizational Access

What a movement / network could coordinate.

## Institutional Access

What institutions could authorize or constrain.

Do not label any of these as Maathai's exact first-person phenomenology.

---

# 20. No Simulated Inner Voice

Do not generate first-person internal monologue such as:

> "I now realize democracy is the real issue."

unless it is an attributable historical quotation shown explicitly as quotation.

Synthetic first-person voice would create false provenance.

---

# 21. Claim-Ceiling Interaction

Claim ceiling should be available when the learner:

- inspects motive;
- compares intended / actual;
- attempts portability;
- enters retrospective mode.

The claim ceiling should not become a permanent floating warning that destroys immersion.

Use contextual reveal.

---

# 22. Remainder Representation

Remainder may appear as:

- missing source;
- disputed interpretation;
- inaccessible motive;
- unknown participant experience;
- unclear causal contribution;
- not-yet-visible consequence.

Do not render remainder as fog, darkness, mystery, or supernatural symbolism by default.

It is epistemic structure.

---

# 23. Portability Gate

After the historical sequence, require the learner to identify:

1. one structural relation;
2. one historical condition that made it possible;
3. one reason it may fail to travel;
4. one claim ceiling.

Candidate relation:

> A bounded practical task can sometimes build capability and expose deeper constraints.

The learner must be free to reject that relation as useful for their own context.

---

# 24. Authorial Return Artifact

The VR session returns a short learner-owned artifact containing:

- structural relation selected, if any;
- evidence or scene that supported it;
- one contextual difference;
- one remainder;
- one question worth carrying forward.

It must not contain:

- a recommendation;
- a prescribed personal goal;
- a score of moral similarity to Maathai;
- an inferred personality profile.

---

# 25. Optional Life Navigation Handoff

After Authorial Return ask:

> Do you want to examine whether this structural relation applies to a situation of your own?

If no:
- end.

If yes:
- pass only the structural relation, claim ceiling, and learner-authored question.

Do not pass historical tactic as recommendation.

---

# 26. OFF

The final scene must end the immersive scaffold.

Required sequence:

**RETURN ARTIFACT  
→ REMOVE HISTORICAL ENVIRONMENT  
→ ORDINARY INTERFACE / EXIT  
→ OFF**

Philosophical completion occurs after return, not at peak immersion.

---

# 27. Safety / Exit Rule

The learner must be able to exit at any point.

The projection should preserve:
- orientation;
- scene position;
- unfinished return artifact.

No penalty for exit.

---

# 28. Acceptance Gates

## VR-M1 — Canonical ownership

All historical claims resolve to the Maathai dossier.

## VR-M2 — Topology fidelity

Only Maathai-warranted modules are active.

## VR-M3 — Hindsight control

Later outcomes remain hidden in earlier scenes.

## VR-M4 — Person firewall

No simulated private mind.

## VR-M5 — Provenance

Source class is recoverable.

## VR-M6 — Capability change

The learner can inspect what changed in reachability, not merely what happened next.

## VR-M7 — Problem-map expansion

The environmental-to-governance transition is represented as changed task structure, not as retroactive original intention.

## VR-M8 — Portability firewall

Historical success does not become recommendation.

## VR-M9 — Authorial Return

Learner owns the return artifact.

## VR-M10 — OFF

Scene ends outside immersion.

## VR-M11 — Life Navigation optional

No forced personal transfer.

## VR-M12 — Three-topology regression preservation

The Maathai projection does not become the universal Lives VR template.

# 29. Prototype Research Measures

When executable, test:

- provenance recall;
- hindsight contamination;
- capability-change comprehension;
- distinction between intended and actual consequence;
- overidentification with historical person;
- structural-transfer quality;
- recommendation inflation;
- Return-artifact quality.

Do not infer effectiveness from presence or engagement alone.

---

# 30. Public Lives ↔ VR Contract

The Public Lives route sends:

- selected state;
- selected transition;
- source IDs;
- active topology modules;
- claim ceiling;
- hidden-future rules.

VR returns:

- inspected relation;
- learner-authored structural insight;
- contextual difference;
- remainder;
- Return token.

No historical fact flows from VR back into the canonical dossier.

---

# 31. Reuse Rule

This Maathai specification may contribute reusable interaction patterns:

- hindsight gate;
- provenance inspector;
- capability-delta view;
- Return artifact;
- OFF transition.

It may not contribute a mandatory historical scene order for Aristotle, Mandela, or future lives.

> **Reusable interaction ≠ reusable biography topology.**

---

# 32. Claim Ceiling

This specification does not establish:

- VR learning benefit;
- increased empathy;
- improved critical thinking;
- better historical judgment;
- durable transfer;
- that Maathai's transformation is best understood only through capability expansion.

Those are empirical research questions.

---

# 33. REOPEN Conditions

Reopen if:

1. scene construction requires unsupported historical detail;
2. learners mistake reconstruction for first-person access;
3. hindsight leakage remains high;
4. provenance controls are ignored or confusing;
5. capability growth is interpreted as inevitable progress;
6. governance expansion is read as original 1977 intent;
7. portability produces imitation rather than structural comparison;
8. OFF / Return fails to transfer insight beyond the scene;
9. future Aristotle or Mandela VR specs require a different interaction architecture that reveals hidden Maathai assumptions.

---

# 34. Constitutional Decision

**PASS — RETAIN AS MAATHAI VR 2.0 PROJECTION SPECIFICATION.**

This specification is ready for an executable prototype only after a separate implementation build compiles the canonical dossier and public-route profile into tested scene data.
