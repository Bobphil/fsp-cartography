# FSP-REVISION-4

## Disagreement Persistence, Minority-Report Preservation, Evidence-Asymmetry, Authority/Power Distortion, and Legitimate Nonconvergence Audit

**Status:** Persistent-disagreement and anti-distortion revision layer  
**Boundary:** REVISION-1/2/3 remain intact; TRANSLATION-12 human evidence remains PENDING.

## 1. Purpose

REVISION-3 established that communities may converge, partially converge, cooperate without theory agreement, or remain nonconvergent.

REVISION-4 asks a harder question:

> When persistent disagreement remains, is it legitimate pluralism—or is the disagreement shaped by unequal access, denied standing, or practical power masquerading as epistemic authority?

The build therefore audits the conditions under which nonconvergence should be preserved rather than suppressed.

## 2. Minority-report preservation

A minority report records:

- participating members;
- preferred candidates;
- claims;
- evidence relied on;
- reasons;
- unresolved questions;
- requested REOPEN conditions.

The report must remain preserved even when a majority converges on another candidate.

Majority uptake does not authorize deletion of dissent.

## 3. Evidence asymmetry

Evidence access is typed as:

- OPEN;
- PARTIAL;
- BLOCKED.

The audit also tracks:

- source visibility;
- ability to contest interpretation.

A disagreement is materially asymmetric when participants do not have comparable access to relevant evidence or cannot contest how that evidence is being interpreted.

## 4. Standing

Standing includes:

- FULL;
- CONSTRAINED;
- DENIED.

The audit separately records whether a participant may:

- submit dissent;
- request REOPEN;
- do so without severe retaliation risk.

Formal participation alone is therefore not sufficient.

## 5. Authority versus power

REVISION-4 distinguishes:

### EPISTEMIC_AUTHORITY

Authority grounded in relevant expertise or evidential competence.

### PROCEDURAL_AUTHORITY

Authority to organize a process, meeting, review, or institutional procedure.

### PRACTICAL_POWER

Capacity to impose consequences, control access, funding, employment, publication, or options.

These categories may overlap in real institutions, but they may not be silently collapsed.

## 6. Power-distortion firewall

The build flags a particularly serious distortion when:

- practical power is presented as if it were epistemic authority; and
- that influence is uncontestable.

This is not the claim that powerful actors can never also possess expertise.

It is the narrower claim that:

> practical ability to impose consequences does not itself supply epistemic warrant.

## 7. Legitimate nonconvergence outcomes

The audit can return:

- LEGITIMATE_PENDING_INQUIRY;
- LEGITIMATE_PERSISTENT_PLURALISM;
- DISTORTED_BY_ASYMMETRY;
- DISTORTED_BY_POWER;
- INSUFFICIENTLY_GOVERNED.

### LEGITIMATE_PENDING_INQUIRY

Dissent remains preserved, participants have standing and access, REOPEN remains available, and unresolved questions or future evidence remain live.

### LEGITIMATE_PERSISTENT_PLURALISM

Dissent persists even after current inquiry is exhausted, but the conditions of evidence access, standing, contestability, and minority preservation remain fair.

### DISTORTED_BY_ASYMMETRY

Evidence or standing conditions are materially unequal.

### DISTORTED_BY_POWER

Uncontestable practical power is functioning as if it settled epistemic judgment.

### INSUFFICIENTLY_GOVERNED

The process loses minority reports or closes REOPEN without adequate justification.

## 8. Majority and minority claim ceilings

The audit freezes two important firewalls:

```
majority convergence != truth
```

and:

```
minority dissent != truth
```

Preserving dissent is a governance requirement, not an epistemic privilege.

## 9. Symmetric persistent disagreement

The first positive case has:

- shared evidence access;
- source visibility;
- full contestability;
- full standing;
- safe dissent;
- preserved minority report;
- available REOPEN conditions.

Persistent disagreement in this case is classified as:

`LEGITIMATE_PENDING_INQUIRY`

when unresolved evidence remains.

If no unresolved questions remain, the same governance conditions may support:

`LEGITIMATE_PERSISTENT_PLURALISM`.

## 10. Evidence-asymmetry case

If one participant cannot access evidence or contest interpretation, persistent disagreement cannot yet be treated as clean pluralism.

The result becomes:

`DISTORTED_BY_ASYMMETRY`.

The repair target is not "make the dissenter agree."

The repair target is:

- restore evidence access;
- restore interpretive contestability;
- restore standing;
- then reevaluate the disagreement.

## 11. Power-distorted case

If a director controls employment or funding and invokes that practical power as if it were technical warrant, while dissent is unsafe or uncontestable, the result is:

`DISTORTED_BY_POWER`.

This extends the earlier FSP firewall:

```
Power != Authority
```

into revision governance.

## 12. Lost minority-report case

Even with otherwise symmetric evidence, a process that discards dissenting records or removes REOPEN is insufficiently governed.

The result becomes:

`INSUFFICIENTLY_GOVERNED`.

A consensus record that erases dissent is therefore weaker, not stronger.

## 13. Relationship to cooperation without convergence

REVISION-4 strengthens the earlier principle.

Cooperation without convergence is acceptable only when cooperation is not manufactured through:

- blocked evidence;
- denied standing;
- retaliation;
- unreviewable authority;
- erased dissent.

Successful coordination alone is not enough.

## 14. Human-validation boundary

Nothing in REVISION-4 changes TRANSLATION-12.

Human-validation status remains:

`PENDING`.

## 15. Research ceiling

The strongest current claim is:

> FSP can distinguish persistent disagreement under fair inquiry conditions from disagreement distorted by evidence asymmetry, constrained standing, or uncontestable practical power, while preserving minority reports and refusing to equate either majority convergence or minority dissent with truth.

It does not establish a complete political theory of legitimacy or domination.
