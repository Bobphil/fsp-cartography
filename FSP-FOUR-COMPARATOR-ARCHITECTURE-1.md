# FSP-FOUR-COMPARATOR-ARCHITECTURE-1

## Simon, Brandom, Price, Ismael: Bounded Complexity, Inferential Normativity, Pragmatic Vocabulary, Situated Agency, and FSP Transition Governance

**Status:** ADVERSARIAL COMPARATOR SYNTHESIS — VERSION 1  
**Parents:** FSP-SIMON-COMPARATOR-1; FSP-BRANDOM-INFERENTIAL-GOVERNANCE-1; FSP-PRICE-ISMAEL-SITUATED-PRAGMATISM-1; FSP-CONSTITUTIONAL-COMPRESSION-1; FSP-RECOMPOSITION-GOVERNANCE-1  
**Comparators:** Herbert Simon; Robert Brandom; Huw Price; Jenann Ismael  
**Primary purpose:** Test whether the four comparators are genuinely complementary, where they conflict, whether FSP merely combines them or contributes a distinct governance architecture, whether any FSP Core element disappears under the comparison, and whether a missing Core element is revealed.  
**Primary claim ceiling:** This build does not claim that Simon, Brandom, Price, and Ismael form one philosophical school; that their positions are mutually consistent; that FSP resolves their disagreements; or that FSP supersedes them. The comparator architecture is diagnostic.

---

# 1. The Four Questions

Each comparator begins from a different pressure.

## Simon

> **How can finite agents and systems cope with complexity?**

Primary resources:

- bounded rationality;
- satisficing;
- selective search;
- design;
- hierarchy;
- near-decomposability.

## Brandom

> **How are discursive commitments and entitlements normatively articulated?**

Primary resources:

- commitment;
- entitlement;
- material inference;
- incompatibility;
- scorekeeping;
- recognition.

## Price

> **Why assume that our vocabularies all represent the world in the same way, and what practical functions do they serve?**

Primary resources:

- subject naturalism;
- anti-uniform representationalism;
- global expressivist pressure;
- vocabulary-function analysis;
- semantic/ontological restraint.

## Ismael

> **How does an embedded, self-locating agent inhabit and act within an objectively described world?**

Primary resources:

- self-location;
- situated perspective;
- observer/agent distinction;
- reflexivity;
- objective–situated reconstruction.

## FSP

> **How should transitions among partial articulations, scales, roles, practices, institutions, judgments, actions, and consequences be governed?**

Primary resources:

- Partial Map;
- Relation;
- Transition;
- License/Warrant;
- Claim Ceiling;
- Authorization;
- Remainder;
- REOPEN;
- Return.

# 2. Why This Must Be Adversarial

A superficial synthesis would assign one layer to each philosopher:

```text
Simon → complexity
Price → language
Brandom → inference
Ismael → agency
FSP → integration
```

That is too easy.

The comparators overlap and conflict.

Examples:

- Simon’s functional abstraction can suppress self-location that Ismael treats as practically decisive.
- Brandom’s inferential articulation can appear too uniform relative to Price’s functional pluralism.
- Price’s anti-representational pressure can appear too deflationary for Ismael’s realist reconstruction of objective/situated relations.
- Ismael’s self-locating perspective can remain underdescribed normatively without Brandomian entitlement.
- Brandomian entitlement can remain underdescribed practically without Simonian bounded search and Ismaelian agency.
- Simonian design can become normatively under-governed without standing and authorization.

The value of the comparison lies in preserving these tensions.

# 4. Simon–Brandom Tension

Simon abstracts toward functional tractability.

Brandom thickens the normative structure of discursive commitments.

A Simonian agent may search effectively without representing its search as a public space of reasons.

A Brandomian participant may possess richly articulated commitments without solving a complex search problem.

Thus:

> **Problem-solving competence ≠ discursive entitlement.**

and:

> **Discursive entitlement ≠ search competence.**

FSP should keep both separate.

---

# 5. Simon–Price Tension

Simon often depends on representations sufficiently stable to support search and design.

Price asks whether philosophers overinterpret such representations as uniformly mirroring worldly structure.

The useful synthesis is:

```text
SIMON:
representation can make search tractable

PRICE:
tractability does not determine semantic ontology
```

FSP therefore preserves:

> **Useful problem representation ≠ final representational metaphysics.**

This strengthens Partial Map.

---

# 6. Simon–Ismael Tension

Simon’s bounded agent is situated in an environment, but many formal problem-solving treatments can abstract away from the agent’s self-location.

Ismael makes self-location structurally explicit.

Thus:

```text
SIMON:
What states are reachable?

ISMAEL:
Reachable from where, by whom, as which agent?
```

FSP gains:

> **Global possibility ≠ reachable-from-here possibility.**

This refines Transformation Architecture without adding Core.

---

# 7. Brandom–Price Tension

This is one of the most important conflicts.

Brandom gives inferential role a deep explanatory function in conceptual content.

Price resists assuming that all vocabularies share one representational or even one functional structure.

Thus:

```text
BRANDOM:
What follows from the commitment?

PRICE:
What is this vocabulary doing in the first place?
```

FSP should not resolve this by choosing one.

Instead:

> **Vocabulary function may determine which inferential analysis is relevant.**

This puts function prior to inferential audit when practice type is unclear.

---

# 8. Brandom–Ismael Tension

Brandom begins with discursive participants capable of normative scorekeeping.

Ismael begins with physically situated agents whose perspective includes self-location and possible action.

The tension is:

> **Discursive subject ≠ complete situated agent.**

An agent can be:

- perceptually embedded;
- self-locating;
- practically capable;

without yet participating in Brandomian discursive scorekeeping.

FSP therefore preserves:

```text
situated agency
→ possible discursive articulation
```

rather than identifying the two.

---

# 9. Price–Ismael Tension

Price pushes toward pragmatic explanation of vocabulary and away from premature ontology.

Ismael often seeks realist reconstruction of situated agency within objective physical description.

This tension is productive.

Price warns:

> Do not infer ontology from vocabulary use.

Ismael warns:

> Do not treat perspectival structure as mere linguistic convenience when it reflects real relations between agent and world.

FSP therefore adopts:

> **Pragmatic dependence ≠ ontological nonexistence.**

and:

> **Real structure ≠ one privileged vocabulary.**

This may be one of the deepest joint lessons.

---

# 10. Price–Brandom–Ismael Sequence

A useful but nonmandatory order is:

```text
ISMAEL:
Where is the agent and what can it access/do?

PRICE:
What function does this vocabulary/practice serve?

BRANDOM:
What commitments, entitlements, and incompatibilities structure the discourse?

FSP:
What survives when the result crosses a boundary?
```

This is not a psychological sequence.

It is a diagnostic decomposition.

# 12. Does FSP Merely Combine the Four?

This is the decisive test.

If FSP were only:

```text
Simon + Brandom + Price + Ismael
```

then the constitutional Core should disappear when the four are jointly applied.

It does not.

Several FSP functions remain underowned by the comparator set.

---

# 13. FSP Distinctive Function A — Boundary Governance

The four comparators help explain:

- complexity;
- inference;
- vocabulary;
- situated agency.

But none centrally owns the general question:

> **What license survives when a locally warranted result crosses into a different target, scale, role, institution, representation, or action context?**

This is FSP’s recomposition problem.

Thus:

> **Boundary portability remains a distinct FSP governance function.**

---

# 14. FSP Distinctive Function B — Claim Ceilings

Brandomian entitlement does not by itself specify how far a result may travel.

Pricean function does not specify the maximum inferential strength of a claim.

Simon’s satisficing threshold is not an epistemic ceiling.

Ismael’s self-location does not bound inference.

Therefore:

> **Claim Ceiling survives four-comparator ablation.**

It remains Core.

---

# 15. FSP Distinctive Function C — Authorization Separation

None of the four comparators, taken together, automatically yields:

```text
supported judgment
≠
authorized intervention
```

Brandom approaches action normatively, but FSP’s explicit separation among:

- epistemic standing;
- institutional authority;
- moral standing;
- consent;
- practical power;

remains independently useful.

Therefore:

> **Authorization separation survives.**

---

# 16. FSP Distinctive Function D — Remainder

Each comparator has forms of incompleteness:

- Simon: bounded search;
- Brandom: unentitled or unarticulated commitments;
- Price: semantic/metaphysical restraint;
- Ismael: perspective reconstruction.

But FSP’s explicit rule:

> **What did not cross must remain visible**

is not reducible to any one of them.

Remainder can be:

- evidential;
- translational;
- phenomenal;
- normative;
- institutional;
- historical.

Therefore:

> **Remainder survives.**

---

# 17. FSP Distinctive Function E — Return

Simon has feedback and adaptation.

Brandom has revision of normative statuses.

Price has practice/vocabulary revision.

Ismael has re-location.

FSP combines these into:

> **Consequential re-entry into changed lived Doing.**

Return tracks:

- consequences;
- changed capability;
- changed maps;
- changed self-location;
- changed institutions;
- repair;
- REOPEN.

This integrated role remains distinctive.

---

# 18. FSP Distinctive Function F — Failure Routing

The comparator set does not itself provide the FSP architecture:

```text
symptom
→ failure mechanism
→ Tool
→ Firewall
→ claim ceiling
→ repair
→ REOPEN
```

This is a governance/diagnostic contribution.

It remains FSP-specific.

# 20. Does the Four-Way Comparison Reveal a Missing Core Primitive?

Candidate additions:

- Function;
- Self-location;
- Perspective;
- Decomposition;
- Entitlement.

Each fails constitutional promotion.

## Function

Important under Price, but not every transition is best governed by vocabulary function.

**Necessary decompression, not Core.**

## Self-location

Crucial when reachability depends on agent position.

Not universal to every inquiry transition.

**Necessary decompression.**

## Perspective

Too broad unless typed.

Already reconstructible through Partial Map + agent relation.

**No Core promotion.**

## Decomposition

Central to Simon but not required as explicit object in every inquiry.

**Meta-method / decompression.**

## Entitlement

Central to Brandomian discursive transition but too narrow for all licenses.

**Warrant subtype.**

### Verdict

> **No missing Core primitive is revealed.**

## Problem: Metaphysical inflation from vocabulary

Route first to **Price**.

Route first to **Ismael**.

- who may act?
- what remains?
- what returns?

# 31. Price Prevents Brandom Overreach

Freeze this comparator-level rule:

> **Do not assume inferential articulation exhausts vocabulary function.**

Before deep Brandomian audit, ask whether the vocabulary’s role is:

- expressive;
- coordinative;
- predictive;
- classificatory;
- deliberative;
- representational;
- mixed.

This is a direct Price correction.

---

# 32. Ismael Prevents Price Over-Deflation

Freeze:

> **Pragmatic dependence ≠ mere linguistic projection.**

A vocabulary may matter pragmatically because agents occupy real relations of access, control, and self-location.

This is a direct Ismael correction.

---

# 33. Brandom Prevents Price Under-Normativity

Freeze:

> **Vocabulary function alone does not determine entitlement.**

Once a practice contains assertions and commitments, inferential entitlement matters.

This is a Brandom correction.

---

# 34. Simon Prevents Discursive Over-Intellectualization

Freeze:

> **Intelligent coping need not require explicit discursive scorekeeping.**

Bounded search, habit, heuristic action, and environmental structure may precede or bypass explicit reasons.

This is a Simon correction to an overly discursive architecture.

---

# 35. Ismael Prevents Simonian View-from-Nowhere Search

Freeze:

> **Search space ≠ reachable-from-here space.**

A formal state space may contain possibilities unavailable to this agent from its current location, access, and capability.

# 36. FSP Prevents Comparator Export

The central FSP rule is:

> **Success inside one comparator does not authorize export into another domain without renewed warrant.**

Examples:

- Simonian functional success ≠ Brandomian entitlement.
- Brandomian entitlement ≠ Ismaelian agency.
- Pricean vocabulary utility ≠ ontological nonexistence.
- Ismaelian self-location ≠ moral standing.

This is recomposition governance applied to philosophy itself.

# 49. Is FSP a Metatheory?

The comparison raises a danger.

If FSP merely routes among other theories, it may become only a metatheory.

The answer must be bounded.

FSP is meta-governance in one sense:

- it governs transitions among maps and claims.

But it also contains substantive commitments:

- maps are partial;
- warrant does not automatically travel;
- judgment does not automatically authorize action;
- remainder must remain visible;
- consequences must return;
- settlements remain reopenable.

Thus:

> **FSP is not ontologically thick, but it is normatively and procedurally non-neutral.**

This is an important self-description.

---

# 50. FSP’s Distinctive Thesis

After the four-comparator ablation, the strongest FSP thesis is:

> **Situated agents must act through partial articulations whose local success does not automatically license their export across changed targets, scales, vocabularies, perspectives, institutions, or action contexts; responsible inquiry therefore requires explicit transition governance, bounded claims, authorization separation, visible remainder, and consequential Return.**

None of the four comparators alone supplies this complete governance claim.

> **Comparator set ≠ mandatory workflow.**
