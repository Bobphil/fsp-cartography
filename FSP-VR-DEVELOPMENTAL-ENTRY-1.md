# FSP-VR-DEVELOPMENTAL-ENTRY-1

## Minimal Doing; Embodied Control; Other Agents; Dependency; Early Social Evaluation; Similarity and Difference; Helping/Hindering; Proto-Normative Response; Replication and Claim Ceilings; Cultural Formation; Situated Development; and Transition to the Life Lottery

**Status:** NON-DEPLOYED DEVELOPMENTAL FRONT-DOOR PROTOTYPE — VERSION 1  
**Parents:** FSP-VR-VISUAL-GRAMMAR-1; FSP-VR-TEMPORAL-LENS-1; FSP-VR-MAATHAI-FIELD-3  
**Primary purpose:** Let a framework-naive learner encounter FSP first as situated Doing rather than as philosophical vocabulary.

# 1. Governing Principle

The learner begins before explicit FSP terminology.

The experience should disclose, in sequence:

**EVENT → ACTION → CONTROL → RESISTANCE → OTHER AGENT → DEPENDENCE → CONSEQUENCE → EXPECTATION → SOCIAL EVALUATION → CULTURAL FORMATION → SITUATED LIFE**

The learner encounters the problem before the name of the tool.

> **Experience first; vocabulary later.**

# 2. Pedagogical Zero

The opening blank field is a pedagogical device, not a metaphysical claim that the learner exists nowhere.

Initial sequence:

1. Something happens.
2. Some changes covary with action.
3. Some things resist control.
4. Another agent appears.
5. The learner depends on responses they do not control.

Only later may the learner revisit what the opening already presupposed.

# 3. Minimal Doing

The first interactive layer uses only:

- action;
- consequence;
- recurrence;
- failure;
- uncertainty.

No moral vocabulary appears.

The learner discovers:

> **some changes are connected to what I do.**

Claim ceiling:

> This does not establish a mature concept of self, agency, causation, or free will.

# 4. Embodied Control

A hand-like body part or equivalent controllable element is introduced.

The learner can:
- initiate movement;
- observe regular covariance;
- discover that not everything in the field responds the same way.

Early distinction:

**CONTROLLED CHANGE ≠ UNCONTROLLED CHANGE**

Later FSP vocabulary:
- capability;
- constraint;
- agent locus;
- relation.

# 5. Other Agents

A second agent responds independently.

The learner discovers:

**MY DOING ≠ OTHER DOING**

The other agent:
- sometimes responds;
- sometimes does not;
- has independent movement;
- cannot be treated as an extension of the learner.

This is the first experiential preparation for Connected Doing.

# 6. Dependency

The learner encounters states such as:
- discomfort;
- hunger-like need;
- warmth/care;
- delayed response.

A signaling action may alter the probability of caregiver response.

The learner discovers:

> **my action can matter without giving me control.**

This establishes a primitive distinction among:
- action;
- influence;
- dependence;
- uncertainty.

# 7. Consequence and Expectation

Repeated action-consequence relations allow expectations to form.

The environment occasionally violates expectation.

This prepares:

**REGULARITY ≠ NECESSITY**

and later:
- induction;
- uncertainty;
- model revision;
- REOPEN.

# 8. Early Social Evaluation

The learner is shown simplified third-party interactions involving help and hindrance.

The system does not announce a correct moral interpretation.

Instead, after the learner reacts, an evidence layer may reveal:

## Early evidence map

### Hamlin, Wynn & Bloom (2007)
Reported that 6- and 10-month-old infants preferred a helper over a hinderer in a hill-climbing paradigm.

### Later evidence
A large preregistered ManyBabies replication tested 1,018 infants across 37 laboratories on five continents; among included infants, helper preference in the social condition did not differ from chance.

### 2026 meta-analysis / replication work
In the box-opening paradigm, a meta-analysis across 15 experiments estimated an overall helper-choice proportion near 58%, with heterogeneity and larger effects concentrated in earlier North American work.

Claim ceiling:

> The evidence does not establish that infants possess adult morality, explicit moral concepts, or stable judgments of good and evil.

# 9. Developmental Evidence Grammar

Developmental claims use an explicit evidence object:

**OBSERVATION**
What the infant or child did.

**INTERPRETATION**
What researchers propose the behavior indicates.

**ALTERNATIVE**
Other plausible explanations.

**REPLICATION**
How reliably the effect has reproduced.

**CLAIM CEILING**
What does not yet follow.

Evidence classes may include:
- behavioral experiment;
- looking-time paradigm;
- reaching / choice paradigm;
- observational study;
- caregiver report;
- longitudinal study;
- cross-cultural replication;
- multi-lab replication;
- developmental neuroscience.

# 10. Similarity and Difference

A learner encounters agents who share or differ in trivial preferences.

Evidence layer:

- Mahajan & Wynn (2012) reported that preverbal infants preferred others who shared certain preferences with them.
- Hamlin et al. (2013) reported that 9- and 14-month-olds sometimes preferred agents who treated similar others positively and dissimilar others negatively.

Firewall:

> **early preference ≠ moral endorsement**
>
> **similarity attraction ≠ justified exclusion**
>
> **observed tendency ≠ immutable trait**

# 11. Proto-Normative Response

The learner experiences:
- approval;
- disapproval;
- reward;
- withdrawal;
- social response.

Do not collapse these into morality.

Freeze:

**SOCIAL APPROVAL ≠ MORAL JUSTIFICATION**

and:

**PREDISPOSITION ≠ NORM ≠ REASON ≠ JUSTIFICATION ≠ AUTHORIZATION**

# 12. Cultural Formation

The agent develops within recursive interaction among:

- caregiver response;
- imitation;
- language;
- reward/punishment;
- family norms;
- peer groups;
- religion or its absence;
- institutions;
- gender expectations;
- identity formation;
- education;
- media;
- historical conditions.

No single layer owns the person.

> **BIOLOGY ≠ DESTINY**
>
> **CULTURE ≠ TOTAL EXPLANATION**
>
> **PERSON ≠ DOSSIER**

# 13. Situated Development

The learner now sees that capability changes over time.

New paths open because:
- language develops;
- mobility develops;
- relationships accumulate;
- institutions become accessible;
- roles become available;
- social standing changes.

Other paths close or become harder.

This is the first developmental use of temporal breathing.

# 14. Transition to the Life Lottery

After the developmental sequence:

> **Every human Doing develops somewhere.**
>
> **You do not choose where you begin.**

Then transition to:

**FSP-VR-SITUATED-ENTRY / LIFE LOTTERY**

The developmental entry supplies the learner with an experiential grammar for:
- body;
- relation;
- other agents;
- capability;
- dependency;
- consequence;
- uncertainty;
- norm formation;
- culture;
- time.

# 15. Progressive Vocabulary Reveal

Do not begin with FSP terms.

Reveal terms only after the learner has encountered their function.

Example:

experience:
> some things move when I act.

later label:
> capability.

experience:
> another person does not move as I command.

later label:
> independent agent.

experience:
> I expect a response and it fails.

later label:
> model revision / REOPEN.

experience:
> researchers disagree about what infant choice means.

later label:
> claim ceiling / alternative explanation / replication.

# 18. Global Firewalls

- Doing ≠ reflective theory of Doing.
- Control ≠ total control.
- Influence ≠ authority.
- Regularity ≠ necessity.
- Preference ≠ justification.
- Social approval ≠ moral justification.
- Similarity preference ≠ legitimate exclusion.
- Population tendency ≠ individual destiny.
- Early developmental effect ≠ adult moral faculty.
- Experimental observation ≠ interpretation.
- Initial study ≠ settled result.
- Replication failure ≠ proof of the opposite.
- Culture ≠ complete explanation.
- Biology ≠ destiny.

# 19. Claim Ceiling

This prototype does not claim to recreate infant phenomenology.

It is a pedagogical reconstruction of increasingly rich situated Doing.

It uses developmental research to constrain and interrogate the map, not to claim direct access to infant experience.
