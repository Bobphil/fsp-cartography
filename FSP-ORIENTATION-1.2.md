# FSP-ORIENTATION-1.2

## Phenomenal Doing, Existential Inquiry, Human-Centered Inquiry Relation, Explanation/Meaning Firewall, and Complexity-System Comparator Boundary

### Status

Constitutional refinement of FSP-ORIENTATION-1.1.

This build does not create a new existential doctrine and does not replace domain-native evidence. It clarifies why a full-stack inquiry architecture can legitimately address deeply personal questions while remaining answerable to public reasons, evidence, other persons, and domain-specific knowledge.

## 1. Phenomenal Doing as inquiry origin

Human inquiry begins before a proposition is fully formed.

A person is already living, acting, perceiving, remembering, anticipating, caring, relating, and inhabiting institutions and environments. Something within that ongoing Doing becomes salient enough to notice. It may appear as surprise, conflict, suffering, wonder, uncertainty, desire, breakdown, beauty, fear, contradiction, or a question that will not go away.

FSP calls attention to this **phenomenal Doing** because inquiry requires a point of entry. The first-person field is where an issue acquires salience and stakes.

This does not make first-person experience infallible. Phenomenology-first means first in inquiry order, not supreme in evidential authority.

The inquiry may have to move far beyond the person's first account through evidence, other perspectives, history, science, institutional knowledge, conceptual revision, and public criticism.

## 2. Existential inquiry as a full-stack case

Questions such as **Why am I here?**, **What is the meaning of my life?**, **What am I?**, **What should I become?**, and **What may I carry forward?** are not exceptions to the FSP architecture. They are especially demanding instances of it.

They begin in lived Doing, but may expand through cosmology, biology, neuroscience, psychology, history, culture, relationships, institutions, ethics, religion, and philosophy.

No one domain automatically owns the whole question.

FSP therefore governs how contributions from different domains enter the inquiry, what they license, what they leave open, and how the inquiry eventually returns to the person.

## 3. The human being at the center of the inquiry relation

FSP is human-centered in a specific and limited sense.

The human being remains at the center of the **inquiry relation** because salience, questioning, interpretation, judgment, authorization, responsibility, consequence, and authorship are lived by persons.

This is not anthropocentric cosmology.

> **Phenomenology-first does not mean human-centered cosmology. It means that human inquiry must begin from and eventually return to the situated conditions under which anything becomes a question for a human being.**

A cosmological theory may locate human beings in the universe. A biological theory may explain evolutionary inheritance. A neuroscience model may illuminate mechanisms of experience. A social-system model may expose structures shaping action.

None of those maps is identical with the person whose life is being lived.

**Map ≠ World ≠ Person.**

## 4. Explanation/Meaning firewall

FSP now freezes two additional firewalls:

> **Explanation of the conditions that produced a person ≠ authorship of that person's meaning.**

> **Causal location in a system ≠ existential location within a life.**

A highly successful causal explanation may constrain an existential inquiry. It may overturn cherished assumptions or reveal conditions the person did not previously understand.

But explaining how a person arose does not by itself decide what that person should value, which relationships matter, what commitments should be owned, or how a finite life should be lived.

This is not permission for arbitrary private meaning. Authorial Return remains answerable to evidence, reasons, consequences, other persons, and legitimate standing.

## 5. Three distinguishable questions

**World-generative:** What structures, patterns, or possibilities can arise from these rules, interactions, histories, or constraints?

**Inquiry-generative:** How does a bounded human being come to notice, represent, test, interpret, and understand some part of that world?

**Authorial:** What may this person responsibly make of that understanding in the course of a life?

FSP prevents success at one level from silently becoming authority at another.

## 6. Complexity-system comparator boundary

FSP can make extensive use of complexity science, self-organizing systems, computational models, generative rule spaces, emergence, networks, and related approaches.

Such approaches may be exceptionally powerful at the world-generative level: explaining how complex patterns arise, persist, transform, or become possible.

FSP is not a rival universal complexity ontology. Its distinctive concern is the governed trajectory of situated inquiry through a world that may itself be complex.

> **World-generative explanation ≠ situated inquiry reconstruction ≠ Authorial Return.**

The layers can inform one another. They cannot be silently collapsed.

## 7. Reconciliation with FSP-ORIENTATION-1.1

The 1.1 information-transformation arc remains unchanged:

```text
Lived Doing → Noticing → Attention → Mapping → Inquiry → Representation → Evidence & Interpretation → Revision → Possibility → Judgment → Authorization → Action → Consequence → Authorial Return → Changed Lived Doing → REOPEN
```

Version 1.2 clarifies the first and returning terms. **Lived Doing** is not merely an input container; **Authorial Return** is not merely output delivery.

## 8. Claim ceiling

FSP-ORIENTATION-1.2 does not claim that first-person experience is incorrigible, that human beings are the metaphysical center of reality, that existential questions have one universal answer, that scientific explanation is irrelevant to meaning, that meaning is unconstrained private invention, that complexity or generative-system approaches are mistaken, or that FSP supplies a rival fundamental physics, cosmology, or universal ontology.

It claims that a philosophy of full-stack human inquiry must preserve the relations among phenomenal entry, public inquiry, explanatory expansion, bounded judgment, action, consequence, and Authorial Return.
