# FSP-VR 2.0

## Experiential Decompression, Historical Transformation, and Authorial Return

**Status:** SOURCE-REVIEWED RESEARCH ORIENTATION — NOT PUBLIC-READY  
**Research regions:** Governance and Translation; Situated Agency and Transformation; Mind, Perception, and Phenomenology.

Virtual reality is often described as immersive because it surrounds the learner with a spatially organized environment. FSP-VR begins from a narrower and more testable proposition: immersive environments may be useful when a philosophical problem depends on relations that ordinary prose or working memory compress too aggressively.

The proposed role of VR is therefore not to become the canonical center of Full-Stack Philosophy and not to simulate another person's true mind. It is to act as a projection and cognitive prosthesis: selectively decompress relations among evidence, provenance, perspective, institutional structure, possibility, capability, consequence, and uncertainty, then return the learner to ordinary lived judgment.

The governing cycle is:

**LIVED COMPRESSION → VR DECOMPRESSION → GOVERNED INSPECTION → AUTHORIAL RECOMPRESSION → RETURN TO LIVED DOING**

Recent systematic reviews of immersive learning report promising effects in some educational contexts, particularly where active manipulation, constructive activity, or procedural transfer matter. But the literature also reports heterogeneous designs, outcomes, cognitive-load effects, and boundary conditions. FSP-VR therefore does not infer that immersion itself improves critical thinking.

Its research hypothesis is more specific:

> A carefully designed immersive projection may improve selected forms of relational inspection when the target learning problem is difficult because important relations are compressed, temporally separated, spatially distributed, or hidden behind a single narrative perspective.

That hypothesis must be tested.

Lives in Transformation provides the strongest first vertical slice because it makes several VR-specific risks and opportunities explicit. The learner can enter an earlier historical state with later outcomes hidden, inspect what possibilities and constraints were available then, make a bounded prediction, and only afterward reveal the historical consequence.

This creates a hindsight-control architecture:

**historical coherence after the fact ≠ earlier predictability.**

The historical person remains outside the simulation. VR may display public evidence, testimony, reconstructed institutional context, and source provenance. It may not claim to display the person's true first-person experience or private motive.

Thus:

**VR reconstruction ≠ person**  
**perspective reconstruction ≠ phenomenal possession**

Perspective itself must be decomposed. A camera position is not yet a situated perspective. The environment should distinguish access, self-location, salience, practical capability, institutional standing, and what remains inaccessible.

The most important VR design principle is OFF.

A scene is philosophically incomplete if it ends in immersion. FSP requires:

**IMMERSIVE FIELD → BOUNDED RETURN ARTIFACT → OFF → LIVED WORLD**

The learner must regain authorship.

### Claim ceiling

This orientation does not claim that VR improves critical thinking, empathy, historical judgment, or transfer merely because it is immersive. Current learning research supports a context-sensitive design hypothesis, not a universal benefit. FSP-VR remains a prototype research programme until direct tests of provenance comprehension, hindsight control, structural transfer, overidentification, and Authorial Return are completed.

### Research frontier

The first prototype should test whether a Maathai Lives-in-Transformation slice can preserve provenance and hindsight control while helping a learner identify one structural relation without converting admiration into recommendation.
