## Certified adversarial role

Galileo is the second whole-inquiry narrative spine and the first case used to test whether the contract survives a coupled epistemic/institutional history.

Unlike Neptune/Vulcan, the central problem cannot be represented as a comparatively clean anomaly/repair trajectory.

The Galileo spine must preserve interacting but non-identical changes in:

- evidence;
- instrument standing;
- conceptual meaning;
- standards of demonstration;
- interpretive jurisdiction;
- institutional authority;
- practical power.

## Whole inquiry narrative

The public narrative carries the reader through:

- the inherited early-modern cosmological field;
- 1609–1610 telescope work and `Sidereus Nuncius`;
- lunar observations and the Jovian satellites;
- the phases of Venus and changing constraints on cosmological systems;
- 1613–1616 disputes over demonstration, scriptural interpretation, and jurisdiction;
- the 1632 `Dialogue`;
- the 1633 trial, condemnation, and house arrest.

The narrative explicitly blocks both:

1. `new telescope evidence = complete proof of the entire Copernican system`;
2. `institutional condemnation = epistemic proof of geocentrism`.

## Four apertures

### Chapter 1

Evidence constrains the theory space without uniquely selecting a complete cosmology.

### Chapter 2

Historically situated terms such as `demonstration`, `hypothesis`, `motion`, `heavens`, and `authority` can change inferential and institutional role.

### Chapter 3

Controlled probes may vary cosmological accommodation or standing while holding historical evidence fixed.

### Chapter 4

The formalism tracks evidence, meaning, standing, jurisdiction, and power as coupled but distinct dimensions.

## Temporal anchoring

The spine includes explicit milestones for:

- 1543–1609 inherited field;

- 1610–1613 Venus phases and widening Copernican support;
- 1613–1616 interpretation / demonstration / jurisdiction;

The same temporal firewall remains certified:

## Adversarial firewalls

The Galileo extension certifies:

- instrument standing ≠ theory finality;
- interpretive jurisdiction ≠ epistemic truth;
- institutional condemnation ≠ empirical demonstration;
- practical power can constrain inquiry without becoming evidential warrant;
- probe possibility ≠ historical actuality;
- historical chronology ≠ canonical state identity.

## Claim ceiling

This certification shows that the whole-inquiry narrative architecture survives a case where evidential, semantic, jurisdictional, and power dimensions change together.

It does not establish a final historiography of Galileo.

Interpretive disputes about the 1616 injunction, Galileo's proof claims, institutional motives, political context, and the balance among scientific and theological factors remain REOPENable.

The successful regression establishes architecture portability, not historical closure.
