# Perception, Agency, Intelligence Across Scales, and Ethical Futures

## A Full-Stack Philosophy Orientation to Minds, Living Systems, Transformation, and Intervention

**Status:** SOURCE-REVIEWED RESEARCH ORIENTATION — NOT PUBLIC-READY  
**Research regions:** Mind, Perception, and Phenomenology; Intelligence Across Scales; Situated Agency and Transformation; Science, Models, and Interoperability.

A human being encounters a world from somewhere. Perception is shaped by sensory input, prior organization, bodily condition, action, attention, and history. Predictive-processing approaches make this especially vivid: perception is modeled not as passive copying but as structured inference in which incoming signals and prior expectations interact. FSP does not require one predictive-processing theory to be final. It uses the research to sharpen a more general point: an organism's public environment, its accessible information, its practical possibilities, and its lived significance are not identical.

Hence:

**same public world ≠ same access ≠ same salience ≠ same action possibilities ≠ same situated significance.**

This connects perception to agency. An organism is not outside the domain it models. It acts within that domain, changes its access through movement, and depends on selective representations because exhaustive representation is impossible.

The same issue appears across biological scales. Research associated with Michael Levin and collaborators asks how cells and tissues coordinate toward anatomical and functional outcomes and how biological systems respond to perturbation. Planarian work is particularly valuable because it permits explicit experimental study of regeneration, adaptation, and memory. Shomrat and Levin reported behavioral evidence consistent with memory retrieval after head regeneration, while later work on barium-chloride exposure showed that planarians can regenerate heads adapted to a previously damaging electrochemical environment. These results are scientifically striking, but FSP resists an immediate leap from adaptive competence to a preferred ontology of mind.

The relevant research question is:

> Which structural relations genuinely travel when we compare problem-solving, memory, adaptation, goal-directedness, and intelligence across biological and artificial systems?

This demands decompression of the word *intelligence*. Information processing, flexible adaptation, target-state regulation, memory, search, representation, consciousness, and moral standing are not one property.

Thus:

**problem-solving ≠ consciousness**  
**goal-directedness ≠ human-like thought**  
**intelligence ≠ moral standing**  
**analogy ≠ identity**

Synthetic multicellular systems such as Xenobots intensify this issue. Their construction involves computational design, biological tissue, human assembly, emergent behavior, and environmental interaction. FSP treats this as a model case of heterogeneous inquiry because different explanatory maps are necessary and their relations must be governed.

The same architecture applies to AI. Artificial systems may navigate high-dimensional problem spaces, learn statistical structure, generate outputs, and participate in distributed decisions. But **representation ≠ self-location ≠ agency ≠ standing ≠ authorization**. Impressive performance in one task cannot settle every question about intelligence, understanding, consciousness, or moral status.

Objective Phenomenology adds another necessary firewall. Behavioral sophistication, ecological fit, neural organization, or computational reconstruction may constrain hypotheses about another system's experience, but they do not amount to possession of that experience. FSP therefore treats cross-species and cross-system consciousness research as progressive constraint with explicit phenomenal remainder.

The ethical stakes arise when understanding becomes intervention. Regenerative medicine, synthetic biology, neural augmentation, AI, and human enhancement create new capabilities. But **capability ≠ authorization**. The existence of an intervention does not determine who may use it, who bears its risks, whether consent is adequate, or how the intervention changes institutions and future possibility structures.

The Four-Move surface organizes this research:

**PORTABILITY:** what travels across level, organism, model, or substrate?  
**CEILING:** what does the evidence actually establish?  
**AUTHORIZATION:** who may intervene, redesign, deploy, or decide?  
**RETURN:** what happens after intervention?  
**REMAINDER:** what cannot yet be translated or settled?

### Claim ceiling

This orientation does not establish one unified theory of perception, intelligence, consciousness, biological agency, or artificial agency. It does not infer consciousness from adaptation or moral standing from intelligence. It treats these as connected but non-identical research targets.

### Research frontier

Key questions include how assembly history changes future capability, how multiscale biological regulation should be represented, which structural comparisons between biological and artificial intelligence are warranted, how objective phenomenology can constrain experience without claiming first-person equivalence, and how emerging interventions should be governed.
