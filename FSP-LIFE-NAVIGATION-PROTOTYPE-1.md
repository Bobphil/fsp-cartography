# FSP-LIFE-NAVIGATION-PROTOTYPE-1

## Student-Facing Prototype for HERE → THERE Navigation, Three-Future Role Play, Transformation Stack Diagnosis, Capability Paths, Milestones and Warrants, Assumption Ledgers, Weekly Return, Advice Portability, Beauty/Awe/Matters Reflection, and Instructor Guardrails

**Status:** STUDENT-FACING PROTOTYPE — NOT YET CLASSROOM-CERTIFIED  
**Parent:** FSP-SITUATED-LIFE-NAVIGATION-1  
**Inherited authorities:** FSP-TRANSFORMATION-ARCHITECTURE-1; FSP-FOUR-MOVE-GOVERNANCE-1; FSP-CLASS-BRIDGE-1; FSP-PROJECTION-PROFILES-1  
**Primary purpose:** Test whether the situated-life-navigation architecture can be compressed into a usable student/individual workflow without creating excessive burden, invading privacy, prescribing life goals, or displacing domain experts.  
**Primary claim ceiling:** This prototype supports structured reflection and planning. It is not therapy, medical advice, financial advice, religious direction, career certification, or a prediction of the student's future.

---

The student should not need to learn the FSP formalism first.

The visible workflow is:


Standing questions:

The deeper FSP architecture remains available to the instructor or advanced student as decompression.

# 2. Student Opening

## A Future You Can Examine

You are not being asked to predict your future.

You are not being asked to choose one permanent identity.

You are being asked to examine a possible future closely enough to discover:

- what would have to change;
- what capabilities would have to develop;
- what assumptions the future depends on;
- what evidence would show real progress;
- what other people or institutions matter;
- what might make you revise the plan.

The goal is not certainty.

The goal is a better map.

---

# 3. Student Firewall

Keep these distinctions visible:

> **My current map of myself ≠ myself.**

> **An imagined future ≠ a predicted future.**

> **A goal ≠ a guarantee.**

> **Confidence ≠ evidence.**

> **A deadline ≠ proof that change occurred.**

> **A plan can be revised without becoming a failure.**

# 30. Instructor Role

The instructor is not being asked to decide:

- which future the student should choose;
- whether a relationship should continue;
- whether a religion is true;
- whether a health treatment is appropriate;
- which investment is best;
- whether the student needs therapy.

The instructor role is:

> **governed inquiry facilitator.**

---

# 31. Instructor Debrief Questions

Ask:

- What did you discover that you had been treating as one problem but was actually several?
- Which part of the future was easiest to imagine but hardest to warrant?
- What capability did you initially overlook?
- What assumption is carrying the most weight?
- What advice did not travel cleanly into your situation?
- What option would you like to preserve?
- What would make you reopen your plan?

Avoid:

- “Which future will you choose?”
- “What is your real problem?”
- “Why have you not fixed this?”
- “What should your life purpose be?”

# 32. Privacy Firewall

Students must not be required to disclose:

- medical diagnoses;
- trauma;
- religious belief;
- sexuality;
- family conflict;
- financial details;
- immigration status;
- criminal history;
- mental-health history;
- other sensitive personal information.

Students may use:

- a fictionalized case;
- a low-stakes personal goal;
- a career or study scenario;
- a hypothetical future.

Assessment should be based on quality of inquiry, not intimacy of disclosure.

---

# 33. Privacy Rule

Freeze:

> **Personal relevance ≠ required personal exposure.**

A student can demonstrate excellent life-navigation reasoning with a low-stakes or fictional case.

---

# 35. High-Stakes Decompression Trigger

The daily heuristic should be decompressed when:

- consequences are difficult to reverse;
- other people bear major costs;
- the student faces medical/legal/financial risk;
- institutional permission is required;
- the evidence is weak;
- multiple values conflict;
- the plan repeatedly fails.

Then open:

- Transformation Stack;
- Assumption Ledger;
- affected-party analysis;
- domain-source check;
- stronger milestones;
- REOPEN.

# 39. Advice-Portability Mini-Case

Case:

A popular productivity speaker says:

> “Successful people eliminate excuses, wake early, and attack their hardest task before breakfast.”

Student asks:

1. What is the advice trying to accomplish?
2. What assumptions does it make about schedule, health, work, family, and chronotype?
3. Which part is a heuristic?
4. Which part is an empirical claim?
5. Which part might be motivational rhetoric?
6. What travels into my situation?
7. What does not?
8. What would count as success?
9. What would make me stop using it?

The point is not to attack the speaker.

The point is to govern portability.

---

# 40. Religious-Practice Mini-Case

Case:

A student says a weekly religious practice gives structure, community, and meaning.

Do not ask the student to prove or disprove the religion.

Ask:

- What function does the practice play?
- What claims about its effects are experiential?
- Which claims are theological?
- What obligations follow for the student?
- Which obligations affect others?
- What authority does the community claim?
- What remains outside the scope of this exercise?

This tests translation without theological reduction.

# 43. Prototype Rubric

Assess reasoning, not life outcome.

## Criterion 1 — Situated description

Can the student distinguish observed current conditions from broad identity judgments?

## Criterion 2 — Transition clarity

Can the student identify what actually has to change?

## Criterion 3 — Warrant

Can the student name evidence appropriate to the transition?

## Criterion 4 — Remainder

Can the student identify a material unresolved assumption?

## Criterion 5 — Return

Can the student name what would make the plan change?

### Suggested scoring

- 0 = missing;
- 1 = present but vague;
- 2 = clear and bounded.

Maximum: 10.

Do not score:

- ambition;
- emotional disclosure;
- chosen career;
- religious belief;
- personal values.

# 59. Strongest Supported Claim

> **A practical student-facing life-navigation prototype can be constructed from existing FSP transformation, capability, four-move governance, and Return architecture without creating new Core primitives. The prototype gives students a compressed HERE → THERE → BRIDGE → TEST → RETURN workflow, with optional decompression into future-role probes, capability paths, milestones, assumption ledgers, affected-party review, advice portability, ritualized Return, and Beauty/Awe/Matters reflection. It preserves self-authorship, privacy, domain authority, and goal revisability while making personal transitions more inspectable.**

---

# 60. REOPEN Conditions

Reopen immediately if:

1. students feel pressured to disclose sensitive information;
2. the exercise feels therapeutic rather than educational;
3. students interpret future scenarios as predictions;
4. instructors begin recommending substantive life goals;
5. milestones become self-worth measures;
6. religious practices are trivialized or endorsed;
7. high-stakes advice is treated as classroom judgment;
8. the stack creates more confusion than insight;
9. the 25-minute compression cannot stand alone;
10. a simpler framework performs equally well.
