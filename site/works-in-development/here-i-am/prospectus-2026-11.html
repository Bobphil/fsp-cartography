FULL-STACK PHILOSOPHY · PROPOSED LITERARY PROJECT

Here I Am: Meditations

Augustine, Descartes, and the development of human understanding

Complete reading copy of the preceding response, with reconstructed embedded vector graphics. November 2026.

I think you have identified a particularly promising direction for the next stage of FSP—and perhaps a literary and philosophical form that has been implicit in our research for some time.

Both Augustine's Confessions and Descartes' Meditations accomplish something quite different from presenting a completed philosophical system. They invite the reader to participate in the unfolding of philosophical understanding from the perspective of a situated individual.

In each case, the development of the investigator becomes part of the philosophical investigation.

Our Here I Am thought experiment, the recovery of your early ORG research, and the subsequent development of Understanding Understanding suggest that we might now construct a contemporary version of this philosophical undertaking.

But ours would have some substantially different characteristics.

What we might develop would combine Augustine's diachronic investigation of a life with Descartes' invitation to undertake philosophical activities oneself.

To those we could add the extraordinary scientific resources that have become available in the intervening centuries.

2. Augustine: a life becomes an object of philosophical inquiry

Augustine's Confessions is particularly relevant because it does not remain simply an autobiography.

In Book X, Augustine turns from recounting his earlier life toward investigating memory and the conditions of self-understanding. In Book XI, he undertakes his extraordinary examination of time, including the relationships among memory, present attention and expectation. His investigations remain oriented toward God and the theological understanding of human existence.

There is a particularly interesting parallel with our developing architecture.

Our contemporary investigator can undertake a journey into their own history that extends considerably beyond what they remember.

I can investigate the development of my organism before I possessed autobiographical memory. I can investigate my evolutionary ancestors. I can investigate the development of the physical universe, biological organization, language and cultural practices.

I can acquire scientific resources that transform my understanding of the conditions through which I became possible.

And I can investigate the intellectual encounters through which those scientific resources became intelligible to me.

This would give us a contemporary form of philosophical autobiography, informed by scientific understanding and explicitly concerned with the continuing development of the individual.

3. Descartes: philosophy as something the reader undertakes

Descartes' Meditations offers a different but complementary possibility.

Descartes did not intend the work simply as an exposition of philosophical arguments. His preface invites readers to undertake the meditative activities themselves. The reader is meant to participate in an exercise of philosophical thinking.

This has a particularly important relationship to our pedagogical interests.

Our objective has never been merely to communicate the intellectual architecture of FSP to other individuals.

It has been to enable them to undertake activities through which they can develop and investigate their own understanding.

There is, however, a fundamental difference between our proposed entrance and Descartes' method.

Descartes systematically suspends beliefs in search of secure foundations.

Our investigator begins by attending to the extraordinary conditions of their present engagement.

Rather than attempting initially to eliminate acquired resources, we seek to make their histories, contributions and limitations increasingly intelligible.

Cartesian movementFSP movement
What can I doubt?What am I presently attending to?

Our methodology would place less emphasis on achieving an epistemically secure foundation from which everything else could be established.

Instead, we would investigate the possibility of increasingly adequate, productive and corrigible engagement with the world.

4. A third important predecessor: Pierre Hadot

I would also bring Pierre Hadot into this discussion, especially his Philosophy as a Way of Life.

His understanding of ancient philosophy as involving exercises through which individuals transform their ways of attending, thinking and living provides an additional precedent.

The philosophical work we are contemplating would not be merely a theory of intellectual development.

It would itself become an instrument of intellectual development.

This is the relationship among philosophical content, philosophical activity and the transformation of the individual that we have been approaching throughout FSP.

6. A possible architecture of twelve meditations

I can envision the work developing through twelve meditations, each beginning within the individual's present engagement and subsequently opening a wider territory of inquiry.

  1. IHere I Am
    I attend to the extraordinary fact of my present existence.
  2. IIHow Extraordinary That I Am Here
    I investigate the cosmic, evolutionary and biological histories through which I became possible.
  3. IIIThe Many That Make Me One
    I investigate the collective intelligence and biological organization enabling my individual capacities.
  4. IVThe World That Becomes Significant to Me
    I explore embodiment, perception, semiosis and my developing relationships with the world.
  5. VHow I Became the Person I Am
    I investigate memory, relationships, culture, education and my particular assembly history.
  6. VIThe Worlds of Other Beings
    I investigate the diverse capacities through which humans and other organisms engage with their worlds.
  7. VIIThe Maps Through Which I Understand
    I examine my languages, theories, assumptions and partial representations.
  8. VIIIWhen My Understanding Changes
    I investigate intellectual encounters, conceptual transformations and newly available competencies.
  9. IXUnderstanding My Understanding
    I turn my acquired intellectual resources upon the conditions of their own development.
  10. XWhat We Can Become Together
    I explore collective intelligence and Joint Task Spaces without surrendering individual perspectives.
  11. XIWhat Is Worth Becoming?
    I investigate the relationship between intelligence, purposes, ethical responsibility and flourishing.
  12. XIIHere I Am Again
    I return to my present existence, reconsidering what has become intelligible and what might yet become possible.

The twelfth meditation would return the individual to the opening words.

But the significance of those words may have changed.

The destination is not simply a conclusion. It is a potentially transformed entrance into further inquiry.

This would be our principle of consequential Return expressed in literary form.

7. The author's journey and the reader's journey

I think there is an opportunity here that neither a conventional philosophical autobiography nor a conventional philosophical textbook would adequately capture.

We could construct the work through two parallel narratives.

Your narrative could begin with your own present intellectual engagement, return to your 1970–1972 investigations with Sebeok and Peirce, and then explore the significance of your later encounters with relativity, information theory, Simon, Pagels, Lloyd, Floridi and Levin.

The reader would be invited to investigate their own intellectual history.

They might possess completely different resources. Their philosophical commitments might differ substantially from yours.

They might discover that other scientific, artistic, religious or philosophical traditions have played central roles in their development.

The architecture would accommodate those possibilities.

This is where our work could become importantly different from presenting a prescribed path toward a particular philosophical destination.

8. The contemporary scientific dimension

Neither Augustine nor Descartes possessed the scientific resources now available to us.

Imagine what an individual undertaking these meditations today could investigate.

The extraordinary feature of this journey is that an individual can come to understand aspects of histories that occurred long before their existence, using competencies that are themselves achievements of those histories.

The meditations could make that extraordinary recursive relationship increasingly intelligible.

The experience of appreciating one's existence could become philosophically significant without requiring a predetermined theological or metaphysical interpretation.

9. What the opening might sound like

I would write in a more personal and contemplative voice than we have used in our canonical research essay.

Something along these lines:

Meditation I — Here I Am

Here I am.

I have begun with these words on many occasions, but their significance has changed as my understanding of the conditions through which I exist has developed.

There was a time when I might have regarded the most immediate philosophical question as one concerning the nature of my experience or the possibility of my knowledge.

Today I find myself drawn toward another question.

How extraordinary is it that I am here at all?

I find myself engaged with a world whose history extends far beyond anything I can remember. My existence depends upon processes that I did not initiate, organizations that I did not construct, and relationships whose significance I am only gradually becoming capable of appreciating.

Among the extraordinary achievements of those histories is my present ability to investigate them.

I can attend to the existence of my own body and begin investigating the remarkable organizations through which its activities become possible.

I can consider other beings whose worlds become significant to them through capacities quite different from mine.

I can investigate the histories through which human beings developed languages, mathematics, scientific theories and philosophical traditions.

I can encounter the intellectual constructions of individuals who lived long before me and find that those encounters transform the possibilities of my present understanding.

And I can begin investigating the history through which I became capable of undertaking these very activities.

More than fifty years ago, I was already concerned with some of these questions. My investigations of organisms, their capacities and their semiotic relationships led me toward problems concerning the organization of languages, theories and understanding.

At that time, I could not anticipate many of the intellectual resources that would subsequently become available to me.

Nor could I anticipate how those resources would transform the significance of my earlier inquiries.

When I now return to those questions, I am not returning as the individual who originally posed them.

I have become someone through whose acquired resources those earlier questions can become intelligible in new ways.

And I find myself wondering what further possibilities of understanding might become available.

I invite you to undertake a related investigation.

Your history is not mine. The relationships that have become significant to you may be quite different from those that have become significant to me. You need not adopt my philosophical commitments or follow the path through which I have arrived at my present understanding.

Begin where you are.

Attend to the remarkable circumstance that you are here, capable of attending to your own existence, capable of investigating aspects of its history, and capable of imagining possibilities that have not yet become actual.

What might become intelligible to you through such an inquiry?

And how might that understanding change the possibilities of the life you have yet to live?

This is only a specimen of the voice and method. I would want the actual autobiographical passages to emerge from your own recollections and written reflections, rather than have us invent details to create a satisfying narrative.

10. A meditation can become an investigative environment

There is also something we can accomplish with this work that Augustine and Descartes could not accomplish through the technologies of their times.

Each meditation could have three progressively deeper entrances.

  1. The literary meditation
    A readable, self-contained philosophical narrative requiring no software or specialized background.
  2. The individual's inquiry
    Optional reflective activities through which the reader investigates their own experiences, histories, competencies and possibilities.
  3. The experiential laboratory
    Inspectable scientific resources, interactive maps, synthetic worlds, alternative intellectual architectures and Joint Task Spaces.

A reader could enjoy the book without ever entering the digital environment.

Another reader might spend months investigating the relationships opened by a particular meditation.

A third might construct a developing personal map through which earlier meditations become newly significant.

This reconnects the proposed work directly with our Here I Am application and our longer-term VR ambitions.

The book would be an entrance into the environment rather than merely a manual for using it.

11. The Human Understanding Pilot acquires a new significance

I would now reconsider our immediate research plans in light of this possibility.

We should retain the existing Human Understanding Pilot, including its independent baseline and source-guided encounters. Its methodological distinctions are valuable.

But alongside that formal investigation, I would invite you to begin writing your own meditations.

The two activities would complement each other without being conflated.

12. Where this might eventually lead

I can now see our larger collection developing into three complementary forms of philosophical activity.

The philosophical research develops the investigative architecture.

The meditations invite the individual into its experiential territory.

The Joint Task Spaces extend those activities toward the development of collective human possibilities.

This could become a natural public-facing organization of our entire research program.

13. How I would proceed

I would initiate a new bounded project:

FSP-MEDITATIONS-1

Authorial Philosophical Narrative, Augustinian and Cartesian Comparative Foundations, Scientific Self-Understanding, Twelve-Meditation Architecture, Parallel Reader Inquiry, Human Understanding Pilot Integration, and Experiential Return.

Its first objective would not be to write the complete book immediately.

I would develop the prologue and the first three meditations substantially, establish the appropriate narrative voice, and then investigate whether the work can successfully move among personal recollection, philosophical reasoning, contemporary science and invitations to the reader.

I would keep the canonical research statement frozen during this initial literary development.

The later meditations could then emerge partly through your actual continuing inquiry, rather than being entirely anticipated by us.

14. A deeper relationship to the philosophical tradition

There is a beautiful progression we can consider without claiming that our project supersedes its predecessors.

  • Socrates
    How should I examine my life?
  • Augustine
    How do I understand the course and ultimate significance of my life?
  • Descartes
    Through what activities can I establish the foundations of my understanding?
  • Our proposed meditations
    How did my capacities for understanding become possible, how might they continue to develop, and how can I participate responsibly in developing the possibilities of other beings?

Our undertaking would belong to that longstanding tradition of philosophy as an activity through which the investigator is potentially transformed.

Its contemporary character would arise from our scientific understanding of assembly histories, the collective constitution of intelligence, the diversity of semiotic organizations, the availability of computational cognitive prostheses and the extraordinary possibilities of collective inquiry.

Its ethical significance would arise from recognizing that we can participate in shaping the conditions through which future beings will develop.

And its openness would arise from recognizing that neither the author nor the reader has arrived at a final intellectual destination.

I think this may ultimately become one of the most accessible and personally significant expressions of Full-Stack Philosophy.

Rather than asking the reader to accept our philosophical architecture, we would invite them to experience the phenomenon that motivated its development.

We would begin together, but individually, with the simplest of observations:

Here I am.

And after a sustained journey through the histories, organizations, relationships and possibilities that become available to us, we would return to those same words.

Here I am again.

What might have changed is not the words, but the organization of understanding through which they have become meaningful.
