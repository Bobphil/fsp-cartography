Here I Am

Twelve Meditations on Becoming, Understanding, and Responsibility

Provisional revised subtitle; the first edition remains available unchanged.

1. An invitation, not a completed book

A cup on a table can be a point of departure. Attend to its weight, its surface, the way it is held. Who made it? What histories enabled this encounter? Who else has used it, and what might it mean to offer it to someone? The inquiry can begin with a familiar thing rather than a declaration about the grandeur of existence.

This prospectus proposes a work in which philosophical narrative, scientific self-understanding and practical reflection remain in dialogue. The reader's life is not the author's life. Neither must arrive at the other's conclusions. The project is related to Understanding Understanding, the Human Understanding Pilot and the Joint Task Space research, but is not a report of completed human research.

2. Here I am: attention and response

In Genesis 22, Exodus 3 and 1 Samuel 3, the phrase traditionally rendered “Here I am” appears in scenes of being called. These narratives are different and deserve their own interpretation [S1]. Levinas's Otherwise than Being presents responsibility as prior to the self's voluntary assumption of it [S2]. This challenges a philosophy that might mistake first-person access for ethical priority.

The opening meditation attends to situated existence. The final meditation returns to the same words as a possible response to others. This is a proposed literary movement, not an argument that responsibility begins only after self-reflection. Responsibility may already be present at the beginning. Readers need not accept a theological interpretation or Levinas's entire philosophy.

3. Philosophical predecessors in dialogue

Augustine's Confessions brings particular episodes, memory and time into a theological investigation of a life [S5]. Descartes' Meditations asks readers to undertake a first-person inquiry, using methodical doubt and subsequent reconstruction [S4]. Peirce's 1868 criticism of Cartesian doubt provides a historical hinge: inquiry starts with actual commitments, and reasons for doubt can emerge within it [S3]. The proposed work is fallibilist, but does not claim to reproduce all of Peirce's philosophical commitments.

Hadot's work makes practice central, rather than treating philosophy as exposition alone [S6]. Marcus Aurelius and Montaigne invite attention to limits and particularity; Husserl's Fifth Cartesian Meditation raises intersubjectivity [S9, S11]. Buddhist mindfulness of breathing belongs to a distinct contemplative tradition with its own aims [S12]; it may challenge rather than merely illustrate our assumptions. Uexküll and Sebeok offer routes toward understanding differently organized beings [S10].

Scientific interlocutors include James and Levin on adaptive intelligence, Uexküll and Sebeok on organism–environment relationships, Peirce on signs, Simon on organization, Pagels and Lloyd on complexity and computation, Floridi on abstraction, and Reichenbach on coordination. This is a research agenda, not a claim of unified doctrine.

4. Twelve meditations

Each meditation begins with a possible concrete anchor and ends with a short practice. These are invitations, not assertions about the author's memories. Allow five to fifteen minutes, adapt or omit any exercise, and keep responses private if desired. No emotional outcome is prescribed.

I. Here I Am

Possible anchor: A hand or a cup.

What do I notice, and what do I infer?

Practice: Attend to an ordinary object for three minutes. Write two observations and two interpretations. Leave one uncertainty open.

II. The Histories Within This Moment

Possible anchor: A glass of water.

Which histories made this encounter possible?

Practice: Take a view from above: move from the object to the household, Earth and deeper history. Mark one known connection and one question at each scale.

III. The Many That Make Me One

Possible anchor: A bodily movement or a healing scratch.

How do coordinated processes enable individual activity?

Practice: Choose an everyday movement. Identify three enabling processes and distinguish what you observe from what biological research tells you.

IV. How I Became the Person I Am

Possible anchor: A photograph or dated notebook.

How did my particular capacities develop?

Practice: Record a recollection before interpreting it. Date the record; distinguish memory, documentary evidence and current interpretation.

V. Those Who Made Me Possible

Possible anchor: A letter, borrowed tool or remembered instruction.

How have particular people contributed to my life?

Practice: Name privately one contribution you received and one way of acknowledging it. Gratitude, ambivalence and disagreement are all permitted.

VI. The Worlds of Other Beings

Possible anchor: A bird, plant or another person's activity.

What can I know about another being's engagement?

Practice: Observe without disturbing. Separate behavior from inferred competency and possible experience. Identify a way to test an inference without presuming access to its experience.

VII. The Maps Through Which I Understand

Possible anchor: A word, map or measuring instrument.

What does my representation preserve or omit?

Practice: Make two descriptions of the same object for different purposes. Identify one distinction each makes available and one each leaves out.

VIII. When Understanding Changes

Possible anchor: A text revisited or a corrected belief.

How do my intellectual resources change?

Practice: Choose an earlier belief and compare it with your current view. Identify a reason for change and a plausible rival account. No change is acceptable.

IX. What I Will Not Become

Possible anchor: An unfinished task or worn object.

What do loss, mortality and irreversible choices mean?

Practice: Choose a manageable limitation, not necessarily a personal loss. Distinguish what cannot be restored, what remains open and what care might still be possible. Skip or substitute if desired.

X. What We Can Become Together

Possible anchor: A shared undertaking.

How can distinct people act together without becoming identical?

Practice: Sketch one collaboration, its contributions, differing purposes and one alternative arrangement. Identify who is absent from the decision.

XI. What Is Worth Becoming?

Possible anchor: A decision with competing purposes.

Which ends deserve pursuit and who is affected?

Practice: Examine a modest decision. Identify a benefit, a burden, an affected person's standing and a reason to reconsider. Do not treat reflection as authorization.

XII. Here I Am Again

Possible anchor: The opening object or scene.

What might it mean to respond to others?

Practice: Return to the opening record. Note what changed and what did not. Identify a bounded response, or a reason to refrain, without promising a transformation.

5. Finitude and particular others

A finite life cannot pursue every end. Some choices are irreversible, and repair may be incomplete or impossible. A capacity to find alternative means does not guarantee recovery. The meditations investigate what can be accepted, what can still be done and what consequences remain. Others enter the narrative as particular people, not merely contributors to a collective task: persons who teach, care, disagree, resist or are affected. No obligation to reconcile, disclose or express gratitude is imposed.

The literary work must stand independently. A laboratory may make an issue inspectable, but is not evidence that a reader has developed a competency.

7. Plain language and claim ceilings

TermWorking meaning

Interpretive architectureOrganized concepts and relationships used to understand something.
Assembly historiesPhysical, biological, cultural and personal histories enabling a capacity; not technical assembly theory [S13].
Joint Task SpaceAn organized setting for coordinating activities among distinct participants.
ReturnReconsideration in light of subsequent encounters and consequences.

James identifies pursuit of ends and choice of means as a criterion of mentality [S7]. Levin explicitly argues that intelligence is collective and uses the Jamesian alternative-means conception to investigate competencies across scales [S8]. Biological observations, Levin's theoretical interpretation and FSP's proposed extensions must remain distinct. Coordinated cells do not establish a shared phenomenal subject; successful goal pursuit does not establish ethical legitimacy. The synthetic FSP studies investigate stipulated mechanisms, not a validated theory of human cognition.

8. Author and reader: separate journeys

The author's recollections of ORG research in 1970–1972, work with Sebeok and Peircean semiotics are starting materials, not independently verified contemporary records. No autobiographical specimen is supplied in this edition. A notebook, memory or conversation may become a scene only after the author provides it. Readers can investigate their own histories without adopting this genealogy.

9. Protecting the human study

Preserve an independent dated record before drafting a meditation about it. Record prior exposure, including the AI-written specimen in the first edition and this revised prospectus. The earlier influence cannot be undone. Distinguish recollection, surviving documentation and present interpretation. Keep independent and assisted records separate. Preserve original snapshots; corrections are new dated records. Compare literary drafts with earlier accounts without rewriting the baseline.

The Human Understanding Pilot remains voluntary and separate. Do not disclose transfer tasks, evaluation criteria or participant records in the public book. No human findings are asserted. A reader may skip any practice; the exercises are not research enrollment.

10. Where we proceed

First preserve the present prospectus and independent authorial records. Then develop a prologue and Meditations I–III only, grounded in material supplied by the author. Review their voice, scientific accuracy, practices and ethical arc before commissioning the remaining nine. The human pilot and literary project may proceed in parallel only where independent records are protected. Further revision is contingent on actual evidence and authorial decisions.

The title is shared by Jonathan Safran Foer's 2016 novel [S14]; the provisional subtitle distinguishes this philosophical project. No claim of title exclusivity is made.
