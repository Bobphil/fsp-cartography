# FSP-VR-TEMPORAL-LENS-1

## Freeze, Slow, Normal, Accelerate; Contingency Expansion; Gate Decomposition; Temporal Zoom; and Recomposition

**Status:** TEMPORAL-INQUIRY PROJECTION — VERSION 1  
**Parents:** FSP-VR-VISUAL-GRAMMAR-1; FSP-VR-MAATHAI-FIELD-1  
**First implementation:** Maathai circa 1976 visual field  
**Deployment:** NON-DEPLOYED

# 1. Governing Claim

Time is not only represented by radial distance.

The learner should be able to **change the temporal resolution of the field**.

The temporal lens has four modes:

- FREEZE;
- SLOW;
- NORMAL;
- ACCELERATE.

These modes do not change history.

They change how much of the possibility field is exposed and how much decomposition is available.

# 2. Freeze

Freeze holds the scene at the present historical state.

Purpose:
- inspect the near field;
- decompose relations;
- inspect provenance;
- inspect constraints;
- inspect gate requirements;
- identify unknowns.

Freeze privileges logical and relational depth over temporal extension.

> **Freeze ≠ timelessness.**

It is a temporary analytical hold on a historically situated state.

# 3. Slow

Slow exposes near-future paths whose prerequisites are substantially present or partly developable.

Purpose:
- inspect how capability can accumulate;
- see how relationships or resources can move from partial toward present;
- compare several live transitions;
- preserve uncertainty.

Slow should make becoming visible without implying inevitability.

# 4. Normal

Normal exposes the middle possibility field.

Purpose:
- inspect institutionally mediated paths;
- reveal dependency chains;
- compare present capability with missing authorization;
- show how several contingencies must align.

# 5. Accelerate

Accelerate exposes structurally distant futures.

Purpose:
- make long-range possibilities inspectable;
- expose the accumulation of contingencies;
- show how multiple requirements must become present;
- preserve the distinction between possibility and prediction.

Accelerate must never collapse distant possibility into forecast.

> **Visible future ≠ predicted future.**

# 6. Temporal Zoom

The learner may move:

**near field → mid field → distant field → near field**

without losing the original state.

The central historical locus remains anchored.

Temporal zoom changes the horizon of inspection, not the historical present itself.

# 7. Contingency Stack

Every path has a stack of requirements.

As temporal range increases, the learner should see more of:

- capabilities;
- resources;
- relationships;
- institutional access;
- authorization;
- evidence;
- timing;
- unknowns.

Distant paths should generally expose more unresolved dependencies than near paths.

# 8. Gate Decomposition

A gate should show which requirements are:

- PRESENT;
- PARTIAL;
- ABSENT;
- UNKNOWN.

The temporal lens may expose more distant gates, but it may not silently upgrade their requirements.

# 9. Time-Mode Visibility

For the first implementation:

FREEZE:
- depth 1 only;
- maximum decomposition;
- all provenance/remainder available.

SLOW:
- depths 1–2;
- strong decomposition.

NORMAL:
- depths 1–3;
- balanced field.

ACCELERATE:
- depths 1–4;
- full contingency field.

# 10. Anti-Prediction Rule

The temporal lens fails if:

- farther paths appear as forecasts;
- temporal speed implies probability;
- distant possibilities glow as desirable outcomes;
- the historical path is visually privileged;
- missing conditions are hidden.

# 11. Recomposition

After moving through temporal modes, the learner returns to ordinary time and answers:

- What was possible immediately?
- What required development?
- What required institutions?
- What required structural change?
- Which distant future depended on the largest contingency stack?
- What remained unknown even when that future was visible?

# 12. Empirical Questions

1. Does Freeze help the learner inspect relations more deeply?
2. Does Slow make capability accumulation easier to see?
3. Does Accelerate reveal dependency chains without being read as prediction?
4. Can the learner move back inward without losing the present-state anchor?
5. Can the learner explain why a distant possibility is distant?
6. Can the learner distinguish temporal remoteness from probability?
7. Does the contingency stack make gates more intelligible?

# 13. Claim Ceiling

This temporal lens is a cognitive-prosthesis hypothesis.

It does not establish:
- that four time modes are optimal;
- that radial distance is the best visual metaphor;
- that users will interpret speed correctly;
- that distant futures are exhaustive;
- that temporal zoom improves critical thinking.

# 14. Constitutional Decision

**PASS — IMPLEMENT AS A NON-DEPLOYED TEMPORAL LENS ON THE MAATHAI FIELD.**
