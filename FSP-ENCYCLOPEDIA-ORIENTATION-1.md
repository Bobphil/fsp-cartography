# FSP-ENCYCLOPEDIA-ORIENTATION-1

## Public FSP Orientation

### Purpose

The FSP Encyclopedia now requires an explicit answer to the questions a first-time reader is entitled to ask:

- What is Full-Stack Philosophy?
- Why does it exist?
- Why is it called full-stack?
- What does it govern?
- What is it not?
- Do I need to learn it before using the domain libraries?

The orientation is public-facing, ordinary-language first, and intentionally less formal than the Core or Formal & Executable reference layers.

## Public definition

> Full-Stack Philosophy is a way of making the interacting layers of human inquiry inspectable without pretending that every domain has the same ontology, evidence, authority, or method.

## Origin problem

Human inquiry does not happen only inside arguments. People inherit concepts, institutions, practices, evidence, relationships, identities, technologies, and histories. They must act before uncertainty disappears.

Problems arise when transitions between those layers become invisible:

- map becomes world;
- model success becomes ontology;
- possibility becomes recommendation;
- recommendation becomes authorization;
- historical explanation becomes present justification;
- one person's representation becomes a claim about another person;
- institutional authority becomes evidential truth;
- a current judgment becomes final closure.

FSP exists to make those transitions inspectable and governable.

## Full-stack meaning

Full-stack is a metaphor for following inquiry across interacting levels:

```text
Lived Doing
→ Inquiry
→ Representation
→ Revision
→ Bounded Judgment
→ Authorial Return
→ REOPEN
```

The stack may include lived experience, concepts, evidence, provenance, models, institutions, power, possibilities, relations, and action.

It is not a universal ontology.

## Constitutional commitments

### Authorial Capability

Inquiry should increase the person's capacity to inspect, revise, and own a bounded judgment.

### Cognitive Prosthesis

Tools, simulations, representations, and AI may extend inquiry without replacing the person, domain expertise, or legitimate authority.

### Translational Governance

Movement between persons, models, disciplines, institutions, and representations must preserve provenance, standing, meaning, and claim ceilings rather than silently changing them.

### Return to Lived Doing

Inquiry should return to the person, practice, community, or institution whose actual Doing gave the inquiry its stakes.

## Governing distinctions

- Map ≠ World ≠ Person
- Generate ≠ Recommend ≠ Authorize
- Possibility ≠ prediction
- Historical explanation ≠ present justification
- Model success ≠ ontology
- Shared representation ≠ historical sameness
- Evidence ≠ full theory choice
- Authority ≠ truth
- Current judgment ≠ final closure

## What FSP is not

FSP is not:

- another ethical theory;
- a universal philosophy-of-science theory;
- a single decision procedure;
- a universal ontology;
- a vocabulary students must master before doing philosophy;
- a substitute for disciplinary expertise or evidence.

## No-framework-prerequisite boundary

The Encyclopedia is domain-first.

A reader may enter Ethics, History of Philosophy, Inquiry & Science, Logic, Aesthetics, or another domain without first learning FSP.

Courses may use FSP distinctions quietly when useful without becoming FSP courses.

The deeper architecture remains available for readers who want to inspect it.

## Public navigation consequence

The top-level Encyclopedia navigation should include:

`What is FSP?`

This is distinct from:

- **Core**, which inventories shared FSP distinctions;
- **About**, which explains Encyclopedia organization and governance;
- **Domains**, which provides disciplinary entry;
- **Formal & Executable**, which exposes deeper schemas and invariants.

## Claim ceiling

This orientation explains the purpose and public constitutional commitments of FSP.

It does not attempt a complete academic defense, intellectual history, comparator analysis, or formal derivation of FSP. Those belong in deeper research and reference layers.
