# FSP-JTS-FL-NURSING-HOME-STATES-1

## Gulf Pines Four-State Joint Task Space Progression

**Status:** LAYER-1 WORKED STATE SEQUENCE  
**Parent:** FSP-JTS-FL-NURSING-HOME-1  
**Purpose:** Make cooperation without convergence visible as inquiry changes over time.  
**Case status:** Gulf Pines is fictional/composite. Florida historical backtest remains a separate empirical evidence layer.

---

# State architecture

Each state records:

- **Observed / reported**
- **Inferred / hypothesized**
- **ABCD shift**
- **Activated Tools**
- **Standing / authorization**
- **Remainder**
- **Claim ceiling**
- **REOPEN trigger**
- **Carry-forward**

The point is not to simulate perfect decision-making.

The point is to expose how a governed shared map changes.

---

# JTS-S0 — Early Signal

## Situation

Over two days, three Gulf Pines residents develop cough, fever, or fatigue. One CNA calls out sick. Another worker reports that two residents on the same hallway “seem off.” Families have not yet been notified of any outbreak.

No facility-wide testing result is available.

## Observed / reported

- three residents have new respiratory symptoms;
- one CNA is absent;
- frontline staff report two additional residents may be changing;
- symptoms cluster partly on one hallway;
- no confirmed outbreak count exists yet.

## Inferred / hypothesized

Possible hypotheses include:

- a transmissible respiratory outbreak is beginning;
- unrelated illnesses are occurring;
- staff transmission is possible;
- resident clustering may be coincidental;
- current staffing may become strained if absences rise.

None of these is yet established.

## ABCD shift

### Agency
Who can initiate testing, clinical assessment, isolation precautions, staffing contingency, and family communication?

### Mattering
Immediate safety matters, but so do resident fear, dignity, and the meaning of “home.”

### Context
Baseline staffing, building layout, infection-control history, resident acuity, and local disease prevalence may matter.

### Dynamism
The most important fact is that the situation is changing faster than the map is stabilizing.

## Activated Tools

**T01 — First Map Audit**  
What has actually been observed?

**T02 — Reason-Giving Audit**  
Which claims are observations and which are outbreak hypotheses?

**T09 — Consequence / Harm Audit**  
What are the risks of acting too slowly versus acting too broadly?

## Standing / authorization

Clinical staff may initiate assessment and precautions within role.

Administration may activate contingency procedures.

No one yet has sufficient warrant to treat every resident or staff member as infected.

## Remainder

- pathogen identity;
- transmission route;
- number of affected residents;
- staff exposure;
- family exposure;
- local community prevalence;
- likely duration;
- actual staffing reserve.

## Claim ceiling

> **Early cluster ≠ confirmed facility-wide outbreak.**

## REOPEN trigger

REOPEN immediately on:
- positive testing;
- new symptomatic residents;
- staff illness increase;
- clinical deterioration;
- contradictory diagnostic evidence.

## Carry-forward

Carry forward:
- symptom cluster;
- staffing vulnerability;
- uncertainty;
- need for rapid evidence update.

Do not carry forward:
- assumed cause;
- assumed transmission chain;
- facility-wide infection status.

# JTS-S1 — Widening Map

## New information

Within 36 hours:

- two residents test positive for a transmissible respiratory infection;
- one additional resident becomes symptomatic;
- two staff members report symptoms;
- a daughter reports that her mother becomes severely distressed when isolated and has difficulty using video calls;
- night-shift staff report that full-room isolation is difficult under current coverage.

## What changed

The outbreak hypothesis is now substantially stronger.

But the intervention problem also becomes more complex.

The map now contains not only transmission risk but also:
- staffing feasibility;
- resident-specific capability;
- communication burden;
- family knowledge;
- implementation constraints.

## ABCD shift

### Agency
Formal infection-control options may exceed actual night-shift capability.

### Mattering
Infection prevention and relational continuity are both morally salient.

### Context
A restriction that is tolerable for one resident may be highly damaging for another.

### Dynamism
Both infection and intervention burden are now changing.

## Activated Tools

**T07 — Perspective / Moral-Visibility Audit**  
Whose burden was absent from the first map?

**T12 — Effective-Capability Audit**  
Can residents actually maintain communication and orientation under isolation?

**T09 — Consequence / Harm Audit**  
How should infection risk be weighed against isolation harm and staff overload?

**T04 — Control / Moral-Range Audit**  
What can workers realistically control under current staffing?

## Standing / authorization

Clinical staff have stronger warrant for infection-control recommendations.

Residents retain standing regarding burdens and preferences where possible.

Families may contribute resident-specific knowledge but do not automatically acquire unrestricted proxy authority.

Administration may coordinate policy but must account for feasibility and resident rights.

## Remainder

- exact transmission network;
- whether staff shortage will worsen;
- resident-specific effects of prolonged restriction;
- whether alternative safe-contact arrangements are feasible;
- whether all units face equal risk.

## Firewalls

> **Clinical indication ≠ unrestricted facility-wide authorization.**

> **Formal option ≠ effective capability.**

> **Family concern ≠ unrestricted proxy authority.**

## Claim ceiling

> **Confirmed transmission supports heightened response; it does not yet justify one uniform restriction for every resident.**

## REOPEN trigger

REOPEN on:
- unit-specific case distribution;
- staffing threshold breach;
- resident deterioration linked to isolation;
- new public-health guidance;
- evidence that precautions are operationally infeasible.

## Carry-forward

Carry forward:
- confirmed transmission;
- resident-specific burden;
- staffing feasibility as a first-class constraint;
- family knowledge as provenance-bearing input.

# JTS-S2 — Intervention Pressure

## New information

By day five:

- cases rise on two units;
- several staff are absent;
- administration proposes a temporary facility-wide visitation suspension;
- clinical leadership supports stronger restrictions;
- some families object;
- one resident with cognitive impairment stops eating reliably after several days of reduced family contact;
- staff report that room isolation increases fall risk for some residents;
- a regulator reminds the facility of infection-control duties but leaves implementation details partly local.

## The Joint Task Space is now under pressure

The question is no longer merely:

> What is happening?

It is now:

> What may legitimately be done, by whom, to whom, for how long, and under what review conditions?

## Activated Tools

**T06 — Legitimacy / Standing Audit**  
Who may impose consequential restrictions?

**T08 — Respect / Autonomy Audit**  
Does the intervention preserve residents as agents rather than merely manage them?

**T09 — Consequence / Harm Audit**  
Who bears infection risk, isolation burden, fall risk, nutritional risk, and staffing burden?

**T12 — Effective-Capability Audit**  
What alternatives are actually usable for residents with sensory, cognitive, or technological barriers?

**T10 — Background Power Audit**  
Are chronic staffing constraints making a formally neutral policy disproportionately burdensome?

## Standing / authorization partition

Clinical leadership:
- standing to recommend stronger infection-control measures.

Administration:
- authority to implement facility policy within legal and regulatory limits.

Residents:
- direct moral standing over burdens; variable legal decisional authority.

Proxies:
- decision authority only where legally applicable.

Regulators:
- authority over compliance requirements, not complete authority over local ethical tradeoffs.

Families:
- strong relational and informational standing, not automatic implementation authority.

## Translation problem

Clinical language:
“reduce contact.”

Resident-life translation:
“remove familiar people, routines, touch, advocacy, orientation, and assistance.”

Operational translation:
“increase staff burden because staff must replace functions families were informally providing.”

These are not identical descriptions.

All must remain visible.

## Remainder ledger

- infection reduction attributable to visitation suspension;
- resident-specific harm from continued restriction;
- informal care families had supplied;
- unequal burden across cognitive and mobility profiles;
- whether staffing shortage makes the policy self-defeating.

## Firewalls

> **Benefit ≠ authorization.**

> **Power ≠ legitimacy.**

> **Uniform rule ≠ equal practical burden.**

> **Policy simplicity ≠ ethical adequacy.**

## Claim ceiling

> **A facility-wide restriction may be provisionally justified under high transmission pressure, but current evidence does not license treating its burden as equal, harmless, or indefinitely acceptable.**

## Repair families generated

- time-limited restriction with scheduled review;
- resident-specific exceptions;
- PPE-supported essential caregiver access;
- enhanced staff communication support;
- alternative contact methods matched to resident capability;
- unit-specific rather than facility-wide restrictions where evidence permits;
- temporary staffing reinforcement.

These are generated options, not automatic recommendations.

## REOPEN trigger

Mandatory review if:
- case growth slows;
- staffing improves;
- resident harm rises;
- safe caregiver access becomes feasible;
- new testing narrows affected units;
- implementation proves counterproductive.

# JTS-S3 — Revised Bounded Judgment

## New information

After another week:

- new cases decline substantially;
- one unit remains affected;
- staffing begins to recover;
- resident distress and deconditioning are increasingly visible;
- essential caregiver visits with PPE have occurred without identified transmission events;
- families and staff report that individualized exceptions are workable but administratively burdensome.

## Map revision

The original emergency justification has weakened.

The burdens of the emergency intervention have become more visible.

The shared task now changes from emergency suppression toward differentiated recovery.

## Activated Tools

**T01 — First Map Audit**  
What new harms are now visible?

**T06 — Legitimacy / Standing Audit**  
Does the original authorization still hold under changed conditions?

**T09 — Consequence / Harm Audit**  
How have the balance and distribution of harms changed?

**T12 — Effective-Capability Audit**  
Which residents need restored supports first?

**T02 — Reason-Giving Audit**  
Do the original reasons still support the same restriction?

## Bounded joint judgment

A defensible revised judgment is:

> Current transmission conditions no longer justify maintaining the original facility-wide restriction in its initial form. Restrictions should narrow toward affected units and resident-specific risk, while safe caregiver access and capability-restoring supports expand. The facility should continue infection surveillance and preserve the ability to re-escalate if transmission increases.

This is not final closure.

## Standing / authorization update

Administration remains authorized to revise policy.

Clinical leadership retains standing over infection-risk assessment.

Residents and proxies gain increased practical relevance as restrictions narrow and individualized choices reopen.

Frontline staff remain essential to feasibility review.

## Remainder

- risk of resurgence;
- long-term consequences of isolation already incurred;
- whether staffing vulnerabilities are temporary or structural;
- what permanent preparedness changes are warranted.

## Historical backtest contribution

The Florida backtest may now enter as a structural-review prompt:

If Gulf Pines also exhibited low staffing and weak inspection history, that profile would justify deeper investigation of structural resilience.

It would not establish that those factors caused the outbreak.

Firewall:

> **Structural warning ≠ retrospective blame.**

## Claim ceiling

> **Improved conditions justify policy revision; they do not prove the emergency response was unnecessary or that future risk is absent.**

## REOPEN conditions

Re-escalate or reconsider if:
- cases rise again;
- staffing drops below safety threshold;
- a new unit cluster appears;
- caregiver access shows evidence of transmission;
- new evidence changes risk assumptions.

## Authorial Return

The Joint Task Space returns:

- updated evidence;
- changed burdens;
- revised standing map;
- narrowed authorization;
- remaining uncertainty;
- structural repair questions;
- explicit REOPEN conditions.

The system does not own the decision.

# Layer-1 acceptance test

This layer passes if a reader can see:

1. that the shared map changes;
2. that Tools activate because the topology changes;
3. that standing and authorization are state-dependent;
4. that remainder persists rather than disappearing;
5. that claim ceilings change with evidence;
6. that REOPEN is part of the architecture rather than an afterthought;
7. that no state requires participants to converge on one ontology or ethical theory.

> **Joint Task Space ≠ static multidisciplinary meeting.**

A Joint Task Space is a temporally governed inquiry environment.
