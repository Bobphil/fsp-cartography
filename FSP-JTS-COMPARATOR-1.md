# FSP-JTS-COMPARATOR-1

## Asteria-2047 — Cross-Domain Joint Task Space Comparator

**Status:** CROSS-DOMAIN PORTABILITY STRESS TEST  
**Comparator baseline:** Gulf Pines five-layer Joint Task Space  
**Case:** Asteria-2047 — fictional planetary-defense scenario  
**Real governance context:** planetary-defense observation, warning, and mission-planning structures modeled on current IAWN / SMPAG / national-space-agency practice  
**Purpose:** Test whether the Gulf Pines grammar remains useful under long-horizon uncertainty, distributed authority, model dependence, potential irreversibility, future-person standing, and cross-jurisdiction risk transfer.

---

# 1. Why this comparator

Gulf Pines is demanding, but it has stabilizing features: identifiable affected populations, bounded institutional authority, short time horizons, rapidly updating evidence, and many reversible interventions.

Planetary defense removes many of those stabilizers.

A credible asteroid-impact threat may involve decades between discovery and possible impact, uncertain orbital and physical models, shifting impact probabilities, changing geographic risk corridors, multiple sovereign states, no single global executive authority, large benefits from early action, large costs from premature action, possible transfer of risk from one region to another, future persons who cannot participate, and interventions that may be difficult or impossible to reverse.

This is therefore a strong portability test.

---

# 2. Fictional scenario — Asteria-2047

In late 2026, astronomers discover a previously unknown near-Earth object, provisionally named **Asteria-2047** for this exercise.

Initial working assumptions:

- possible Earth impact in 2047;
- current estimated impact probability: 1.8%;
- approximate diameter: 250–400 metres;
- composition: uncertain;
- rotation state: incompletely characterized;
- impact corridor: broad and model-dependent;
- possible consequences: severe regional devastation if impact occurs;
- additional observations are expected to narrow the orbit substantially.

A reconnaissance mission could improve physical characterization.

A kinetic-deflection mission would generally benefit from earlier lead time.

Waiting improves knowledge but may reduce intervention options.

Acting too early under poorly characterized conditions could fail, fragment the object, shift the risk corridor, or create geopolitical conflict over transferred risk.

The task is not:

> “Should humanity deflect the asteroid?”

The task is:

> **How should heterogeneous scientific, governmental, technical, ethical, and international actors govern evidence, standing, action, risk transfer, and revision under deep uncertainty when delay and intervention are both consequential?**

---

# 3. Real governance anchor

This fictional scenario assumes a real-world environment in which:

- observatories and scientific institutions contribute detection, monitoring, and characterization;
- international warning networks aggregate and communicate impact-risk information;
- space agencies assess possible mission responses;
- national governments retain sovereign authority;
- international bodies facilitate coordination;
- civil-protection systems plan consequence management.

The comparator does not assume a single world government.

Authority is structurally distributed.

---

# 4. Comparator hypothesis

The Gulf Pines grammar is portable if it can govern:

STATE → STANDING → TRANSLATION → REPAIR → AMPLIFICATION → REOPEN

without forcing planetary defense into nursing-home concepts or inventing a new universal ontology.

The test is whether the same thin governance functions remain useful.

---

# 5. Layer 1 — STATE under long-horizon uncertainty

## Primary question

> What changed?

The answer is not simply “impact probability increased.”

The state includes probability distribution, orbital uncertainty, observation quality, model sensitivity, time remaining, physical-characterization uncertainty, intervention windows, geographic risk corridor, and confidence in consequence models.

Asteria therefore stresses Layer 1 in a new way:

> **State is partly a distribution over possible futures, not only a description of present conditions.**

### Example states

## A0 — Detection

- preliminary orbit;
- high uncertainty;
- 0.2% estimated impact probability;
- no stable impact corridor.

Claim ceiling:

> **Detection ≠ credible impact threat.**

## A1 — Credible Threat

- additional observations;
- 1.8% estimated probability;
- 2047 encounter persists across model updates;
- broad possible impact corridor.

Claim ceiling:

> **Nontrivial probability ≠ predicted impact.**

## A2 — Decision Window

- probability remains material;
- reconnaissance launch window approaching;
- deflection lead-time advantage becoming consequential;
- physical properties remain uncertain.

Claim ceiling:

> **Urgency ≠ certainty.**

## A3 — Intervention Commitment

- mission design selected;
- trajectory change proposed;
- some uncertainty remains;
- intervention may shift impact-risk geography.

Claim ceiling:

> **Expected risk reduction ≠ zero transferred risk.**

## A4 — Post-intervention Monitoring

- object orbit changed;
- uncertainty recalculated;
- residual risk monitored.

Claim ceiling:

> **Successful intervention model ≠ final safety determination.**

### Layer-1 portability result

**PASS.**

The state concept survives but must explicitly allow probabilistic and counterfactual state representations.

---

# 6. Layer 2 — STANDING under distributed authority

## Primary question

> Who may know, recommend, authorize, implement, and bear risk?

### Astronomers / orbit analysts

Evidential authority:
- observations;
- orbit determination;
- uncertainty;
- impact probability.

Authorization:
none by virtue of scientific expertise.

Firewall:

> **Impact-model expertise ≠ mission authorization.**

### Planetary-defense scientists / engineers

Evidential authority:
- mission feasibility;
- deflection performance;
- physical characterization;
- failure modes.

Recommendation standing:
strong.

Authorization:
distributed through governmental and intergovernmental structures.

### National governments

Authority:
- launch authorization;
- funding;
- sovereign action;
- civil protection;
- diplomacy.

Standing:
strong for populations potentially affected and national resources.

Limitation:
no single state automatically possesses universal authorization over global risk redistribution.

### International coordination bodies

Role:
- information sharing;
- mission-planning coordination;
- communication;
- facilitation.

Authority:
important but not equivalent to a global sovereign.

### Populations inside current risk corridor

Moral standing:
direct and potentially extreme.

Problem:
the corridor may change as models update.

### Populations outside current risk corridor

Standing:
not zero.

A deflection maneuver may move risk.

### Future persons

Moral standing:
substantial but non-participatory.

They cannot directly authorize or object.

### Lower-capacity states

Standing:
high where populations may be affected.

Practical power:
may be low.

Firewall:

> **Low technical capability ≠ low standing.**

### Layer-2 portability result

**PASS WITH PRESSURE.**

The standing/authorization distinction survives strongly.

New stressor:

> **Standing may be probabilistic, geographically mobile, and temporally extended.**

Possible future field:
standing basis = PRESENT | FUTURE | PROBABILISTIC | TRANSFERRED_RISK

Do not freeze that field yet.

# 7. Layer 3 — TRANSLATION under model dependence

## Primary question

> What travels, and what remains?

Asteria is intensely model-dependent.

The shared task may contain telescope observations, orbital estimates, probability distributions, physical models, mission simulations, casualty models, economic models, geopolitical risk assessments, legal interpretations, and ethical judgments.

### Example chain

Observation:

> measured astrometric positions

becomes:

> orbit estimate

becomes:

> impact probability

becomes:

> geographic risk corridor

becomes:

> expected consequence estimate

becomes:

> mission recommendation.

Each step introduces assumptions.

Firewall:

> **Observation ≠ orbit model ≠ impact probability ≠ consequence forecast ≠ policy recommendation.**

### Model ensemble

Different models may yield different impact probabilities, corridor widths, fragmentation outcomes, deflection success probabilities, and casualty estimates.

FSP must preserve model plurality rather than manufacture one false consensus number.

### Remainder

Likely remainder includes:

- unmodeled physical properties;
- model-form uncertainty;
- tail risks;
- unknown fragmentation behavior;
- geopolitical interpretation;
- future technological capabilities;
- future population distribution.

### Layer-3 portability result

**STRONG PASS.**

Translation/remainder becomes even more important than in Gulf Pines.

Potential stressor:
model ensembles may require explicit representation of competing models rather than one translated representation.

Existing grammar can handle this through multiple translation objects.

No new primitive required yet.

---

# 8. Layer 4 — REPAIR under irreversibility and option value

## Primary question

> What can be tried, in what order, under what bounds?

The word “repair” still works if understood broadly as strategy under diagnosed pressure.

Strategy families include:

### Observation repair
- additional telescope observations;
- radar characterization;
- improved orbit determination.

### Knowledge repair
- reconnaissance mission;
- physical characterization;
- composition analysis.

### Mitigation repair
- kinetic impactor;
- slow-push techniques;
- disruption options;
- combinations.

### Consequence-management repair
- evacuation;
- civil protection;
- continuity planning.

### Governance repair
- international decision protocol;
- risk-transfer review;
- transparent communication;
- authorization thresholds.

### Research repair
- improve deflection models;
- reduce uncertainty in material response.

### Sequencing tension

Act early:
- more deflection leverage;
- less knowledge.

Wait:
- better characterization;
- less intervention time.

Firewall:

> **More information ≠ always better if waiting destroys intervention options.**

And:

> **Earlier action ≠ automatically safer if model uncertainty is high.**

### Reversibility

Some actions are highly reversible:
- observations;
- simulations;
- planning.

Some are partly reversible:
- mission development.

Some may be effectively irreversible:
- executed kinetic deflection;
- disruption attempt.

Layer 4 already contains the reversibility grammar needed.

### Risk-transfer test

A mission may reduce total expected impact probability while shifting the corridor toward another population.

Therefore repair requires:
- benefit assessment;
- transferred-risk assessment;
- standing review;
- authorization review;
- explicit claim ceiling.

Firewall:

> **Global expected-risk reduction ≠ legitimate unilateral risk transfer.**

### Layer-4 portability result

**PASS WITH NEW EMPHASIS.**

Existing sequencing, reversibility, option value, and authorization separation survive.

---

# 9. Layer 5 — AMPLIFICATION across nations and institutions

## Primary question

> What happens when a bounded scientific or strategic judgment becomes institutional infrastructure?

Examples:

- international notification thresholds;
- mission-planning thresholds;
- national emergency protocols;
- funding rules;
- public communication standards;
- launch-authorization procedures;
- evacuation planning;
- model standards.

A threshold such as “probability exceeds X” may become a governance trigger.

But probability is model-dependent and time-varying.

Firewall:

> **Threshold crossing ≠ complete decision justification.**

### Institutional sedimentation

A historical threshold may persist after observation capability, mission technology, model calibration, or geopolitical conditions change.

Therefore planetary-defense governance also needs institutional REOPEN.

### Upward/downward learning

Observational experience, exercises, mission tests, near misses, and real interventions should update thresholds, models, communication procedures, and mission-planning rules.

### Layer-5 portability result

**PASS.**

Institutional amplification generalizes beyond facility governance.

The object changes from facility policy to multinational infrastructure, but the governance problem remains recognizable.

# 10. REOPEN under changing probability

Asteria gives REOPEN a different topology.

REOPEN may be triggered by:

- new observations;
- revised orbit;
- new physical characterization;
- changed impact probability;
- narrower corridor;
- mission failure;
- mission success;
- new technology;
- geopolitical change;
- changed legal authority;
- evidence of risk transfer;
- updated consequence estimates.

Critically:

REOPEN may reduce concern.

It may also increase concern.

It may change the strategy family entirely.

Firewall:

> **REOPEN ≠ escalation.**

---

# 11. RETURN under distributed authorship

Gulf Pines returns to identifiable institutional actors.

Asteria has no single equivalent author.

Return therefore becomes distributed.

Possible Return targets:

- scientific warning network;
- mission-planning group;
- national governments;
- affected governments;
- civil-protection agencies;
- international diplomatic forums;
- public communication authorities.

The FSP architecture must not fabricate a single author where governance is genuinely plural.

Firewall:

> **Distributed governance ≠ absent governance.**

And:

> **Coordination architecture ≠ global sovereign authority.**

### Return portability result

**PASS WITH PRESSURE.**

Authorial Return remains meaningful, but its target may be plural and institutionally distributed.

---

# 12. Gulf Pines vs Asteria

| Dimension | Gulf Pines | Asteria-2047 |
|---|---|---|
| Time horizon | days/weeks | decades |
| Evidence | clinical + operational | observational + probabilistic + model-based |
| Authority | bounded institution + regulators | distributed national/international |
| Affected population | identifiable residents/staff/families | uncertain and changing global populations |
| Standing | present and local | present + future + probabilistic |
| Intervention | mostly reversible/bounded | potentially irreversible |
| Risk transfer | among residents/staff/families | across regions/nations/generations |
| Models | supportive | constitutive of risk representation |
| REOPEN cadence | hours/days | observations, years, mission milestones |
| Amplification | facility/state/federal policy | global warning/mission/governance infrastructure |

Despite these differences, the same five functions remain useful.

# 13. Non-duplication regression

The comparator preserves canonical ownership.

Layer 1 owns probabilistic state change, not mission authorization.

Layer 2 owns distributed standing and authorization, not orbital inference.

Layer 3 owns model translation and remainder, not mission selection.

Layer 4 owns strategy families, sequencing, option value, and irreversibility, not international legitimacy by itself.

Layer 5 owns institutional thresholds, protocols, amplification, and feedback, not local scientific truth.

**PASS.**

A credible but uncertain long-horizon impact threat persists.

No single actor; scientific, national, international, and affected-population standing differ.

Observation, reconnaissance, mitigation, and consequence-management options must be sequenced.

Nontrivial risk does not equal predicted impact; expected risk reduction does not authorize unilateral risk transfer.

# 16. Return / REOPEN regression

Return remains possible.

It is plural rather than singular.

REOPEN remains structurally central.

**PASS.**

Pressure point:
the architecture should avoid language implying one final author.

Potential refinement:
RETURN_TARGET may be singular or plural.

This is compatible with the existing field and does not yet require a new primitive.

---

# 17. New stressors discovered

The comparator exposes five stressors not prominent in Gulf Pines:

1. **Probabilistic standing** — Who counts as potentially affected when the risk corridor is uncertain?
2. **Future-person standing** — How should people not yet born enter bounded judgment?
3. **Risk transfer** — Can an intervention lower aggregate risk while moving risk onto another population?
4. **Model plurality** — What if no single model deserves canonical status?
5. **Distributed Return** — What if no single actor can legitimately author the final decision?

These are genuine stressors.

They do not yet require a sixth layer.

They appear expressible within standing, translation, repair, amplification, and Return.

---

# 18. Comparator verdict

## Overall verdict

**PASS — PORTABLE WITH PRESSURE**

The Gulf Pines five-layer grammar survives a radically different topology:

- local → global;
- short-horizon → long-horizon;
- mostly observed → deeply model-mediated;
- bounded authority → distributed authority;
- present actors → future persons;
- reversible interventions → potentially irreversible intervention;
- local burden shift → cross-border risk transfer.

No layer had to be replaced.

No new universal layer was required.

The strongest pressure appears inside existing layers rather than between them.

---

# 19. What the PASS licenses

This comparator licenses the provisional claim that:

> **The five-layer Joint Task Space grammar is not merely a nursing-home or institutional-ethics architecture. It appears capable of governing at least two substantially different inquiry topologies while preserving domain-native differences.**

It also licenses:

- retaining Gulf Pines as the first canonical reference implementation;
- adding Asteria-2047 as the first strong cross-domain comparator;
- beginning a small comparator corpus;
- testing whether the five discovered stressors recur.

---

# 20. What the PASS does not license

It does not establish:

- universal domain portability;
- that five layers are optimal;
- that planetary-defense governance should adopt FSP;
- that any specific intervention is justified;
- that all future-person or international-standing problems are solved;
- that model plurality is fully governed.

---

# 21. Reopen conditions

Reopen the Joint Task Space freeze if future comparators show that:

- probabilistic standing cannot be represented cleanly;
- future-person standing requires a distinct governance primitive;
- risk transfer cannot be handled within Repair + Standing;
- model plurality overwhelms the Translation layer;
- distributed Return produces unresolved authorization ambiguity;
- irreversible decisions require a new layer rather than stronger sequencing rules;
- Quick compression ceases to preserve meaning.
