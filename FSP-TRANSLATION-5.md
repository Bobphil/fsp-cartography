# FSP-TRANSLATION-5

## Cross-Domain Replication, Domain-Native Comparator Case, Primitive-Ablation Audit, and Publication-Claim Ceiling

**Status:** Cross-domain replication research layer  
**Boundary:** No Cartography-kernel change.

## 1. Purpose

TRANSLATION-1 through TRANSLATION-4 developed and stress-tested an ontologically agnostic translation grammar in one environmental-governance domain.

TRANSLATION-5 asks whether the same core primitives remain useful in a structurally different domain.

The comparator case is persistent-pain care.

This domain differs from the forest case because it includes:

- privileged first-person symptom access;
- clinical mechanism uncertainty;
- professional recommendation;
- insurer authorization;
- consent and treatment acceptability;
- cultural or spiritual meaning;
- agency risks created by unequal control over care access.

The replication question is therefore not:

> Can the forest formalism be copied into medicine?

It is:

> Which primitives survive when the domain's native topology changes?

## 2. Comparator PRFs

The persistent-pain case contains four PRFs:

- P — patient lived-experience framework;
- C — clinical biomedical framework;
- I — insurance/utilization framework;
- S — cultural-spiritual meaning framework.

Each has distinct evidence, standing, background commitments, ontology commitments, and claim ceilings.

The test explicitly protects several boundaries:

- first-person suffering is evidence but not a unique mechanism proof;
- clinical expertise licenses bounded recommendations but does not erase patient standing;
- insurer authorization is institutionally real but is not clinical truth;
- cultural-spiritual meaning may affect acceptable care without being imposed as shared metaphysics.

## 3. Cross-domain replication

The forest and persistent-pain cases are compared at the level of required primitives.

Replication passes only if:

- at least two domains are present;
- each has a complete directed translation matrix;
- at least one primitive survives across all domains.

The stronger result sought here is that the same full primitive set remains useful:

- TASK
- EVIDENCE
- WARRANT
- BACKGROUND
- CLAIM_CEILING
- ONTOLOGY
- STANDING
- REMAINDER
- AUXILIARY
- AGENCY

The domains may differ in their native topology while sharing this governance grammar.

## 4. Domain-native structure is not flattened

Cross-domain replication does not require the two domains to share substantive entities.

Forest inquiry includes watershed viability, property, place relation, stewardship, land-use institutions, and ecological consequence.

Persistent-pain care includes suffering, clinical mechanisms, function, coverage rules, treatment burden, consent, and cultural-spiritual meaning.

The shared FSP layer therefore operates at the level of governed translation rather than domain-specific ontology.

## 5. Primitive ablation

A primitive counts as non-redundant when removing it causes a recognizable loss.

The first ablation audit tests:

- TASK -> loss of task-boundedness;
- EVIDENCE -> loss of evidence provenance;
- WARRANT -> loss of licensing structure;
- BACKGROUND -> loss of context sensitivity;
- CLAIM_CEILING -> loss of claim restraint;
- ONTOLOGY -> loss of explicit ontological pluralism;
- STANDING -> loss of standing and anti-domination visibility;
- REMAINDER -> loss of translation-loss tracking;
- AUXILIARY -> loss of sensitivity to hidden support;
- AGENCY -> loss of anti-domination diagnostics.

This does not prove these are the minimal possible primitives.

It tests whether any of them are currently decorative.

## 6. Why the pain domain is a strong comparator

The pain case creates failure modes that the forest case cannot.

For example:

- a patient can have epistemic standing regarding experienced pain that no external observer can replace;
- a clinician can have standing regarding benefit-risk that the patient may not possess;
- an insurer can possess practical decision power without possessing medical authority over diagnosis;
- cultural or spiritual meaning may shape treatment acceptability without deciding biomedical mechanism.

If FSP collapses these distinctions, the cross-domain test should fail.

## 7. Publication-claim ceiling

TRANSLATION-5 adds an explicit publication ceiling.

The ceiling is not based on conceptual ambition.

It is based on the evidence currently earned.

With two structurally different constructed domains, the strongest licensed claim is:

> The tested FSP translation grammar has replicated across two structurally different constructed domains, and several primitives perform non-redundant representational work under ablation.

The following remain prohibited:

- all human PRFs are mutually translatable;
- FSP is universally domain-general;
- FSP proves the Duhem-Quine thesis;
- FSP supplies a complete theory of domination;
- two constructed cases establish validity for all medicine, ecology, religion, ethics, or culture.

## 8. What would raise the ceiling

A higher claim ceiling would require at least:

- additional domain-native replications;
- externally sourced cases rather than only constructed fixtures;
- comparator theories or alternative formalisms;
- out-of-sample case reconstruction;
- independent failure cases;
- human validation that the formal distinctions remain intelligible and non-distorting.

## 9. Relation to FSP-VR

Cross-domain replication matters directly for VR.

If the same governance primitives survive both forest conflict and pain care, VR can use a common interaction grammar while allowing the environments themselves to remain radically different.

The user could learn:

- in the forest, why property, ecology, stewardship, and relational ontology do not collapse into one map;
- in pain care, why patient experience, clinical expertise, insurer power, and cultural meaning do not collapse into one authority structure.

This is exactly the kind of transfer FSP-VR needs if it is to become an experiential integrator rather than a domain-specific simulation.

## 10. Research ceiling

TRANSLATION-5 is a cross-domain replication, not a universal validation.

Its strongest justified result is:

> A common task-relative translation and agency-governance grammar has survived two structurally different constructed domains without requiring the domains to share substantive ontology.

Recommended next research direction:

> replicate in a third domain whose primary difficulty is not environmental governance or care, such as historical interpretation, scientific model pluralism, or political legitimacy, and introduce an external comparator formalism.
