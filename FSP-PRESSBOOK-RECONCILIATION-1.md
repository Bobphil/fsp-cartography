# 1. Purpose

The frozen Reason-for-Being paper changes the relation between FSP and the two courses.

FSP is no longer best presented primarily as a classroom method.

It is a cross-domain governance architecture that can help track consequential inquiry across:

```text
lived Doing
↔ selective attention
↔ articulation / map
↔ evidence and reasons
↔ Tool-guided inspection
↔ Firewall-governed transitions
↔ bounded judgment
↔ standing / recommendation / authorization
↔ coordinated action
↔ institutional amplification
↔ consequences / capability effects
↔ repair
↔ REOPEN
↔ Authorial Return
↔ lived Doing
```

The Pressbooks should not reproduce this whole architecture chapter by chapter.

Instead:

> **The Pressbooks remain domain-native texts. FSP supplies the thin governance architecture that helps students move among lived experience, disciplinary inquiry, cases, judgment, institutions, and Return.**

This reconciliation therefore audits what should be:

- **KEEP**
- **INTRODUCTION-PATCH**
- **CHAPTER-MICRO-PATCH**
- **WORKBOOK-PATCH**
- **CASE-PATCH**
- **JOURNAL-PROTECT**
- **LAB-CULMINATION**
- **ENCYCLOPEDIA-LINK**
- **NO CHANGE**

The default is **KEEP**.

A patch is warranted only when the frozen canonical architecture now exposes a real omission or a cleaner student-facing relation.

# 3. Domain-native divergence

The two courses should share governance without becoming mirror images.

## Ethics primarily asks

- What is happening?
- What is the current map?
- What reasons support the judgment?
- What shaped the situation?
- Who is affected?
- Who has standing?
- What burdens and capabilities are at stake?
- What does the judgment authorize?
- What happens when it becomes institutional?
- What would REOPEN it?
- Who must now author the next action?

Canonical Ethics movement:

```text
lived ethical situation
→ map
→ reasons
→ bounded ethical judgment
→ standing
→ recommendation / authorization
→ action
→ consequence / capability effect
→ repair
→ REOPEN
→ Return
```

## Inquiry primarily asks

- What was observed or made measurable?
- What representation made the phenomenon available?
- What inference was licensed?
- What alternatives remained live?
- What concepts, instruments, or institutions shaped the inquiry?
- What did the model earn?
- What remainder stayed unresolved?
- What changed the inquiry state?
- What would REOPEN the settlement?
- What should be carried forward?

Canonical Inquiry movement:

```text
encounter / observation
→ representation
→ question
→ hypothesis / model
→ evidence
→ inferential license
→ alternatives
→ bounded epistemic judgment
→ institutional validation
→ anomaly / resistance
→ revision / REOPEN
→ Return to inquiry
```

Firewall:

> **Shared governance grammar ≠ shared inquiry topology.**

# 4. Cross-book modification policy

The reconciliation does **not** authorize:

- rewriting historical chapters into FSP prose;
- replacing philosophers or scientific episodes with Tool labels;
- putting Tool codes in front of students when human-readable names suffice;
- requiring Encyclopedia study before reading a Pressbook chapter;
- turning every case into a Joint Task Space;
- converting the Personal Ethics Journal into a structured FSP worksheet;
- inserting formal schemas where domain-native narrative already works;
- replacing tacit/practical judgment with explicit checklist completion.

The reconciliation **does** authorize:

- one compact front-matter update per book;
- selective claim-ceiling language;
- selective remainder prompts;
- clearer standing/authorization distinctions in Ethics;
- clearer model/representation/remainder distinctions in Inquiry;
- stronger Return prompts in workbooks;
- a late-course Joint Task Space culmination;
- optional Encyclopedia depth links;
- explicit preservation of domain-native inquiry topology.

# 5. Front-matter reconciliation — Ethics

## Existing status

KEEP the current front-matter identity:

**How We Will Think Through Ethical Problems**  
*A Critical-Thinking Guide for This Book*

KEEP the existing six student questions:

1. What is my map?
2. What are the reasons?
3. What shaped the situation?
4. Who is affected and who has standing?
5. What is my claim ceiling?
6. What would REOPEN this?

## Required INTRODUCTION-PATCH

Add one short paragraph after the six-question routine:

> These questions do not replace ethical theories or historical philosophers. They help you notice where an ethical inquiry is changing character. A judgment may be well supported without yet authorizing an intervention. A policy may be lawful without fully representing the people who live under it. A useful map may still leave something important out. As the course progresses, we will therefore ask not only what judgment is supported, but what remains outside the map, what the judgment authorizes, what happens when it becomes institutional, and what should return to lived action.

Add three compact distinctions later in the front matter:

> **Untranslated remainder ≠ irrelevant remainder.**

> **Supported judgment ≠ unrestricted authorization.**

> **Return means renewed responsibility in the changed situation, not merely repeating the original judgment.**

Do not add the full Joint Task Space grammar here.

# 7. Remainder integration

Remainder becomes a recurring but low-burden prompt in both books.

## Ethics remainder question

Use selectively:

> **What person, experience, burden, relationship, capability, or local knowledge is not represented well in the current map?**

Potential Ethics remainder objects:

- resident distress;
- family care;
- coercive background conditions;
- informal labor;
- practical dependency;
- moral injury;
- unequal burden;
- identity-relevant context;
- tacit professional knowledge.

## Inquiry remainder question

Use selectively:

> **What observation, uncertainty, alternative, model dependence, historical meaning, or domain-native feature is not carried well by the current representation?**

Potential Inquiry remainder objects:

- anomalous observation;
- auxiliary assumption;
- model-form uncertainty;
- conceptual role lost in translation;
- historical context;
- tacit experimental practice;
- instrument limitation;
- unresolved interpretation.

## Frequency rule

Do not add a remainder prompt to every chapter mechanically.

Use it when:

- a map is clearly lossy;
- a translation occurs;
- a metric/model compresses lived or empirical complexity;
- an institution amplifies the representation;
- a historical framework change changes what can be said.

Firewall:

> **Remainder prompt ≠ required mystery box.**

# 10. Return expansion — both courses

## Ethics Output Card terminal sequence

2. Claim ceiling
3. Authorization boundary
4. Next action / responsible response

1. Current state of the inquiry

# 17A. Four-Move Governance Reconciliation

The recent constitutional compression changes the hidden governance grammar but does not require a Pressbook rewrite.

```text
PRESSBOOK STUDENT SURFACE
domain-native narrative + six-question routine
        ↓
WORKBOOK
chapter-specific Tool / move
        ↓
OUTPUT CARD
Portability + Ceiling + Authorization when relevant + Remainder + Return
        ↓
JOURNAL
reflective lived authorship
```

The four moves function as a **cross-course semantic envelope**:

## Portability

Ask what from the philosopher, historical episode, case, measurement, model, or prior result may responsibly be carried into the present judgment.

## Ceiling

State the strongest conclusion earned.

## Authorization

In Ethics, progressively distinguish judgment, recommendation, consent, standing, authority, and implementation.

In Inquiry, use Authorization only when epistemic results cross into public, institutional, technological, policy, or intervention contexts.

## Return

Return the bounded judgment to consequences, changed conditions, successor inquiry, and explicit REOPEN.

## Remainder

Keep unresolved persons, burdens, alternatives, translation loss, uncertainty, and contested standing visible throughout.

### Reconciliation rule

> **The Pressbooks teach the domain; the four moves govern transfer through the domain.**
