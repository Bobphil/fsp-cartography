# 1. History of Philosophy

## What this domain makes visible

The History of Philosophy domain treats philosophy historically as a set of situated human inquiries rather than as a sequence of doctrines detached from their problem contexts.

It asks:

- Where are we in the historical problem space?
- What became salient or problematic?
- What public philosophical artifact was produced?
- What kind of inquiry was being performed?
- What gave particular moves standing?
- What was challenged, inherited, sedimented, or reopened?
- What became available for later inquiry?

The canonical historical spine is:

```text
situated Doing
→ public artifact
→ uptake / contest
→ conceptual sediment
→ changed task space
→ REOPEN
```

## Native inquiry topologies

The domain does not assume one universal philosophical form.

Existing stress tests preserve multiple topologies, including:

- serial / reconstructive inquiry;
- dialogical / aporetic inquiry;
- genealogical / diagnostic inquiry;
- field / transition inquiry;
- historically situated model change.

The PHIL-HIST stress tests established a light constitutional shell:

```text
Locate
→ Represent
→ Attribute
→ Type
→ License
→ Challenge
→ Fork / REOPEN
→ Continue
```

This is a governance shell, not a claim that every philosopher reasons in the same sequence.

## What this domain contributes to FSP

History of Philosophy contributes:

- context recovery;
- public-artifact reconstruction;
- inquiry-topology preservation;
- attribution discipline;
- distinction between reconstruction and endorsement;
- inheritance and sedimentation tracking;
- historical provenance;
- selective carry-forward;
- REOPEN across changing task spaces.

It also supplies an important corrective to overly argument-centered approaches:

> **Argument analysis is a local zoom, not a universal history-of-philosophy chapter structure.**

## Constitutional firewalls

Dialogical / aporetic:

> **Speaker utterance ≠ authorial commitment.**

> **Refutation ≠ positive doctrine.**

> **Aporia ≠ failure.**

Genealogical / diagnostic:

> **Origin ≠ validity.**

> **Historical explanation ≠ normative verdict.**

> **Psychological diagnosis ≠ complete refutation.**

> **Rhetorical force ≠ evidential force.**

These are historical-governance constraints, not interpretations of every text.

## What this domain does not own

History of Philosophy does not by itself determine:

- whether an inherited claim is true;
- whether historical origin defeats present validity;
- whether a reconstructed argument is sound;
- whether an institution should now act;
- whether later categories should be projected backward.

Firewall:

> **Historical reconstruction ≠ present justification.**

## Carry Forward

The distinctive public question is:

> **What may we carry forward?**

Carry-forward is not simple retention.

A historical object may be:

- retained;
- narrowed;
- translated;
- contested;
- recontextualized;
- abandoned;
- reopened.

The domain records change, loss, and unresolved remainder rather than pretending inheritance is seamless.

## Worked entrances

Public resources already available:

- *What May We Carry Forward?* Pressbook;
- History of Inquiry SUPER workspace.

The future Encyclopedia role is to connect those native historical routes to canonical concepts, Tools, cases, source ledgers, and cross-domain comparisons without turning the historical course into an FSP manual.

## Claim ceiling

The existing PHIL-HIST regression work demonstrates design conformance across multiple inquiry topologies.

It does not establish philosophical correctness, pedagogical superiority, or exhaustive coverage of the history of philosophy.

> **Topology preservation ≠ historical or philosophical validation.**

# 2. Logic & Argument

## What this domain makes visible

Logic & Argument makes inferential structure inspectable.

It asks:

- What is the claim?
- What supports it?
- Is support linked or convergent?
- What warrant connects grounds to claim?
- What backing supports the warrant?
- What qualifies the conclusion?
- What defeats or attacks the argument?
- What is the scope?
- What is the provenance?
- What authority or standing is being carried across?

The domain preserves ordinary logical and argumentation analysis while adding governance around transitions.

## Toulmin crosswalk and typed argument records

The existing EHI-ARG architecture crosswalks:

- claim;
- grounds;
- warrant;
- backing;
- qualifier;
- rebuttal

into provenance-bearing typed records.

The Narrowing / Upgrading / Inheritance discipline blocks unlicensed movement of:

- status;
- force;
- authority

across type boundaries.

## Attack-type grammar

The current attack grammar distinguishes at least seven targets:

- ground;
- claim;
- warrant;
- backing;
- scope;
- provenance;
- authority.

This matters because not every disagreement attacks the same part of an argument.

## Executable argument behavior

The executable harness distinguishes:

- linked support;
- convergent support;
- unsupported cycles;
- selective REOPEN;
- independently revalidated support.

Settled behaviors include:

- linked support becomes unsupported when a required premise is defeated;
- convergent support may remain supported when one independent source fails;
- unsupported cycles remain unsupported;
- REOPEN propagates through current dependents and stops where support has been independently revalidated.

## What this domain contributes to FSP

Logic & Argument contributes:

- inferential audit;
- typed support relations;
- attack targeting;
- warrant visibility;
- provenance;
- scope control;
- defeater handling;
- selective dependency-sensitive REOPEN;
- separation of argumentative support from authority and authorization.

The strongest public claim is not that FSP replaces mature argumentation systems.

It is:

> **Argument structures can be embedded inside a wider governance architecture without losing provenance, claim ceilings, authority boundaries, or selective revision.**

## Comparator result

In authored internal tests:

- the full EHI-ARG harness classified all 25 authored discrimination cases correctly;
- simpler Toulmin and structured-graph baselines each classified 12/25;
- all tested ablations lost at least one case.

But a stronger comparator changed the interpretation.

Across 36 authored cases:

- EHI and the Strong Combined comparator both reached complete parity.

Therefore:

> **Internal discrimination ≠ superiority to mature argumentation systems.**

The public domain should frame EHI-ARG as a possible integration/governance layer, not a novel universal argumentation theory.

## AIF-compatible direction

The later architecture moves toward:

- AIF-compatible argument graphs;
- governance metadata;
- published-corpus interoperability;
- provenance-preserving annotations;
- selective revision dependency.

The most important design lesson is:

> **Challenge ≠ automatic REOPEN.**

A challenge or correction event is primary.

REOPEN should be derived only where current dependency requires revision.

## What this domain does not own

Logic & Argument does not by itself determine:

- what should matter morally;
- who has institutional authorization;
- whether historical context defeats a claim;
- whether a scientific model is ontologically true;
- whether a supported conclusion should become policy.

Firewall:

> **Supported argument ≠ authorized action.**

And:

> **Logical support ≠ complete practical justification.**

## Worked entrance

The current public Encyclopedia can expose Logic & Argument as an explanatory domain before a full argument browser is certified.

Its first public role should be:

- explain the governance contribution;
- connect to Critical Thinking;
- connect to T02 Examination / Reason-Giving Audit;
- show attack-target distinctions;
- show selective REOPEN;
- preserve the claim ceiling around novelty and superiority.

A dedicated executable public argument explorer can come later.

## Claim ceiling

The existing tests support:

- authored internal discrimination;
- comparator parity;
- schema/governance coverage;
- selective-REOPEN design.

They do not establish:

- empirical human reliability;
- mathematical novelty;
- philosophical priority;
- superiority over mature argumentation frameworks.

> **Governance integration ≠ new argumentation theory.**

# 3. Cross-domain relation

History of Philosophy and Logic & Argument overlap but must not collapse.

History asks:

> What kind of inquiry was this, in this historical problem space, and what became available afterward?

Logic & Argument asks:

> What supports what, what is attacked, and what follows?

A historical inquiry may contain arguments.

But the argument graph is not the whole historical inquiry.

Likewise, an argument can be validly represented without reconstructing the full historical Doing that produced it.

Firewall:

> **Argument structure ≠ historical inquiry topology.**

This is the key boundary between the two domains.

# 4. Public reader rule

History of Philosophy should foreground:

- native inquiry topology;
- context;
- artifact;
- uptake;
- sedimentation;
- Carry Forward / REOPEN.

Logic & Argument should foreground:

- support;
- warrant;
- attack;
- scope;
- provenance;
- dependency;
- selective REOPEN.

Both connect to the FSP constitutional core.

Neither becomes the universal grammar of the Encyclopedia.

This deepening makes the two domains publicly coherent.

It does not mean either domain is fully ingested into the Encyclopedia.

> **Public entrance ≠ completed domain library.**
