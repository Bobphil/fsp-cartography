# FSP-REVISION-9 Certification

Status: **PASS AS INSTITUTIONAL REPAIR-SEQUENCING / CORRIGIBILITY GOVERNANCE BUILD**

REVISION-9 extends REVISION-8 from identifying repair windows to governing the repair process itself.

Certified:

- repair steps are typed, ordered, and dependency-sensitive;
- institutional repair remains non-recommending;
- capability gains, losses, and unknowns remain visible by group;
- no aggregate net score can erase distributional conflict;
- limited Pareto-style improvement is identified only when no represented losses or unknowns remain;
- exit, voice, and appeal are audited independently;
- formal exit does not count as meaningful when costs are prohibitive;
- appeal requires independence;
- reversible repair supports experimentation only when rollback remains actually available;
- irreversible repair triggers a higher-evidence-burden flag;
- every expected generation may retain REOPEN standing;
- evidence, capability, and minority-report review remain part of later-generation reopening;
- first-generation approval does not bind future generations.

Current strongest claim:

> FSP can represent institutional repair as an ordered, group-sensitive, corrigible process that preserves capability tradeoffs, audits meaningful exit/voice/appeal, distinguishes reversible from irreversible changes, and keeps REOPEN available across generations without collapsing repair into an aggregate optimization rule.

Not licensed:

- a universal optimal repair sequence;
- a complete compensation principle;
- a complete theory of democratic legitimacy;
- a complete theory of intergenerational justice;
- the claim that reversible repair is always preferable;
- any upgrade to TRANSLATION-12 human-validation status.
