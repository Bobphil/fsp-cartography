## Certified Encyclopedia identity

The FSP Encyclopedia is defined as a multi-domain shared resource for inquiry, judgment, interpretation, action, institutions, and philosophical practice.

The scientific-inquiry collection is the first mature public collection.

It is not the scope definition of the Encyclopedia.

## Domain-ontology firewall

The certified rule is:

`Shared FSP grammar does not imply shared domain ontology.`

Ethics is not required to behave like scientific theory change.

Logic is not required to use the ontology of Ethics.

Aesthetics may introduce perception-, artwork-, interpretation-, and practice-native objects.

New domain-native structures may be added when fidelity requires them.

Architectural elegance may not override domain fidelity.

## Canonical ownership

One canonical resource has one canonical owner.

Routes and projections may:

- select;
- compress;
- annotate;
- sequence;
- project;
- add domain-specific apertures.

They may not silently create a competing canonical resource.

The first ownership examples include:

- canonical Claim Ceiling;
- canonical REOPEN;
- Neptune/Vulcan as a Science case;
- Aristotle as a shared historical master.

The Aristotle pattern freezes the intended historical reuse model:

```text
ARISTOTLE — shared historical master
   ├─ Ethics aperture
   ├─ Logic aperture
   ├─ Aesthetics aperture
   └─ History-of-Philosophy route
```

## Resource depths

Three production depths are certified:

### QUICK

Orientation, recovery, and first-pass student access.

### STANDARD

Full readable Encyclopedia/course account.

### DEEP

Source-rich, formally inspectable, controversy-aware reference.

Depth may compress:

- detail;
- notation;
- source saturation;
- specialist apparatus.

Depth may not change:

- canonical identity;
- provenance;
- claim ceiling;
- historical sequence where applicable;
- domain-native meaning.

## Governance

The certified governance rules include:

1. The Encyclopedia is broader than any one domain, book, course, application, or projection.
2. Shared FSP grammar does not imply shared domain ontology.
3. Domain-native objects remain primary where the universal core is too thin.
4. One canonical resource has one canonical owner.
5. Shared historical figures/texts prefer reusable historical masters plus domain apertures.
6. Course/book route ≠ canonical ownership.
7. Resource depth changes presentation, not semantics.
8. Public status drives discoverability.
9. Cross-domain relations remain typed.
10. New domains may extend ontology when fidelity requires it.
11. Whole↔part return must work at resource, domain, route, and Encyclopedia scales.
12. Production maturity does not imply philosophical priority.

## Claim ceiling

FSP-ENCYCLOPEDIA-SCOPE-1 certifies the multi-domain scope architecture.

It does not yet integrate the full Ethics, History of Philosophy, Logic, or Aesthetics content into the public Encyclopedia.

It also does not determine the final taxonomy of every future domain.

Those integrations and domain boundaries remain REOPENable while inheriting the governance rules certified here.
