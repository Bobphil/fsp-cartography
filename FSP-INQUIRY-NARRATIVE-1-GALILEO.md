# FSP-INQUIRY-NARRATIVE-1 — Galileo Adversarial Extension

## Purpose

Apply the whole-inquiry narrative-spine contract to a case whose difficulty is structurally different from Neptune/Vulcan.

Galileo is a stronger test because the inquiry changes simultaneously across:

- evidence;
- instrument standing;
- conceptual meaning;
- standards of demonstration;
- interpretive jurisdiction;
- institutional authority;
- practical power.

The build therefore tests whether whole-inquiry recovery still works when the historical problem cannot be represented as one clean anomaly/repair trajectory.

## Narrative spine

The Galileo spine carries the reader from the inherited early-modern cosmological field through:

- 1609–1610 telescope work and `Sidereus Nuncius`;
- Jovian satellites and lunar observations;
- the phases of Venus and changing cosmological constraints;
- 1613–1616 disputes over demonstration, scriptural interpretation, and jurisdiction;
- the 1632 `Dialogue`;
- the 1633 trial and condemnation.

The narrative explicitly rejects two compressions:

1. new evidence instantly and uniquely proves the entire Copernican system;
2. institutional condemnation settles the epistemic truth of geocentrism.

## Four chapter apertures

### Chapter 1

Evidence constrains a theory space without automatically selecting a complete system.

### Chapter 2

Words such as `demonstration`, `hypothesis`, `motion`, and `authority` are historically situated and can change inferential role.

### Chapter 3

Controlled probes may vary cosmological accommodation or standing while holding historical evidence fixed.

### Chapter 4

The formalism tracks coupled changes in evidence, meaning, standing, jurisdiction, and power without collapsing them into one variable.

## New firewalls stress-tested

- instrument standing ≠ theory finality;
- interpretive jurisdiction ≠ epistemic truth;
- institutional condemnation ≠ empirical demonstration;
- power can constrain inquiry without becoming evidential warrant;
- probe possibility ≠ historical actuality;
- historical chronology ≠ canonical state identity.

## Claim ceiling

This extension shows that the narrative-spine contract can survive a coupled institutional/epistemic case without reducing the episode either to pure evidence history or pure power history.

It does not certify the Galileo narrative as a final historiography. Source interpretation, milestone granularity, and emphasis remain REOPENable.
