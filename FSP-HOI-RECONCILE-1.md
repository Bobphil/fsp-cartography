# FSP-HOI-RECONCILE-1

## History of Inquiry Pressbook Reconciliation

**Source lock:** PHIL-HIST-TEXT-LOCK-2  
**Book:** *What May We Carry Forward? A History of Philosophy as Situated Human Inquiry*

## Executive decision

This is a reconciliation, not a rewrite.

The locked manuscript already contains the right disciplinary center of gravity: philosophy is encountered historically as situated human inquiry, and the book's recurring developmental movement is:

```text
inherited world
→ live problem
→ situated inquiry
→ public artifact
→ uptake and contest
→ sediment
→ changed later task
→ reopening
```

Recent FSP work strengthens navigation, context recovery, temporal anchoring, canonical linking, and return. It does **not** justify rewriting the historical chapters as an introduction to FSP.

## Locked architecture preserved

Preserve:

- Prologue;
- five Parts;
- Chapters 1–14 identities and order;
- Inquiry Window A after Chapter 2;
- Inquiry Window B after Chapter 3;
- Historical Bridge C inside Chapter 4;
- Inquiry Window D after Chapter 13;
- Chapter 14 as hinge;
- six Contemporary Inquiry Laboratories as Laboratories, not Chapters 15–20;
- Epilogue as final reader return;
- no winner/tournament narrative;
- no secret-FSP/EHI historical teleology;
- domain-native laboratory topology;
- stable-core / versioned-frontier source architecture.

## What the reconciliation adds

Each numbered chapter now receives a reconciliation manifest containing:

1. **Disciplinary question** — what philosophical/historical development the chapter is actually about.
2. **Developmental move** — the change from inherited problem to altered later task.
3. **Whole-inquiry recovery** — inherited world, live problem, situated inquiry, public artifact, uptake/contest, sediment, changed later task, reopening.
4. **Temporal anchor** — a guard against later concepts leaking backward.
5. **Student-facing compression** — ordinary-language habits already native to the book.
6. **Canonical Encyclopedia links** — optional depth/recovery routes.
7. **Preserve locks** — chapter-native topology that reconciliation may not standardize.
8. **No-FSP requirement** — explicit student-success criterion.

## FSP compression policy

The student-facing course may use a small number of already-natural terms:

- claim ceiling;
- carry forward;
- reopening;
- standing;
- map;
- sedimentation;
- Doing.

These terms are used because they clarify philosophical development, not because students are being trained in FSP as a framework.

Formal backplane terms are not course prerequisites:

- FSP state vector;
- relation graph;
- canonical fingerprint;
- projection profile;
- reducer;
- invariant suite;
- formal transition vector;
- ecosystem binding.

The course remains successful if a student never learns those terms.

## Three modes remain distinct

### Historical chapters

Historical prose first. Each inquiry keeps its own topology: dialogical, layered, architectonic, genealogical, reconstructive, therapeutic, or otherwise.

### Historical interventions

Inquiry Windows and Historical Bridge C share navigation but not historical relation type. Pedagogical parallel never becomes genealogy without independent evidence.

### Contemporary laboratories

The Labs retain common governance devices plus domain-native devices. They are current reopenings, not destinations the earlier history was unknowingly approaching.

## Canonical Encyclopedia links

Links are optional by default.

They serve three purposes:

- recovery when a student needs more context;
- deeper inspection for interested readers;
- reuse of canonical cases/concepts without duplicating them in the Pressbook.

Examples include:

- Chapter 5 → Galileo whole inquiry;
- Chapter 7/9 → Newton→Einstein where representation/framework change genuinely helps;
- Chapter 8 → Neptune/Vulcan and DESI for later prediction/revision reopenings;
- Chapter 12 → REOPEN and DESI;
- Chapter 13 → Standing and optional Galileo comparison.

No chapter may require the student to browse the Encyclopedia in order to satisfy its core disciplinary objectives.

## No-FSP-Course regression

The build adds an executable audit.

It fails if:

- formal FSP machinery becomes a student prerequisite;
- a chapter's disciplinary question becomes “learn FSP”;
- historical prose is subordinated to framework vocabulary;
- Later reopening becomes “anticipated” or “predicted”;
- historical comparison becomes genealogy without evidence;
- one common worksheet replaces the three-mode page grammar.

The success criterion is:

> A student can meet every chapter objective, complete course assessments, and explain the book's developmental history without defining FSP or using formal FSP machinery.

## Chapter-level result

All fourteen numbered chapters now have a reconciliation contract.

The contracts preserve their disciplinary development:

1. Socratic examination and bounded aporia.
2. Aristotelian flourishing across a life.
3. Hellenistic philosophy under pressure.
4. Inheritance, revelation, transmission, and authority.
5. Method, mechanism, institutions, and political reconstruction.
6. Cartesian ordered doubt and stage-bounded carry-forward.
7. Representation and the external-world problem.
8. Humean causation, induction, self, and is/ought limits.
9. Kantian conditions of objectivity and autonomy.
10. Welfare, aggregation, liberty, and public use.
11. Genealogy and values acquiring histories.
12. Fallibilist inquiry that can learn.
13. Situated evidence, embodiment, freedom, and power.
14. Plural expertise, prediction, cooperation, and live contemporary inquiry.

## Production consequence

The next Pressbooks implementation should be a **thin patch layer**, not a manuscript rewrite:

- context-recovery boxes;
- temporal/developmental cues;
- selected Later reopening updates;
- optional Encyclopedia links;
- return-to-whole cues;
- source-status clarification where required.

The locked historical prose remains the baseline unless a specific disciplinary gap is demonstrated.

## Claim ceiling

FSP-HOI-RECONCILE-1 certifies a reconciliation architecture and chapter-level patch manifest.

It does not claim that the 135k-word Pressbook has already been fully republished with these patches, nor that all classroom/assessment materials have been regenerated.

Those are downstream production gates.
