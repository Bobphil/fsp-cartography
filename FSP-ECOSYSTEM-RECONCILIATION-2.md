# 2. Present Modesty, Future Ambition

The recent comparator work suggested a cautious present-tense formulation:

> **FSP is not presently a complete account of cognition, meaning, agency, consciousness, or inquiry.**

That statement should remain.

But it should not be confused with a permanent prohibition on comprehensive theory.

FSP can also make a stronger long-horizon claim:

> **FSP is a translational and governance architecture designed to make heterogeneous partial inquiries mutually inspectable, selectively interoperable, recursively corrigible, and progressively integrable. If the relevant domain inquiries were to mature sufficiently—and if their cross-domain bridges could be warranted rather than assumed—FSP or a successor architecture could support the formulation of increasingly comprehensive, and perhaps in principle complete, accounts of cognition, meaning, agency, consciousness, inquiry, and their relations.**

This is not a contradiction.

It distinguishes:

```text
CURRENT ARCHITECTURAL ROLE
≠
FUTURE SYNTHETIC POSSIBILITY
```

---

# 3. The Completion-Horizon Thesis

Adopt provisionally:

## COMPLETION-HORIZON THESIS

> **A governance architecture need not itself contain every mature domain theory in order to be the structure through which those theories could eventually become responsibly integrated.**

FSP's present role is to govern:

- what local theories establish;
- where their results travel;
- what translation is required;
- what remains unresolved;
- what claims survive integration;
- what actions are authorized;
- what later evidence reopens the synthesis.

If domain inquiry continues toward increasingly complete accounts, FSP's role may shift from:

```text
governance among partial maps
```

toward:

```text
governance of an increasingly integrated map-of-maps
```

without assuming that completeness has already been achieved.

---

# 4. Completion Is a Regulative Horizon, Not a Current Status

The word "complete" must be governed.

A future comprehensive account would require, at minimum:

1. mature domain-native theories;
2. explicit bridge relations among them;
3. tested translation rules;
4. preserved remainder;
5. cross-scale and cross-perspectival calibration;
6. no silent ontology inflation;
7. no warrant laundering across boundaries;
8. explicit unresolved alternatives;
9. integration that changes predictions, explanation, or inquiry;
10. continuing REOPEN conditions.

Therefore:

> **Integration ≠ completeness.**

> **Breadth ≠ completeness.**

> **One vocabulary covering many domains ≠ completeness.**

> **Formal unification ≠ empirical completion.**

---

# 5. A Stronger Future FSP Claim — Only Under Earned Conditions

FSP may eventually aspire to something like:

> **FSP provides a comprehensive account of cognition, meaning, agency, consciousness, and inquiry as mutually related structures within lived, biological, social, institutional, technological, and physical reality.**

But that formulation is licensed only if:

- relevant domain sciences are mature enough;
- bridge burdens are explicit;
- ontology differences are either resolved or interoperably bounded;
- phenomenal remainder is appropriately handled;
- agentive and normative transitions are preserved;
- competing models have survived severe tests;
- no unresolved remainder is hidden merely to achieve closure.

Until then, the correct grammar is:

> **FSP is building the translational and governance architecture within which such a comprehensive account could be responsibly pursued.**

---

# 6. FSP as Scaffold for Successor Frameworks

The Completion-Horizon Thesis should not imply that the final architecture must still be called FSP.

A mature successor may:

- replace FSP objects;
- merge distinctions;
- introduce better mathematics;
- absorb domain theories;
- discover that some current boundaries are artifacts;
- discover new irreducible boundaries;
- require representational structures FSP does not presently contain.

Thus:

> **FSP is committed to the inquiry, not to its own permanent vocabulary.**

A successful successor that renders FSP obsolete would be a success of the FSP research programme, not a defeat.

# 36. Research Completion Ladder

To connect present governance with future comprehensiveness, introduce a research ladder.

## R0 — Local Map

A domain has useful local descriptions.

## R1 — Local Warrant

The descriptions support reliable local claims.

## R2 — Internal Integration

Major subdomain relations are mapped.

## R3 — Boundary Portability

Results survive specified cross-context transitions.

## R4 — Cross-Domain Translation

Translation anchors and bridge burdens are explicit.

## R5 — Operational Interoperability

Different domains coordinate without forced ontology identity.

## R6 — Explanatory Integration

Integrated structures produce explanatory or predictive gains.

## R7 — Cross-Perspectival Integration

First-/third-person, agentive/observational, or heterogeneous access relations become explicitly governed.

## R8 — Normative / Authorization Integration

Knowledge-to-action relations are integrated without collapsing epistemic and moral authority.

## R9 — Recursive Return

Consequences systematically update the integrated architecture.

## R10 — Comprehensive Synthesis Candidate

A theory may responsibly claim broad comprehensiveness only when major remainders and alternative bridges are explicit and tested.

This ladder is research governance, not a guaranteed historical sequence.
