---

# 1. Review question

FSP-CT-CANONICAL-1 proposes the opening sequence:

```text
situated human inquiry
→ assembly histories
→ reflective critical thinking
→ governed inquiry
```

This review asks whether that opening can serve as a defensible canonical foundation for later domain projections into the History of Ethics and History of Inquiry Pressbooks.

The test is deliberately adversarial. The question is not whether nearby traditions can be made to sound similar to FSP. The question is whether the canonical narrative:

1. makes claims compatible with relevant empirical and philosophical work;
2. avoids borrowing stronger theses than FSP needs;
3. preserves genuine disagreements among cognitive-scientific and philosophical frameworks;
4. distinguishes philosophical reconstruction from empirical developmental sequence;
5. preserves FSP's own claim ceilings;
6. makes the smallest changes necessary before freeze.

The review therefore distinguishes four relations:

- **supported:** the proposed language is directly compatible with substantial empirical or scholarly work;
- **convergent:** FSP and a comparator independently emphasize a similar structural feature;
- **compatible:** FSP need not conflict with the comparator, but the comparator does not establish the FSP claim;
- **contested:** accepting the FSP language as written would silently take sides in an unresolved dispute.

---

# 2. Executive verdict

The opening triad **survives the stress test**, but it should not yet be frozen.

The strongest result is that the basic human starting point is robust:

> Human inquiry is embodied, environmentally situated, socially scaffolded, historically formed, active, fallible, and capable of revision.

Developmental research on infant attention, active learning, motor development, statistical learning, self-representation, and social understanding supports a picture in which learning emerges through interactions among organism, body, environment, caregivers, and developing capacities rather than through detached adult-style reasoning from the outset.

Phenomenology strongly supports beginning from lived, embodied, perspectival engagement rather than from an abstract spectator. Pragmatism strongly supports inquiry as an activity arising within problematic situations, shaped by habit, action, consequences, and revision. Enactive and broader embodied approaches strongly support organism–environment coupling, action-perception loops, and the importance of bodily engagement.

However, the same comparators expose one important overreach in the current wording:

> **FSP currently risks making "map" sound like a theory of the cognitive mechanism by which all human awareness occurs.**

That is stronger than FSP needs and unnecessarily commits the framework within the representation/enactivism dispute.

The correct repair is not to abandon **Map ≠ World ≠ Person**. It is to clarify the level at which "map" functions in FSP:

> **Map is a governance term for whatever selective, perspective-bound articulation of a situation becomes available for inspection, reasoning, communication, coordination, memory, or action. It does not entail that all cognition consists in internal mental representations.**

With that repair, the opening triad becomes considerably stronger.

A second corrective is needed for **assembly history**. Current wording can sound as though persons are externally assembled from discrete influences. Empirical developmental systems work and pragmatist/enactive accounts emphasize active exploration, self-generated feedback, organismic activity, and reciprocal organism–environment change. Assembly history should therefore explicitly include **active self-formation and reciprocal co-construction**, not merely inherited inputs.

A third corrective concerns **reflective inquiry**. Developmental work distinguishes early forms of active learning and prereflective awareness from later explicit conceptual self-representation. Phenomenology also sharply distinguishes prereflective self-awareness from reflective thematization. FSP should therefore state:

> **Inquiry does not begin with reflection. Reflective critical thinking is a later capacity to make some ongoing inquiry processes available for explicit inspection.**

These three corrections are sufficient. No new Tool, failure mode, constitutional primitive, or claim ceiling is required.

# 3. Stress test A — developmental and cognitive science

## 3.1 What survives strongly

The canonical narrative begins with humans as active, situated learners rather than passive recipients of information. This is well aligned with important strands of developmental science.

Work on infant learning emphasizes that infants actively select attentional targets and intervene in their environments rather than merely absorbing sensory input. Research on embodied and embedded learning similarly emphasizes feedback loops among infant activity, caregiver response, perceptual structure, and environmental regularity. Motor-development research shows that bodily capacities alter opportunities for exploration and can generate cascades of cognitive and social development. Statistical-learning research shows sensitivity to regularities in environmental input, while research on attention and memory emphasizes that learning depends on interacting developing systems rather than a single acquisition channel.

This supports the broad FSP starting point:

```text
organism in environment
↔ selective attention and activity
↔ feedback
↔ learning and revision
```

It does **not** support a literal universal sequence of encounter → notice → distinguish → interpret → anticipate → act → revise. The canonical text already protects against this overclaim by labeling that arc a philosophical reconstruction. That firewall should remain.

## 3.2 Important developmental correction: activity precedes explicit reflection

Developmental science makes it especially important not to project adult reflective capacities backward.

Infants can learn statistical regularities, explore actively, form expectations, respond to novelty, learn through action, and participate in social interaction without possessing adult conceptual self-reflection.

Research on self-representation is particularly useful here: explicit or conceptual forms of self-representation emerge developmentally; they should not be conflated with the earlier embodied organization of perception and action.

Therefore the canonical triad should not be read as:

```text
first the child consciously maps
then reflects on the map
then reasons
```

The safer structure is:

```text
lived / active inquiry
→ increasingly articulable maps and habits
→ some inquiry becomes reflectively inspectable
→ explicit critical thinking can govern selected transitions
```

## 3.3 Developmental correction to assembly history

The phrase **assembly history** remains useful, but it needs a reciprocal-development clause.

Current language rightly includes caregivers, language, institutions, culture, technologies, rewards, practices, and accidents. But development is not merely input accumulation. Active exploration generates data; new bodily abilities reorganize what can be encountered; actions alter environments; caregivers respond to children; emerging skills change future opportunities.

Thus:

> **Assembly history is not a list of external causes that built a passive person. It is the temporally extended history through which organism, body, activity, relationships, environments, practices, and institutions reciprocally shaped the capacities and maps now available.**

This wording preserves the FSP concept while avoiding a passive socialization model.

## 3.4 Cognitive-science claim ceiling

Cognitive science contains live disagreements about mental representation, computation, predictive processing, embodied cognition, dynamic systems, and enactivism.

FSP should not use its map metaphor to decide those disputes by definition.

Add the firewall:

> **FSP map ≠ commitment to a particular cognitive architecture.**

The word "map" belongs to the level of inquiry governance: it identifies something selective and inspectable that stands in a relation to what is being engaged, described, remembered, modeled, or communicated.

# 4. Stress test B — phenomenology

## 4.1 Strong convergence

Phenomenology provides strong support for the claim that human inquiry should not be introduced from a view-from-nowhere.

Classical phenomenology analyzes experience as perspectival and intentional. Heidegger emphasizes being-in-the-world and practical involvement. Merleau-Ponty emphasizes embodied perception and the lived body. Contemporary phenomenological accounts distinguish prereflective embodied self-awareness from later reflective awareness.

This converges closely with FSP's decision to begin with **situated human inquiry** rather than propositions or arguments in abstraction.

The important FSP gain is that the person's relation to the world is not initially:

```text
detached observer
→ neutral data
→ explicit inference
```

but more plausibly:

```text
embodied practical engagement
↔ salience
↔ orientation
↔ action
↔ others
↔ emerging articulation
```

This is a better foundation for the Encyclopedia.

## 4.2 Phenomenological pressure on "map"

Phenomenology also exposes the most serious wording risk.

Heideggerian and Merleau-Pontian traditions resist treating ordinary coping as primarily representational. Much skilled activity occurs without an explicit internal model becoming thematic. A hammer can be encountered first as something used, not first as a neutral object represented and subsequently assigned a practical function.

Therefore FSP should avoid:

> Human beings cannot operate on the world except by first constructing representations.

That sentence would be too strong.

Prefer:

> Human beings encounter the world from selective, embodied, historically formed perspectives. When aspects of that engagement become available for recall, articulation, comparison, reasoning, communication, modeling, or coordination, FSP treats the resulting selective articulation as a **map** for governance purposes.

This preserves **Map ≠ World ≠ Person** without turning phenomenology into representationalism.

## 4.3 Reflection must not erase prereflective experience

Phenomenology distinguishes living through experience from making experience thematic.

This strongly supports the FSP triad if the transition is stated carefully:

> **Reflective inquiry does not create inquiry from nothing. It makes some dimensions of already ongoing lived inquiry available for inspection.**

This is preferable to saying simply that critical thinking "begins" when inquiry becomes reflective, because some forms of discrimination, correction, learning, and adaptive responsiveness precede explicit reflection.

## 4.4 FSP delta from phenomenology

FSP should not claim phenomenology as its foundation or treat the traditions as equivalent.

Phenomenology primarily contributes methods and analyses of lived experience, intentionality, embodiment, temporality, intersubjectivity, and practical engagement.

FSP's distinctive question is governance:

> Once something becomes represented, articulated, compared, reasoned from, institutionalized, or acted upon, what licenses the transition and what ceiling constrains it?

Thus:

**phenomenological description ≠ FSP governance**, even where they converge.

# 5. Stress test C — pragmatism

## 5.1 Strong convergence with inquiry rather than static knowledge

Pragmatist philosophy is perhaps the cleanest historical comparator for FSP's process orientation.

Peircean and Deweyan traditions foreground inquiry as an activity rather than treating knowledge only as a finished state. Dewey's account of habit emphasizes that habits arise through organism–environment transactions and can be reconstructed when situations become problematic. Pragmatist inquiry is action-oriented, consequence-sensitive, fallibilist, and revisable.

This strongly converges with:

```text
situation
→ inquiry
→ action
→ consequence
→ revision
```

and with FSP's insistence that current judgment is not final closure.

## 5.2 Assembly history gains philosophical depth from habit

Dewey helps clarify assembly history.

A habit is not merely an inner stored rule, nor a passive imprint. Habits are acquired ways of responding formed through transactions with social and material environments. Group practices become customs. Reflection can arise when habits no longer carry activity adequately through a changed situation.

This supports a revised FSP formulation:

> **Assembly histories sediment repertoires of attention, expectation, action, interpretation, and response; reflective inquiry can make some of those repertoires inspectable when resistance, conflict, surprise, or deliberate examination brings them into view.**

This is stronger than treating assembly history as biography alone.

## 5.3 Pragmatist challenge: do not over-separate thought from action

The current canonical narrative sometimes makes "map → reasoning → judgment → action" look cleaner than lived inquiry usually is.

Pragmatism pressures FSP to emphasize recursive coupling:

```text
acting changes what can be perceived;
what is perceived changes the map;
the map changes possible action;
consequences change confidence;
habits shape what becomes salient.
```

The formal arc should therefore remain a **diagnostic decomposition**, not a claim that natural inquiry proceeds in separate stages.

Add:

> **Transition distinctions are analytic governance distinctions; lived inquiry may realize them concurrently, recursively, or only retrospectively.**

## 5.4 FSP delta from pragmatism

FSP should also preserve its distinct contribution.

Pragmatism offers rich accounts of inquiry, habit, fallibilism, action, and consequences. FSP adds a more explicit governance grammar around:

- map/person firewalls;
- provenance;
- claim ceilings;
- possibility/recommendation/authorization distinctions;
- standing;
- institutional routing;
- failure-mode diagnosis;
- exact REOPEN conditions;
- authorial return across cognitive prostheses and formal systems.

Therefore:

**FSP should acknowledge pragmatist convergence without claiming novelty for inquiry-as-action or fallibilism themselves.**

# 6. Stress test D — enactivism and 4E cognition

## 6.1 Strong convergence

Enactive and embodied approaches emphasize that cognition is not adequately understood as detached symbol manipulation in a brain isolated from body and environment. They stress perception–action coupling, embodiment, environmental embedding, organismic activity, and sense-making.

This converges strongly with the FSP human starting point:

> inquiry occurs through situated Doing.

It also strengthens the importance of **Return to Lived Doing**. Formalization should return to action and experience rather than become a substitute for them.

## 6.2 Direct challenge: representation is contested

Enactivism is not one theory. Some forms reject representational explanation strongly; others are compatible with more modest uses of representation. Embodied cognition as a wider family is heterogeneous.

Therefore FSP must remain neutral.

The canonical narrative should not say:

> all cognition proceeds through maps.

It may say:

> when inquiry produces or relies on a selective articulation that can guide reasoning, memory, communication, coordination, comparison, or action, FSP can govern that articulation as a map.

This lets radical enactivists reject internal mental representations without making the FSP governance vocabulary unusable.

## 6.3 Sense-making and salience

Enactivism also suggests that what counts as relevant is not simply "given" and then mapped neutrally. Organisms encounter environments in relation to needs, capacities, histories, and possible actions.

FSP should therefore strengthen its first-map language:

> **A map is selective before it is explicit: salience, affordance, attention, bodily capacity, learned category, and social practice help shape what becomes available to inquiry.**

Again, this is not a commitment to enactivist ontology. It is a reminder that mapping is already situated.

## 6.4 FSP delta from enactivism

FSP is not an enactivist theory of cognition.

Its purpose is not to decide whether cognition is constituted by organism–environment coupling, whether representation can be eliminated, or where the cognitive system literally ends.

Its question is:

> Given a situated inquirer using embodied skills, explicit representations, social practices, instruments, models, institutions, or cognitive prostheses, how should transitions among these be made inspectable and bounded?

Thus:

**enactive cognition ≠ FSP governed inquiry**, though enactivism is an important comparator and pressure test.

# 7. Internal FSP claim-ceiling regression

The opening triad must pass FSP's own rules.

## CE-01 — Map / World

**PASS WITH CORRECTION.**

The canonical text clearly states Map ≠ World.

But a stronger hidden collapse remains possible:

> selective experience = map.

Repair by distinguishing lived engagement from inspectable map.

## CE-02 — Map / Person

**PASS.**

The text preserves opacity and revisability in understanding others and explicitly rejects direct identity between map and person.

A useful strengthening is:

> assembly history of another person ≠ ownership of that person's self-understanding.

## CE-03 — Evidence / Conclusion

**PASS WITH SOURCE-LAYER REQUIREMENT.**

The opening narrative currently says it is "informed by" empirical disciplines without yet carrying a formal source ledger. Before public freeze, empirical support should be tied to a compact source ledger and the prose should distinguish:

- empirical finding;
- philosophical comparator;
- FSP reconstruction.

## CE-04 — Model / Ontology

**PASS WITH REPRESENTATION FIREWALL.**

The lived-inquiry arc must remain a model of recurring transitions, not an ontology of cognition.

## CE-05 — Origin / Validity

**STRONG PASS.**

Assembly history explicitly preserves formation ≠ justification and origin ≠ validity.

This is one of the triad's strongest internal safeguards.

## CE-06 — Benefit / Authorization

**NOT YET ACTIVE IN OPENING TRIAD, PRESERVED DOWNSTREAM.**

No change required.

## CE-07 — Power / Legitimacy

**PASS DOWNSTREAM.**

Assembly history appropriately includes institutions and power without treating causal influence as legitimate authority.

## CE-08 — Formal Choice / Practical Freedom

**PASS AND STRENGTHENED BY DEVELOPMENTAL/SITUATED REVIEW.**

The review reinforces that action possibilities depend on bodily, environmental, social, and institutional conditions.

## CE-09 — Resource / Capability

**PASS DOWNSTREAM.**

No opening-triad conflict.

## CE-10 — Current / Final

**STRONG PASS.**

Fallibility, revision, and REOPEN are central throughout.

## CE-11 — Probe / Fact

**NOT DIRECTLY ACTIVE.**

No change required.

## CE-12 — Shared Grammar / Ontology

**CRITICAL PASS CONDITION.**

The canonical triad must not become a universal cognitive ontology.

Add explicit wording:

> **Shared recurrence of inquiry vulnerabilities does not establish one universal cognitive mechanism, developmental sequence, or ontology of mind.**

This should become the governing ceiling for the opening triad.

# 9. Required corrective patch for FSP-CT-CANONICAL-1.1

The review recommends a **minimal corrective pass**, not a rewrite.

## Patch A — replace representation-first wording

Where the canonical text currently says:

> Human beings cannot operate on an unmediated totality of the world. We work through selective representations.

Revise toward:

> Human beings encounter the world from embodied, selective, historically formed perspectives. Much practical engagement is not first experienced as an explicit representation. When aspects of a situation become available for recall, articulation, comparison, reasoning, communication, modeling, or coordination, FSP treats that selective articulation as a **map** for governance purposes. The term does not commit FSP to the thesis that all cognition consists in internal mental representations.

## Patch B — make the lived-inquiry arc recursive

Retain the existing arc but introduce it as one possible decomposition:

```text
encounter ↔ notice ↔ distinguish ↔ interpret
        ↘ anticipate ↔ act ↔ consequence/resistance
                     ↖ revise ↙
```

Then state:

> These distinctions identify recurring functions, not mandatory stages. In lived inquiry they may overlap, loop, occur concurrently, or become visible only retrospectively.

## Patch C — strengthen assembly history

Add:

> Assembly history includes endogenous activity as well as inherited conditions. Persons explore, select, resist, practice, improvise, reinterpret, and alter the environments that in turn shape them. "Assembly" therefore names reciprocal historical formation, not construction of a passive person from external parts.

## Patch D — distinguish prereflective and reflective inquiry

Add before the reflective-inquiry section:

> Inquiry does not begin with reflection. Organisms can discriminate, learn, anticipate, act, and adjust without making those processes explicit objects of thought. Reflective inquiry begins when some aspect of this ongoing activity becomes thematically available for inspection, comparison, reason-giving, or deliberate revision.

## Patch E — add cognitive-architecture firewall

Add to the opening claim ceilings:

> **Map language ≠ theory of cognitive architecture.**

and:

> **Shared inquiry grammar ≠ universal developmental sequence or mechanism.**

These can remain local explanatory firewalls rather than new numbered constitutional claim ceilings.

## Patch F — source-ledger distinction

Before public freeze, attach a compact source ledger whose entries are marked:

- **EMPIRICAL REVIEW**
- **PHILOSOPHICAL COMPARATOR**
- **FSP RECONSTRUCTION**

This prevents supporting literature from being misread as empirical validation of the complete FSP architecture.

# 12. Freeze decision

**FSP-CT-CANONICAL-1 is not yet frozen.**

The opening triad passes the substantive review but requires the six corrective patches above.

The appropriate next artifact is:

> **FSP-CT-CANONICAL-1.1 — Representation-Neutral Situated-Inquiry Patch, Reciprocal Assembly-History Revision, Prereflective/Reflective Boundary, Recursive Inquiry Arc, Cognitive-Architecture Firewall, and Source-Ledger Integration.**

After that corrective pass, run a narrow regression:

1. Does Map ≠ World ≠ Person remain intact?
2. Can a representationalist cognitive scientist use the architecture?
3. Can an enactivist use the architecture without accepting internal representation as fundamental?
4. Can a phenomenologist recognize prereflective engagement without having it redescribed as explicit mapping?
5. Can a pragmatist recognize habit, action, consequence, and reconstruction without FSP claiming those as novel discoveries?
6. Does developmental science remain evidence for bounded components rather than proof of the whole FSP architecture?
7. Does assembly history preserve active self-formation and reciprocal environmental shaping?
8. Does reflective critical thinking remain a later explicit governance capacity rather than the origin of inquiry?
9. Do Ethics and History of Inquiry remain free to supply domain-native ontology, evidence, and authority?
10. Has no new Tool or constitutional primitive been introduced merely to solve a wording problem?

If those tests pass, the opening triad is ready to freeze as the canonical seed for the two Pressbook projections.

## 13. Final review ceiling

This review establishes **comparative fit, pressure points, and necessary constraints**.

It does not establish empirical confirmation of FSP as a whole.

The correct ceiling is:

> **Convergence with developmental science, phenomenology, pragmatism, and enactivism supports the plausibility and usefulness of beginning FSP from situated, historically formed, fallible human inquiry. It does not show that these traditions entail FSP, that FSP reduces them to one theory, or that one universal cognitive mechanism underlies every domain. The canonical contribution of FSP remains the governance of transitions among maps, reasons, judgments, standing, action, institutions, consequences, and revision.**
