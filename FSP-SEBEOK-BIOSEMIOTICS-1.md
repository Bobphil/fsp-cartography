# FSP-SEBEOK-BIOSEMIOTICS-1

## Umwelt and PRF, Semiosis Before Discourse, Animal Communication, Interspecies Translation, Semiotic Remainder, Levin Multiscale Agency, Brandom Discursive Normativity, Standing Under Radical Otherness, and Full-Stack Meaning Ecology

**Status:** COMPARATOR / MEANING-ECOLOGY ARCHITECTURE — VERSION 1  
**Purpose:** Test whether FSP can represent organism-relative meaning, animal communication, nonhuman semiosis, and interspecies translation without collapsing causal coupling, signaling, semiosis, agency, phenomenality, discourse, moral standing, or authorization.  
**Primary comparator:** Thomas A. Sebeok's zoosemiotics and biosemiotic legacy, in dialogue with Jakob von Uexküll's Umwelt concept.  
**Secondary comparators:** Michael Levin on multiscale agency and diverse intelligence; Robert Brandom on discursive normativity and reciprocal recognition; Steven Turner on anti-reification; FSP Phenomenal-Node Ecology and Normative Profile architecture.  
**Primary claim ceiling:** Structural fit between FSP and biosemiotic / Umwelt concepts ≠ proof that all living signaling is semiosis, that all semiosis is conscious, or that all semiotic agents have moral standing.

---

# 1. Why Sebeok belongs at the center of this development

FSP has recently widened from:

- human phenomenal worlds;
- PRF assembly histories;
- reciprocal recognition;
- Joint Task Spaces;
- multiscale agency;
- enhanced humans;
- synthetic and hybrid biological systems.

That expansion creates a missing question.

Not only:

> What can another being do?

But:

> **What can count as significant for that being?**

Thomas Sebeok's work on zoosemiotics and his later biosemiotic orientation supplies a major comparator for this question.

Sebeok's original zoosemiotic program explicitly linked semiotics and ethology in the study of signaling behavior across animal species.

That already moves inquiry away from the assumption that communication must be modeled on human language.

Later biosemiotic developments press the question further:

> How do living systems participate in sign relations, context-sensitive signaling, and organism-relative meaning?

This is exactly the conceptual space between Levin's agency and Brandom's discourse that FSP currently needs.

# 3. Sebeok's zoosemiotic contribution

Zoosemiotics was proposed to study animal sign processes at the intersection of semiotics and ethology.

Its importance for FSP lies in refusing a binary:

```text
mechanism
OR
human language
```

Animal communication shows that meaningful or functionally significant relations can be studied without assuming human symbolic discourse.

This gives FSP a middle vocabulary.

---

# 4. Animal communication is not simply reduced human language

Sebeok's work distinguished animal communication from language while also recognizing that human communication contains important zoosemiotic components.

This is valuable because FSP needs to preserve:

> **Communication ≠ language.**

And:

> **Language ≠ exhaustive communication.**

A vocalization, scent, posture, color change, electrical signal, or movement may function communicatively without possessing human syntax or propositional structure.

---

# 5. Signal ≠ sign

Not every physical signal should automatically count as a sign.

A causal perturbation can propagate without being interpreted.

Therefore:

> **Signal transmission ≠ semiosis.**

A semiotic claim requires additional evidence concerning:

- functional role;
- context sensitivity;
- discrimination;
- repertoire;
- receiver response;
- interpretive dependence;
- history or learning;
- system organization.

---

# 6. Sign ≠ symbol

Human symbolic language is only one form of sign use.

So:

> **Semiosis ≠ symbolic language.**

A system may participate in sign relations without:

- grammar;
- proposition;
- reason-giving;
- explicit normativity.

This is the first major bridge between Sebeok and Levin.

---

# 7. Semiosis ≠ consciousness

The fact that a system interprets or responds to signs in a biosemiotic sense does not establish phenomenal experience.

So:

> **Semiosis ≠ consciousness.**

This is essential.

Biosemiotic vocabulary must not become a shortcut to claims about subjectivity.

---

# 8. Semiosis ≠ moral standing

Likewise:

> **Semiosis ≠ moral standing.**

A moral-standing profile may treat semiosis as relevant evidence.

But it does not automatically settle standing.

This keeps normative judgment inside FSP-NORMATIVE-PROFILE-1.

---

# 9. Uexküll's Umwelt

Jakob von Uexküll's Umwelt concept gives the Sebeok comparator its strongest connection to FSP.

An organism does not simply inhabit the entire physical environment as an undifferentiated field.

It selectively encounters features relevant to its organization, capacities, and needs.

This organism-relative meaningful world can be represented as an Umwelt.

For FSP:

> **Environment ≠ Umwelt.**

The physical surroundings may be shared.

The meaningful world is organism-relative.

---

# 10. Umwelt ≠ environment

Two organisms may occupy the same physical place while living through very different meaningful structures.

Different sensory capacities, effectors, ecological needs, and learned histories make different features salient.

Thus:

```text
shared physical environment
↓
species / organism-specific selection
↓
Umwelt
```

This yields a powerful Firewall:

> **Shared environment ≠ shared world of significance.**

---

# 12. Organism ≠ Umwelt ≠ environment

The FSP human Firewall:

> **Person ≠ PRF map ≠ world.**

has a close biosemiotic analogue:

> **Organism ≠ Umwelt ≠ environment.**

Neither map should be mistaken for the being.

Neither map should be mistaken for the entire world.

# 13. Researcher's map ≠ organism's Umwelt

A human observer reconstructs an animal's meaningful world from evidence.

That evidence may include:

- behavior;
- sensory physiology;
- ecology;
- communication;
- learning;
- developmental history;
- controlled experiments.

But:

> **Researcher's map of an Umwelt ≠ the Umwelt.**

This makes remainder unavoidable.

# 14. Semiotic remainder

FSP's existing concept of remainder gains a powerful biological interpretation.

In interspecies inquiry, remainder includes what cannot be confidently translated from:

```text
animal-relative significance
→ human scientific representation
```

Remainder may result from:

- inaccessible sensory ranges;
- unfamiliar salience;
- unknown context;
- species-specific signaling;
- uncertain affective significance;
- observer bias;
- missing ecological information.

So:

> **Untranslated semiotic remainder ≠ irrelevant remainder.**

# 18. Sender intention ≠ message meaning

A system need not possess human-like intentions for a signal to function communicatively.

Therefore:

> **Sender intention ≠ necessary condition of communication.**

And:

> **Sender intention ≠ message meaning.**

This is especially important in animal and biological communication.

---

# 19. Receiver response ≠ decoded meaning

A receiver may respond reliably to a signal.

That does not automatically prove that the researcher has correctly identified the meaning.

So:

> **Reliable response ≠ recovered meaning.**

The warrant requires contextual and comparative evidence.

---

# 20. Repertoire ≠ language

An animal may have a structured repertoire of signals.

But:

> **Signal repertoire ≠ language.**

Language claims require stronger evidence concerning compositionality, syntax, generativity, displacement, or other relevant criteria.

---

# 21. Code metaphor ≠ literal code identity

Biosemiotic and communication theory often use "code."

FSP should preserve:

> **Useful code metaphor ≠ identical mechanism across genetics, animal signaling, and human language.**

The term can support comparison without erasing differences.

---

# 22. Context is constitutive

The same signal can have different significance depending on:

- social relation;
- environment;
- timing;
- threat;
- reproductive condition;
- learned history;
- receiver state.

Therefore:

> **Signal form ≠ context-independent meaning.**

This connects strongly with FSP Context and Contingency.

---

# 23. ABCD across species

ABCD can be extended cautiously.

## Agency

What can this organism do?

## Beauty / Awe / Mattering

For humans, this includes lived significance.

For nonhumans, any extension must be evidence-bound rather than projected anthropomorphically.

## Context and Contingency

What ecological and developmental history shapes the sign relation?

## Dynamics

How do signaling, learning, relationships, and environment change over time?

This yields:

> **ABCD portability ≠ identical content across species.**

---

# 24. Animal communication and phenomenal uncertainty

Some animal communication may provide evidence relevant to phenomenal life.

But:

> **Communication competence ≠ demonstrated phenomenality.**

The evidence must remain separately typed.

# 25. Interspecies translation

FSP's human translation grammar becomes much harder across species.

A human and an animal may differ in:

- sensory modalities;
- salience;
- motor capacities;
- temporal scale;
- social structure;
- learning;
- ecology.

So interspecies translation should be represented as:

```text
Human PRF
↔
human scientific model of animal Umwelt
↔
animal semiotic evidence
↔
animal Umwelt
```

The final term is never directly possessed by the human researcher.

---

# 26. Translation ≠ identity across species

A human translation such as:

> "alarm call"

may be useful.

But it is a model.

Therefore:

> **Human gloss ≠ animal-native meaning.**

This is a special case of:

> **Translation ≠ identity.**

# 29. Radical otherness

The Sebeok comparator intensifies FSP's representational humility.

The other being may differ not merely in belief but in the structure of meaningful experience.

So:

> **Different PRF ≠ merely different opinion.**

Across species, the divergence may involve different:

- sensory worlds;
- temporal horizons;
- ecological salience;
- communication modalities;
- bodily capacities.

FSP must therefore handle **radical otherness**.

# 30. Standing under radical otherness

A being's inability to communicate in human language cannot automatically defeat standing.

Thus:

> **Human-language absence ≠ absence of morally relevant agency or experience.**

And:

> **Failure of human translation ≠ absence of significance for the other.**

These are major normative Firewalls.

# 72. Umwelt disruption as capability pressure

If an organism's meaningful environmental cues are disrupted, its capabilities may decline.

So FSP can connect:

```text
Umwelt disruption
→ impaired orientation / communication
→ capability loss
→ standing implications under relevant profile
```

The normative implication remains profile-dependent.

# 73. Meaning ecology and Joint Task Spaces

A future multispecies JTS architecture should ask:

- Is there evidence of a genuinely shared task?
- How is coordination signaled?
- What does each participant discriminate?
- What is translated by humans?
- What remains opaque?
- Who defines success?
- Whose capabilities are affected?

This avoids pretending that all participants share the same task description.

---

# 74. Shared task ≠ shared meaning

Even when two species coordinate:

> **Shared task ≠ shared meaning.**

A human and dog may coordinate successfully while understanding the situation differently.

This is an important JTS Firewall.

---

# 75. Cooperation without semantic convergence

This gives a new form of the FSP principle:

> **Cooperation without convergence.**

Across species it becomes:

> **coordination without shared conceptual world.**

That may be one of the strongest demonstrations of the principle.

# 76. Claim ceiling

This build supports:

> **FSP can use Sebeok/Uexküll as a major comparator for representing organism-relative significance and animal communication, thereby filling the conceptual gap between Levin-style multiscale competence and Brandom-style discursive normativity.**

It does not establish:

- that every living system is semiotic in the same sense;
- that all signaling is interpretation;
- that Umwelt implies consciousness;
- that animal communication is language;
- that semiosis settles moral standing;
- that Sebeok, Levin, Brandom, Turner, and FSP share one ontology;
- that FSP has solved interspecies translation.

# 79. Source ledger

## Thomas A. Sebeok — zoosemiotics

Sebeok introduced "zoosemiotics" in the 1960s to name the intersection of semiotics and ethology focused on signaling behavior across animal species.

Primary accessible source:
- Indiana University Press, *How Animals Communicate*, chapter "Zoosemiotic Components of Human Communication."
- https://publish.iupress.indiana.edu/read/how-animals-communicate/section/920b3312-f453-4777-99de-c616c8640691

## Thomas A. Sebeok — Animal Communication

Sebeok edited the major 1968 interdisciplinary collection *Animal Communication: Techniques of Study and Results of Research*.

Accessible project page:
- https://publish.iupress.indiana.edu/projects/animal-communication

## Sebeok research framework

Later zoosemiotic scholarship reconstructs Sebeok's questions around coding, channels, receiver interpretation, repertoire, code properties, meaning, and context.

Accessible source:
- *Biosemiotics*, "A Semiotic Modern Synthesis"

Contemporary biosemiotic literature treats Umwelt as the organism-relative environment structured by perceptual and effectual cues, and notes Sebeok's interpretation of Umwelt as an animal's model of its surrounding world.

Accessible source:

# 80. Final synthesis

The Sebeok comparator changes the shape of FSP's developing agency architecture.

The question is no longer only:

> What can this being do?

It is also:

> What is significant for this being?

And then:

> How do we know?

> How does it signal?

> What does the receiver discriminate?

> What context changes the interpretation?

> What part of the being's meaningful world is inaccessible to our map?

> What does that uncertainty license?

> What does it not license?

This gives FSP a richer pathway from living organization to human normativity:

```text
physical coupling
→ signaling
→ possible semiosis
→ organism-relative meaning / Umwelt
→ agency
→ possible phenomenality
→ human PRF where applicable
→ discursive normativity where applicable
→ normative profile
→ standing
→ authorization
```

The major new discipline is:

> **Do not require another kind of being to become human-like before allowing its meaningful world to become an object of serious inquiry.**

But the equal and opposite discipline is:

> **Do not turn every signal, competence, or unfamiliar behavior into consciousness, personhood, or moral standing without the warrants needed for those stronger claims.**

Sebeok therefore belongs neither as a footnote to Levin nor as a precursor superseded by later work.

He supplies a major missing comparator:

> **Levin expands the possible scales of agency.  
> Sebeok expands the possible forms of meaning.  
> Brandom specifies a distinctive form of discursive normative meaning.  
> Turner disciplines claims about social-scale agency.  
> FSP governs the transitions among them without requiring ontological convergence.**
