# I. Bounded Rationality, Complexity, Design, and Decomposition — Herbert Simon

## 1. Herbert A. Simon. *The Sciences of the Artificial*. 3rd ed. MIT Press, 1996.
Link: https://mitpress.mit.edu/9780262264495/the-sciences-of-the-artificial/  
**Rating:** **READ FIRST**

Simon’s most useful single book for the current FSP phase. The chapters on design, hierarchy, problem representation, bounded rationality, and artificial systems supply the strongest external explanation of why selective decomposition and compressed representations can remain useful under complexity. FSP’s recent contribution becomes clearer against Simon: decomposition makes inquiry tractable; FSP asks how recomposition is governed.

**FSP builds:** FSP-SIMON-COMPARATOR-1; FSP-NEAR-DECOMPOSABILITY-STRESS-1; FSP-FOUR-COMPARATOR-ARCHITECTURE-1; FSP-COMPARATOR-REGRESSION-1.

## 2. Herbert A. Simon. “Rational Choice and the Structure of the Environment.” *Psychological Review* 63, no. 2 (1956): 129–138.
DOI: https://doi.org/10.1037/h0042769  
**Rating:** **READ FIRST**

A compact source for the ecological structure of bounded rationality and satisficing. It is especially valuable now because FSP has separated the adequacy of a local bounded solution from the question of whether its warrant travels into a new context.

**FSP builds:** FSP-SIMON-COMPARATOR-1; FSP-GOVERNANCE-RESIDUE-1.

## 3. Herbert A. Simon. “The Architecture of Complexity.” *Proceedings of the American Philosophical Society* 106, no. 6 (1962): 467–482.
Link: https://www.jstor.org/stable/985254  
**Rating:** **READ FIRST**

The key text for hierarchy and near-decomposability. It now reads almost like a prehistory of our distinction between legitimate local compression and the need for explicit governance when weakly coupled components are recomposed.

**FSP builds:** FSP-SIMON-COMPARATOR-1; FSP-NEAR-DECOMPOSABILITY-STRESS-1; FSP-RECOMPOSITION-GOVERNANCE-1.

## 4. Herbert A. Simon. *Administrative Behavior*. 4th ed. Free Press, 1997; orig. 1947.
Link: https://www.simonandschuster.com/books/Administrative-Behavior/Herbert-A-Simon/9780684835822  
**Rating:** **SKIM**

Useful for FSP’s institutional arm because bounded decision is not merely individual but organizational. Read selectively for authority, organizational premises, and how decision environments structure what counts as a reasonable action.

**FSP builds:** FSP-SIMON-COMPARATOR-1; institutional amplification work; FSP-FOUR-COMPARATOR-ARCHITECTURE-1.

## 5. Herbert A. Simon. *Models of Bounded Rationality*, Vols. 1–3. MIT Press, 1982–1997.
Link: https://mitpress.mit.edu/9780262690874/models-of-bounded-rationality-volume-1/  
**Rating:** **REFERENCE ONLY**

A large source collection rather than the best entry point. Keep it available when we need to trace specific Simonian claims about procedural rationality, search, economics, organizations, or problem solving without relying on secondary summaries.

**FSP builds:** Simon comparator program; future formal comparison work.

# II. Inferential Normativity, Commitment, Entitlement, and Scorekeeping — Robert Brandom

## 6. Robert B. Brandom. *Making It Explicit: Reasoning, Representing, and Discursive Commitment*. Harvard University Press, 1994.
Link: https://www.hup.harvard.edu/books/9780674543300  
**Rating:** **READ FIRST / SELECTIVE REREAD**

The major background source for commitment, entitlement, deontic scorekeeping, material inference, and recognition. The recent FSP gain is not to reject Brandom but to locate his machinery as an especially powerful account of discursive inferential transitions inside a wider transition-governance architecture.

**FSP builds:** FSP-BRANDOM-RECIPROCITY-1; FSP-BRANDOM-INFERENTIAL-GOVERNANCE-1; FSP-FOUR-COMPARATOR-ARCHITECTURE-1.

## 7. Robert B. Brandom. “Inferentialism, Normative Pragmatism, and Metalinguistic Expressivism.” John Dewey Lectures, 2018.
Link: https://sites.pitt.edu/~rbrandom/Texts/Inferentialism_Normative_Pragmatism_and.pdf  
**Rating:** **READ FIRST**

Probably the best concise Brandom reread for present purposes. It foregrounds the inferential articulation of conceptual content and helps us sharpen the FSP distinction between inferential entitlement and other kinds of warrant.

**FSP builds:** FSP-BRANDOM-INFERENTIAL-GOVERNANCE-1; FSP-COMPARATOR-REGRESSION-1.

## 8. Robert B. Brandom. “Action, Norms, and Practical Reasoning.” *Philosophical Perspectives* 12 (1998): 127–139.
Link: https://sites.pitt.edu/~rbrandom/Texts/Action_Norms_and_Practical_Reasoning.pdf  
**Rating:** **READ FIRST**

Important corrective against treating Brandom as if he stopped at assertion. The entry/exit-transition analysis helps distinguish Brandom’s practical reasoning from FSP’s additional separation of warranted judgment, legitimate authorization, downstream consequence, and Return.

**FSP builds:** FSP-BRANDOM-INFERENTIAL-GOVERNANCE-1.

## 9. Robert B. Brandom. “Conceptual Content and Discursive Practice.”
Link: https://sites.pitt.edu/~rbrandom/Texts/Conceptual_Content_and_Discursive_Practi.pdf  
**Rating:** **SKIM**

Useful for commitment/entitlement update structures and deontic scorekeeping. It became especially relevant to our seven-view app controls: SAID ≠ INFERRED, attributed commitment ≠ acknowledged commitment, and provenance-sensitive maps of others.

**FSP builds:** FSP-BRANDOM-INFERENTIAL-GOVERNANCE-1; seven-view architecture.

## 10. Robert B. Brandom. *Between Saying and Doing: Towards an Analytic Pragmatism*. Oxford University Press, 2008.
DOI: https://doi.org/10.1093/acprof:oso/9780199542871.001.0001  
**Rating:** **SKIM**

Potentially valuable for FSP because it explicitly studies relations among vocabularies and practices rather than only static semantic content. It is worth revisiting in light of TranslationAnchor, operational sufficiency, and the Pricean pressure to distinguish vocabulary functions.

**FSP builds:** Brandom comparator; ontological interoperability; future translation-governance work.

## 11. Robert B. Brandom. *A Spirit of Trust: A Reading of Hegel’s Phenomenology*. Harvard University Press, 2019.
Link: https://www.hup.harvard.edu/books/9780674976818  
**Rating:** **REFERENCE ONLY**

Mainly relevant to recognition, recollection, and diachronic normative reconstruction. FSP’s source-ledger and claim-ceiling requirements should be compared against Brandom’s recollective rationality when we revisit historical reconstruction.

**FSP builds:** FSP-BRANDOM-RECIPROCITY-1; FSP-BRANDOM-INFERENTIAL-GOVERNANCE-1.

# III. Pragmatic Naturalism, Expressivism, and Anti-Representationalism — Huw Price

## 12. Huw Price. “Naturalism without Representationalism.” In *Expressivism, Pragmatism and Representationalism*. Cambridge University Press, 2013.
DOI: https://doi.org/10.1017/CBO9780511842498.003  
**Rating:** **READ FIRST**

The central source for subject naturalism: begin with natural agents and their practices before assuming that philosophical vocabularies mirror a ready-made ontology. This directly supports FSP’s new formulation of Partial Map as a selective usable articulation rather than a uniformly representational object.

**FSP builds:** FSP-PRICE-ISMAEL-SITUATED-PRAGMATISM-1; FSP-FOUR-COMPARATOR-ARCHITECTURE-1; FSP-COMPARATOR-REGRESSION-1.

## 13. Huw Price. “Two Expressivist Programmes, Two Bifurcations.” In *Expressivism, Pragmatism and Representationalism*. Cambridge University Press, 2013, pp. 22–44.
DOI: https://doi.org/10.1017/CBO9780511842498.004  
**Rating:** **READ FIRST**

Especially relevant because Price directly contrasts Brandomian and Blackburn-style expressivist strategies. It gives FSP a principled reason not to assume that all vocabularies have one uniform inferential or representational function.

**FSP builds:** FSP-PRICE-ISMAEL-SITUATED-PRAGMATISM-1; FSP-FOUR-COMPARATOR-ARCHITECTURE-1.

## 14. Huw Price. “Pluralism, ‘World’ and the Primacy of Science.” In *Expressivism, Pragmatism and Representationalism*. Cambridge University Press, 2013.
Link: https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/expressivism-pragmatism-and-representationalism/pluralism-world-and-the-primacy-of-science/36D68B9E7E4E28D497A504513FB87E39  
**Rating:** **READ FIRST**

Important for domain pluralism without requiring semantic or ontological uniformity. It sits very close to the FSP result that portable governance grammar does not entail shared ontology across physics, ethics, medicine, institutions, or phenomenology.

**FSP builds:** Price–Ismael comparator; Ontological Interoperability.

## 15. Huw Price. “Global Expressivism by the Method of Differences.” *Royal Institute of Philosophy Supplements* 86 (2019): 133–154.
DOI: https://doi.org/10.1017/S1358246119000109  
**Rating:** **READ FIRST**

A compact statement of Price’s global expressivist strategy. Especially useful for our Semantic Descent Firewall: vocabulary use ≠ ontological license.

**FSP builds:** FSP-PRICE-ISMAEL-SITUATED-PRAGMATISM-1.

## 16. Huw Price. *Time’s Arrow and Archimedes’ Point*. Oxford University Press, 1996.
Link: https://global.oup.com/academic/product/times-arrow-and-archimedes-point-9780195117981  
**Rating:** **SKIM**

Relevant to perspectival structure, time asymmetry, and the danger of importing agent-centered asymmetries directly into fundamental physics. It also forms useful background to Price’s later exchange with Ismael on agents, observers, and the temporal standpoint.

**FSP builds:** Price–Ismael situated pragmatism; temporal interoperability research frontier.

## 17. Huw Price. *Facts and the Function of Truth*. Blackwell, 1988.
Link: https://philpapers.org/rec/PRIFAT  
**Rating:** **REFERENCE ONLY**

An earlier foundation for Price’s functional/pragmatic treatment of truth talk. Retain as background if we later ask whether FSP’s “claim ceiling” presupposes too substantive a representational conception of truth.

**FSP builds:** supporting background for Price comparator.

# IV. Situated Agency, Self-Location, Physics, and Experience — Jenann Ismael

## 18. Jenann Ismael. *The Situated Self*. Oxford University Press, 2007.
Link: https://academic.oup.com/book/2503  
**Rating:** **READ FIRST**

One of the highest-priority books for the present FSP phase. Its self-location and reflexive mapping framework supports the distinction between a world model and an agent’s location within that model, sharpening our ideas of situated map, capability, and Return.

**FSP builds:** FSP-PRICE-ISMAEL-SITUATED-PRAGMATISM-1; FSP-FOUR-COMPARATOR-ARCHITECTURE-1.

## 19. Jenann Ismael. “How Do Causes Depend on Us? The Many Faces of Perspectivalism.” *Synthese* 193 (2016): 245–267.
Link: https://www.jenanni.com/papers/HowDoCausesDependonUs.pdf  
**Rating:** **READ FIRST**

Directly engages Price’s perspectival treatment of causation and distinguishes pragmatic dependence, contextual relativization, and indexicality. This is extremely useful for FSP because “perspective-relative” must not become an undifferentiated explanation.

**FSP builds:** FSP-PRICE-ISMAEL-SITUATED-PRAGMATISM-1.

## 20. Jenann Ismael. “A Participatory Universe in the Realist Mode: On the Separation of Observational and Agentive Perspectives in Classical and Quantum Mechanics.” *Foundations of Physics* 55 (2025): 35.
DOI: https://doi.org/10.1007/s10701-025-00843-4  
**Rating:** **READ FIRST**

The most directly relevant recent Ismael paper. It sharpens the distinction between observer and agent, providing external support for FSP’s sequence: can observe ≠ can predict ≠ can intervene ≠ is authorized to intervene.

**FSP builds:** FSP-PRICE-ISMAEL-SITUATED-PRAGMATISM-1; FSP-FOUR-MOVE-GOVERNANCE-1.

## 21. Jenann Ismael. “Why Physics Should Care about the Mind, and How to Think about it Without Worrying about the Mind-Body Problem.” In *Consciousness and Quantum Mechanics*, Oxford University Press, 2022, pp. 156–176.
DOI: https://doi.org/10.1093/oso/9780197501665.003.0008  
**Rating:** **READ FIRST**

Highly relevant to the research arm at the junction of physics and experience. It helps motivate a disciplined attempt to reconstruct observer/agent structure inside physics without pretending that a physical description straightforwardly exhausts consciousness.

**FSP builds:** Objective Phenomenology; Price–Ismael situated pragmatism.

## 22. Jenann Ismael. “The Open Universe: Totality, Self-reference and Time.” *Australasian Philosophical Review* 8, no. 3 (2024): 216–233.
DOI: https://doi.org/10.1080/24740500.2022.2155200  
**Rating:** **READ FIRST**

Explores embedded agency, self-reference, temporal openness, and the asymmetry between past and future from inside the universe. Especially relevant to FSP’s emerging interpretation of Return as re-location in a changed field of possibilities.

**FSP builds:** Price–Ismael situated pragmatism; Transformation Architecture; temporal interoperability.

## 23. Jenann Ismael. “Reflections on the Asymmetry of Causation.” *Interface Focus* 13 (2023): 20220081.
DOI: https://doi.org/10.1098/rsfs.2022.0081  
**Rating:** **SKIM**

A useful shorter complement to “How Do Causes Depend on Us?” It helps connect causal direction, agent perspective, and objective structure without reducing causation to subjective projection.

**FSP builds:** Price–Ismael situated pragmatism; causal-warrant decomposition.

## 24. Adrien Doerig, Rowan P. Sommers, Katja Seeliger, Blake Richards, Jenann Ismael, et al. “The Neuroconnectionist Research Programme.” *Nature Reviews Neuroscience* 24 (2023): 431–450.
DOI: https://doi.org/10.1038/s41583-023-00705-w  
**Rating:** **READ FIRST**

Methodologically important because it treats neuroconnectionism as a research programme whose value lies partly in generating progressive empirical work rather than as a fully settled ontology of mind. This resembles FSP’s insistence that a formal research architecture should generate discriminating inquiry without overclaiming what its representations are.

**FSP builds:** research-frontier architecture; Objective Phenomenology; comparator work.

## 25. Jenann Ismael. *How Physics Makes Us Free*. Oxford University Press, 2016.
Link: https://global.oup.com/academic/product/how-physics-makes-us-free-9780190090586  
**Rating:** **SKIM**

Useful for the broader relation between physical description, agency, control, and deliberation. It can serve as a check on whether FSP’s observer/agent and possibility/authorization distinctions are making unnecessary metaphysical assumptions.

**FSP builds:** Transformation Architecture; situated agency comparator.

# V. Consciousness, Objective Phenomenology, Umwelt, and Cross-Species Evidence

## 26. Thomas Nagel. *What Is It Like to Be a Bat?* 50th-anniversary ed. Oxford University Press, 2024.
DOI: https://doi.org/10.1093/oso/9780197752791.001.0001  
**Rating:** **READ FIRST**

Do not treat this merely as a reprint of the 1974 essay. The volume includes “Further Thoughts: The Psychophysical Nexus,” making it especially useful for our present attempt to separate structural constraint, objective reconstruction, and residual qualitative character.

**FSP builds:** FSP-OBJECTIVE-PHENOMENOLOGY-1.

## 27. Thomas Nagel. “What Is It Like to Be a Bat?” *The Philosophical Review* 83, no. 4 (1974): 435–450.
DOI: https://doi.org/10.2307/2183914  
**Rating:** **SKIM / REREAD**

The classic source for the irreducible perspectival character of consciousness. Our recent use is narrower than many standard readings: it sets a ceiling on third-person possession while not implying that objective science can discover nothing structurally relevant about another organism’s experience.

**FSP builds:** Objective Phenomenology.

## 28. Thomas Nagel. “Objective Bat Phenomenology.” *Animal Sentience* 9, no. 35 (2024).
DOI: https://doi.org/10.51291/2377-7478.1864  
**Rating:** **READ FIRST**

A crucial recent clarification. Nagel explicitly permits an objective phenomenological project that progressively learns structural features of another creature’s experience while retaining the limit imposed by first-person qualitative character.

**FSP builds:** FSP-OBJECTIVE-PHENOMENOLOGY-1.

## 29. Thomas Nagel. “Further Thoughts: The Psychophysical Nexus.” In *What Is It Like to Be a Bat?* Oxford University Press, 2024.
Link: https://academic.oup.com/book/56130  
**Rating:** **READ FIRST**

Nagel’s current positive speculation about a deeper psychophysical nexus is useful even if FSP does not endorse it. It clarifies the distinction between an epistemic bridge problem and a metaphysical proposal about what ultimately connects mental and physical structure.

**FSP builds:** Objective Phenomenology; comparator claim-ceiling work.

## 30. Andrew Y. Lee. “Objective Phenomenology.” *Erkenntnis* 89 (2024): 1197–1216; online 2022.
DOI: https://doi.org/10.1007/s10670-022-00576-0  
**Rating:** **READ FIRST**

Argues that structural facts about phenomenal experience can be objective even when other aspects remain essentially subjective. It provides one of the cleanest external philosophical supports for FSP’s “better structural constraint ≠ qualitative identity” firewall.

**FSP builds:** FSP-OBJECTIVE-PHENOMENOLOGY-1.

## 31. Jakob von Uexküll. *A Foray into the Worlds of Animals and Humans, with A Theory of Meaning*. Trans. Joseph D. O’Neil. University of Minnesota Press, 2010.
Link: https://www.upress.umn.edu/9780816659005/a-foray-into-the-worlds-of-animals-and-humans/  
**Rating:** **READ FIRST**

Foundational for organism-relative Umwelt and the idea that different organisms inhabit differently structured fields of significance and action. It provides historical depth for FSP’s AccessRelation, Umwelt Constraint Map, and organism-relative Partial Map.

**FSP builds:** Objective Phenomenology; Heterogeneous Intelligence Formalism; animal-communication frontier.

## 32. Thomas A. Sebeok. *Signs: An Introduction to Semiotics*. 2nd ed. University of Toronto Press, 2001.
Link: https://utorontopress.com/9780802084726/signs/  
**Rating:** **SKIM**

Useful for biosemiotic mediation and for separating signal, interpretation, representation, and significance. It is especially valuable when FSP moves from sensory access into cross-species communication without assuming human linguistic categories.

**FSP builds:** Objective Phenomenology; Heterogeneous Intelligence; animal communication research frontier.

## 33. Aya Goldshtein et al. “Acoustic Cognitive Map-Based Navigation in Echolocating Bats.” *Science* 386 (2024): 561–567.
DOI: https://doi.org/10.1126/science.adn6269  
**Rating:** **READ FIRST**

A major empirical anchor for reconstructing the bat’s accessible world. It allows FSP to distinguish what echolocation supports in navigation, spatial memory, and action from stronger claims about intrinsic phenomenal character.

**FSP builds:** FSP-OBJECTIVE-PHENOMENOLOGY-1.

# VIII. Diachronic Inquiry, Theory Change, Models, and Research Programmes

# IX. Causality, Models, Statistical Testing, and Scientific Bridge Burden

## 51. Judea Pearl. *Causality: Models, Reasoning, and Inference*. 2nd ed. Cambridge University Press, 2009.
DOI: https://doi.org/10.1017/CBO9780511803161  
**Rating:** **READ FIRST / SELECTIVE**

A formal benchmark for distinguishing association, intervention, counterfactual dependence, and causal structure. Particularly important for FSP’s Causal Confidence Inflation firewall and any future attempt to formalize “causal warrant” as a distinct transition license.

**FSP builds:** Recomposition Governance; medicine; neuroscience; causal-warrant frontier.

## 52. James Woodward. *Making Things Happen: A Theory of Causal Explanation*. Oxford University Press, 2003.
DOI: https://doi.org/10.1093/0195155270.001.0001  
**Rating:** **READ FIRST**

Interventionist causation is highly relevant to FSP’s observer/agent distinction and its separation of predictive from causal and actionable structure. It is a natural bridge between Ismael’s agentive perspective and formal causal inference.

**FSP builds:** Price–Ismael comparator; causal-warrant decomposition; Transformation Architecture.

## 54. Deborah G. Mayo. *Statistical Inference as Severe Testing: How to Get Beyond the Statistics Wars*. Cambridge University Press, 2018.
DOI: https://doi.org/10.1017/9781107286184  
**Rating:** **READ FIRST**

Mayo’s emphasis on error probing, severity, and auditing is particularly relevant to the FSP research arm. It provides a rigorous comparator for our claim that a framework should expose where evidence could have failed rather than merely attach confidence to successful fits.

**FSP builds:** research formalism; claim-ceiling and evidence-governance work.

**FSP builds:** Partial Map clarification; Price comparator; model/world governance.

## 56. DESI Collaboration (A. G. Adame et al.). “DESI 2024 VI: Cosmological Constraints from the Measurements of Baryon Acoustic Oscillations.” *Journal of Cosmology and Astroparticle Physics* 2025(02): 021.
