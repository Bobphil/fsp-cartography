# FSP-REVISION-5

## Epistemic-Injustice / Testimonial-Hermeneutical Distortion, Standing Repair, Counter-Public Evidence, Contestability Restoration, and Revision-Reentry Conditions

**Status:** Epistemic-standing and interpretive-repair revision layer  
**Boundary:** REVISION-1 through REVISION-4 remain intact; TRANSLATION-12 human evidence remains PENDING.

## 1. Purpose

REVISION-4 established that persistent disagreement may be distorted by unequal evidence access, constrained standing, or practical power.

REVISION-5 asks a more specific question:

> Even when someone is formally allowed to participate, are their testimony and interpretive resources treated in ways that systematically reduce their credibility or intelligibility?

The build therefore separates testimonial distortion from hermeneutical distortion and makes repair/re-entry conditions explicit.

## 2. Testimonial distortion

A testimonial record contains:

- speaker;
- claim;
- evidence;
- assigned credibility;
- stated credibility basis;
- identity-linked prejudice indicators;
- content-linked prejudice indicators;
- opportunity to respond.

The audit identifies a **possible testimonial injustice candidate** when credibility is assigned as a deficit and represented prejudice indicators are present.

It may also identify credibility excess.

The audit does not infer the truth or falsity of the testimony.

## 3. Hermeneutical distortion

A hermeneutical record contains:

- the reported experience;
- available interpretive resources;
- missing concepts;
- adequacy of the available vocabulary;
- whether the person is excluded from meaning-making;
- whether they may propose new concepts.

A represented interpretive gap is not automatically an injustice.

The stronger candidate requires both:

- an interpretive gap; and
- exclusion from participation in shaping or contesting the interpretive resources.

## 4. Standing repair

Standing repair may restore:

- testimony submission;
- question raising;
- hypothesis proposal;
- concept proposal;
- interpretive contestability;
- safer participation.

The aim is not to force acceptance of the excluded claim.

The aim is to restore the conditions under which it can enter inquiry and be evaluated.

## 5. Counter-public evidence

Counter-public evidence can include:

- independently contributed evidence;
- alternative interpretive resources;
- alternative claims;
- provenance.

Such evidence must remain capable of challenging the dominant interpretation.

But it is not auto-privileged.

The firewall is:

```
excluded evidence becoming visible
!=
excluded evidence becoming automatically true
```

## 6. Contestability restoration

Contestability repair may restore the ability to:

- challenge credibility assessments;
- challenge interpretive resources;
- add counter-evidence;
- request independent review;
- request REOPEN.

This extends REVISION-4's standing audit from "may dissent" to "may contest how credibility and meaning are assigned."

## 7. Revision re-entry

A previously excluded candidate may re-enter the revision process only if the relevant governance conditions have been repaired.

Possible statuses are:

- REENTER;
- REENTER_WITH_REMAINDER;
- BLOCKED_STANDING;
- BLOCKED_CONTESTABILITY;
- BLOCKED_PROVENANCE;
- BLOCKED_UNRESOLVED_DISTORTION.

## 8. Re-entry does not authorize truth

A repaired claim can regain standing in the revision frontier.

That does not make it correct.

Therefore:

```
re-entry != validation
```

and:

```
counter-public status != epistemic privilege
```

## 9. Credibility-deficit case

The first case contains a speaker whose credibility is discounted through represented group-linked prejudice indicators and who lacks an opportunity to respond.

The audit may flag:

- possible testimonial injustice;
- procedural deficit;
- credibility distortion.

It may not conclude that the reported claim is true.

## 10. Hermeneutical-gap case

The second case contains an experience that cannot be adequately represented with the dominant vocabulary, while the speaker is excluded from concept formation.

The audit may flag:

- interpretive gap;
- exclusion from meaning-making;
- possible hermeneutical injustice.

The repair target is expansion of interpretive resources and participation, not forced adoption of a new vocabulary.

## 11. Counter-public repair case

A counter-public resource can make previously obscure evidence describable and contestable.

But it must preserve:

- contributors;
- evidence IDs;
- provenance;
- its status as a challenge rather than a privileged truth source.

## 12. Re-entry with remainder

A perspective may be allowed back into inquiry even when some interpretive gap remains.

In that case the status is:

`REENTER_WITH_REMAINDER`

rather than blocked or falsely declared resolved.

This preserves the broader FSP remainder principle.

## 13. Retaliation and provenance blocks

Re-entry remains blocked when:

- retaliation risk is unresolved;
- credibility distortion remains unresolved;
- provenance is not visible;
- standing is not restored;
- contestability remains unavailable.

Thus repair must be material and procedural, not symbolic.

## 14. Relationship to Fricker and later epistemic-injustice work

The build is inspired by the distinction between testimonial injustice and hermeneutical injustice, while keeping the FSP representation deliberately narrower than a full theory of epistemic injustice.

It also leaves room for broader forms of epistemic exclusion, silencing, and smothering without claiming to exhaust them.

## 15. Human-validation boundary

Nothing in REVISION-5 changes TRANSLATION-12.

Human-validation status remains:

`PENDING`.

## 16. Research ceiling

The strongest current claim is:

> FSP can distinguish represented credibility distortion from interpretive-resource gaps, model repairs to standing and contestability, preserve counter-public evidence without auto-privileging it, and specify when a previously excluded perspective may re-enter a revision process while retaining explicit remainder and provenance.

It does not establish a complete theory of epistemic injustice.
