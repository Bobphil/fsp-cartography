# FSP-COMPARATOR-REGRESSION-1

## Four-Comparator Ablation, Core Irreducibility, Missing-Function Test, and Governance Residue

**Status:** COMPARATOR REGRESSION / CORE IRREDUCIBILITY TEST — VERSION 1  
**Parents:** FSP-FOUR-COMPARATOR-ARCHITECTURE-1; FSP-CONSTITUTIONAL-COMPRESSION-1; FSP-CONSTITUTIONAL-REGRESSION-1; FSP-RECOMPOSITION-GOVERNANCE-1  
**Comparators:** Herbert Simon; Robert Brandom; Huw Price; Jenann Ismael  
**Primary purpose:** Grant the strongest plausible explanatory contribution to each comparator, then test what remains architecturally necessary in FSP after individual and joint ablation.  
**Primary question:** If Simon, Brandom, Price, and Ismael together explain most of the relevant phenomena, what governance function remains specifically FSP?  
**Primary claim ceiling:** This regression establishes only an architectural residue relative to the present comparator set. It does not prove historical originality, philosophical superiority, exhaustive uniqueness, or that no future comparator could absorb part of the residue.

---

# 1. Why This Regression Is Harder Than Comparison

A comparator study can overstate distinctiveness simply by describing differences.

A proper regression must do something stronger.

It must assume the comparator is as powerful as reasonably possible and ask:

```text
IF WE GIVE THE COMPARATOR EVERYTHING IT CAN PLAUSIBLY OWN,
WHAT FSP FUNCTION DISAPPEARS?
WHAT FSP FUNCTION REMAINS?
```

The test therefore distinguishes:

## Explanatory coverage

A comparator helps explain why some FSP structure is useful or necessary.

## Governance ownership

A framework supplies the rule that determines what may responsibly cross a boundary, what follows, what does not, what action is authorized, what remains unresolved, and how consequences reopen inquiry.

The central methodological rule is:

> **Coverage ≠ ownership.**

---

# 2. Regression Procedure

For each comparator:

1. Grant its strongest plausible contribution.
2. Remove FSP structures that are fully replaced.
3. Preserve only functions not adequately reconstructed.
4. Record the residue.
5. Repeat with all four comparators combined.
6. Test whether the residue is:
   - irreducible;
   - composite but useful;
   - redundant;
   - unsupported.
7. Test whether the comparison reveals a missing Core primitive.

# 11. Is the Residue Really Irreducible?

The word “irreducible” requires care.

The residue may be reconstructible by combining resources from the comparators.

That does not yet make it redundant.

The relevant test is functional ownership.

Could one assemble:

- Simonian decomposition;
- Brandomian entitlement;
- Pricean semantic restraint;
- Ismaelian situated agency

into a system that performs FSP governance?

Yes, in principle.

But once one adds:

- portability checks;
- ceilings;
- authorization separation;
- remainder;
- routing;
- Return;

one has constructed something functionally very close to FSP.

Therefore:

> **FSP distinctiveness lies less in unique ingredients than in a distinctive governance composition.**

This is an important claim ceiling.

---

# 12. Ingredient Originality vs Architectural Originality

The regression rejects an overly strong originality test.

FSP does not need every component to be historically novel.

A better distinction is:

## Ingredient originality

Is each concept absent from prior philosophy?

Likely no.

## Architectural originality

Is there a distinctive configuration that performs a governance function not supplied as such by the comparators?

Current answer:

**Yes, provisionally.**

Thus:

> **Conceptual inheritance ≠ architectural redundancy.**

# 33. Eclecticism Test

A serious danger is that FSP simply collects useful concepts from many philosophers.

The test is:

> If we remove FSP governance, do the comparator pieces still tell us how to move among one another?

Current answer:

**No, not in one explicit architecture.**

Simon does not tell us how Brandomian entitlement should travel into Ismaelian agentive action.

Price does not tell us when Simonian design becomes authorized intervention.

Brandom does not tell us when Ismaelian situated reconstruction licenses ontological commitment.

Ismael does not tell us how institutional consequences should trigger repair.

Thus:

> **FSP’s role is connective and regulatory, not encyclopedic.**

This is the strongest defense against eclecticism.

# 43. REOPEN Conditions

Reopen the distinctiveness claim if:

1. another framework supplies the same cross-boundary governance architecture more directly;
2. Brandomian entitlement can be generalized without distortion to measurement, scale, translation, and authorization transitions;

5. Simonian procedural rationality can absorb normative portability and remainder without changing its character;
6. the governance residue cannot improve diagnosis, pedagogy, or research practice;
7. the residue proves to be merely a restatement of familiar critical-thinking norms.
