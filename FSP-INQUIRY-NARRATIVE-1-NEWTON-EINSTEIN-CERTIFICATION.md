## Certified third-case role

Newton→Einstein is the third whole-inquiry narrative spine and tests a distinct scientific-change topology.

The central architectural pressure is:

`deep conceptual transformation + preserved bounded empirical success`

The narrative architecture must avoid both:

1. complete continuity, as if Einstein merely supplied corrected values inside unchanged Newtonian concepts;
2. complete replacement, as if Newtonian mechanics became irrational, unintelligible, or practically useless after relativity.

## Whole-inquiry narrative

The spine carries the reader through:

- the long-standing success of Newtonian mechanics and gravitation;
- 1905 special relativity;
- changed roles for simultaneity, space, time, and inertial description;
- the unresolved problem of gravitation;
- 1907–1911 equivalence-principle reasoning;
- 1915 general relativity and Mercury's anomalous perihelion;
- 1919 and later empirical testing;
- continued Newtonian validity as a highly accurate approximation in bounded regimes.

## Four chapter apertures

### Chapter 1

Revision pressure acts on an exceptionally successful framework.

Later relativistic success does not prove that one particular revision path was logically mandatory in advance.

### Chapter 2

Meaning change is represented through semantic versions and inferential roles.

Same term does not guarantee same meaning.

Conceptual discontinuity does not entail total incomparability.

### Chapter 3

Simultaneity and elevator probes expose dependency structures.

Local equivalence remains bounded by locality and tidal remainder.

Thought-experiment insight remains distinct from empirical confirmation.

### Chapter 4

The diachronic formalism must represent:

- what changed;
- what was lost;
- what was gained;
- what was reinterpreted;
- what was preserved approximately or in limiting regimes.

## Stress-tested firewalls

The Newton→Einstein extension certifies:

- later theory ≠ simple deletion of earlier theory;
- preserved empirical success ≠ unchanged conceptual meaning;
- same term ≠ same inferential role;
- conceptual discontinuity ≠ total incomparability;
- approximation / limiting recovery ≠ global theoretical identity;
- local equivalence ≠ global ontological identity;
- thought-experiment insight ≠ empirical confirmation;
- historical chronology ≠ canonical state identity.
