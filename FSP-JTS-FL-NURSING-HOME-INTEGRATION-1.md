# FSP-JTS-FL-NURSING-HOME-INTEGRATION-1

## Gulf Pines Five-Layer Vertical Integration Pass

**Status:** INTEGRATION PASS — NOT A NEW LAYER  
**Parent:** FSP-JTS-FL-NURSING-HOME-1  
**Depends on:** Layers 1–5  
**Integration issue:** Visitation restriction during active outbreak conditions  
**Purpose:** Test whether one issue can move vertically through all five layers without conceptual duplication, reader disorientation, or loss of claim ceilings.

---

# 1. Why this integration pass matters

Five strong layers can still fail as an architecture if each becomes a separate module that restates the same problem in different vocabulary.

The Joint Task Space succeeds only if a reader can follow one issue through:

1. **what changed;**
2. **who may act;**
3. **how domains translate;**
4. **what repairs are available;**
5. **what happens when the repair becomes infrastructure.**

The layers therefore have distinct jobs.

```text
Layer 1 — WHEN / WHAT CHANGED?
Layer 2 — WHO MAY KNOW, RECOMMEND, AUTHORIZE, IMPLEMENT?
Layer 3 — HOW DO DIFFERENT REPRESENTATIONS TRAVEL?
Layer 4 — WHAT REPAIRS CAN BE SEQUENCED?
Layer 5 — WHAT HAPPENS WHEN THE JUDGMENT BECOMES POLICY / INFRASTRUCTURE?
```

No layer should redo another layer's work.

---

# 2. The integration issue

At Gulf Pines, active respiratory transmission increases across two units.

Clinical leadership recommends reducing interpersonal contact.

Administration considers a temporary facility-wide visitation restriction.

Families object.

Some residents are highly dependent on family contact for orientation, communication, feeding support, or emotional stability.

Staff report that they cannot replace all of those functions safely under current staffing.

The vertical question is:

> **How should the Joint Task Space govern the movement from clinical concern to possible visitation restriction, through authorization, implementation, review, and possible regulatory learning?**

---

# 3. Layer 1 — Temporal Inquiry State

## Job of Layer 1

Layer 1 does not decide whether visitation should be restricted.

It establishes the current inquiry state.

At **S2 — Intervention Pressure**, the shared map contains:

- confirmed active transmission;
- staffing instability;
- growing pressure for stronger restrictions;
- resident-specific isolation harms;
- family knowledge;
- implementation difficulty;
- regulatory infection-control duties.

Layer 1 asks:

> What is different now from S1?

Answer:

The task has changed from identifying and widening the problem to making a consequential institutional decision.

### Layer-1 output

```text
STATE: S2
PRESSURE: active outbreak + implementation strain
NEW DECISION: whether to restrict visitation
KEY UNCERTAINTY: scope, duration, unequal burden, effectiveness
REOPEN: transmission change, staffing change, burden change, new evidence
```

### Claim ceiling

> **Changed conditions justify a new decision point; they do not determine the decision.**

### Carry-forward to Layer 2

Carry:
- current evidence state;
- affected groups;
- uncertainty;
- decision requiring authorization.

Do not carry:
- assumed legitimacy;
- assumed policy scope;
- assumed implementation feasibility.

---

# 4. Layer 2 — Standing / Authorization

## Job of Layer 2

Layer 2 does not decide whether the clinical evidence is correct.

It asks who has which governance relation to the proposed action.

### Standing partition

Clinical leadership:
- strong evidential authority on infection risk;
- strong recommendation standing.

Residents:
- primary moral standing over burdens;
- resident-specific decisional authority where applicable.

Families:
- longitudinal knowledge;
- advocacy;
- proxy authority only where legally valid.

Frontline staff:
- strong feasibility evidence;
- implementation responsibility.

Administration:
- facility-level authorization authority within law, rights, and regulation.

Regulators:
- compliance authority and public-interest standing.

### Layer-2 output

```text
RECOMMENDER: clinical leadership
AFFECTED: residents, staff, families
AUTHORIZER: appropriate facility authority within legal/regulatory bounds
IMPLEMENTERS: frontline/operations
REQUIRED CONSULTATION: resident/proxy + frontline feasibility
AUTHORIZATION SCOPE: not yet settled
AUTHORIZATION EXPIRY: must be explicit
```

### Firewalls

> **Clinical expertise ≠ total authorization.**

> **Family concern ≠ unrestricted proxy authority.**

> **Implementation responsibility ≠ policy authority.**

### Carry-forward to Layer 3

Carry:
- source actor;
- standing type;
- recommendation state;
- authorization boundary.

Do not carry:
- assumption that all participants mean the same thing by "restriction" or "harm."

---

# 5. Layer 3 — Translation / Remainder

## Job of Layer 3

Layer 3 does not decide who has authority.

It makes the domain crossings inspectable.

### Translation chain

Clinical source:

> "Reduce high-risk contact during active transmission."

Possible institutional translation:

> "Suspend visitation."

Resident-life translation:

> "Remove familiar people, touch, advocacy, orientation, and informal care."

Operational translation:

> "Shift family-supplied functions onto already strained staff."

Regulatory translation:

> "Meet infection-control duties while respecting resident rights and applicable exceptions."

These are related.

They are not identical.

### Translation object

```text
source_domain: CLINICAL
source_object: transmission-control recommendation
translated_target: facility visitation policy
provenance: active multi-unit transmission evidence
assumption: reduced visitation materially reduces exposure
remainder:
  resident-specific capability burden
  informal family-care functions
  staffing substitution burden
  exact marginal transmission reduction
claim_ceiling:
  recommendation does not establish one uniform authorized policy
```

### Layer-3 output

The phrase "restrict visitation" is now decompressed into multiple domain meanings.

### Firewalls

> **Translation ≠ identity.**

> **Clinical recommendation ≠ authorized policy.**

> **Untranslated remainder ≠ irrelevant remainder.**

### Carry-forward to Layer 4

Carry:
- explicit repair target;
- known burdens;
- unresolved remainder;
- translation assumptions;
- claim ceiling.

Do not carry:
- one preferred solution.

---

# 6. Layer 4 — Repair / Strategy Sequencing

## Job of Layer 4

Layer 4 does not decide who may authorize.

It generates and sequences repair families.

### Candidate strategy family

Instead of one binary choice:

```text
VISITATION OPEN
vs
VISITATION CLOSED
```

the Joint Task Space generates a bundle:

```text
clinical:
  unit-specific transmission precautions

staffing:
  temporary coverage reinforcement

resident capability:
  essential-caregiver exceptions

family/relational:
  scheduled communication + safe contact support

governance:
  72-hour authorization review + burden monitoring
```

### Sequencing tests

Prerequisite:
Is PPE/screening capacity available for caregiver exceptions?

Dependency:
Does safe caregiver access require extra staff?

Conflict:
Does the restriction reduce transmission while increasing falls, distress, or feeding difficulty?

Order:
Should unit-specific evidence be used before a facility-wide rule?

### Action-state movement

```text
GENERATED:
  essential-caregiver exception

POSSIBLE:
  PPE + screening + staffing available

RECOMMENDED:
  clinical + capability review supports bounded access

AUTHORIZED:
  facility authority approves defined exception

IMPLEMENTED:
  caregiver visit occurs under protocol

REVIEWED:
  infection, burden, feasibility assessed
```

### Layer-4 output

A repair bundle with:
- prerequisites;
- sequencing;
- scope;
- reversibility;
- stop conditions;
- review interval.

### Firewalls

> **Generated ≠ Recommended ≠ Authorized.**

> **Individually reasonable repairs ≠ jointly coherent bundle.**

> **Implemented ≠ successful.**

### Carry-forward to Layer 5

Carry:
- actual implemented policy;
- review conditions;
- observed burdens;
- exceptions;
- implementation data;
- stop conditions.

Do not carry:
- assumption that a useful local bundle should become a general rule.

---

# 7. Layer 5 — Institutional Amplification

## Job of Layer 5

Layer 5 does not redesign the local repair.

It asks what happens if the local judgment becomes repeated infrastructure.

### Facility amplification

A temporary Gulf Pines rule may become:

- standing visitation policy;
- software default;
- emergency checklist item;
- staff training rule;
- exception form;
- metric.

New question:

> Does the policy remain justified when the state changes from S2 to S3?

### Institutional feedback

Implementation generates:

- exception frequency;
- resident harm;
- staffing burden;
- caregiver feasibility;
- infection outcomes;
- local workarounds.

These can enter an upward feedback packet.

### Regulatory amplification

Across multiple facilities, agencies may see a pattern:

- visitation restrictions are often necessary;
- some resident groups experience disproportionate capability loss;
- family members perform unmeasured care functions;
- exception pathways vary widely;
- staffing capacity changes policy feasibility.

This may justify regulatory inquiry.

It does not itself dictate a new rule.

### Layer-5 output

```text
LOCAL_POLICY_EFFECT
EXCEPTION_PATTERN
BURDEN_PATTERN
IMPLEMENTATION_FAILURE
REMAINDER
POSSIBLE_SYSTEMIC_PATTERN
REGULATORY_REOPEN_TRIGGER
```

### Firewalls

> **Local case ≠ population rule.**

> **Evidence sufficient to REOPEN ≠ evidence sufficient to replace.**

> **Repeated use ≠ continuing justification.**

# 8. The vertical chain

The complete vertical path is:

```text
S2: active outbreak changes the inquiry state
        ↓
Layer 1
identify new decision pressure
        ↓
Layer 2
partition evidence / standing / authorization
        ↓
Layer 3
translate clinical recommendation without erasing resident,
staff, family, or regulatory meaning
        ↓
Layer 4
generate and sequence a bounded repair bundle
        ↓
authorized + implemented local policy
        ↓
Layer 5
inspect amplification, sedimentation, metrics, exceptions,
and upward regulatory learning
        ↓
new evidence
        ↓
REOPEN
        ↓
S3 revised bounded judgment
```

This is one inquiry, not five modules.

---

# 9. Non-duplication rule

Each layer owns one primary question.

| Layer | Owns | Must not re-own |
|---|---|---|
| 1 Temporal State | What changed? | authority, translation, strategy, regulation |
| 2 Standing / Authorization | Who may do what? | evidence chronology, translation semantics, strategy design |
| 3 Translation / Remainder | What travels and what is lost? | authorization decision, strategy selection |
| 4 Repair / Sequencing | What repair family and order? | standing assignment, regulatory generalization |
| 5 Institutional Amplification | What happens when scaled/sedimented? | local clinical diagnosis, local strategy generation |

If a layer begins re-solving another layer's question, the architecture is drifting.

---

# 10. Carry-forward contract

A layer should pass forward only what the next layer needs.

## Layer 1 → 2
Pass:
- current state;
- decision pressure;
- uncertainty;
- affected actors.

## Layer 2 → 3
Pass:
- source roles;
- standing;
- recommendation/authorization state.

## Layer 3 → 4
Pass:
- translated problem;
- provenance;
- remainder;
- claim ceiling.

## Layer 4 → 5
Pass:
- implemented repair;
- scope;
- expiry;
- metrics;
- observed effects;
- exceptions.

## Layer 5 → Layer 1 / REOPEN
Pass back:
- changed conditions;
- institutional effects;
- recurring failure patterns;
- new evidence;
- regulatory changes.

This creates a governed loop rather than a one-way pipeline.

---

# 11. Reader compression

The public reader should not force every reader through all five layers every time.

Use progressive disclosure.

## Quick path

```text
What changed?
→ Who may decide?
→ What repair is being considered?
→ What would REOPEN it?
```

## Standard path

Add:
- translation;
- remainder;
- sequencing;
- authorization expiry.

## Deep path

Add:
- full provenance;
- action-state transitions;
- institutional amplification;
- regulatory feedback;
- grammar-level REOPEN.

Firewall:

> **Progressive disclosure ≠ semantic mutation.**

---

# 12. Usability test

The five-layer stack passes only if a reader can answer, at any point:

1. Where am I?
2. What question does this layer own?
3. What changed from the previous layer?
4. What was carried forward?
5. What remains unresolved?
6. Where can I return?
7. What would REOPEN the inquiry?

If not, the architecture is too complex.

---

# 13. Integration failure modes

## Layer repetition
The same standing or claim-ceiling explanation appears in every layer.

Repair:
canonical ownership + links.

## Vocabulary inflation
Each layer invents new terminology for an existing distinction.

Repair:
reuse canonical terms.

## Reader tunneling
The user enters a deep layer and loses the case.

Repair:
persistent case/state header + Return.

## Pipeline illusion
The five layers appear mandatory and linear.

Repair:
show conditional routing and re-entry.

## False completeness
Completing five layers appears to settle the problem.

Repair:

> **Five-layer pass ≠ final closure.**

## Governance overload
The architecture becomes harder to use than the problem warrants.

Repair:
Quick / Standard / Deep projection.

---

# 14. Integration acceptance tests

The integration passes if:

1. one issue can be followed vertically through all five layers;
2. each layer has one distinct primary job;
3. no layer silently strengthens the previous layer's claim;
4. carry-forward is explicit;
5. remainder survives;
6. standing survives translation;
7. repair remains separate from authorization;
8. institutional amplification receives actual implementation evidence;
9. REOPEN returns the inquiry to a changed state;
10. a Quick path remains possible without semantic distortion.

---

# 15. Integrated result

The five-layer architecture can now be compressed as:

```text
STATE
  What changed?

STANDING
  Who may know, recommend, authorize, implement?

TRANSLATION
  What travels, and what remains?

REPAIR
  What can be tried, in what order, under what bounds?

AMPLIFICATION
  What happens when the judgment becomes infrastructure?

REOPEN
  What changed enough to begin again?
```

The governing conclusion is:

> **A usable Joint Task Space is not five separate analyses. It is one corrigible inquiry in which state, standing, translation, repair, and amplification remain distinct but continuously communicate.**
