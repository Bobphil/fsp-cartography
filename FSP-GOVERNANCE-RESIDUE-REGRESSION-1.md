# FSP-GOVERNANCE-RESIDUE-REGRESSION-1

## Six-Role Ablation, Independent Necessity Test, Absorption Test, and Minimal Governance Residue

**Status:** GOVERNANCE RESIDUE REGRESSION / ROLE ABLATION — VERSION 1  
**Parent:** FSP-GOVERNANCE-RESIDUE-1  
**Primary purpose:** Remove each of the six governance-residue roles one at a time and test whether medicine, consciousness, institutions, scientific inquiry, and AI remain adequately governed.  
**Roles under test:** Boundary Portability; Claim Ceiling; Authorization Separation; Remainder Visibility; Failure Routing; Consequential Return.  
**Primary question:** Which roles are independently necessary, which can be absorbed by another, and which should be demoted from distinctive residue to operational support?  
**Primary claim ceiling:** This regression tests architectural necessity inside the present FSP ecology. It does not establish psychological indispensability, classroom usability, historical originality, or final minimality.

---

# 1. Ablation Method

For each role:

1. remove the role;
2. preserve the other five;
3. rerun medicine, consciousness, institutions, science, and AI;
4. record governance loss;
5. test whether another role absorbs the lost function;
6. classify the role as:
   - independently necessary;
   - absorbable;
   - operational support;
   - domain-local.

The standard of failure is not conceptual discomfort.

A role survives only if its removal changes what may responsibly be claimed, authorized, left unresolved, repaired, reopened, or returned.

# 2. Baseline Six-Role Architecture

The parent build proposed:

BOUNDARY PORTABILITY  
→ CLAIM CEILING  
→ AUTHORIZATION SEPARATION  
→ REMAINDER VISIBILITY  
→ FAILURE ROUTING  
→ CONSEQUENTIAL RETURN

The regression does not assume all six survive.

# 3. Ablation A — Remove Boundary Portability

## What remains

- Claim Ceiling;
- Authorization Separation;
- Remainder Visibility;
- Failure Routing;
- Consequential Return.

## Immediate problem

Without Boundary Portability, the architecture can still ask:

- what follows;
- who may act;
- what remains;
- what failed;
- what happened.

But it loses the explicit trigger:

> **Has a locally warranted result entered a changed context where its warrant conditions must be rechecked?**

The remaining roles become reactive rather than transition-sensitive.

## Medicine

Population evidence can still be bounded by a claim ceiling, but without Portability the reason for rechecking the patient-level transfer is underdescribed.

## Consciousness

A ceiling can still block functional→phenomenal inflation, but the architecture no longer explicitly identifies the boundary where warrant conditions changed.

## Institutions

Local→regulatory transfer becomes harder to detect before failure.

## Science

Measurement→model→ontology transitions lose the common crossing test.

## AI

Task competence→authority can be blocked by Authorization, but the epistemic transfer itself is no longer explicitly governed.

## Absorption test

Can Claim Ceiling absorb Portability?

No.

Ceiling answers how far the claim may go **after** the crossing is under inspection.

It does not by itself identify the crossing.

Can Failure Routing absorb Portability?

No.

Routing requires a diagnosed risk; it does not supply the general transfer test.

### Verdict

**BOUNDARY PORTABILITY — INDEPENDENTLY NECESSARY.**

# 4. Ablation B — Remove Claim Ceiling

## What remains

- Boundary Portability;
- Authorization Separation;
- Remainder Visibility;
- Failure Routing;
- Consequential Return.

## Immediate problem

The architecture can identify that a boundary changed, but cannot state the strongest conclusion that survives.

It can say:

> warrant needs rechecking

but not:

> this much, and no more, currently follows.

## Medicine

Population→patient portability can be inspected, but there is no explicit bounded arrival such as:

> supports a probabilistic estimate, not a guaranteed outcome.

## Consciousness

Functional→phenomenal crossing can be flagged, but the architecture loses the explicit stopping rule:

> structural constraint ≠ phenomenal identity.

## Institutions

Local success→policy can be inspected, but there is no formal limit on generalization.

## Science

Model success→ontology loses its clearest governance barrier.

## AI

Benchmark performance→general competence has no explicit inferential ceiling.

## Absorption test

Can Remainder absorb Claim Ceiling?

No.

Remainder says what remains unresolved.

It does not state the strongest licensed conclusion.

Can Portability absorb Claim Ceiling?

No.

Portability answers whether warrant survives; it does not specify the bounded strength of the surviving claim.

### Verdict

**CLAIM CEILING — INDEPENDENTLY NECESSARY.**

---

# 5. Ablation C — Remove Authorization Separation

## What remains

- Boundary Portability;
- Claim Ceiling;
- Remainder Visibility;
- Failure Routing;
- Consequential Return.

## Immediate problem

The architecture can govern belief and inference, but cannot prevent:

> well-supported judgment → legitimate intervention

from becoming automatic.

## Medicine

This is catastrophic.

A sound clinical recommendation becomes indistinguishable from patient consent.

## Consciousness

Descriptive inquiry may survive, but intervention, manipulation, or identity-shaping research loses a separate permission check.

## Institutions

Expertise can silently become authority.

## Science

Technical success can be converted into policy authority without additional governance.

## AI

Recommendation, prediction, or classification can silently become delegated action.

## Absorption test

Can Claim Ceiling absorb Authorization?

No.

A claim can remain epistemically modest while action is still unauthorized.

Can Return absorb Authorization?

No.

Consequences can reveal harm after action, but governance must also prevent unauthorized action before it occurs.

### Verdict

**AUTHORIZATION SEPARATION — INDEPENDENTLY NECESSARY.**

---

# 6. Ablation D — Remove Remainder Visibility

## What remains

- Boundary Portability;
- Claim Ceiling;
- Authorization Separation;
- Failure Routing;
- Consequential Return.

## Immediate problem

The architecture can limit claims, but whatever failed to cross becomes structurally invisible.

This creates false completeness.

## Medicine

A treatment estimate can be bounded, but unresolved patient-specific interactions, values, and long-term effects are no longer explicitly preserved.

## Consciousness

The architecture can say “do not infer phenomenal identity,” but cannot preserve the qualitative or perspectival residue as a first-class unresolved output.

## Institutions

Lost context and exception structure disappear from the record.

## Science

Alternative models, untested mechanisms, and ontology uncertainty become mere absence rather than tracked remainder.

## AI

Unknown failure modes and provenance gaps disappear into generic uncertainty.

## Absorption test

Can Claim Ceiling absorb Remainder?

No.

A ceiling tells what does not follow; Remainder tells what remains unresolved and may matter later.

Can Return absorb Remainder?

Partly, but too late.

Return can reveal omitted factors after deployment, but Remainder must survive before action as well.

### Verdict

**REMAINDER VISIBILITY — INDEPENDENTLY NECESSARY.**

---

# 7. Ablation E — Remove Failure Routing

## What remains

- Boundary Portability;
- Claim Ceiling;
- Authorization Separation;
- Remainder Visibility;
- Consequential Return.

## Immediate problem

The architecture loses a compact way to distinguish why a transition failed.

But does governance itself collapse?

Not necessarily.

A user can still ask:

- did warrant survive?
- what follows?
- who may act?
- what remains?
- what happened?

## Medicine

The case remains governable without naming Omission Failure or Background-Condition Blindness.

Repair becomes less efficient and less diagnostic, but the major limits remain visible.

## Consciousness

The system can still preserve a phenomenal ceiling and remainder without routing to Inferential Overreach or Causal Confidence Inflation.

## Institutions

Policy transfer can still be bounded and authorization-checked.

The lack of routing mainly harms repair precision.

## Science

Model→ontology governance survives.

## AI

Competence→authority governance survives.

## Absorption test

Can Boundary Portability + Claim Ceiling + Remainder perform most of Routing’s governance function?

Yes.

They identify:

- where the transfer occurred;
- what no longer follows;
- what remains unresolved.

Authorization and Return then handle action and feedback.

Failure Routing adds:

- faster diagnosis;
- Tool selection;
- repair specificity;
- pedagogical usability.

But it does not appear independently necessary for the core governance contract.

### Verdict

**FAILURE ROUTING — ABSORBABLE AS OPERATIONAL SUPPORT.**

Recommendation:

> Demote Failure Routing from distinctive residue to canonical-support meta-governance.

This mirrors the earlier treatment of Recomposition Risk.

# 8. Ablation F — Remove Consequential Return

## What remains

- Boundary Portability;
- Claim Ceiling;
- Authorization Separation;
- Remainder Visibility;
- Failure Routing.

## Immediate problem

The architecture becomes pre-action governance only.

It cannot require consequences to feed back into the inquiry.

## Medicine

Outcome no longer updates diagnosis, treatment plan, patient preference, or future options.

## Consciousness

New experiments no longer revise the constraint map through an explicit return loop.

## Institutions

Policy effects cannot reopen the rule as part of the architecture.

## Science

Failed predictions and new observations become external events rather than governed re-entry.

## AI

Deployment harms, drift, and changing trust do not automatically alter scope or authority.

## Absorption test

Can Remainder absorb Return?

No.

Remainder is what is unresolved at the transition.

Return is what the world gives back after inquiry or action.

Can Portability absorb Return?

No.

Portability is forward transfer.

Return is recursive consequence.

### Verdict

**CONSEQUENTIAL RETURN — INDEPENDENTLY NECESSARY.**

# 12. Pairwise Absorption Test — Portability + Ceiling

Could Portability and Claim Ceiling merge into one role?

Candidate:

> Portability = how far warrant travels.

This sounds promising.

But the two can diverge.

A result may be fully portable to a new context while still supporting only a limited claim.

Example:

A calibrated measurement transfers correctly, yet only establishes a confidence interval, not mechanism.

Therefore:

> **Portability ≠ inferential strength.**

### Verdict

KEEP SEPARATE.

---

# 13. Pairwise Absorption Test — Ceiling + Remainder

Could a ceiling implicitly encode remainder?

No.

Example:

Ceiling:

> structural evidence does not establish phenomenal identity.

Remainder:

> qualitative character remains unresolved.

The ceiling is a limit.

The remainder is preserved content about what is unresolved.

### Verdict

KEEP SEPARATE.

---

# 14. Pairwise Absorption Test — Authorization + Ceiling

Could authorization be another kind of ceiling?

No.

A claim ceiling bounds assertion.

Authorization bounds action.

The two can vary independently.

### Verdict

KEEP SEPARATE.

---

# 15. Pairwise Absorption Test — Remainder + Return

Could Return carry all unresolved content forward?

Not safely.

Remainder must be visible before action.

Return occurs after consequences or further inquiry.

### Verdict

KEEP SEPARATE.

---

# 16. Pairwise Absorption Test — Portability + Return

Could the whole architecture become one recursive portability loop?

Not yet.

Portability is prospective:

> may the result travel?

Return is retrospective/recursive:

> what happened after the result entered the world?

They are dual directions, not duplicates.

### Verdict

KEEP SEPARATE.

# 31. Is Authorization Always Present?

No.

Some inquiry remains descriptive.

But Authorization Separation remains a residue role because whenever action, recommendation, classification, intervention, delegation, or institutionalization occurs, epistemic warrant alone is insufficient.

A role need not be active in every case to be constitutionally portable.

---

# 32. Is Portability Always Present?

Not every local inquiry crosses an obvious external boundary.

But even staying “local” can involve:

- evidence→claim;
- model→interpretation;
- observation→judgment.

Portability therefore includes bounded movement within an inquiry, not merely cross-domain transfer.

This prevents the architecture from becoming too narrow.

---

# 33. Is Claim Ceiling Always Present?

Every governed claim has some upper bound, even if the bound is trivial.

In practice, the role becomes explicit only under pressure.

Thus Claim Ceiling remains a latent governance role.

---

# 34. Is Return Always Present?

If nothing is acted upon and no further inquiry occurs, Return may be minimal.

But the architecture requires corrigibility in principle.

Return can therefore be:

- empirical;
- practical;
- institutional;
- interpretive;
- successor-oriented.
