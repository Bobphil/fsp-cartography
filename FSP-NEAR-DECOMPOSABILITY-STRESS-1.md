# FSP-NEAR-DECOMPOSABILITY-STRESS-1

## Seven-Case Stress Test of Simonian Near-Decomposability, FSP Compression, Recomposition Burden, and Consequential Return

**Status:** CROSS-DOMAIN STRESS TEST — VERSION 1  
**Parents:** FSP-SIMON-COMPARATOR-1; FSP-CONSTITUTIONAL-COMPRESSION-1; FSP-CONSTITUTIONAL-REGRESSION-1  
**Primary purpose:** Test whether near-decomposability is genuinely useful for deciding when FSP compression is safe, when formal decompression is required, and where recomposition becomes high-burden.  
**Cases:** Neptune/Vulcan; Challenger/Boisjoly; bat objective phenomenology; patient medicine; planaria adaptation; institutional safety; psychedelic/neural/phenomenal inference.  
**Primary claim ceiling:** This document does not establish that these domains are objectively partitioned into fixed subsystems, that Simonian near-decomposability applies universally, or that FSP has identified a universal metric of coupling. It tests whether a claim-relative decomposition/recomposition discipline improves diagnostic clarity.

---

# 1. Stress-Test Question

The Simon comparator yielded a candidate synthesis:

```text
NEAR-DECOMPOSABILITY
→ TRACTABLE PARTIAL MAPS
→ BOUNDED SEARCH / DESIGN
→ GOVERNED TRANSITIONS
→ CLAIM CEILINGS / REMAINDERS
→ AUTHORIZED ACTION
→ CONSEQUENCE
→ REOPEN
→ RETURN
```

That synthesis now requires stress.

The central question is:

> **Can FSP use near-decomposability to distinguish when a partial map is adequate for a bounded claim from when omitted coupling makes recomposition dangerous?**

A stronger version is:

> **Does the same constitutional Core reliably tell us when to stay compressed and when to open a specialized layer?**

---

# 2. Near-Decomposability Must Be Claim-Relative

This stress test rejects a simplistic classification:

```text
domain X = near-decomposable
domain Y = strongly coupled
```

The better formulation is:

> **A system is near-decomposable only relative to a target, scale, time horizon, and claim.**

The same case may permit aggressive compression for one claim and require extensive decompression for another.

Examples:

- Neptune/Vulcan may be decomposable for comparing anomaly-response strategies but not for reconstructing full nineteenth-century astronomical practice.
- A bat sensory system may be decomposable for navigation competence while remaining high-burden for phenomenal claims.
- A patient may be decomposable into organ-level risk factors for one decision while remaining strongly coupled across physiology, medication, goals, and social context for another.
- A psychedelic study may be decomposable for receptor occupancy or network statistics while becoming strongly coupled when inferring enduring value or identity change.

This yields the first stress-test rule:

> **Near-decomposability is indexed to the claim.**

# 4. Failure Taxonomy

The Simon comparator proposed five failure types.

This stress test operationalizes them.

## ND-F1 — Bad Decomposition

A relevant coupling is omitted.

## ND-F2 — Bad Target Binding

The analysis decomposes the wrong target or silently shifts target.

## ND-F3 — Bad Recomposition

A locally valid result is carried across a boundary without sufficient warrant.

## ND-F4 — Bad Authorization

A correct or useful result is used by an actor lacking standing or authority.

## ND-F5 — Bad Return

Consequences fail to update the next inquiry state.

The stress test asks whether these five types are sufficient across heterogeneous cases.

# 5. Case A — Neptune / Vulcan

## 5.1 Target Claim

How should an astronomical anomaly alter confidence in a theory, auxiliaries, measurement practices, or ontology?

## 5.2 Candidate Decomposition

A useful compressed decomposition is:

```text
observation
→ residual anomaly
→ auxiliary/model adjustment
→ candidate explanation
→ subsequent evidence
```

For some philosophical purposes, Neptune and Vulcan can be compared through the same inquiry topology.

## 5.3 Strong Couplings

Strong couplings include:

- theory ↔ auxiliary assumptions;
- observation ↔ instrument/calculation practices;
- anomaly ↔ background astronomical commitments;
- prediction ↔ later observation;
- success/failure ↔ reinterpretation of the problem space.

## 5.4 Weak or Temporarily Negligible Couplings

For a narrow comparison of anomaly-handling:

- many biographical details;
- some institutional histories;
- full mathematical derivations;
- unrelated astronomical observations

may remain compressed.

## 5.5 Safe Compression

Safe claim:

> Both cases can be compared as episodes in which anomaly did not mechanically dictate one immediate theoretical response.

This preserves the relation structure without claiming identity of historical context.

## 5.6 Decomposition Failure Risk

Failure occurs if the compressed pattern becomes:

> Neptune and Vulcan were the same kind of case.

They were not.

Their later evidential trajectories and theoretical resolutions differ.

## 5.7 Recomposition Bridge

```text
local anomaly topology
→ broader philosophy-of-science claim
```

## 5.8 Bridge Burden

Moderate.

The philosophical comparison is strong when restricted to:

- underdetermination;
- auxiliary adjustment;
- temporal development of inquiry.

It becomes high-burden if used to infer:

- identical scientific rationality;
- identical ontology;
- universal theory-change mechanism.

## 5.9 Remainder

- historical context;
- mathematical detail;
- institutional differences;
- different later resolutions.

## 5.10 Failure Detection

Primary risks:

- ND-F2 Bad Target Binding;
- ND-F3 Bad Recomposition.

## 5.11 Return

The case returns to scientific inquiry with:

> **An anomaly changes the inquiry state, but does not by itself determine which part of the map must change.**

### Verdict

**NEAR-DECOMPOSABILITY: HIGH for bounded inquiry-topology comparison; LOW for total historical equivalence.**

This is a PASS for claim-relative compression.

# 6. Case B — Challenger / Boisjoly

## 6.1 Target Claim

How can technically local evidence fail when carried through organizational communication and authority structures?

## 6.2 Candidate Decomposition

```text
engineering evidence
→ risk interpretation
→ communication
→ managerial review
→ launch authority
→ action
```

This appears modular.

But the modules are strongly connected by institutional power.

## 6.3 Strong Couplings

- engineering judgment ↔ organizational hierarchy;
- technical uncertainty ↔ schedule pressure;
- communication framing ↔ decision authority;
- dissent ↔ institutional standing;
- local evidence ↔ launch consequence.

These couplings cannot be treated as weak.

## 6.4 Weak or Temporarily Negligible Couplings

Some technical details can remain compressed for an ethics/inquiry lesson if the claim concerns governance rather than mechanical reconstruction.

But the engineering evidence itself cannot be reduced to “engineers were worried.”

## 6.5 Safe Compression

Safe:

> Technical evidence, communication, and authority can be represented as distinct analytic layers.

Unsafe:

> They operated independently.

The key structure is not decomposition alone but constrained recomposition.

## 6.6 Decomposition Failure Risk

A bad decomposition isolates engineering evidence from:

- authority;
- schedule;
- organizational incentives;
- who had decision rights.

This converts a coupled institutional failure into a mere technical-error story.

## 6.7 Recomposition Bridge

```text
technical judgment
→ management decision
→ institutional action
```

## 6.8 Bridge Burden

High.

The bridge carries:

- epistemic translation;
- uncertainty;
- authority;
- institutional pressure;
- consequence.

## 6.9 Remainder

- unresolved technical uncertainty;
- incomplete communication;
- uncertainty about how decision-makers interpreted evidence;
- structural pressures not fully captured by any one actor’s map.

## 6.10 Authorization Boundary

Crucial.

Technical expertise does not equal final launch authority.

But authority cannot legitimately erase relevant technical evidence.

Thus:

> **Epistemic standing ≠ decision authority.**

and:

> **Decision authority ≠ license to suppress evidence.**

## 6.11 Failure Detection

Primary:

- ND-F1 Bad Decomposition;
- ND-F3 Bad Recomposition;
- ND-F4 Bad Authorization;
- ND-F5 Bad Return.

## 6.12 Return

A consequential Return must alter:

- communication routes;
- dissent protection;
- decision procedures;
- evidence retention;
- safety review.

### Verdict

**NEAR-DECOMPOSABILITY: LOW once technical evidence enters organizational decision.**

The system is analyzable into layers, but not safely independent.

This is a major stress-test success because FSP detects coupling exactly where local technical maps become institutionally executable.

# 7. Case C — Bat Objective Phenomenology

## 7.1 Target Claim

How much can heterogeneous evidence constrain the structure of bat experience without claiming first-person equivalence?

## 7.2 Candidate Decomposition

```text
environmental structure
→ detectable acoustic structure
→ discriminable structure
→ actionable navigation structure
→ candidate phenomenal structure
```

## 7.3 Strong Couplings

Within navigation:

- active sensing ↔ echo return;
- motor behavior ↔ sensory sampling;
- memory ↔ route use;
- multimodal integration ↔ performance.

These are strongly coupled.

## 7.4 Weak or Temporarily Negligible Couplings

For some narrow questions:

- precise qualitative feel;
- full neural mechanism;
- social significance

can remain outside the target.

## 7.5 Safe Compression

Safe:

> Acoustic structure supports spatially useful discrimination and navigation.

Unsafe:

> Spatially useful acoustic processing tells us what echolocation feels like.

## 7.6 Decomposition Failure Risk

The key danger is not underdecomposition but illicit recomposition.

A valid chain:

```text
physical echo
→ sensory access
→ discrimination
→ action
```

does not automatically extend to:

```text
→ qualitative character
```

## 7.7 Recomposition Bridge

```text
functional / behavioral structure
→ phenomenal structure
```

## 7.8 Bridge Burden

Very high.

This is one of the highest-burden bridges in FSP.

## 7.9 Remainder

Phenomenal Translation Remainder:

- intrinsic qualitative character;
- underdetermined phenomenal organization;
- first-person possession.

## 7.10 Authorization Boundary

Not primarily an intervention issue in this case, but standing remains separate from inferred phenomenology.

> **Phenomenal uncertainty ≠ absence of standing.**

## 7.11 Failure Detection

Primary:

- ND-F3 Bad Recomposition.

Secondary:

- ND-F2 Bad Target Binding if “navigation” silently becomes “experience.”

## 7.12 Return

Return is epistemic:

> Stronger structural constraint with preserved qualitative remainder.

### Verdict

**NEAR-DECOMPOSABILITY: HIGH through sensory-functional levels; VERY LOW at the functional→phenomenal bridge.**

This is a decisive demonstration that decomposability changes by transition.

# 8. Case D — Patient Medicine

## 8.1 Target Claim

How should population-level evidence be carried into a patient-level treatment judgment?

## 8.2 Candidate Decomposition

```text
population evidence
→ subgroup evidence
→ patient state
→ treatment estimate
→ clinical judgment
→ recommendation
→ consent / authorization
→ intervention
→ outcome
```

## 8.3 Strong Couplings

Potentially strong:

- comorbidity ↔ treatment risk;
- medication ↔ physiology;
- patient goals ↔ acceptable benefit/harm;
- social context ↔ feasibility;
- diagnostic uncertainty ↔ treatment choice;
- treatment ↔ future options.

## 8.4 Weak or Temporarily Negligible Couplings

For a narrow laboratory question, many social and preference variables may be irrelevant.

For treatment choice, they may become decisive.

## 8.5 Safe Compression

Safe:

> Population evidence can constrain expected outcomes for a patient.

Unsafe:

> Population average = patient outcome.

## 8.6 Decomposition Failure Risk

A common bad decomposition treats:

- disease;
- patient;
- intervention;
- context

as independent modules.

The practical case may instead be strongly coupled.

## 8.7 Recomposition Bridge

```text
population estimate
→ individual recommendation
```

## 8.8 Bridge Burden

High.

Burden includes:

- external validity;
- subgroup fit;
- comorbidities;
- patient values;
- uncertainty;
- alternatives;
- feasibility.

## 8.9 Remainder

- individual response uncertainty;
- unmeasured interactions;
- preference-sensitive outcomes;
- long-term effects;
- practical constraints.

## 8.10 Authorization Boundary

Essential.

A well-supported recommendation does not itself authorize treatment.

```text
clinical judgment
≠
patient consent
```

## 8.11 Failure Detection

All five failure types may occur.

Especially:

- ND-F1 Bad Decomposition;
- ND-F3 Bad Recomposition;
- ND-F4 Bad Authorization;
- ND-F5 Bad Return.

## 8.12 Return

Outcome must update:

- diagnosis;
- treatment plan;
- patient preferences;
- future options;
- evidence interpretation.

### Verdict

**NEAR-DECOMPOSABILITY: MODERATE for evidence generation; LOW for patient-level intervention.**

Medicine strongly validates the distinction between tractable scientific decomposition and high-burden practical recomposition.

# 9. Case E — Planaria Adaptation

## 9.1 Target Claim

How should adaptation to perturbation be represented when current capability depends on prior history and may alter future reachability?

## 9.2 Candidate Decomposition

```text
perturbation
→ local physiological / transcriptional response
→ organism-level tolerance
→ altered future response capacity
```

## 9.3 Strong Couplings

- organism state ↔ prior assembly history;
- local molecular change ↔ organism viability;
- environment ↔ adaptive response;
- present response ↔ future capability.

## 9.4 Weak or Temporarily Negligible Couplings

Some molecular detail can remain compressed if the target is the topology of adaptation rather than mechanism.

But mechanistic claims cannot ignore it.

## 9.5 Safe Compression

Safe:

> History-sensitive adaptation changes future capability.

Unsafe:

> Successful adaptation proves a particular search mechanism or global intelligence.

## 9.6 Decomposition Failure Risk

Two opposite errors:

1. flatten adaptation into one local mechanism;
2. inflate organism-level success into evidence for a preferred global theory.

## 9.7 Recomposition Bridge

```text
local biological change
→ organism-level adaptive competence
```

and potentially:

```text
organism adaptation
→ general theory of agency/search
```

The second bridge carries much higher burden.

## 9.8 Bridge Burden

Moderate for local→organism functional claims.

Very high for organism→general agency theory.

## 9.9 Remainder

- precise mechanism;
- search architecture;
- role of history;
- generalizability across organisms.

## 9.10 Authorization Boundary

Minimal in the descriptive case, but becomes significant in intervention or synthetic-biology contexts.

## 9.11 Failure Detection

Primary:

- ND-F3 Bad Recomposition.

Secondary:

- ND-F2 Bad Target Binding.

## 9.12 Return

Return is transformation-sensitive:

> The system may return to a changed state in which its future feasible set differs from its starting state.

### Verdict

**NEAR-DECOMPOSABILITY: MODERATE and target-dependent.**

The case validates FSP’s distinction between local mechanism, organism-level capability, and general agency theory.

# 10. Case F — Institutional Safety

## 10.1 Target Claim

When can local safety judgments be scaled into institutional rules without losing the conditions that made them reasonable?

## 10.2 Candidate Decomposition

```text
local event
→ local judgment
→ facility policy
→ metric / workflow
→ regulatory category
→ system-wide rule
```

## 10.3 Strong Couplings

- local context ↔ policy effect;
- metric ↔ behavior;
- authority ↔ implementation;
- rule ↔ exception structure;
- policy ↔ burden distribution;
- feedback ↔ institutional learning.

## 10.4 Weak or Temporarily Negligible Couplings

At the local level, some system-wide heterogeneity may be safely ignored.

At regulatory scale, it cannot.

## 10.5 Safe Compression

Safe:

> A local pattern may justify REOPEN at a higher level.

Unsafe:

> A local success warrants universal rule replacement.

## 10.6 Decomposition Failure Risk

Institutional systems are especially vulnerable to apparent modularity.

A policy can look locally separable while altering:

- staffing;
- reporting;
- incentives;
- exception handling;
- family burden;
- frontline behavior.

## 10.7 Recomposition Bridge

```text
local evidence
→ institutional rule
```

## 10.8 Bridge Burden

Very high.

Institutional amplification changes:

- scale;
- persistence;
- reversibility;
- affected population;
- burden distribution.

## 10.9 Remainder

At each upward move:

- context is stripped;
- exceptions disappear;
- local uncertainty may be compressed;
- provenance may weaken.

This can accumulate as **remainder debt**.

## 10.10 Authorization Boundary

Central.

A local actor may have epistemic standing without regulatory authority.

A regulator may have authority while lacking local knowledge.

The system therefore requires explicit bridge governance.

## 10.11 Failure Detection

All five failure types are active.

Especially:

- ND-F1;
- ND-F3;
- ND-F4;
- ND-F5.

## 10.12 Return

Institutional Return requires feedback channels that can reopen:

- policy;
- metric;
- workflow;
- authority assumptions.

### Verdict

**NEAR-DECOMPOSABILITY: LOW at scale-up boundaries.**

Local subsystems may be tractable, but institutional recomposition is high-burden by default.

This is one of the strongest confirmations of the Simon–FSP synthesis.

# 11. Case G — Psychedelic / Neural / Phenomenal Inference

## 11.1 Target Claim

How should receptor, neural-dynamics, subjective phenomenology, therapeutic outcome, and enduring value/identity change be related without collapsing them into one explanatory chain?

## 11.2 Candidate Decomposition

A useful current map is:

```text
5-HT2A receptor action
→ altered network dynamics / entropy / connectivity
→ relaxed high-level priors / altered self-processing
→ acute phenomenology
→ integration / context
→ later behavior, values, symptoms, identity
```

This map is heuristically powerful.

It is also a high-risk recomposition pathway.

## 11.3 Strong Couplings

Potentially strong:

- pharmacology ↔ network dynamics;
- network dynamics ↔ predictive/self-processing;
- phenomenology ↔ expectancy;
- phenomenology ↔ therapeutic setting;
- suggestibility ↔ social framing;
- acute experience ↔ integration;
- prior identity structure ↔ later change;
- plasticity ↔ environment during consolidation.

The later the chain, the stronger contextual coupling becomes.

## 11.4 Weak or Temporarily Negligible Couplings

For receptor pharmacology, biography may be temporarily negligible.

For long-term value or identity change, it cannot be.

For fMRI preprocessing, first-person sacredness may not be required.

For explaining noetic experience, preprocessing alone is radically insufficient.

## 11.5 Safe Compression

Safe:

> Psychedelics can alter receptor-level signaling, network dynamics, and reported phenomenology, and these domains can be related through empirical bridges.

Unsafe:

> A receptor or network state explains the intrinsic meaning, truth, or value of the experience.

Also unsafe:

> A mystical-type report establishes metaphysical truth.

## 11.6 Decomposition Failure Risk

There are several.

### Neural reduction

```text
neural correlate
→ experience explained
```

### Phenomenal inflation

```text
felt noetic certainty
→ proposition is true
```

### Clinical overreach

```text
acute phenomenology
→ durable therapeutic benefit
```

### Cultural erasure

```text
questionnaire score
→ universal phenomenology
```

### Integration blindness

```text
acute plasticity
→ stable beneficial reorganization
```

without tracking post-session context.

## 11.7 Recomposition Bridges

At least five distinct bridges must remain separate:

```text
B1 receptor → network dynamics
B2 network dynamics → self/predictive organization
B3 self/predictive organization → lived phenomenology
B4 acute phenomenology → therapeutic/behavioral outcome
B5 acute change → durable identity/value reorganization
```

Each has a different evidence topology.

## 11.8 Bridge Burden

- B1: moderate;
- B2: moderate-high;
- B3: very high;
- B4: high;
- B5: very high.

The burden increases as claims move from mechanism toward meaning, enduring value, and identity.

## 11.9 fMRI Sub-Bridge

Even within the neural layer:

```text
neural activity
→ hemodynamic response
→ scanner signal
→ preprocessing
→ statistical model
→ network interpretation
```

This is itself a multi-bridge evidence chain.

Thus “the brain showed X” is already a compression.

## 11.10 Questionnaire Sub-Bridge

Similarly:

```text
lived experience
→ remembered report
→ questionnaire item
→ construct score
→ group statistic
```

A mystical-experience score is useful compression.

It is not final ontology.

## 11.11 Phenomenal Remainder

Even with excellent receptor, fMRI, behavioral, and questionnaire data, remain:

- intrinsic qualitative character;
- lived meaning;
- truth-status of noetic interpretations;
- personal identity significance;
- cultural interpretation;
- long-term integration dynamics.

## 11.12 Authorization Boundary

Especially important because psychedelic interventions alter:

- experience;
- suggestibility;
- identity;
- value salience.

Therefore:

> **Therapeutic promise ≠ authorization to shape belief or identity without explicit governance.**

The distinction between autonomy-supportive exploration and external-field capture becomes relevant here.

## 11.13 Consequential Return

Return must include:

- post-acute integration;
- stability or brittleness of change;
- autonomy;
- generalization beyond the therapeutic field;
- adverse effects;
- revised goals;
- REOPEN if initial coherence does not persist.

## 11.14 Failure Detection

All five failure types occur.

The most distinctive are:

- ND-F3 Bad Recomposition;
- ND-F4 Bad Authorization;
- ND-F5 Bad Return.

## 11.15 Verdict

**NEAR-DECOMPOSABILITY: HIGH inside narrow measurement/mechanism modules; LOW across neural→phenomenal→meaning→identity bridges.**

This is the strongest case against treating “multilevel explanation” as automatic integration.

# 13. First Major Result — Near-Decomposability Is Transition-Specific

The seven cases reject the idea that near-decomposability should classify whole systems.

The same case often contains:

- low-burden local transitions;
- medium-burden cross-scale transitions;
- very high-burden normative or phenomenal transitions.

Therefore:

> **Near-decomposability belongs on the transition, not merely on the system.**

This is the strongest candidate conceptual gain from the stress test.

However, it does **not** yet require a new Core object.

It can be represented through existing:

- relation;
- transition;
- bridge burden;
- target binding;
- remainder.

---

# 14. Second Major Result — Recomposition Is Often More Dangerous Than Decomposition

Across the seven cases, bad decomposition matters.

But bad recomposition appears even more often.

Examples:

- anomaly topology → universal philosophy of science;
- engineering evidence → launch decision;
- navigation competence → phenomenal character;
- population evidence → patient treatment;
- local adaptation → general intelligence theory;
- local safety success → regulation;
- neural dynamics → metaphysical or identity conclusion.

Thus:

> **FSP’s distinctive diagnostic value may lie less in decomposing complexity than in governing recomposition.**

This sharpens the Simon comparator.

Simon strongly motivates decomposition.

FSP’s strongest addition is recomposition governance.

---

# 15. Third Major Result — Claim Ceilings Track Coupling

As coupling increases, claim ceilings generally need to tighten.

Not because strong coupling makes knowledge impossible.

But because more relations must be preserved for the inference to remain valid.

This suggests:

```text
greater hidden coupling
→ greater bridge burden
→ stronger need for decompression
→ tighter provisional claim ceiling
```

This remains a qualitative relation.

No scalar coupling metric is yet justified.

---

# 16. Fourth Major Result — Strong Coupling Is Not the Same as High Burden

These must remain separate.

A transition can be:

- strongly coupled but well-understood;
- weakly coupled but poorly calibrated;
- mechanistically simple but normatively high-burden;
- computationally tractable but phenomenally high-burden.

Therefore:

> **Coupling strength ≠ bridge burden.**

Bridge burden also depends on:

- target claim;
- evidence;
- ontology;
- authorization;
- consequence;
- translation.

This is an important firewall.

---

# 17. Fifth Major Result — Time Scale Matters

Several cases change decomposability over time.

### Challenger

A local technical concern becomes high-consequence when compressed into a launch decision.

### Medicine

An acute response may be locally decomposable; long-term treatment effects may not be.

### Planaria

History changes future response.

### Institutional safety

Rules sediment and generate new feedback.

### Psychedelics

Acute neural/phenomenal change may be more tractable than durable identity/value reorganization.

Therefore:

> **Near-decomposability at time t ≠ near-decomposability across the full trajectory.**

Temporal horizon should be treated as part of target binding.

---

# 18. Sixth Major Result — Return Detects Hidden Coupling

A system may appear decomposable until consequences return.

This is especially visible in:

- medicine;
- institutions;
- transformation;
- psychedelics.

Unexpected Return can reveal couplings omitted by the original map.

Therefore:

> **Return is not only consequence tracking; it is an empirical detector of failed decomposition.**

This strengthens Return’s constitutional status.

---

# 19. Compression Safety Test

The seven cases support a practical test.

Compression is provisionally safe when:

1. target binding is explicit;
2. omitted relations are unlikely to affect the current claim;
3. local evidence remains valid under the compressed boundary;
4. recomposition path is known;
5. bridge burden is low or visible;
6. remainder is recorded;
7. consequences are limited or reversible;
8. Return can expose error.

Compression becomes unsafe when:

- omitted coupling changes the claim;
- the claim crosses scales or ontologies;
- authorization changes at the boundary;
- affected parties disappear;
- institutional amplification increases persistence;
- phenomenology is inferred from function;
- long-term capability or identity is altered;
- Return is blocked.

---

# 20. Decompression Trigger Revision

The constitutional decompression trigger should now explicitly include:

> **suspected coupling instability**

and:

> **recomposition across a boundary where local validity may not survive.**

This is a refinement of the parent build, not a new constitutional primitive.

---

# 21. Bad Decomposition vs Bad Recomposition

The stress test supports a stronger diagnostic pair.

## Bad Decomposition

The map cuts across a relation that matters.

Question:

> What did we separate that must remain connected for this claim?

## Bad Recomposition

The analysis reunites local results as though no transformation occurred across the boundary.

Question:

> What changed when we carried this result into the next scale, representation, role, or action?

This pair may become an important teaching and research device.

---

# 22. Case-Specific Compression Failures

## Neptune/Vulcan

Failure if historical analogy becomes universal sameness.

## Challenger

Failure if technical and institutional maps are separated at the decision boundary.

## Bat

Failure if functional structure is recomposed as phenomenal identity.

## Medicine

Failure if population and patient are treated as the same evidential target.

## Planaria

Failure if organism-level success becomes proof of preferred mechanism or agency theory.

## Institutional safety

Failure if local policy success becomes universal infrastructure without preserving context.

## Psychedelics

Failure if receptor, neural, phenomenal, therapeutic, and identity maps are treated as one explanatory chain.

The stress test does **not** force a new constitutional primitive.

Every detected problem can still be governed using:

```text

Target Binding
Bridge Burden
Claim Ceiling
Remainder
Authorization

```

Therefore:

> **FSP-CONSTITUTIONAL-COMPRESSION-1 survives the seven-case near-decomposability stress test.**

The test does suggest one optional research aid:

```text
CouplingAnnotation = {
  transition,
  targetClaim,
  couplingStatus,
  timeHorizon,
  evidence,
  consequenceSensitivity,
  uncertainty
}
```

But this object should **not** be added yet.

Why?

Because current BridgeBurden + TargetBinding + Relation records may already perform the needed work.

Under the No Decorative Type rule:

> **Coupling annotation remains a candidate view, not a new object.**

---

# 25. Simon Comparator Verdict

The stress test strengthens Simon’s role.

Near-decomposability genuinely helps diagnose:

- where local maps are tractable;
- where subsystem boundaries are useful;
- where time-scale separation permits compression.

But it does not by itself govern:

- recomposition;
- authorization;
- standing;
- phenomenological overreach;
- institutional amplification;
- Return.

Thus the Simon–FSP relation survives.

# 26. Strongest Supported Cross-Case Claim

> **Across seven heterogeneous cases, near-decomposability is useful when treated as claim- and transition-relative rather than as a global property of a domain. Local compression is often justified, but recomposition across scale, representation, institution, patient, phenomenology, or identity introduces new burdens that cannot be inferred from local success alone. FSP’s existing Bridge, Claim Ceiling, Remainder, Authorization, REOPEN, and Return machinery detects these burdens without requiring expansion of the constitutional Core.**

# 28. REOPEN Conditions

Reopen this architecture if:

1. a future case requires a distinction not representable through current bridge governance;
2. coupling annotation repeatedly changes judgment in ways BridgeBurden cannot capture;
3. strong-coupling cases show that the Relation–Transition grammar hides rather than clarifies dynamics;
4. a domain supplies a robust quantitative coupling measure that materially improves claim ceilings;
5. Simonian near-decomposability proves irrelevant to successful compression;
6. Return fails to reveal omitted coupling in consequential cases;
7. the decomposition/recomposition distinction duplicates an existing FSP failure pattern without added value.

# 34. Final Statement

Simon gives us a reason to trust selective decomposition.

The seven cases give us a reason not to trust recomposition automatically.

The emerging architecture is:

```text
COMPLEX SITUATION
↓
CLAIM-RELATIVE DECOMPOSITION
↓
LOCAL MAP
↓
LOCAL WARRANT
↓
BOUNDARY CROSSING
↓
RECOMPOSITION BURDEN
↓
CLAIM CEILING / REMAINDER
↓
AUTHORIZATION
↓
CONSEQUENCE
↓
RETURN
↓
TEST WHETHER THE ORIGINAL DECOMPOSITION WAS ADEQUATE
```

The central result is:

> **A partial map may be good enough locally and still become dangerous when carried across the wrong boundary.**

And the FSP discipline is:

> **Compress where relations are weak enough for the claim. Decompress where coupling becomes consequential. Govern every recomposition.**
