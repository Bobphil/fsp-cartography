# FSP-NORMATIVE-PROFILE-1

## Formal Grammar vs Adopted Ethical Constitution, PRF-Relative Standing Definitions, Representability Without Endorsement, Moral-Standing Provenance, Classification Firewall, and REOPEN of Normative Commitments

**Status:** CONSTITUTIONAL CLARIFICATION — VERSION 1  
**Role:** Distinguish FSP's thin governance grammar from substantive normative profiles adopted within that grammar.  
**Scope:** Moral standing, classification, endorsement, competing PRF moral grammars, provenance, and REOPEN.  
**Primary claim ceiling:** FSP can govern normative disagreement without claiming that one substantive moral constitution is built irrevisably into the formal grammar.

---

# 1. Why this clarification is necessary

Recent FSP work has increasingly relied on distinctions such as:

- standing;
- legitimacy;
- authorization;
- reciprocal recognition;
- capability;
- care;
- anti-domination;
- moral worth.

These are philosophically important.

But a danger has emerged.

If FSP treats one current ethical projection as though it were silently built into the formal architecture itself, the framework risks confusing:

1. the grammar that governs inquiry;
2. a substantive ethical constitution adopted within that grammar;
3. competing PRF-relative moral grammars.

This document separates them.

---

# 2. Three layers

## Layer A — Thin formal governance grammar

The thin grammar includes constraints such as:

> **Map ≠ World ≠ Person.**

> **Evidence ≠ warrant.**

> **Recommendation ≠ authorization.**

> **Perspective representation ≠ endorsement.**

> **Standing must be typed rather than assumed.**

> **Remainder must remain representable.**

> **Claim ceilings constrain transition strength.**

> **REOPEN remains available.**

These are governance rules for making inquiry inspectable.

They do not by themselves determine who possesses ultimate moral standing.

---

## Layer B — Adopted normative profile

A community, investigator, course, application, or implementation may adopt a substantive ethical constitution.

For example:

```text
NORMATIVE PROFILE NP-HUMAN-RECIPROCITY
--------------------------------------
Commitment:
All human beings possess moral standing.

Supporting orientation:
Reciprocity
Phenomenal personhood
Care
Capability
Non-domination
Historical ethical inquiry

Operational consequences:
Standing cannot be removed merely because another PRF fails to recognize it.
Classification cannot by itself erase moral worth.
Authorization must respect this standing.
```

This is a genuine normative commitment.

It is not merely descriptive.

But it is not treated as unrevisable metaphysical structure.

---

## Layer C — Competing PRF-relative normative grammars

Different PRFs may define moral membership differently.

Examples:

```text
NP-A:
all human beings have full moral standing

NP-B:
only citizens have full standing

NP-C:
only members of a religious community have full standing

NP-D:
standing extends to humans, animals, and ecosystems

NP-E:
standing depends on rational agency

NP-F:
standing depends on relational membership
```

FSP can represent each grammar.

Representation does not imply endorsement.

# 3. Representability without endorsement

FSP must be able to model normative systems that it rejects under a currently adopted ethical profile.

So:

> **Representable normative grammar ≠ FSP-endorsed normative grammar.**

A morally exclusionary PRF may still be represented in detail:

- classification rules;
- reasons;
- authority sources;
- commitments;
- entitlements;
- recognition structures;
- task spaces;
- institutions;
- social power;
- historical effects.

This is necessary for accurate inquiry.

But accuracy of representation does not confer legitimacy.

---

# 4. Current FSP normative commitment is profile-bound

When this implementation says:

> all human beings possess moral standing

that statement should be understood as:

> **Under the currently adopted normative profile, all human beings are treated as possessing moral standing.**

The statement is:

- explicit;
- substantive;
- operational;
- revisable under governed REOPEN.

It is not:

- hidden;
- metaphysically neutral;
- necessarily universal;
- permanently frozen.

---

# 5. Strong commitment and REOPEN are compatible

REOPEN does not mean present neutrality.

A normative profile can be strongly adopted now.

It can guide:

- standing;
- refusal;
- authorization;
- institutional limits;
- capability protection;
- anti-domination.

And still remain open to revision.

So:

> **REOPENability ≠ normative neutrality.**

And:

> **Current commitment ≠ final closure.**

This is structurally similar to scientific judgment.

A claim can be strongly supported without being unrevisable.

# 6. Provenance of moral standing

Every adopted moral-standing rule should carry provenance.

A normative profile should record:

- who adopts it;
- what philosophical traditions support it;
- which historical cases shaped it;
- which experiences motivate it;
- which ethical Tools support it;
- what counterexamples have been considered;
- what its claim ceiling is;
- what would REOPEN it.

Example:

```text
Profile:
NP-HUMAN-RECIPROCITY

Standing rule:
All human beings possess moral standing.

Provenance:
Reciprocity
Phenomenal-node interpretation
Kantian respect
Care
Capability
Anti-domination
Historical failures of exclusion
Brandom comparator
History of Ethics

Claim ceiling:
Does not settle all questions about degree, priority, conflict, nonhuman standing, or metaphysical personhood.

REOPEN:
Better argument
Counterexample
Internal contradiction
Expanded standing theory
Future-agent case
Nonhuman case
Empirical or conceptual failure
```

# 7. Classification Firewall

Normative exclusion often begins with classification.

Examples include:

- "not fully human";
- "outsider";
- "enemy";
- "heretic";
- "incapable";
- "criminal class";
- "uncivilized";
- "nonperson";
- "undeserving."

A category can become the bridge to a moral conclusion.

So FSP requires a distinct Firewall:

> **PRF-defined classification ≠ independently established moral standing.**

Or more compactly:

> **Classification ≠ moral standing determination.**

A classification may be:

- descriptively useful;
- legally operative;
- culturally important;
- institutionally enforced.

But it does not automatically license moral exclusion.

---

# 8. Classification provenance

Every consequential classification should expose:

- category definition;
- source;
- classifier;
- institutional use;
- affected persons;
- evidence;
- inferred properties;
- normative consequences;
- contestability;
- alternative classifications;
- remainder.

This is especially important when classification changes standing.

---

# 9. Human-being definitions are themselves PRF-relative

Different PRFs may define "human being" differently.

They may appeal to:

- biology;
- theology;
- ancestry;
- citizenship;
- rationality;
- legal status;
- ethnicity;
- caste;
- lineage;
- culture;
- embodiment;
- personhood.

FSP should not hide this variation.

It should represent:

```text
PRF
→ category definition
→ standing rule
→ authorization consequences
```

The category definition becomes inspectable.

---

# 10. Category membership and moral standing must remain separate

Even if a category definition is internally coherent, the moral inference requires a second step.

```text
X does not satisfy category C
```

does not automatically imply:

```text
X lacks moral standing
```

That transition requires a normative warrant.

So:

> **Category non-membership ≠ moral exclusion.**

This is one of the most important gates in the profile architecture.

---

# 11. Multiple profile comparison

FSP can compare normative profiles without forcing premature convergence.

For profiles NP-A and NP-B:

```text
NP-A:
category rule
standing rule
warrant
consequences

NP-B:
category rule
standing rule
warrant
consequences
```

Comparison asks:

- what differs?
- what is shared?
- what classifications do the work?
- who gains/loses standing?
- what evidence is relevant?
- which warrants are invoked?
- what consequences follow?
- what is sedimented?
- what would REOPEN each profile?

---

# 12. No neutral God's-eye profile

FSP should not claim to occupy a view from nowhere.

Every normative audit occurs from some profile.

Therefore:

> **Normative audit ≠ perspective-free judgment.**

The audit must expose:

- profile identity;
- profile provenance;
- standing rules;
- classification rules;
- claim ceiling.

This makes normative comparison more transparent.

---

# 13. But profile-relativity is not relativism

The existence of multiple profiles does not imply that all are equally good.

A profile can be criticized for:

- contradiction;
- unsupported classification;
- factual error;
- arbitrary exclusion;
- domination;
- capability destruction;
- incoherent authorization;
- consequences;
- inability to survive its own reciprocity test.

So:

> **Profile relativity ≠ normative equivalence.**

The framework supports disagreement while preserving criticism.

---

# 14. Brandomian recognition and normative profiles

The Brandom comparator showed that recognition communities can institute normative statuses internally.

FSP now distinguishes:

```text
recognition within PRF/community
≠
profile-independent moral truth
```

A community may recognize only its members.

That fact is socially real.

It may stabilize cooperation.

But under NP-HUMAN-RECIPROCITY, the exclusion does not erase the standing of outsiders.

---

# 15. Social recognition vs adopted moral standing

Suppose Community A denies moral standing to Group B.

FSP records:

```text
Community A:
B lacks standing

Institution A:
B has no formal standing

Effective reality:
B may have little power

NP-HUMAN-RECIPROCITY:
B retains moral standing
```

These are four different facts.

They must not be collapsed.

# 16. Competing task spaces remain representable

A morally exclusionary community may still coordinate.

It can form:

- political organizations;
- economic networks;
- religious institutions;
- cultural institutions;
- militias;
- bureaucracies;
- media systems;
- educational systems.

FSP can represent these task spaces.

But:

> **Functional coordination ≠ legitimate normative status.**

The current profile may reject the group's exclusions while still modeling its social effectiveness.

---

# 17. Normative profile affects JTS legitimacy

A Joint Task Space has at least two evaluations.

## Functional evaluation

Can participants coordinate effectively?

## Normative-profile evaluation

Is the task legitimate under the adopted profile?

A JTS may pass the first and fail the second.

Thus:

> **JTS functionality ≠ JTS legitimacy.**

# 22. Formal grammar remains thinner

The formal grammar does not say:

```text

```

as an unrevisable primitive.

That is the thinner governance layer.

# 26. REOPEN does not suspend protection automatically

A challenge to the profile does not immediately remove current protections.

Until a profile is revised:

- standing remains active;
- authorization constraints remain active;
- institutional protections remain active.

So:

> **Challenge ≠ automatic suspension.**

This prevents REOPEN from becoming governance instability.

This preserves:

> **Cooperation without convergence.**

But profile conflict may also block legitimate shared tasks.


At that point, a once-explicit ethical commitment may become invisible.

This is why profile provenance must survive institutional amplification.

# 44. Final constitutional formulation

FSP should neither hide its ethical commitments nor pretend they are beyond revision.

It should say what profile it is using.

It should say why.

It should show how that profile affects standing and authorization.

It should represent competing grammars accurately.

It should distinguish representation from endorsement.

It should preserve the possibility of criticism.

And it should keep its own moral constitution open to reasoned revision.

The central constitutional sequence is:

> **Thin grammar → explicit normative profile → typed standing/classification → warranted judgment → authorization → consequence → sedimentation → REOPEN.**

The deepest Firewall is:

> **A moral grammar can govern strongly without pretending to be the final grammar of morality.**
