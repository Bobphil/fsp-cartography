# FSP-HUMAN-FIDELITY-1A

## First Live Session Packet, Consent/Facilitator Script, Participant Record Form, Coding Sheet, and Trial-Run Execution

**Status:** Operational first-session packet  
**Protocol:** FSP-HUMAN-FIDELITY-1.0  
**Packet version:** FSP-HUMAN-FIDELITY-1A.0

## 1. Purpose

This packet prepares the first real participant session for the FSP seven-view human-fidelity trial.

It is designed to test whether the seven-view journey preserves:

- the participant's own lived situation;
- the participant's own wording;
- authorship of Return;
- optional recognition moments;
- freedom from questionnaire-like form filling.

This packet does **not** itself constitute institutional ethics approval, IRB approval, recruitment authorization, or empirical human evidence.

## 2. Participant information / consent script

Suggested facilitator language:

> You are being invited to try a research prototype designed to help a person map and reflect on a situation they are already dealing with. The purpose of this session is to learn how the experience feels to you—not to evaluate you, diagnose you, or tell you what decision to make.
>
> Please choose a real situation from your own life that is meaningful enough to explore but does not require you to disclose anything private or sensitive. You do not need to discuss medical, legal, sexual, financial, employment, or other sensitive details.
>
> You may skip any question or section. You may leave a section sparse or empty if it does not fit your situation. You may stop the session at any time. There is no requirement to reach a conclusion.
>
> The system may organize what you say, but it should not decide for you. If the wording feels wrong, please correct it. Your correction should take priority over the system's wording.
>
> After the journey, I will ask several questions about whether the map felt like your situation, whether it felt like filling out a form, whether the final judgment felt like yours, and whether anything important was missing or distorted.
>
> Your participation is voluntary. Do you have any questions before deciding whether to continue?

Consent checklist:

- voluntary participation confirmed;
- participant-owned lived case confirmed;
- no sensitive disclosure required;
- right to skip confirmed;
- right to withdraw confirmed;
- data use explained;
- questions answered;
- recording permission separately obtained if recording is used;
- consent confirmed before beginning.

## 3. Facilitator script

### BEFORE

Confirm consent, voluntary participation, skip/withdraw rights, and the non-sensitive-disclosure boundary.

Do **not** explain the REVISION research vocabulary.

Do **not** tell the participant what recognition responses are expected.

### START

Ask:

> What are you trying to work through?

If the task is not formed, accept that.

Ask:

> Do you have a clear task yet, or are you still orienting?

Preserve the participant's wording.

### PEOPLE

Ask:

> Who matters in this situation?

Then:

> What did they actually say or do, and what are you inferring?

Do not treat an inference as a report.

### INSTITUTION

Ask:

> What institutions, rules, or constraints matter here?

Then:

> Which constraints are verified, assumed, or still unclear?

Permit an empty or sparse view.

### H/S/F

Ask:

> What is inherited from the past, what matters now, and what may matter going forward?

Do not force historical depth when it is not useful.

### CONNECTIONS

Ask:

> How are the people and institutions connected?

Then:

> Where is there practical power, authority, dependence, or a possible shared task?

Do not force a shared task if none is present.

### INVESTIGATE

Ask:

> What possibilities are still live?

Then:

> What would help you learn more before committing?

Use progressive disclosure only if the case itself creates pressure.

### RETURN

Ask:

> What is your current bounded judgment?

Then:

> What are you not claiming yet, and what would make you reconsider?

Do not recommend the answer.

## 4. Recognition elicitation

After the journey, ask the recognition questions without telling the participant that recognition should occur.

### THERE_I_AM

> Was there any point where a part of the map felt especially accurate or recognizable to you? If so, what made it feel that way?

Record:

- YES / PARTLY / NO / NOT_SURE / DECLINED;
- participant verbatim;
- whether recognition was spontaneous or prompted.

### YES_HERE_I_AM

> Did the larger map help you locate where you were in the situation, or did it make the situation feel more abstract?

Record verbatim.

### I_SEE_ME

> Looking back across the journey, did you notice any change in how you understood your own thinking or situation?

Record verbatim.

## 5. Form-filling probe

Ask directly:

> Did any part feel like filling out a form rather than thinking through your situation? Which part?

Then code separately:

- felt forced to complete;
- repeated self unnecessarily;
- prompts fit situation;
- could leave views sparse;
- system seemed to decide for me.

## 6. Journey interview

Use the frozen eight questions:

1. Did the map continue to feel like it was about the same situation you started with? What changed, if anything?
2. Where did the system preserve your own words well, and where did it feel like your meaning had been changed?
3. Was there any point where a part of the map felt especially accurate or recognizable to you? If so, what made it feel that way?
4. Did the larger map help you locate where you were in the situation, or did it make the situation feel more abstract?
5. Looking back across the journey, did you notice any change in how you understood your own thinking or situation?
6. Did any part feel like filling out a form rather than thinking through your situation? Which part?
7. At the end, did the judgment feel like yours, the system's, or something in between? Why?
8. What important part of the situation was missing, distorted, or hard to represent?
