# FSP-FOUR-MOVE-GOVERNANCE-1

## Portability, Claim Ceiling, Authorization, Return; Remainder as Cross-Cutting Invariant; Projection-Fidelity and Semantic-Drift Test

**Status:** COMPRESSION / PROJECTION-GOVERNANCE BUILD — VERSION 1  
**Parents:** FSP-GOVERNANCE-RESIDUE-REGRESSION-1; FSP-GOVERNANCE-RESIDUE-1; FSP-CONSTITUTIONAL-COMPRESSION-1; FSP-CT-CANONICAL-1.1; FSP-PROJECTION-PROFILES-1  
**Primary purpose:** Test whether the compressed four-move form can replace the six-role governance residue in research records, the public reader, the seven-view application, ethics workbooks, and Encyclopedia architecture without semantic drift.  
**Four moves:** Portability → Claim Ceiling → Authorization → Return  
**Invariant:** Remainder remains visible throughout.  
**Primary claim ceiling:** This build does not replace the full FSP Constitution, canonical Tool ecology, Failure Atlas, or domain-native methods. It defines a minimal projection grammar and a recovery contract back to the richer architecture.

---

# 1. Why a Four-Move Build Is Needed

The preceding regression found that the six-role residue could be reduced.

Failure Routing remained useful but was demoted to operational support.

Remainder remained indispensable but was better treated as a cross-cutting invariant than as a sequential stage.

The resulting candidate was:

```text
PORTABILITY
→ CEILING
→ AUTHORIZATION
→ RETURN
↺

REMAINDER: ALWAYS VISIBLE
```

The present build asks a harder question:

> **Can this form actually carry FSP across its major projections without hiding distinctions the larger architecture was built to protect?**

Compression is useful only if the richer structure can be recovered when needed.

---

# 2. The Four Moves

## Move 1 — Portability

Question:

> **Will the warrant travel?**

Portability asks whether a locally warranted result remains warranted when carried into a changed:

- target;
- scale;
- representation;
- population;
- institution;
- role;
- ontology;
- time horizon;
- action context.

Portability is not suspicion.

A crossing may be fully warranted.

The role is to make the crossing inspectable.

---

## Move 2 — Claim Ceiling

Question:

> **How far may the claim go?**

The claim ceiling states the strongest conclusion licensed after portability has been tested.

Examples:

- association ≠ causation;
- prediction ≠ mechanism;
- functional structure ≠ phenomenal identity;
- population evidence ≠ guaranteed patient outcome;
- local success ≠ universal policy.

The Ceiling is not generic caution.

It is the bounded arrival after a transition.

---

## Move 3 — Authorization

Question:

> **Who may act?**

Authorization asks whether a warranted judgment licenses:

- recommendation;
- classification;
- intervention;
- delegation;
- institutionalization;
- constraint;
- policy;
- implementation.

The firewall remains:

> **Warranted judgment ≠ authorized action.**

Authorization may require consent, standing, role, jurisdiction, proportionality, reversibility, and affected-party review.

---

## Move 4 — Return

Question:

> **What happened, and what should reopen?**

Return is consequential re-entry into lived Doing.

It includes:

- observed effects;
- changed capabilities;
- changed burdens;
- repair;
- revised self-location where relevant;
- successor conditions;
- REOPEN triggers.

Return is not reporting.

It closes the loop by changing the next inquiry state.

---

# 3. The Remainder Invariant

Remainder is not a fifth move.

It is the standing question:

> **What did not cross?**

Remainder may arise during:

- observation;
- measurement;
- translation;
- inference;
- scale crossing;
- authorization;
- implementation;
- Return.

Thus:

```text
PORTABILITY [remainder]
→ CEILING [remainder]
→ AUTHORIZATION [remainder]
→ RETURN [remainder]
↺
```

The invariant is:

> **What does not cross must remain visible.**

---

# 4. Why Remainder Must Remain Cross-Cutting

If Remainder were placed only after Ceiling, FSP would risk treating unresolved content as a late-stage epistemic leftover.

But remainder can include:

- unobserved features before inference;
- translation loss before claim formation;
- standing ambiguity before authorization;
- unanticipated consequences after implementation.

Therefore:

> **Remainder is a state of incomplete carry-forward, not one chronological step.**
